# R02 — Let the agent decide when to ask, defer to or hand control to the user
_parent: **Let the Agent Decide When to Involve the User** ([`agent_initiated_user_interaction`](../main/agent_initiated_user_interaction.md)) · status: candidate_pattern · domain: board-induced (2026-10-19), from wave-1 weak fits_

## Operation
give the agent an explicit channel to the user — an ASK action that poses a follow-up question, a per-step confidence score that triggers human intervention below a threshold, or a suggest-then-execute loop in which the user can pause, reject, take over and resume — feed the user's answer or actions back into the agent's history, and score both task success and the asking behaviour (timing, precision/false positives, intervention rate, human step share)

- Transfer: mixed-initiative interaction from HCI/dialogue (clarification questions, learning to defer) applied to GUI step loops; ambiguity is created by deleting instruction content, and need-to-ask labels come from human annotators or a strong critic model.

- Status: candidate_pattern (4 member papers)

## When it works
- The ask decision is a trained, explicit output: with an ASK action and dual-stream evaluation, step success recovers to up to 99.4% of the full-instruction level on AndroidControl, with ASK timing precision up to 0.947 and false-positive rate ≤ 0.005 (2503.24180#board)
- A per-step confidence score is learned and thresholded, with the threshold swept: on its complex-scenario set TSR is 88.20 vs 14.29 for the probed OS-Atlas-Pro-7B, and on real devices TSR rises 32 -> 70 when the requested human help is given; a fine-tuned interaction beats prompt-based asking (HSR 86.87 vs 0.00 for prompted OS-Atlas-Pro-7B) (2503.16465#board)
- Collaboration is measured against both fully autonomous and human-only modes with human effort counted: copilot mode reaches 0.95 success vs 0.48 autonomous and 0.89 human-only, with humans taking 1.14 of 7.50 steps on average (2501.16609#board)
- Need-to-ask labels are validated: inter-annotator kappa 0.64 and necessity-score correlation 0.78 on 200 doubly annotated episodes; LLM-generated informative-step decisions pass human verification at 90% (2503.19537#board, 2503.24180#board)

## When it fails
- The user is an oracle or a simulation: requested interventions are answered with the ground-truth action, follow-up answers are LLM-generated QA pairs, so success with help credits the oracle rather than the agent (2503.16465#board, 2503.24180#board)
- Prompted detection over-asks: precision 0.08-0.19 against recall up to 0.84, from rigid unnecessary confirmations and navigation questions; over-asking cost is not counted where success is the only metric (2503.19537#board)
- Confidence labels come from a proprietary critic and depend on it: replacing GPT-4o with Qwen-VL-Max drops HSR 86.87 -> 57.63 and causes more interventions (2503.16465#board)
- Evaluation is offline or tiny: single-reference offline trajectories mis-score early ASKs (step success before the ASK step drops without the dual-stream fix) and Mind2Web SR recovers only to ~69-78%; the live study is 27 WebArena tasks with three authors as the humans and a possible ordering bias (2503.24180#board, 2501.16609#board)
- Scope is narrow: at most one interaction per episode, a single omitted step of information, and the agent is assumed never to need navigation help (2503.19537#board, 2503.24180#board)

## Counterexamples
- What counts as a reason to ask is contested: one formulation forbids asking for navigation help and counts only information, confirmation and infeasibility, while another asks precisely when the agent's own action confidence is low (2503.19537#board, 2503.16465#board)
- A weak backbone shifts work to the human rather than gaining autonomy: with Llama-8B in copilot mode humans take 4.15 of 8.92 steps and agent-driven completion is 0.05 (2501.16609#board)
