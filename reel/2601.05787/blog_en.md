# BEPA: turning a small pool of framework-agent traces into on-policy guidance for an end-to-end GUI agent

*A CUA Radar reading of arXiv 2601.05787v2 (Wang, Zhang, Zhang, Qian, Lu). This is a third-party summary of what the paper reports, not an endorsement by the authors. Poster: `poster.png`. Paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05787v2 · Code: https://github.com/LEON-gittech/Verl_GUI.git*

## The problem: experts the learner cannot imitate

On OSWorld-Verified, the paper reports that framework-based agents such as Agent S2 and Jedi-7B with o3 reach 33.00 to 42.40 percent success, while specialised end-to-end (E2E) policies such as UITARS1.5-7B and OpenCUA-7B sit around 23 to 24 percent. OSWorld-Verified also exposes only a few hundred interactive, verifiable tasks, and expert trajectories have to be collected by running strong agents in the environments and verifying each episode. The paper asks how RLVR (reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards) can best use a small pool of such expert trajectories to train an E2E policy.

The obvious move is to mix the expert traces into on-policy RL. The paper argues this is brittle for two reasons. Structurally, framework traces interleave planner, executor and grounder roles and often use tool-level actions that a single screenshot-to-action policy cannot imitate. Distributionally, even after format conversion the traces lie far from the base policy. The authors report that static integration does not close the gap and can degrade UITARS1.5-7B below pure GRPO.

## What BEPA is

**The agent.** The agent is UITARS1.5-7B, an end-to-end desktop agent. At each step it observes a screenshot and the task instruction and emits a text trace of reasoning followed by a UI-TARS action: click, drag, scroll, type, hotkey, left double-click, right-click, wait or finished, with pixel coordinates. The paper focuses on full desktop control (OSWorld-Verified, 369 tasks, fresh VM snapshot, 15-step limit). On the CUA Radar board the pattern is K20, under “train the policy on own, replayed and corrected trajectories”.

**The mechanism.** BEPA (Bi-Level Expert-to-Policy Assimilation) is a component added to GRPO training with two levels.

- **LEVEL-1, self-rolled execution.** Each successful Agent S2 trace is abstracted into a short natural-language plan by GPT-4o. The plan is appended to the task instruction and the base policy acts in the environment itself. Rollouts that pass the OSWorld verifier form the seed set. Of 115 expert traces (the paper notes a 76% success rate on re-rolling), 88 survive. Being generated by the policy, they lie much closer to its distribution than the raw expert traces.
- **LEVEL-2, off-policy assimilation.** A per-task cache is initialised from the seed set. During GRPO training, whenever the current policy succeeds on a task, one of its successful rollouts (random by default) replaces the cached trace. When all N = 8 on-policy rollouts fail and a cached trace exists, the cached trace replaces the first failed rollout, under the original instruction without the plan prefix. This guarantees at least one positive signal in the group; advantages are group-normalised and the PPO-style clipped objective is used.

**The training signal** is a deterministic binary verifier from OSWorld. Training uses N = 8, learning rate 1e-6, 4 epochs and no KL term. The expert source is Agent S2 (115 successful traces). Training tasks: of a pool of 150 tasks solvable by Agent S2 or by the base policy at Pass@5, 120 plus 8 others form a 128-task training set; the remaining solvable tasks plus other tasks form a 241-task held-out set.

## Results as reported

All OSWorld-Verified numbers below are **online** success rates (%) with the 15-step budget. “Overall” is the mean ± std over 3 runs on all 369 tasks; the split columns are printed without std.

| Method            | Overall          | Train (128) | Held-out (241) |
|-------------------|------------------|-------------|----------------|
| UITARS1.5-7B      | 22.87 ± 0.97     | 55.12       | 5.74           |
| GRPO              | 23.60 ± 1.15     | 58.02       | 5.32           |
| Trace Replacement | 23.91 ± 2.35     | 66.50       | 1.29           |
| LUFFY             | 24.11 ± 2.10     | 65.44       | 2.16           |
| **BEPA**          | **32.13 ± 0.25** | **73.23**   | **10.30**      |

The paper states BEPA improves over UITARS1.5-7B by +9.26 points (+40.5% relative) and over GRPO by +8.53 points (+36.1% relative). SFT (17.65), SFT+RL (14.74) and RL+SFT (20.88) all land below the base policy’s 22.87. The same table lists Agent S2 at 33.00 and GUI-Owl-7B at 32.10, which are different systems.

**Online-Mind2Web** (live websites, 300 tasks, scored by the benchmark’s WebJudge with o4-mini as judge): BEPA reaches an average success rate of 28.52, against 23.28 for UITARS1.5-7B, 24.81 for GRPO and 24.79 for LUFFY. The paper presents this as a held-out benchmark; the judge itself is the benchmark’s, and the paper does not report a separate check of it.

**MMBench-GUI is offline** (single screenshots, no environment interaction) and is kept apart: on L1 content understanding, BEPA scores 90.46 (easy), 91.91 (medium) and 92.60 (hard), versus 85.43, 88.75 and 91.05 for GRPO. On L2 element grounding the average is 88.76 (basic) and 61.56 (advanced), versus 85.48 and 59.56 for GRPO. L3 and L4 are not reported because their configurations were not public at the time of writing.

## What the paper’s own controls say

**Component ablation (Table 1, overall, 3 runs).** Trace Replacement gets 23.91, the LEVEL-1 variant 27.30, the LEVEL-2 variant 29.74 and the full BEPA 32.13. On the 54-task expert-only set (expert-solvable tasks the untrained base cannot solve at Pass@5), the numbers are 18.52, 25.93, 29.18 and 35.19. The paper reads LEVEL-1 as making expert solutions reachable, with a pronounced entropy drop and limited transfer (5.05 held-out), and LEVEL-2 as keeping guidance aligned (7.48 held-out). The paper does not spell out the exact definitions of the variants beyond the method description.

**Distribution diagnostics.** On a 19-task subset where BEPA triggers cache updates, raw Agent S2 traces place 58.33% of tokens below probability 0.2 under the base policy (JS divergence to on-policy 0.2040); conversion alone gives 32.48% (0.1676); LEVEL-1 gives 10.53%; LEVEL-2 gives 19.88% against 26.95% for on-policy rollouts, with JS 0.0366. The paper calls these descriptive statistics, not a formal test.

**Sensitivity (3 runs).** Group size N = 4, 8, 16 gives 29.85 ± 1.12, 32.13 ± 0.25 and 33.40 ± 0.58. Cache update rules Random, Highest LogProb and Shortest Step give 32.13, 33.12 and 33.55; the authors call the differences marginal given the overlapping std.

**Other experts and backbones (single run).** With Agent S2 as expert BEPA reaches 32.40, with GUI-Owl 31.55 and with both 34.71, against 24.50 for GRPO on UITARS1.5-7B. On OpenCUA-7B with Agent S2, BEPA reaches 31.98 versus 24.30 for the base. The per-domain table is labelled as best single-run success rates.

## Where the evidence is thinner

- **Training and testing share the benchmark.** The 128 training tasks and the 241 held-out tasks both come from OSWorld-Verified, and the Overall number averages over both. The training split reaches 73.23 while the held-out split reaches 10.30.
- **One backbone for the main results.** The paper’s limitations state that the study instantiates BEPA only on GUI-based computer-use benchmarks, with a specific backbone, Agent S2 and GUI-Owl as expert sources and a particular plan-extraction pipeline.
- **Cost.** The paper describes its Verl-GUI training framework but gives no wall-clock or dollar cost for a run.
- **Multi-app tasks.** BEPA reaches 8 multi-application successes where Agent S2 solves 22 (Figure 15). The authors explain this by saying that when both the base policy and the expert traces are far from the target distribution, bi-level assimilation alone is insufficient.
- Baseline numbers differ slightly between Table 1 (means) and Table 7 (best single run), for example UITARS1.5-7B 22.87 versus 23.66.

## CUA Radar’s read

CUA Radar scores this paper **70 (★★★)**, evidence level **online**. The record credits the OSWorld-Verified online evaluation, standard deviations over 3 runs, strong baselines (GRPO, SFT variants, LUFFY, Trace Replacement), a component ablation and released code. Held-out environments, budget matching and cost reporting are rated as partial. The judgment also flags AP5, training and evaluation data from the same environments, and notes the single backbone with Agent S2 and GUI-Owl experts. CUA Radar did not rerun any experiment; every number here comes from the paper (version v2).
