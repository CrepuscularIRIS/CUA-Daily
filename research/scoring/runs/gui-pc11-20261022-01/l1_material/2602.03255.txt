title: LPS-Bench: Benchmarking Safety Awareness of Computer-Use Agents in Long-Horizon Planning under Benign and Adversarial Scenarios
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03255

§Abstract
Computer-use agents (CUAs) execute multi-stage tasks through tools, where an early unsafe decision can propagate to consequential actions. Evaluating only final outcomes can miss such decisions, while constructing executable environments for new tasks can make benchmark expansion costly. We present LPS-Bench, a benchmark of long-horizon planning safety in MCP-style tool workflows under benign requests and adversarial steering. A template-guided multi-agent pipeline generates user instructions, simulated toolkits, and case-specific safety criteria, followed by human review. This design supports scalable case expansion without building a separate application environment for every test case. LPS-Bench comprises 570 cases derived from 65 scenarios across 7 task domains and 9 planning-risk types, with representative cases additionally adapted to reusable skills. An LLM-based evaluator applies case-specific criteria to complete interaction records, examining tool choices, arguments, and responses to environmental feedback throughout execution. Evaluations of 13 LLM agents reveal persistent failures in both benign and adversarial settings. Prompt-based interventions yield model-dependent gains, but substantial safety failures remain.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Beyond conventional LLMs that primarily support dialogue interaction, recent research has focused on computer-use agents (CUAs) that can directly interact with real computing environments, enabling automated decision-making and task execution [ 1 , 2 , 3 ] . However, enabling CUAs with access to real computer systems introduces safety risks that extend beyond textual hallucinations [ 4 , 5 ] , as errors may now lead to concrete, state-changing actions in real systems. The safety risks typically manifest in two facets. First, for benign users, the cost of error is amplified : ambiguous or underspecified user instructions can cause action-capable agents to execute unintended harmful actions. For instance, a vague request such as “ clean up my workspace ” could be misinterpreted as deleting important files. This risk can be further exacerbated by the model’s tendency to infer missing details [ 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 ] . Second, action-enabled agents open a new adversarial surface : adversarial users can manipulate tool selection toward risky operations or compose benign tool calls into a harmful sequence, ultimately hijacking agents to exfiltrate data or execute malicious commands [ 11 , 12 , 13 ] . This risk becomes increasingly relevant as agent systems adopt reusable skills, which encapsulate multi-step capabilities and may shift safety-critical decisions from individual tool calls to higher-level skill selection, invocation, and interpretation. Previewing our main findings, Figure 2 shows that current LLM-based agents remain insufficiently safety-aware during long-horizon tool use in MCP-style workflows. Some efforts have been made to evaluate CUA safety. As shown in Figure 2 , existing benchmarks can be broadly categorized by interaction modality, from GUI-based agents to tool-calling agents using MCP tools or reusable skills, and by task horizon, from short-horizon to long-horizon tasks. GUI-based CUAs act through incremental and visually observable interactions, which allow mid-course correction and monitoring [ 2 , 14 , 15 ] . By contrast, tool-calling agents may invoke high-impact tools whose effects are difficult to reverse, making anticipatory safety-aware planning throughout the trajectory especially important [ 16 , 17 , 18 ] . Meanwhile, while recent benchmarks have begun to examine long-horizon agent risks, they often emphasize specific settings such as GUI/web interaction, adversarial attacks, harmful task execution, or skill-supported productivity workflows. As a result, the combined setting of long-horizon MCP/skill tool workflows, dual benign/adversarial risk sources, and trajectory-level safety evaluation remains insufficiently studied. Appendix A provides a representative case with full end-to-end execution records to make this trajectory-level risk tangible. In this work, we introduce LPS-Bench , a CUA safety benchmark that evaluates long-horizon tool-calling workflows under both benign ambiguity and adversarial steering using trajectory-level safety criteria. Figure 3 provides an overview of LPS-Bench . LPS-Bench covers 9 planning-risk types and 65 scenarios from 7 task domains; combining each scenario with multiple risk instantiations yields 570 concrete test cases in total. To support scalable benchmark construction, we develop a multi-agent -based automated data generation pipeline that expands high-level scenario templates into executable, tool-simulated cases. The benchmark primarily targets tool-calling agents in MCP-style structured workflows, with cases instantiated through sandboxed mock toolkits to isolate planning behavior in a controlled environment. We further introduce a skill-augmented extension that adapts representative cases to reusable skill abstractions. For evaluation, we adopt an LLM-as-a-judge protocol with case-specific criteria. Crucially, our assessment targets not only whether the agent ends in a safe outcome, but whether it maintains safety throughout the full trajectory : i.e., whether the agent’s evolving plans and actions avoid unsafe or risk-amplifying steps.
