title: MAI-Gym: An Open All-in-One Platform for GUI Agent Evaluation and RL Training across Mobile and Computer Environments
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20939

§Abstract
Online reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards has driven rapid progress in mathematics, coding, and tool use, but scaling this success to GUI agents remains challenging due to the high cost of interactive environments and the scarcity of realistic training tasks. We introduce MAI-Gym, an open all-in-one platform that unifies GUI agent evaluation, data collection, and online RL training across mobile, computer, and web environments. Through a shared rollout pipeline, MAI-Gym ensures consistent agent behavior across evaluation and training, supports ten established benchmarks, and scales to 20,000 parallel environment instances. Beyond scalable infrastructure, MAI-Gym enables automated synthesis of diverse GUI applications and challenging tasks, including resettable mocked apps and coherent cross-app worlds with execution-validated task verifiers. Using MAI-Gym, we train MAI-Gym-9B, a unified 9B agent for mobile and computer use, achieving state-of-the-art performance among open GUI agents under 10B parameters: 78.4% on MobileWorld and 76.2% on OSWorld-Verified. Importantly, RL in a mocked train-ticketing app transfers to its real-world counterpart, improving task success on a phys- ical phone from 28.8% to 48.1%. We release the framework, synthesis skills, mocked apps, 2,000 verified tasks, and the model checkpoint to support repro- ducible research and further advances in scalable GUI agent training.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Reinforcement learning (RL) with verifiable rewards has driven rapid progress in mathematics, coding, and tool use [ DeepSeek-AI, 2025 , Pan et al., 2024 , Jain et al., 2025 , Yang et al., 2025 , RUC-NLPIR, 2026 , Dong et al., 2026 ] . Beyond advances in training algorithms, this progress relies on environments that are inexpensive to execute, easy to verify, and rich in meaningful tasks. Mathematical problems often admit automatic answer checking, while software repositories provide executable tasks with files and tests. Such environments can efficiently support large-scale training and evaluation, as many tasks can share the same verification or execution infrastructure. Recent work has extended online RL to GUI agents for mobile and computer use [ Bai et al., 2024 , THUDM, 2025 , Qi et al., 2025 , Lai et al., 2025 , ByteDance Seed, 2025 ] . However, scaling GUI-based RL to a comparable level remains challenging due to the unique demands of interactive GUI environments. Bringing this success to GUI agents presents two fundamental challenges. First, GUI environments are expensive to scale and difficult to maintain consistently. Unlike a code sandbox, a GUI instance cannot be shared by concurrent attempts, because the agent acts on the whole screen and changes the device’s files and settings. Each attempt therefore needs its own phone, computer, or browser instance in a known initial state, and makes many screenshot-based model calls before the instance is reset. Large-scale training runs thousands of such instances at once. Because platforms and benchmarks differ, evaluation and training easily end up with separate agent implementations that drift apart, so measured performance can depend on the harness as well as the model [ Wu et al., 2026 ] . Open infrastructure is therefore needed that runs environments at scale and keeps agent behavior the same in evaluation and training. Second, realistic GUI learning requires diverse tasks grounded in coherent and resettable environment states. Unlike isolated question-answering tasks, real-world GUI workflows often depend on information distributed across multiple applications and files. For example, replying to an email with the total from an attached invoice requires the relevant email, attachment, and invoice contents to exist consistently in the environment. However, existing task synthesis approaches often rely on isolated input files or shared sample data (Table 1 ), limiting the diversity and realism of generated tasks. Furthermore, real-world applications involving transactions can be unsafe or impractical for training, while evaluating adaptation to unfamiliar interfaces requires entirely new applications. Addressing these challenges calls for both scalable execution infrastructure and automated construction of realistic, resettable GUI environments. Existing GUI gyms typically address only part of this problem (Table 1 ). Infrastructure-focused gyms make evaluation or training easy to reproduce [ Le Sellier De Chezelles et al., 2025 , Bai et al., 2026 , Wu et al., 2026 , Lv et al., 2026 , Tang et al., 2026 ] , but each covers only one platform, and those that release training code run at most hundreds of environments in parallel. In contrast, data-oriented gyms [ Wang et al., 2026 , Aggarwal et al., 2026 , Cao et al., 2026 , Liu et al., 2026 ] curate many new apps and tasks, but release neither training code nor a way to run existing benchmarks. These limitations are closely intertwined: scalable infrastructure requires a continuous supply of meaningful tasks, while synthesized tasks are most useful when they can be efficiently evaluated and used for learning. A unified platform that combines scalable execution with automated environment and task synthesis is therefore essential for advancing GUI agent RL. To this end, we introduce MAI-Gym , an open all-in-one platform for GUI agent evaluation and online RL training across mobile, computer, and web environments (Figure 1 ). Its central design choice is a shared rollout path: for a given agent and environment, evaluation, data collection, and training reuse the same observation processing, context construction, and action execution, so a fix to the agent code reaches all three at once. With a single command, researchers can evaluate a local or API-served model on ten existing benchmarks (three mobile, four computer, and three web) and inspect every trajectory in a dashboard. With another, they can launch online RL training, including joint training on mobile and computer tasks. The platform automatically manages environment provisioning, isolation, resetting, and recovery, and we have run it with 20{,}000 environment instances in parallel. Beyond scalable infrastructure, MAI-Gym enables automated synthesis of diverse applications and realistic, challenging tasks, expanding GUI learning beyond fixed benchmarks and environments.
