title: WebArxiv: A Reproducible Benchmark for Evaluating Multimodal Web Agents on arXiv Tasks
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00938

§Abstract
Foundation models now enable autonomous agents to interact with real-world websites, but existing benchmarks emphasize general-purpose browsing, underrepresent research-oriented environments and scholarly discovery workflows, and often depend on live sites whose changing content and structure undermine reproducibility. arXiv provides a realistic, reproducible, hierarchically structured, information-centric testbed without privacy-sensitive interactions. We introduce WebArxiv, a static-snapshot benchmark comprising 510 time-invariant tasks, each with a unique deterministic ground truth. Its diverse, realistic scholarly tasks go beyond simple information lookup and rule following to emphasize multi-constraint paper retrieval, fine-grained content extraction, and cross-paper comparison. Evaluations of a range of foundation-model-based web agents show that WebArxiv remains challenging. Behavioral analysis reveals that agents over-rely on fixed interaction histories, causing incomplete or repetitive reasoning. We therefore equip agents with a lightweight dynamic-memory mechanism for adaptive retrieval and reasoning over relevant context. The benchmark and code are available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/74E4423BVNW/README.md.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Rapid advances in large language models (LLMs) and large multimodal models (LMMs) enable autonomous agents to perform increasingly complex tasks on real-world websites [ 8 , 31 ] . Research-oriented agents must additionally interpret structured scholarly metadata, navigate hierarchical taxonomies, and support scholarly workflows. Yet existing evaluations emphasize broad assistant tasks [ 20 ] or general web browsing [ 16 ] ; scientific benchmarks [ 2 , 28 ] instead assess downstream scientific problem solving. Workflows on real research platforms therefore remain underexplored. arXiv is an attractive testbed because it is stable and transparent, provides well-structured metadata, and has minimal personalization. WebVoyager [ 12 ] includes arXiv among 15 live websites, but its arXiv tasks mainly test basic navigation rather than scholarly workflows, and answers may change as live pages evolve. Its automatic GPT-4V evaluator uses model-based judgment rather than deterministic exact matching. These differences motivate a stable evaluation setting for scientific workflows. We introduce WebArxiv, covering diverse, realistic scholarly tasks beyond basic information queries and rule compliance, including multi-condition paper retrieval, deep content extraction, and cross-paper comparison. Static arXiv snapshots make every task time-invariant, while reference action trajectories and deterministic ground truths enable consistent, verifiable comparisons among agents. We evaluate ten representative configurations, spanning general-purpose LMM agents such as GPT-4o [ 22 ] and specialized systems such as OpenWebAgent [ 14 ] . The strongest model, GPT-o1, reaches 47.6% overall success, followed by Gemini-2.5 at 44.4%, while most models remain below 38.0% and struggle especially with multi-condition advanced search and paper comparison. Qualitative trajectory inspection finds repeated actions after agents rely on short recent histories. Dynamic memory instead retrieves task-relevant prior interactions, helping avoid error loops and reuse information during retries; the experiments report improvements for nearly all evaluated models. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce WebArxiv, a benchmark that enables reproducible evaluation of multimodal web agents within the hierarchically structured arXiv environment. • We conduct a comprehensive evaluation of ten advanced web agents on WebArxiv, demonstrating clear baseline performance across different task types and query complexity levels. • Through detailed analyses, we find that existing web agents tend to over-rely on fixed interaction histories. To address this issue, we introduce a lightweight dynamic memory mechanism that allows web agents to adaptively leverage past interactions.
