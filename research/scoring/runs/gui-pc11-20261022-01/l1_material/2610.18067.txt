title: WorkRover: Scaling Long-Horizon Cowork Agent Training Via Recursive Environment Evolution
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18067

§Abstract
Scaling interactive environments is important for training long-horizon agents through reinforcement learning. These environments often require further testing and refinement after initial generation, and correcting defects in a task may also require updating its work context and verifier. However, existing construction pipelines offer limited support for such iterative refinement while keeping these components aligned. We introduce WorkRover, a framework for training long-horizon cowork agents through recursive environment evolution. Its harness-driven synthesis enables construction agents to jointly build and validate semantically aligned work contexts, executable tasks, and evidence-grounded verifiers, adapting their actions to workspace state and execution feedback rather than following a fixed construction workflow. To support environment evolution, we introduce Factorial Compatibility Replay (FCR), which uses rollout feedback to evaluate combinations of component revisions through dependency-closed replay and selects the smallest validated repair among tested candidates while preserving task requirements and evaluation constraints. To provide finer-grained training signals, we propose Verifier-to-Turn Credit Assignment (VTCA), which augments trajectory-level GRPO advantages with signals from deterministic workspace-progress checks and assigns a bounded share of positive-progress credit to prerequisite turns using recorded tool-output and artifact dependencies. Using WorkRover, we post-train Qwen3.5-35B-A3B to obtain WorkRover-35B-A3B and evaluate it on several cowork-agent benchmarks. Its reported scores exceed those of Qwen3.5-35B-A3B by 26.27 percentage points on JobBench, 29.32 percentage points on OfficeQA Pro, and 413.25 Elo points on GDPval v2. These results suggest that WorkRover offers a promising foundation for scalable training of long-horizon cowork agents.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Cowork agents powered by large language models (LLMs) perform professional tasks across files, tools, and applications [ 15 ] . These tasks unfold over long sequences of dependent actions, requiring agents to locate relevant materials [ 31 , 17 , 37 ] , track workspace changes, and maintain consistency across related artifacts [ 20 , 33 , 3 , 32 ] . Training these agents through reinforcement learning (RL) depends on environments that support diverse, executable workflows and provide reliable feedback on task outcomes [ 11 , 5 , 26 , 10 , 41 , 45 , 49 ] . Recent work has explored automated environment and task synthesis to expand the range of workspaces and tasks available for agent training [ 30 , 2 , 18 ] . However, expanding the number of synthesized environments does not by itself ensure reliable training feedback. In a cowork environment, the work context must support the task’s requirements, and the verifier must assess the requested outcomes while accepting valid alternative solutions. Agent rollouts can expose missing dependencies, inconsistent task specifications, or verification errors that remain after initial construction [ 47 ] . Such defects can make valid behavior appear unsuccessful or allow incorrect results to be rewarded. Repairing these defects often requires coordinated revisions within a task environment: a correction to the work context or task specification may also require updating the verifier to keep execution and evaluation aligned. Existing construction pipelines offer limited support for maintaining this alignment throughout repeated revisions. This motivates recursive environment evolution : repeatedly using execution feedback to identify defects, coordinate component revisions, and revalidate the revised environment while preserving the original task requirements and evaluation constraints. Reliable terminal evaluation also leaves a credit-assignment challenge: useful steps can occur in failed trajectories and redundant actions in successful ones, while earlier information-gathering turns may only contribute through later workspace changes [ 34 , 6 ] . To this end, we introduce WorkRover , a framework for long-horizon cowork agent training that integrates harness-driven environment synthesis, recursive environment evolution, and verifier-grounded policy learning ( Figure 2 (b) ). Its harness tracks component dependencies and enables construction agents to jointly build and validate semantically aligned work contexts, executable tasks, and evidence-grounded verifiers. These agents adapt their construction actions to workspace state and execution feedback rather than following a fixed workflow. Independent reference executions from a clean initial workspace establish task feasibility, while verifier validation tests incorrect outcomes and valid alternatives against instruction-grounded requirements rather than a prescribed solution path. For environment evolution, we introduce Factorial Compatibility Replay (FCR), which evaluates combinations of fixed repair proposals across the work context, task, harness, and verifier. Each combination is rebuilt with its dependency-induced updates and tested under matched rollout conditions. FCR selects the smallest validated repair among the tested combinations, subject to preserving task requirements and evaluation constraints and passing regression checks on affected tasks. Fresh rollouts confirm selected repairs and provide evidence for subsequent evolution. For policy learning, we introduce Verifier-to-Turn Credit Assignment (VTCA) to supplement trajectory-level GRPO advantages with finer-grained feedback. Frozen deterministic checks measure progress and setbacks in recorded workspace states, while tool-output and artifact dependencies identify prerequisite turns eligible for a bounded share of positive-progress credit. This provides turn-level learning signals without intermediate judge calls. We post-train Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with WorkRover and evaluate the resulting model on five external benchmarks spanning occupational tasks, professional deliverables, cross-application workflows, and document-grounded reasoning. WorkRover -35B-A3B exceeds the reported backbone scores on all five benchmarks, with gains of 26.27 percentage points on JobBench, 29.32 percentage points on OfficeQA Pro, and 413.25 Elo points on GDPval v2. Controlled experiments further examine the contributions of environment evolution and verifier-grounded learning. Under matched repair budget caps, FCR improves task-package synthesis pass rate by 6.0 percentage points over full regeneration. A separate maintenance evaluation shows that FCR also resolves naturally occurring defects more reliably while reducing unintended changes to task intent. Construction ablations also show that following file dependencies and matching files with compatible skills improve task yield and executability, beyond assembling workspaces through topical similarity alone.
