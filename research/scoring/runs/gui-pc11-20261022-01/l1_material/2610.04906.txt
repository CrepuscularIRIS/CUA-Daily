title: Scaling Verifiable Environments for Long-horizon Work Agents
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04906

§Abstract
Work agents operate over digital artifacts to execute professional knowledge-intensive work, requiring training environments that support long-horizon interaction and trustworthy verification. However, hand-crafted environments incur prohibitive engineering overhead that prevents environment scaling, whereas synthesis methods sacrifice workspace complexity, realism, or grounded verifiability. To bridge this gap, we introduce WorkForge, a scalable synthesis framework for constructing verifiable work-agent environments from real-world resources. Starting from expert workflows, WorkForge first identifies the resources, decisions, and deliverables required by each workflow. It then retrieves relevant real-world files and organizes them into a workspace. WorkForge inspects the workspace to extract concrete, checkable facts about its content. These factual anchors fix which task types the workspace can support and how their outcomes can be verified. Therefore, WorkForge derives each task's instructions, solution plan, and complementary programmatic and semantic verifiers directly from these factual anchors, keeping verification traceable to observable workspace evidence. Furthermore, we construct 16.7K verifiable environments across 40 professional domains, with workspaces collectively covering 60 file types. Post-training Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base improves GDPVal from 45.5 to 73.6 and APEX Score from 5.0 to 21.3, while enabling Qwen3.5-27B to achieve highly competitive performance and outperform strong competitors. Our analyses confirm the efficacy of the proposed method and reveal consistent scaling behaviors across both data volume and interaction horizons.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Autonomous agents mark a fundamental paradigm shift in large language models, transitioning foundation models from passive conversational assistants to active digital workers capable of executing complex, multi-step professional knowledge work ( drouin2024workarena ; boisvert2024workarenapp ; huang2024mlagentbench ; patwardhan2026gdpval ; vidgen2026apex ) . Agent assistants such as Claude Code and Codex illustrate this paradigm through iterative exploration, tool orchestration, and execution over digital workspaces ( anthropic2025claudecode ; openai2025codex ) . Unlike conversational or API-calling routines, professional workflows center on concrete deliverables: users specify objectives, and the solution path is embedded within the workspace ( tang2026workspacebench ) . Producing these deliverables requires agents to untangle multiple file dependencies and make decisions in authentic workspace artifacts ( zhou2024webarena ; trivedi2024appworld ; zhang2026entropy ; xi2026agentgymrl ) . Cultivating agents capable of such long-horizon workflow fundamentally demands agent environments that couple authentic operational friction with trustworthy, verifiable supervision ( Dou:2025:Sandbox ; zhang:2025:CRM ; sun2026agentslastexam ; xi2026agentgym2 ) . While expert-curated environments offer high fidelity, their prohibitive overhead severely impedes environment scaling ( zhou2024webarena ; trivedi2024appworld ) . Recent work addresses this bottleneck through automated environment synthesis ( song2026envscaler ; tu2026scaleenv ; cheng2026terminalworld ; fan2026skillsynth ; zhao2026nexforge ; bai2026clawgym ) . However, these methods rely on synthetic environments or task-specific mock resources ( song2026envscaler ; tu2026scaleenv ; bai2026clawgym ) , yielding toy workspaces with limited file diversity and cross-file dependencies. This reduces the exploration demands that characterize real professional work in Figure 1 . Likewise, evaluation criteria not grounded in observable workspace facts risk introducing hallucinated or otherwise unsupported requirements, leading to unreliable judges ( zhang2026agentv ; shi2026facet ) . To address this challenge, we introduce WorkForge , a scalable framework for constructing complex, verifiable work-agent environments from real-world digital resources. WorkForge is motivated by a simple premise: professional tasks and their verification are inseparable from the workspaces in which they are performed. Workspace determines what an agent can explore, while observable workspace facts determine what can be asked and reliably verified. To operationalize this premise, WorkForge formalizes expert workflows as task blueprints, retrieves relevant resources, and profiles the resulting workspaces to recover file roles, dependencies, and factual anchors. These anchors jointly condition task generation and verifier design, keeping evaluation traceable to the workspace: programmatic checks verify deterministic requirements, while semantic rubrics assess open-ended deliverables against the same facts. Autonomous solvers then execute these tasks to yield long-horizon trajectories for post-training. WorkForge instantiates 16.7K verifiable environments across 40 professional domains, with workspaces comprising real artifacts, rich file types and cross-file dependencies. Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-35B-A3B-Base ( qwen2026qwen35 ) on the resulting trajectories improves performance across five benchmarks, including gains of 28.1 points on GDPval and 16.3 points on APEX. Controlled studies display significant benefits with increasing volume and trajectory length, and confirm the efficacy of the proposed method. Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce WorkForge , a scalable framework that constructs verifiable environments from real-world resources, grounding task specifications and verification rubrics in audited workspaces. • We instantiate 16.7K agentic environments across 40 professional domains, featuring complex filesystem and long-horizon solver trajectories. • Empirical experiments demonstrate the efficacy of our method. Notably, fine-tuning on WorkForge data yields substantial gains across five diverse agent benchmarks, improving GDPval by 28.1 points and APEX by 16.3 points.
