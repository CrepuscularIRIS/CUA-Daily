title: MissClick: Execution-Aware Adversarial Attacks on Coordinate Generation in GUI Grounding Models
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03740

§Abstract
Recent GUI visual grounding models generate screen coordinates as digit-token sequences that are parsed into numerical values and mapped to executable clicks. This generation-to-execution interface creates an attack surface that existing objectives over visual representations or coordinate-token sequences do not explicitly model. Although each coordinate digit is predicted as a token, its spatial effect after parsing depends on decimal position: changing a hundreds-place digit by one shifts the coordinate by 100 units, whereas the same change at the ones place shifts it by one. This mismatch motivates attack objectives that account for both numerical coordinate structure and click execution. Moreover, untargeted and targeted attacks require different objectives because they aim to move the click outside the correct region and into an attacker-specified region, respectively. We propose MissClick, an execution-aware white-box attack that aligns optimization with click-level success conditions. MissClick-U maximizes soft-coordinate displacement for untargeted disruption, while MissClick-T minimizes a place-weighted target-digit loss for targeted redirection. On OS-Atlas and UGround across desktop, web, and mobile platforms, MissClick-U achieves untargeted success rates of 75.07% and 72.93% (+16.62 and +30.72 pp), while MissClick-T achieves targeted success rates of 44.86% and 62.67% (+31.73 and +47.06 pp). Among the evaluated objectives, soft-coordinate displacement performs best for untargeted attacks, whereas place-weighted target-digit optimization performs best for targeted attacks, supporting goal-specific execution-aware objective design.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recent advances in vision-language models have accelerated the development of GUI agents that autonomously operate graphical user interfaces ( Hong et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 2025a ; Qin et al., 2025 ; Wang et al., 2026 ; Lin et al., 2025 ) . A core capability of these agents is visual grounding: given a screenshot and a natural-language instruction, the model predicts where to click. A dominant approach formulates this as coordinate generation ( Gou et al., 2025 ; Wu et al., 2025c ) : the model autoregressively produces screen coordinates as sequences of digit tokens (one token per decimal digit), and the resulting string is parsed into coordinates that determine the executed click (Fig. 1 ). The interface between coordinate generation and click execution creates an execution-level attack surface. Because the predicted click can trigger actions such as form submission or navigation, an adversarially shifted click can cause the agent to interact with an unintended UI element on behalf of the user. The security of GUI grounding models is therefore an important concern, yet prior work on adversarial perturbations against GUI grounding remains limited. Zhao et al. (2025) study both untargeted and targeted attacks against GUI grounders. Their objectives optimize either the distance between clean and perturbed image embeddings or the probability of generating a target coordinate sequence. These objectives are defined over intermediate representations or generated token sequences, whereas attack success is ultimately determined by the click executed after parsing the generated coordinates. Consequently, token-level objectives do not explicitly characterize the resulting coordinate changes or click displacement. These execution-level effects depend on which digit values change, their decimal positions, and how the parsed coordinates are mapped to an on-screen click location. This mismatch has direct consequences for attack design because decimal notation assigns different numerical effects to different digit positions. Each coordinate component is assembled from its constituent digits according to their decimal positions: a hundreds-place digit is weighted by 100 , a tens-place digit by 10 , and a ones-place digit by 1 (Sec. 2.2 , Equation 5 ). A single-digit change at a high-order position therefore shifts the executed click by a much larger distance than the same change at a low-order position. An attack objective should therefore account for this numerical structure. The appropriate objective also depends on the attack goal: an untargeted attack succeeds when the executed click falls outside the ground-truth region, whereas a targeted attack succeeds only when it produces a valid click inside an attacker-specified target region. Based on these observations, we propose MissClick , an execution-aware white-box attack on coordinate-generating GUI grounding models. MissClick derives separate objectives for untargeted and targeted attacks from their click-level success conditions. For untargeted attacks, MissClick-U constructs differentiable soft coordinates from digit probabilities and decimal place values, maps them to a soft click, and maximizes its distance from the ground-truth click. For targeted attacks, MissClick-T minimizes a place-weighted target-digit loss that assigns larger weights to higher-order coordinate digits. This directly promotes the target digits during autoregressive generation, since matching an expected soft coordinate does not guarantee that they become the most probable tokens. We evaluate MissClick on OS-Atlas and UGround across desktop, web, and mobile platforms. MissClick-U achieves untargeted attack success rates of 75.07\% and 72.93\% , improving over the strongest baseline by 16.62 and 30.72 percentage points, while MissClick-T achieves targeted success rates of 44.86\% and 62.67\% , with gains of 31.73 and 47.06 percentage points. Among the evaluated objectives, soft-coordinate displacement performs best for untargeted attacks, whereas place-weighted target-digit optimization performs best for targeted attacks. Our main contributions are as follows: • We identify an execution-level attack surface at the interface between coordinate generation and click execution. Because digit values and decimal positions determine the parsed coordinates and resulting click displacement, coordinate outputs should be modeled according to their execution semantics rather than treated merely as ordinary text. • We propose MissClick, an execution-aware attack with separate objectives for untargeted and targeted attacks: soft-coordinate displacement for untargeted attacks and place-weighted target-digit optimization for targeted attacks. Each objective is derived from the corresponding click-level success condition. • Experiments on two representative GUI grounding models across desktop, web, and mobile platforms demonstrate improvements over existing methods in both attack settings.
