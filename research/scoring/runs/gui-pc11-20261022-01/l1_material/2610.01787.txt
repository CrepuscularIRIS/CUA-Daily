arxiv: 2610.01787 (2610.01787v1)
title: Not All Experience Belongs in the Weights: Component Routing for Self-Improving GUI Agents
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01787

§Abstract
Self-improving GUI agents keep the trajectories they produce and return them to the agent, by fine-tuning or by retrieval into the prompt, and studies that compare the two destinations disagree. We attribute this to the unit of experience: a trajectory bundles items with different properties, so a conclusion about the bundle depends on its mix. To address this, (i) we introduce component routing, which splits the experience into locators, procedures, state facts and lessons and sends each component to the context or to the weights, compared on the same items across three backbone families, two environments and three seeds. One pool has two destinations: locators and lessons win in the weights, procedures and state facts in the context. (ii) We fit a rule in two properties measured before any training, recurrence and state-conditionality; it recovers the destination of a held-out backbone family in 24 of 24 cells, two interventions move a component toward the boundary, and routing by the rule beats every whole-trajectory baseline and, by +3.5 points on average, the better single destination of each backbone. (iii) We identify how training and producer-consumer differences change the value of the two destinations: note readout decreases after the same component is written into the weights, most for the items that recur most, context gains increase with the information gap, and weights gains decrease with the policy gap. Code and data will be released.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
GUI agents that operate phones and desktops are increasingly improved with their own traces: an agent runs a batch of tasks, the trajectories are kept, and the experience is returned to the agent. Where that experience should go is an open decision. One line of work fine-tunes the agent on its successful trajectories; another keeps the trajectories, or notes distilled from them, in a library that is retrieved into the prompt. On MobileGym, an 8B agent whose whole pool goes to the weights gains 7.1 points of held-out success and one whose whole pool goes to the context gains 8.0, yet the two destinations disagree on which parts of the pool are responsible: the components that gain most in the weights are the ones that gain least in context (Figure 1 a). Figure 2 shows the smallest version of this. One trajectory on a weather application teaches where the search field is and that a city is saved by tapping Add on its preview; fine-tuning on the whole trajectory learns the first and loses the second. We attribute this to the unit of experience. Existing methods treat a trajectory as the unit: success-filtered fine-tuning distills whole trajectories into the weights ( Zhang et al., 2026 ; Men et al., 2026 ; Fan et al., 2026 ) , and retrieval keeps whole trajectories or trajectory-level skills in context ( Xiong et al., 2026 ; Hu et al., 2026 ; Sui et al., 2026 ) . Comparisons of the two destinations reach opposite conclusions: internalized hints beat the prompt on tool tasks ( Alakuijala et al., 2026 ) , prompt-side updates beat gradient updates on web tasks ( Li et al., 2026 ; Agrawal et al., 2026 ) , and the same skill representation helps one task and hurts another ( Liu et al., 2026b ) . A trajectory bundles items with different properties, so any conclusion about the bundle depends on its mix. Deciding where a piece of experience belongs requires two things: a property of the piece that can be measured before any training, and a comparison in which both destinations consume the same items and are read on the same held-out tasks. Existing comparisons pit one whole pipeline against another, so the items differ before the destinations do ( Alakuijala et al., 2026 ; Gou et al., 2026 ) , and none asks it per component. Core question Which components of an agent’s own experience belong in its context and which in its weights, and does the answer survive training and a change of backbone? We answer this question with component routing . Experience is split into four components, locators, procedures, state facts and lessons, and each component is characterized by two properties measured on the pool before any training: recurrence , how often its key comes up in unrelated tasks, and state-conditionality , how much its usefulness depends on the current state matching its source state. Each component is then sent to the context, to the weights, or to both, on the same items and the same held-out instances, across three backbone families and two environments. Three observations follow. The four components split by destination: the route gain, weights minus context, is +4.9 points for locators and +1.5 for lessons, - 4.2 for procedures and - 4.1 for state facts (Figure 1 a). The two properties account for the split: a rule fitted with the tested backbone held out recovers the sign in 24 of 24 cells, an eightfold training dose moves procedures toward the weights by +3.5 points, and stripping the state condition from state facts costs 3.4. Routing each component by the rule exceeds the better single destination of each backbone by +3.5 points on average over three backbones and two environments, and the reverse assignment by +7.8 (Figure 1 b). Contributions. (i) A measurement of where an agent’s own experience belongs: four components, two pool-level properties, and a paired comparison of the context and the weights on the same items across three backbones, two environments and three seeds. (ii) A routing rule in recurrence and state-conditionality that recovers the winning destination of every component on a held-out backbone family, with two interventions that move a component toward the boundary. (iii) An account of how training, information gaps and policy gaps shape experience reuse: the readout of high-recurrence notes falls to 16 percent once the same items are in the weights, most for the items that recur most, and the context route gains from an information gap while the weights route loses to a policy gap.
