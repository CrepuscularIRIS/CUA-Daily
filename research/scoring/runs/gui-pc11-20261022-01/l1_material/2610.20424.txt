title: BizBench: Unveiling Hidden Challenges in Deploying Long-Horizon Agents for Real-World Business
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20424

§Abstract
Existing long-horizon agent benchmarks often emphasize final task success while providing limited assessment of intermediate execution validity. Yet real-world business operations require agents to navigate extensive, potentially outdated or conflicting policies while satisfying critical constraints throughout execution. These requirements are particularly consequential in high-stakes domains such as payments, where intermediate violations can incur financial losses or regulatory consequences regardless of the final outcome. To bridge this evaluation gap, we introduce BizBench, a multi-turn benchmark grounded in real-world procedures from a leading payments company. Its executable environment integrates 789 knowledge documents and 601 discoverable business tools, with consistent policies, states, and tool behavior. The benchmark comprises 50 tasks involving resource constraints, temporal dependencies, prior commitments, and conflicting or outdated guidance. Each task is validated through reference execution, while deterministic checks assess both goal attainment and execution validity without prescribing a single solution path. Experiments reveal substantial limitations even among frontier models: Claude Opus 5 and GPT-6 Sol achieve strict success rates of only 64.0% and 61.3%, respectively. For Qwen3.8-27B, goal attainment reaches 42.0%, but strict success drops to 32.7%, demonstrating how outcome-based evaluation can mask invalid execution. Beyond success rates, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash averages 191.3 tool calls per run, approximately 70% more than reference executions, revealing substantial execution overhead. Overall, BizBench establishes a testbed for evaluating and advancing long-horizon agents beyond task completion, toward reliable and constraint-compliant execution in complex business environments.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Advances in artificial intelligence are transforming real-world business operations, with customer service providing a representative example. Early FAQ-based systems addressed user queries by matching them against curated question–answer pairs ( Sakata et al., 2019 ; Li et al., 2017a ) . Although effective for isolated queries, these systems offered limited support for contextual understanding across multiple turns. Subsequent advances in deep neural networks enabled end-to-end task-oriented dialogue systems, extending customer service from single-turn question answering to multi-turn task resolution ( Raghu et al., 2021 ; Budzianowski et al., 2018 ; Li et al., 2017b ) . By tracking conversational context and incorporating external knowledge, these systems supported more sophisticated interactions, but remained largely constrained by predefined dialogue acts, slot schemas, and short-horizon state tracking. More recently, the emergence of large language models (LLMs) has driven a further transition toward agentic customer service, enabling increasingly autonomous execution of complex business workflows through policy interpretation, multi-step planning, and external tool orchestration ( Yao et al., 2025 ; Tong et al., 2026 ) . Unlike conventional dialogue systems, such agents can take actions that alter business states, commit resources, and constrain subsequent decisions. Consequently, reliable deployment requires not only successful task completion but also adherence to business requirements throughout the execution process ( Ruan et al., 2024 ) . This transition toward autonomous business execution introduces two fundamental challenges. First, agents must maintain execution validity at critical intermediate steps, rather than merely achieve the desired final outcome. While exploratory actions may be permissible, certain business constraints must remain satisfied throughout execution, and violating them can constitute task failure regardless of the eventual outcome. For example, an unauthorized payment remains a compliance violation even if the transferred funds are subsequently returned. Thus, successful task completion does not necessarily imply valid execution. Second, agents must identify and apply the authoritative business rules governing their actions. In real-world operations, business rules are distributed across heterogeneous sources, including policy manuals, FAQs, and operational notices ( Shi et al., 2026 ) . These sources are maintained by different teams and updated at different times, allowing outdated guidance to coexist with current policies ( Ouyang et al., 2025 ) . Moreover, seemingly conflicting instructions may differ in their applicability across jurisdictions, products, or effective periods. Consequently, retrieving relevant information is insufficient: agents must determine which rules are authoritative, current, and applicable to the specific business context ( Nam et al., 2026 ) . Although existing long-horizon agent benchmarks evaluate policy-governed tool use and knowledge access ( Lu et al., 2025 ) , evaluations centered primarily on final task success may fail to capture violations of critical intermediate constraints or incorrect application of business rules. This limitation leaves an important gap between benchmark performance and the assurance required for reliable deployment in real-world business environments. We therefore introduce BizBench to evaluate whether agents can identify applicable rules before taking consequential actions and complete business requests while maintaining valid execution throughout. The benchmark comprises 50 long-horizon tasks grounded in real-world business workflows from a leading global payments company. These tasks combine tool use with multi-turn customer dialogue: a user simulator provides customer-specific information and responds to approval requests throughout execution. To test rule interpretation before action, we provide current and historical guidance with explicit scopes and effective periods across policy documents. Agents must determine which instructions govern the current request and use accompanying tool documentation to discover and invoke relevant tools. We instantiate these business workflows in a stateful environment, linking them through shared resources, timing requirements, and prior commitments. The environment retains action consequences, so later decisions must account for commitments already made. To detect violations that final task success can conceal, a deterministic evaluator checks both outcomes and recorded execution histories against task goals and applicable rules. Success requires completing the requested goals while satisfying prior authorization requirements, persistent constraints, and restrictions on side effects.
