title: RSI-Anything: Unlocking Recursive Self-Improvement with World Models
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13912

§Abstract
Recursive self-improvement (RSI) aims to transform interaction experience into persistent improvements, yet external feedback is costly and world-model predictions may become unreliable as policies evolve. We introduce RSI-Anything, a domain-general framework built on JEPA-Anything that uses predictive world models to guide action and learning-data selection. Observed feedback drives policy or workflow improvement and conditional world-model adaptation, while component and compatibility checks govern the retention of updated policy-model pairs. Across diverse computational and scientific domains, RSI-Anything demonstrates substantial gains. In code repair, with the coder fixed, success increases from 26.3% with a static verifier to 29.2% through predictive selection and 31.2% after world-model adaptation, at comparable test usage. Graphical user interface (GUI) evaluations showed higher task completion, while average actions decrease by 39.4%. Across five successive policy versions, laboratory-planning scores more than double from 21.80% to 46.01%. In reaction optimization, RSI-Anything achieves 96.15% mean best observed yield versus 82.16% for Bayesian optimization under the same 40-experiment budget. An end-to-end immune-science application further connects workflow evolution and predictive modeling with therapeutic hypothesis generation. These results demonstrate how predictive world models and retained system updates enable recursive improvement across diverse environments.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recursive self-improvement (RSI) turns interaction experience into lasting improvements in a system’s ability to act and learn. Within tasks, feedback refines solutions or guides actions Cobbe et al. (2021) ; Shinn et al. (2023) . Across rounds, self-training and preference optimization support persistent policy or procedural updates Gulcehre et al. (2023) ; Rafailov et al. (2023) ; Zelikman et al. (2022) . Recursiveness requires these updates to be retained and shape subsequent interaction and learning. The central question is how experience from one round improves the system’s ability to act and learn in the next. Learning from external feedback requires executing or evaluating candidates before their consequences can guide improvement, limiting exploration under constrained interaction budgets. World models predict the effects of candidate actions on subsequent states or outcomes Ha and Schmidhuber (2018) ; Hafner et al. (2025) ; Schrittwieser et al. (2020) , enabling prospective evaluation and planning. Within RSI, these predictions can prioritize feedback collection and guide learning-data selection or construction. World models thus provide a predictive foundation for improvement under limited external feedback. JEPA-Anything provides a shared framework for world-model construction across heterogeneous domains Cui et al. (2026b) . Building on joint-embedding predictive architectures Assran et al. (2023) ; Bardes et al. (2024) ; Assran et al. (2025) , it predicts target representations from observed context. Its orthogonal predictive factorization (OPF) decomposes latent targets into complementary factors, learns dedicated predictors, and recombines their outputs into a complete latent state Cui et al. (2026b) . This provides a predictive foundation across domains (Figure 1 ). However, prediction alone does not establish recursive improvement : predicted outcomes must guide interaction, while external feedback must produce updates that persist across subsequent rounds. Closing this loop introduces a coupled-update challenge. Policy updates change the candidate actions and experience distributions on which the world model operates, potentially undermining prediction reliability. Conversely, world-model updates alter candidate rankings, imagined trajectories, and the learning signals guiding policy improvement. Consequently, improving either component independently does not guarantee better joint performance . Reliable RSI therefore requires determining when to update each component, assessing prediction reliability under changing policies, and ensuring that retained policy–model pairs improve subsequent interaction and learning. To address these challenges, we introduce RSI-Anything , a domain-general framework that integrates predictive world models with feedback-driven recursive improvement (Figure 2 ). World-model predictions guide action selection, prioritize external evaluations, and support learning-data selection or construction. External feedback drives policy learning or workflow revision and triggers world-model adaptation when reliability checks warrant it. The policy and world model can evolve asynchronously , with component-level checks evaluating individual updates and compatibility checks determining which policy–model pair is retained . This retained pair shapes subsequent candidates and learning experience, closing the recursive improvement loop. Our evaluations examine whether introducing world-model predictions into RSI improves candidate selection, whether retained policy or model updates yield further improvements, and whether these gains can be achieved under limited external feedback. We investigate these questions across general computational tasks (code repair and GUI interaction), physical-domain planning, and experimental science. We further present an end-to-end application of RSI-Anything in immune science, spanning scientific-agent workflow evolution, immune world-model construction, analysis with self-correction, and prospective therapeutic hypothesis generation Cui et al. (2026a) . Our contributions are threefold: • World models for recursive self-improvement.
