title: HINTBench: Horizon-agent Intrinsic Non-attack Trajectory Benchmark
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13954

§Abstract
Existing agent-safety evaluation has focused mainly on externally induced risks. Yet agents may still enter unsafe trajectories under benign conditions. We study this complementary but underexplored setting through the lens of \emph{intrinsic} risk, where intrinsic failures remain latent, propagate across long-horizon execution, and eventually lead to high-consequence outcomes. To evaluate this setting, we introduce \emph{non-attack intrinsic risk auditing}, a guard-oriented safety evaluation task, and present \textbf{HINTBench}, a benchmark of 596 agent trajectories, comprising 400 synthetic risky trajectories, 136 synthetic safe trajectories, 30 reconstructed real-world risky trajectories, and 30 reconstructed real-world safe trajectories, with an average length of 24.0 steps. HINTBench supports three tasks: risk detection, risk-step localization, and intrinsic failure-type identification, with annotations organized under a unified five-constraint taxonomy. Experiments reveal a substantial capability gap: strong LLMs perform well on trajectory-level risk detection, but the best model remains below 37 on fine-grained Strict-F1 for risk-step localization. Existing off-the-shelf guard models evaluated under their native prompts transfer poorly to this setting. These findings establish intrinsic risk auditing as an open challenge for agent safety.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
As large language models increasingly evolve into agents capable of multi-step reasoning, tool use, and environment interaction, the focus of safety research is also shifting ( Wang et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2026b ) . For agent systems, risk is no longer limited to whether the final output is harmful; instead, it can arise throughout the full process of decision-making, action execution, and interaction with the environment ( Shen et al., 2026 ) . Accordingly, agent safety is moving from static output safety toward a broader examination of safety in dynamic execution ( Shao et al., 2024 ) . Most existing work on agent safety focuses primarily on externally induced risks, such as prompt injection, malicious tool feedback, poisoned memory, and environment manipulation ( Zhan et al., 2024 ; Jiang et al., 2026 ; Zheng et al., 2026 ) . These studies mainly evaluate whether an agent can be hijacked, misled, or induced to perform dangerous actions when external inputs are corrupted or system boundaries are attacked ( Debenedetti et al., 2024 ; Evtimov et al., 2025 ) . This line of work is indispensable for understanding adversarial robustness, but it does not fully cover the risks that arise in benign real-world deployment. Recent trajectory-level benchmarks begin to move beyond pure attack-success evaluation ( Luo et al., 2025 ; Mou et al., 2026 ; Liu et al., 2026b ) . However, they still do not explicitly focus on intrinsic failures under benign conditions, especially when such risks emerge early and propagate through long-horizon execution ( Li et al., 2025 ) . Consider an agent asked to reschedule a meeting: skipping recipient confirmation and sending private notes to the wrong contact can cause an irreversible privacy breach without an external attack. More generally, even with benign instructions, valid tools, and non-adversarial feedback, intrinsic failures can produce high-consequence harm ( Cemri et al., 2025 ) . HINTBench distinguishes these safety-relevant failures from ordinary capability or reliability issues by their potential for irreversible or high-stakes consequences, and therefore excludes ordinary functional bugs. This motivates non-attack intrinsic risk auditing : determining from an execution trajectory whether internal failures have made the run unsafe. In long-horizon execution, failures may remain latent and propagate through later decisions before becoming visible ( Yao et al., 2023 ; Ma et al., 2024 ) . This gap between where risk originates and where it manifests makes detection, localization, and diagnosis difficult ( Cemri et al., 2025 ) . HINTBench evaluates these capabilities through offline trajectory auditing; it does not test whether an online agent–harness system can block, recover from, or otherwise intervene in live execution. We construct HINTBench ( H orizon-agent I ntrinsic N on-attack T rajectory Benchmark) for systematic intrinsic-risk auditing. It supports risk detection, coarse-grained risk-step localization with five constraint categories, and fine-grained risk-step localization with twelve subtypes. HINTBench organizes failures under a unified Goal, Factual, Capability, Procedural, and State taxonomy. It contains 596 trajectories: 400 synthetic risky, 136 synthetic safe, 30 reconstructed real-world risky, and 30 reconstructed real-world safe, with an average length of 24.0 steps. Compared with benchmarks on external attacks or coarse trajectory-level judgment ( Mou et al., 2026 ) , HINTBench targets internal failures under benign conditions, provides step-level supervision, and uses substantially longer trajectories than ATBench ( Liu et al., 2026a ) and R-Judge ( Yuan et al., 2024 ) . Across general-purpose and specialized guard models, localization and fine-grained diagnosis remain challenging, while off-the-shelf guards under native prompts transfer poorly ( Inan et al., 2023 ) . Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We introduce non-attack intrinsic risk auditing , a guard-oriented setting for detecting, localizing, and diagnosing unsafe trajectories caused by internal failures under benign conditions. • We develop HINTBench with trajectory-level labels, risk-step annotations, fine-grained risk types, and a unified five-constraint taxonomy. • We benchmark specialized guards and general-purpose LLMs, finding that localization and fine-grained diagnosis remain challenging, while off-the-shelf guards under native prompts transfer poorly.
