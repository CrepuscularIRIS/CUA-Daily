title: OpenMobile-2: Building Versatile Mobile Agents with Scalable Environments and App-Native Tools
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14157

§Abstract
We introduce OpenMobile-2, a near-frontier mobile agent with fully open training environments and recipes. We make three key advances: (1) Diverse environments with simulated commercial apps: We build MobileGym++, featuring 35 realistic, functionally rich commercial-style apps with cross-app workflows, while preserving full controllability for reset and verification. Together with newly configured apps in Android emulators, this yields a diverse playground spanning over 110 apps. (2) Open training data at scale: Building on this foundation, we curate nearly 12K mobile interaction trajectories for supervised fine-tuning and the largest open collection of verifiable RL training data for mobile agents, comprising over 2K executable tasks with automatic rewards. (3) Hybrid GUI and app-native tool use: We explore an experimental mobile-use setting where apps expose selected functionalities as app-native tools alongside their GUIs, allowing agents to interleave GUI actions and tool calls within a task. We implement this setting in MobileGym++ with over 300 carefully scoped tools across 50+ apps. Additionally, we introduce MobileGym++ Bench for complex, long-horizon commercial mobile scenarios, supporting both GUI-only and hybrid GUI-tool evaluation on a shared task suite. OpenMobile-2 performs competitively across established benchmarks, including AndroidWorld (79.9) and MobileWorld (50.4), while showing promising transfer to real-device mobile use, nearly doubling SPA-Bench performance from 31.9 to 59.6. All resources are available at https://os-copilot.github.io/OpenMobile2-Home/.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Mobile agents built on vision-language models are increasingly capable of understanding smartphone interfaces and automating everyday tasks ( Wang et al., 2025 ; Zhou et al., 2025 ; Xu et al., 2026 ; Gao et al., 2026 ; Zhou et al., 2026 ) . Such progress depends heavily on large-scale interaction experience, typically in the form of trajectories collected across diverse tasks, applications, and environments ( Cheng et al., 2026 ; Team et al., 2026b ) . Gathering such experience independently is costly, while high-quality interaction data remains largely proprietary, leaving the open community with limited training resources and open models trailing well behind. Scaling interaction experience without costly human annotation requires a diverse and controllable mobile playground. Especially for commercial applications, reproducing rich workflows and persistent user states at scale has remained a key obstacle. Existing open efforts ( Sun et al., 2025 ; Cheng et al., 2026 ) largely rely on a small set of applications in Android emulators, mostly open-source apps and system utilities. Such environments provide realistic interaction, but third-party apps often lack reliable reset, while the full Android stack is costly to replicate for large-scale rollout and reinforcement learning ( Rawles et al., 2025 ) . Recent simulated environments offer an alternative path ( Wu et al., 2026a ; Liu et al., 2026b ) , providing efficient reset and parallelization, yet their applications remain few and shallow, with no scalable recipe for extending them. We introduce OpenMobile-2, an open stack of environments, data, and training recipes that puts environment scaling into practice, as shown in Figure 2 . We build MobileGym++ , a large-scale mobile environment featuring diverse, high-fidelity commercial-style applications, built upon the simulation framework of MobileGym ( Wu et al., 2026a ) . In particular, we add 35 functional re-implementations of commercial apps with rich internal state and cross-app workflows, while preserving full controllability for clean reset and verification of task outcomes. Together with newly configured apps in Android emulator, this forms a diverse training playground spanning over 110 apps. Building on this playground, we curate nearly 12K trajectories for supervised fine-tuning and more than 2K executable tasks for reinforcement learning, each equipped with a reliable verifier. We further explore an experimental mobile-use setting that combines GUI interaction with app-native tool use. This setting reflects an emerging shift in computer use, where universal but inefficient GUI interaction is increasingly complemented by efficient yet functionally bounded structured interfaces ( Hu et al., 2026 ; Shi et al., 2026 ) . In MobileGym++ , we expose selected app functionalities through over 300 app-native tools across 50+ apps, e.g., search_course in a fitness app. This enables us to train agents that interleave GUI actions and tool calls within a task. To push mobile agent evaluation toward real-world commercial workflows and hybrid GUI–tool interaction, we introduce MobileGym++ Bench , a suite of 215 long-horizon cross-app tasks with persistent user states and programmatic verification. Every task can be solved with or without tools, so GUI-only and hybrid agents are compared on the same tasks. OpenMobile-2 achieves competitive results on established mobile agent benchmarks, reaching 79.9 on AndroidWorld and 50.4 on MobileWorld. It further transfers to real-device settings, improving SPA-Bench performance from 31.9 to 59.6 and outperforming strong open-source baselines. Further analysis highlights the importance of scaling application diversity for improving agent generalization and reveals the complementary benefits of hybrid GUI and app-native tool use. Our main contributions are: • Environment Scaling with Replicated Applications. MobileGym++ faithfully replicates 35 commercial applications with accessible state in an extendable 110-application playground, overcoming the limited scope of existing open environments. • Open Training Data and Recipes at Scale. We release a fresh collection of nearly 12K diverse trajectories and full recipes, together with over 2K executable RL tasks with reliable verifiers, which largely advance training mobile agents. • A Unified Framework for GUI and App-native Tool Use. We integrate over 300 scoped app-native tools across 50+ apps and introduce MobileGym++ Bench, where GUI-only and hybrid GUI-tool agents can be trained and evaluated on the same long-horizon tasks.
