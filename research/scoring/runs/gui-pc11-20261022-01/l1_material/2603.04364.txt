title: Dual-Modality Multi-Stage Adversarial Safety Training: Robustifying Multimodal Web Agents Against Cross-Modal Attacks
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04364

§Abstract
Multimodal web agents that process both screenshots and accessibility trees are increasingly deployed to interact with web interfaces, yet their dual-stream architecture opens an underexplored attack surface: an adversary who injects content into the webpage DOM simultaneously corrupts both observation channels with a consistent deceptive narrative. Our vulnerability analysis on MiniWob++ reveals that attacks including a visual component far outperform text-only injections, exposing critical gaps in text-centric VLM safety training. Motivated by this finding, we propose Dual-Modality Multi-Stage Adversarial Safety Training (DMAST), a framework that formalizes the agent-attacker interaction as a two-player general-sum Markov game and co-trains both players through a three-stage pipeline: (1) imitation learning from a strong teacher model, (2) oracle-guided supervised fine-tuning that uses a novel zero-acknowledgment strategy to instill task-focused reasoning under adversarial noise, and (3) adversarial reinforcement learning via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) self-play. On out-of-distribution tasks, DMAST nearly halves the attack success rate (41.2\%$\rightarrow$21.4\%) while raising task completion by over 60\% relative (6.2\%$\rightarrow$10.2\%). Our approach outperforms established training-based defenses and complements prompt-based defenses, demonstrating genuine co-evolutionary progress and robust generalization to complex, unseen environments. Code is available at https://github.com/huajianduzhuo-code/DMAST_official.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Web agents powered by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are increasingly deployed to autonomously navigate and interact with web interfaces on behalf of users. Modern agents ingest both a rendered screenshot and a structured accessibility tree (AXTree) extracted from the DOM, leveraging complementary strengths: the screenshot captures rich visual context, while the accessibility tree provides precise labels and types of interactive elements. This dual-stream architecture has driven steady progress on web interaction benchmarks ( Liu et al., 2018 ; Koh et al., 2024 ) , yet it also opens an underexplored attack surface. An adversary who injects content into the webpage’s DOM simultaneously corrupts both channels, presenting the agent with a consistent deceptive narrative that is far harder to detect than a text-only injection. Earlier defense work for web/computer-use agents primarily focused on single-modality or prompt-level threats. However, recent attack-side studies have shown that real-world agent threats can be multimodal and environmental, including environmental injections ( Liao et al., 2025 ) , adversarial pop-ups ( Zhang et al., 2025 ) , multimodal distractions ( Ma et al., 2025 ) , and hybrid Web-OS attack chains ( Liao et al., 2026 ) . Our work complements this attack-side evidence by providing a systematic vulnerability analysis on the MiniWob++ benchmark using a state-of-the-art instruction-tuned VLM. The results reveal that attacks including a visual component (either image-only (34.4% attacker success) or coordinated dual-modality (35.7%)) are much more effective at inducing safety jailbreaks than text-only injections (24.1%). This disparity aligns with recent findings that image-based injections often bypass text-centric safety filters ( Yeo and Choi, 2025 ) , and it demonstrates that current VLMs are not equipped to handle visual deceptions such as typographic overlays, fake system dialogs, or phishing forms embedded in screenshots. Motivated by these findings, we propose Dual-Modality Multi-Stage Adversarial Safety Training (DMAST) , a framework for hardening multimodal web agents through adversarial co-evolution. We formalize the agent–attacker interaction as a two-player general-sum Markov game and design a unified HTML injection mechanism where the attacker generates structured DOM modifications executed in the browser runtime. Since both the screenshot and accessibility tree are rendered from the same DOM, a single injection can alter both modalities, closely modeling real-world threats. Both the agent and attacker are instantiated from the same VLM with shared weights, enabling efficient co-evolution: as the model learns to craft attacks, it simultaneously develops awareness of those patterns, which transfers to defense. Training proceeds through a three-stage pipeline. Stage 1 (Imitation Learning) distills expert trajectories from a stronger teacher model into a smaller student model, providing a stable initialization for both roles. Stage 2 (Oracle-Guided SFT) augments the training data with a novel denoising strategy: an oracle given the attacked view, task goal, and golden action from clean interaction is prompted to generate task-focused Chain-of-Thought reasoning that teaches the student to maintain goal-directed behavior amid adversarial noise, without acknowledging the attack. Stage 3 (Adversarial RL) co-evolves the agent and attacker through self-play using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), driving both players toward increasingly sophisticated strategies. Experiments on held-out MiniWob++ tasks and out-of-distribution VisualWebArena tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of DMAST. On our curated VisualWebArena tasks with adversarial setup, the full pipeline reduces attacker success from 41.2% (base model) to 21.4% while improving agent task completion from 6.2% to 10.2%, outperforming baselines including SPAG ( Cheng et al., 2024 ) , Automatic Red Teaming ( Shi et al., 2025 ; Samvelyan et al., 2024 ) , Online SFT ( Zelikman et al., 2022 ) , and complements pure prompt-based defenses. The training stages provide complementary improvements, with oracle-guided SFT instilling structural priors that persist through RL, and cross-checkpoint evaluation confirms that both agent and attacker capabilities genuinely co-evolve through self-play.
