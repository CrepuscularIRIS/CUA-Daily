title: Learning to Adapt: Self-Improving Web Agent via Cognitive-Aware Exploration
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.31365

§Abstract
Recent advances in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have led to promising progress in web agents. However, existing web agents often rely on handcrafted execution pipelines or expensive expert trajectories, limiting their adaptability to complex, dynamic environments. To address these challenges, we propose SCALE (Self-Cognitive-Aware Learning and Exploration), which leverages three adversarial roles, Selector, Predictor, and Judger to autonomously discover the agent's limitations and expand its cognitive boundaries through environmental exploration. Moreover, we propose SCALE-Hop, a graph exploration strategy that facilitates global planning and helps agents avoid local exploration traps. To further support learning, we construct SCALE-20k, a large-scale dataset collected from 19 real-world websites, containing diverse task types and structured demonstrations generated from SCALE's exploration traces. Experimental results show that our approach significantly improves the performance and generalization of multiple MLLMs in various web environments. Our framework offers a scalable and generalizable solution for building truly autonomous and adaptive web agents.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recently, with the development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), MLLM-based web agents [ 14 , 34 , 33 , 27 ] have demonstrated promising performance in web automation tasks, like product searching [ 11 ] , shopping [ 35 ] , and navigation [ 15 ] . However, most existing web agents heavily rely on the pre-trained capabilities of MLLMs, which are difficult to cover large-scale and diverse web task data [ 23 , 2 ] . There remains a significant gap between their prior knowledge and the highly varied and dynamic real-world web pages. To address this gap, prior works usually depend on the design of manually crafted execution pipelines [ 32 , 12 , 7 ] or on the use of human-annotated expert trajectories [ 30 , 10 , 29 , 3 ] for fine-tuning web agents. However, these two types of paradigms mentioned above still have notable limitations: 1) Reliance on crafted execution pipelines or expert trajectories. These methods either rely on crafted execution pipelines for task execution or depend on costly expert trajectories to fine-tune MLLMs [ 22 , 27 , 16 ] . Although these pipelines and trajectories are often tailored for specific scenarios, they still struggle to cope with the unpredictability and diversity of real-world web environments [ 23 , 8 , 26 ] . Agents in these paradigms tend to act passively and develop dependencies on specific task flows, limiting their ability to explore unfamiliar environments and adapt to dynamic real-world websites. 2) Challenges in evaluating and expanding cognitive boundaries. Cognitive boundaries are the limitations of an agent’s abilities, referring to items and operations that the agent struggles to understand or decide on based on prior knowledge [ 25 ] . It is significant for agents to improve their abilities. However, existing methods have neglected the exploration of their own cognitive boundaries. Therefore, how to evaluate and expand agent capabilities in a personalized and dynamic manner remains a critical challenge. In summary , developing general web agents requires enabling agents to proactively adapt to novel environments and continuously expanding their own cognitive boundaries, instead of relying on specific systems or predefined trajectories. To address these challenges, we propose SCALE ( S elf- C ognitive- A ware L earning and E xploration ), a general framework that removes reliance on expert trajectories and handcrafted pipelines. SCALE enables agents to autonomously discover and expand their cognitive boundaries through self-driven interaction in web environments. Since it operates independently of any specific model architecture, it offers broad generalizability across different models. As shown in Figure 1 , the agent proactively explores without external supervision by identifying its own limitations, triggering exploratory actions in uncertain areas, and adapting based on feedback. To evaluate and refine its understanding, SCALE assigns three roles to the same MLLM: Selector , Predictor , and Judger . The Selector proposes challenging actions, the Predictor anticipates their outcomes, and the Judger verifies prediction accuracy after execution. For example, the Selector may choose to click a logo, believing the model is unsure about its effect. The Predictor then estimates the outcome based on its current knowledge, expecting nothing to happen. However, once executed, the Judger observes a jump to the homepage, revealing a gap in the model’s understanding. This forms a closed-loop process in which the Selector and Predictor engage in an adversarial interaction, with one aiming to challenge the model and the other aiming to predict correctly, while the Judger provides feedback to improve both. The iterative process allows the agent to gradually adapt to complex web environments. Furthermore, we aim to enhance SCALE by extending its exploration capability from local interactions to global reasoning and coordination. We propose the SCALE-Hop strategy to complement it with global planning and control. SCALE-Hop constructs a graph representation of the agent’s interaction history, where each node represents a specific web state. This graph allows the agent to monitor its exploration coverage and make decisions at a higher level. When exploration becomes difficult or complete at a given node, SCALE-Hop evaluates the model’s performance and determines whether to exit and transition to another node that has not been fully explored. This mechanism helps the agent efficiently avoid local traps and supports a more comprehensive understanding of the environment. Based on the raw exploration traces from the SCALE process in 19 real-world websites, we construct SCALE-20k , a large-scale web task dataset.
