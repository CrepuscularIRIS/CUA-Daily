arxiv: 2609.32130 (2609.32130v1)
title: REBASE: Device-Cloud Experience Coherence for GUI Agents Across App Updates
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32130

§Abstract
A graphical user interface (GUI) agent that ships on a phone runs a small model and reuses experience: action paths recorded on earlier runs and cached from the cloud. When an app updates, part of this experience becomes silently wrong. On nine real app version pairs, an agent carrying experience recorded on the old version succeeds 10.1 points less often than one carrying none, and it does not notice the mismatch until 3 steps after acting on it. We propose REBASE, a protocol that keeps the device copy and the cloud copy of the experience coherent across updates. When a version changes, the cloud replays its copy on the new version and the device verifies each recorded step before executing it; when a step still fails, the device sends the cloud evidence in increasing size, starting from an accessibility-subtree diff, until the cloud re-derives an executable patch, keyed by version so that one repair serves the whole fleet. On two Jetson devices, REBASE restores the success rate of stale experience to that of fresh experience recorded on the new version within one episode, at 708x fewer bytes and 47.3x fewer cloud calls than uploading screenshots to the cloud, and 2.5x less device energy than running without experience.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
A graphical user interface (GUI) agent on a phone runs a small model and reuses experience : action paths recorded during earlier runs, consolidated by the vendor in the cloud, and cached on the device [ 1 , 2 ] . An app update can make these paths partly wrong without making them unusable: the failure is silent. Across nine real version pairs, an agent using experience from the previous version succeeds 10.1 points less often than one using none; in 62.2% of executions, a step the update broke still finds an element to act on, so the agent does not notice the mismatch until 3 steps later. Two signals go unused: the vendor’s cloud can obtain the new version before the device does, and the device knows it has updated before it fails. Device–cloud agent and inference systems decide which steps or model parts run in the cloud and what the device uploads [ 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 , 10 ] , and use the cloud to finish the current task, not to correct experience for later runs: MAI-UI uploads screenshot history on failure, and nothing returns to change the device’s next run [ 3 ] . Experience and memory systems keep experience on the machine that uses it, treat the app as fixed, and discuss updates only as a limitation [ 11 , 12 , 13 ] . CoMIC uploads complete trajectories and filters them in the cloud [ 14 ] , whereas OpenJarvis improves the on-device agent in an offline search phase and ships the edits down [ 15 ] . Self-healing test automation repairs a broken element locator offline on one machine [ 16 , 17 , 18 ] , a relocation we adopt. There, a failed script stops; an agent instead continues along a stale path, and a second copy of its experience sits in the cloud. Which evidence should cross the device–cloud link after an update, and when, remains open. REBASE ( Re play- B acked A ttestation with S taged E vidence) treats an app update as a coherence event : the device copy and the cloud copy of the agent’s experience stop agreeing. REBASE reconciles them on two clocks. On the update clock , which ticks when a version changes, the cloud replays its copy on the new version and the device verifies each step before executing it. On the failure clock , which ticks when a step fails, the device sends evidence in increasing size, from a fingerprint and an accessibility-subtree diff up to a screenshot, until the cloud re-derives an executable patch. The link carries a description of what changed, and a screenshot only when smaller evidence fails. Our contributions are threefold: • We measure how consolidated experience fails after an update and what repairs it: the failure is silent, and an accessibility-subtree diff lets the cloud repair 79.1% of failing steps with a per-step median of 36.6 \times fewer bytes than a screenshot. • We design REBASE , a two-clock protocol that selects evidence by the event that triggered it. • We key every repair by version, so cloud re-derivations grow with the steps an update breaks, not with the fleet: in a trace-driven fleet of 100 devices, REBASE re-derives 80.0 \times fewer times than without it. We implement REBASE on two Jetson AGX Orin devices and a Thor gateway, with nine Android apps on two releases each. It restores stale experience to the success rate of fresh experience within one episode, at 708 \times fewer bytes and 47.3 \times fewer cloud calls than uploading screenshots and 2.5 \times less device energy than running without experience. To our knowledge, REBASE is the first system that keeps a GUI agent’s experience coherent across app updates over a device–cloud link and measures its cost on real devices.

## III REBASE Design
III-A Overview and Problem Formulation REBASE has three agents [ 31 , 32 ] . The device agent runs the small model and fingerprint executor over \mathcal{E}_{d}(v) , whose paths were recorded for installed version v . The cloud agent maintains \mathcal{E}_{c}(v) and a strong model. The gateway , on the cloud side, monitors releases, runs a virtual device, and indexes coherence events by version key. One protocol follows two clocks (Fig. 2 ): the update clock ticks at a version change, and the failure clock at a verification mismatch on the device, which raises a coherence event . Formally, a path p=\langle(f_{1},a_{1}),\dots,(f_{n},a_{n})\rangle contains steps that apply action a_{k} to the element identified by fingerprint f_{k}=(r,t,\tilde{b},h) . Its fields are the role, text label, normalized box, and two-ancestor hash. Fingerprints match when f\simeq f^{\prime}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;(r,h)=(r^{\prime},h^{\prime})\ \wedge\ d(t,t^{\prime})\leq\theta_{t}\ \wedge\ \Delta(\tilde{b},\tilde{b}^{\prime})\leq\theta_{b}. (1) Here, d is normalized label edit distance, and \Delta is center displacement in screen units; \Delta=0 when the boxes overlap by at least half. The default thresholds are \theta_{t}=0.2 and \theta_{b}=0.1 . Let \mathcal{S} denote a screen’s fingerprint set. We write f\simeq\mathcal{S} when f matches an element of \mathcal{S} and use the same relation for screens whose sets overlap by at least \theta_{s}=0.5 . Verification \nu_{k} is one when the step- k element exists on \mathcal{S}_{k}(v^{\prime}) and the resulting screen matches the recorded successor \mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{\star} . With \mathbb{I}[\cdot] as the indicator, D(v\!\to\!v^{\prime}) contains each path’s first failing step: \displaystyle\nu_{k}(p,v^{\prime}) \displaystyle=\mathbb{I}\!\left[\,f_{k}\simeq\mathcal{S}_{k}(v^{\prime})\,\right]\;\mathbb{I}\!\left[\,\mathcal{S}_{k+1}(v^{\prime})\simeq\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{\star}\,\right], (2) \displaystyle D(v\!\to\!v^{\prime}) \displaystyle=\bigl\{(p,k):\ \nu_{k}(p,v^{\prime})=0,\ \nu_{j}(p,v^{\prime})=1\ \ \forall j<k\bigr\}. A first-factor pass followed by a second-factor failure is the stale-but-walkable step of Obs. 1 . REBASE selects the answering level \ell_{e} of each event e and an update-time push set \mathcal{U} to maximize autonomous success over the first K episodes [ 33 , 34 , 35 ] : \displaystyle\max_{\{\ell_{e}\},\,\mathcal{U}}\ \ \mathbb{E}\Bigl[\,\sum_{k=1}^{K}y_{k}\Bigr]\hskip 9.24994pt\text{s.t.} \displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{U}}\upsilon_{p}\leq\bar{\upsilon}, (3) \displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{e\in\mathcal{C}}\beta_{e}\Bigr]\leq\bar{\beta},\hskip 18.49988pt\mathbb{E}\Bigl[\sum_{e\in\mathcal{C}}\varepsilon_{e}\Bigr]\leq\bar{\varepsilon}. Here, y_{k} marks autonomous success in episode k and \upsilon_{p} the size of a pushed entry. For event e , \beta_{e} and \varepsilon_{e} denote bytes and joules; \mathcal{C} is the event set, and barred terms are budgets. The recovery index T_{r} is zero if all episodes succeed, \max\{k:y_{k}=0\} if recovery occurs by K , and K+1 otherwise. Since \sum_{k=1}^{K}y_{k}\geq K-T_{r} , the objective favors T_{r}\in\{0,1\} . Equation ( 3 ) selects link evidence; execution remains on the device and repair in the cloud, where the strong model runs, a candidate patch can be tested on the virtual device rather than on the user’s app [ 36 , 37 ] , and a tested patch serves every device under its version key. When budget permits, \mathcal{U} contains replayable D entries; each push costs about as much as the smallest evidence and prevents a later event. A stale-but-walkable step executes at most once per device, because its successor screen is observed only after the action; every other executed step passes \nu_{k} on v^{\prime} or comes from a patch committed for v^{\prime} . III-B Cloud Replay and On-Device Verification When v^{\prime} is obtainable, the gateway installs it on the virtual device and replays each path in \mathcal{E}_{c}(v) until \nu_{k} fails, without a model call. The list I(v^{\prime})=\{(p,k)\in D(v\!\to\!v^{\prime}):p\in\mathcal{V}\} restricts D to the replayable set \mathcal{V} . Its share \gamma=|\mathcal{V}|/|\mathcal{E}_{c}(v)| is 86.7% on our apps; paths blocked by login, payment, or device state remain for device verification. The gateway re-derives a patch for each entry, stores it in the patch set P(v^{\prime}) , pushes one bit per path plus a step index, and serves patches on demand. If an update precedes the push, or the cloud could not replay a path, the device marks the app’s paths Unverified (v^{\prime}) . During execution, it tests \nu_{k} without a model call: the first factor before the action and the second afterward. A failure raises an event. A stale-but-walkable step therefore costs one action rather than the lag in Obs. 1 ; passing steps form a verified prefix . For a flagged step, the device pulls its patch before reaching it. Phases I and II of Algorithm 1 cover both cases. III-C Staged Evidence and Patch Return The failure clock sends the smallest evidence first [ 38 ] (Obs. 2 ). The re-derivation operator \mathcal{R} maps evidence \xi_{\ell} to a verified patch or \bot . Level \xi_{0} contains the fingerprint and failing step; with app \alpha and version v^{\prime} , they identify the version key. Level \xi_{1} is the recorded-to-current accessibility-subtree diff, rooted at the expected element’s grandparent and extending three levels with one sibling hop. Levels \xi_{2} and \xi_{3} contain a low-resolution crop around the expected box and the screenshot, with bytes \beta_{0}<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<\beta_{3} . As in locator repair [ 17 , 16 ] , the cloud proposes candidate elements and tests the step on the virtual device. It commits a successful patch to P(v^{\prime}) or returns \bot . The first successful level resolves the event. (4) Past the knee, a crop or screenshot adds about 9.4 points at a multiple of the diff’s bytes, so the ladder closes most events at \xi_{0} or \xi_{1} . Because the ladder ends at the screenshot, it repairs every step that a screenshot alone repairs, at the expected bytes of ( 4 ) rather than \beta_{3} per event. REBASE keys events by \kappa=(\alpha,v^{\prime},f) , where \alpha identifies the app, rather than by device. The first report of \kappa triggers one re-derivation; later reports send only \xi_{0} and receive \pi_{\kappa} from P(v^{\prime}) [ 41 , 42 , 43 ] . If N_{\kappa} devices report \kappa , the cloud performs M=\sum_{\kappa}\mathbb{I}\!\left[\,N_{\kappa}\geq 1\,\right]\leq|D(v\!\to\!v^{\prime})| re-derivations. Without the key, \tilde{M}=\sum_{\kappa}N_{\kappa}\approx N\omega\,|D(v\!\to\!v^{\prime})| , where \omega is the fraction of N devices that break on that fingerprint. Keyed re-derivation therefore depends on distinct update-broken fingerprints, not fleet size. III-E Coverage and Fallbacks If a screen lacks an accessibility tree, as with a canvas or opaque web view, \xi_{1} is empty and the ladder moves from \xi_{0} to \xi_{2} . Every measured failure in our nine apps exposed a subtree. Paths unreachable on the virtual device, and apps unavailable to the cloud, skip replay but retain device verification and the failure clock [ 44 ] . The device confirms each returned patch through the next verified step.
