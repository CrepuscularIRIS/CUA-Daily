title: VeriCUA: Learning Proactive Verification for Reliable GUI Agent Rewards
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26469

§Abstract
Reinforcement learning for computer-use agents increasingly relies on proactive reward agents that interact with the post-execution GUI environment to verify what recorded screenshots cannot reveal. The reliability of such verification hinges on two coupled decisions: what to check and when to stop. Existing methods leave these decisions to prompted foundation models. However, neither scaling up the model nor providing few-shot examples reliably improves verification: prompted Judges ranging from 4B to GPT-5.4 all plateau at 68%-70% macro-average agreement. This is because the value of a check depends on the environment rather than the task alone, and becomes apparent only through execution. Learning from execution is itself constrained, as the Actor's terminal state cannot be faithfully replicated during training, allowing only one verification attempt per state. We introduce VeriCUA, a proactive reward agent that learns its verification strategy from these single attempts. A trainable Judge decides what to check and when to stop, while a frozen Probe executes the checks in the live environment. Online reinforcement learning optimizes the Judge for both verdict correctness and efficient Probe use, with returns normalized across terminal states. Trained only on OSWorld and evaluated without further training on MobileWorld and WebArena-Verified, VeriCUA achieves the highest reward agreement across all three benchmarks and lies on the agreement-cost Pareto frontier among proactive verifiers. It surpasses few-shot GPT-5.4 by 5.36 points with 47.6%-59.8% fewer Probe tokens, and even a 1B VeriCUA exceeds every prompted Judge. As the reward for Actor RL, it raises task success by 3.21 points overall and 5.40 points on held-out multi-application tasks. These results identify the verification strategy, not verifier scale, as the key to reliable GUI rewards.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Training computer-use agents (CUAs) with reinforcement learning requires reliable rewards for task completion ( Lv et al., 2026 ; Lai et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026 ) . Rule-based rewards are precise but brittle and difficult to scale ( Xie et al., 2024 ; El Hattami et al., 2025 ; Kong et al., 2026 ) , while passive model-based rewards are more flexible but can only verify what is exposed in the available observations ( Qi et al., 2025 ; Bai et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2025 ; Yang et al., 2025 ) . Decisive evidence, such as the contents of a saved file or other hidden application state, may therefore remain unobserved ( Cai et al., 2025 ) . Proactive verification addresses this limitation by interacting with the post-execution environment to acquire the missing evidence before issuing a verdict ( Dai et al., 2026 ; Cui et al., 2026 ; Shi et al., 2026 ) . Because such verification is performed for every training rollout, it must be not only accurate but also efficient enough to avoid invoking an expensive model for each reward. Interaction alone, however, does not guarantee a reliable verdict. Reliability depends on the verification strategy, which consists of two coupled decisions the task leaves open: what to check and when to stop . For a task that requires notes on every slide, one slide without notes already establishes failure ( Figure 1 (b)). The prompted verifier in Figure 1 (a) instead requests a slide-by-slide comparison and keeps scrolling without reaching a verdict. Scale does not fix this. Under the same verification loop, prompted Judges from a 4B model to GPT-5.4 all reach 68\% – 70\% macro-average agreement, and few-shot examples add at most 0.6 points ( Table 1 ). Our key observation is that a check’s value is revealed by executing it, not by reading the task . A prompted Judge sees the task and the recorded screenshots, which show what a plausible check looks like but not what evidence it will return. That depends on where the evidence resides, the state the Actor left, and what the Probe can reach. When to stop depends on how often screenshot-only verdicts turn out to be correct. The strategy must therefore be learned by executing verification and comparing its verdicts with true outcomes. Learning from execution, however, faces a single-attempt constraint : each Actor terminal state admits only one verification attempt, which rules out within-state rollout comparisons. An Actor starts from a resettable initial state, so RL can compare several rollouts from the same state ( Shao et al., 2024 ) . A GUI verifier starts from the Actor’s terminal state, the product of one stochastic execution. Re-execution may not reproduce it, identical screenshots can hide different application state, and probing itself changes it. We introduce VeriCUA , a proactive GUI reward agent that learns its verification strategy under this constraint. A trainable Judge decides what to check and when to stop, and a frozen GUI Probe executes the checks. Freezing the Probe ties outcome differences to the Judge and lets the Probe be swapped without retraining. We initialize the Judge with outcome-filtered teacher traces and then train it with online RL on complete attempts graded by executable outcomes, so each check is credited by the evidence it actually yields. The reward also depends on whether the Probe was used, which lets a single attempt signal whether stopping was right. Returns are normalized across terminal states in each batch ( Hu et al., 2025 ) , so no environment forking is required. We evaluate VeriCUA on OSWorld ( Xie et al., 2024 ) , MobileWorld ( Kong et al., 2026 ) , and WebArena-Verified ( El Hattami et al., 2025 ) . First, strategy matters more than scale. VeriCUA surpasses few-shot GPT-5.4 by 5.36 macro-average points with 47.6\% – 59.8\% fewer Probe tokens and lies on the agreement–cost Pareto frontier ( Figure 4 ). Even VeriCUA -1B outperforms prompted GPT-5.4 ( OpenAI, 2026 ) and its teacher Kimi-K2.6 ( Kimi, 2026 ) . Second, the learned strategy transfers. Trained only on OSWorld, the Judge achieves the highest agreement on unseen mobile and web environments. Third, better rewards train better agents. VeriCUA rewards raise downstream Pass@4 by 3.21 points overall and by 5.40 points on held-out MultiApp tasks.
