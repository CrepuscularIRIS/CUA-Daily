title: CaptchaAgent: Verifiable Visual Reasoning for CAPTCHA Solving
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12866

§Abstract
Modern reasoning CAPTCHAs require fine-grained perception, compositional relation reasoning, and grounded browser interaction. End-to-end vision-language model agents remain brittle in this setting because a missed object, incorrect count, or confused relation can propagate directly to the final action. To address this problem, we present CaptchaAgent, a structured visual reasoning framework for interactive CAPTCHA solving. Instead of predicting an action directly from a screenshot, CaptchaAgent first constructs a visual intermediate representation (V-IR) that explicitly records entities, attributes, and spatial or topological relations. A symbol-only ASCII protocol further requires the parser to commit to a concrete object set and count. Building on this global representation, localized evidence refinement revisits ambiguous regions at higher effective resolution, while counterfactual consistency verification tests whether the resulting action remains stable under task-preserving transformations. Across three proprietary backbones on Open CaptchaWorld, CaptchaAgent improves Pass@1 by 21.3-25.8 percentage points over a commercially available browser agent baseline evaluated with the same backbones. On the separate offline Oedipus-data benchmark, it further improves average Pass@1 by 9.2-12.3 percentage points over the Oedipus solver across three matched backbones. The intermediate traces show that exact counting, continuous geometry, and ordered execution remain major sources of automation difficulty for the evaluated agents, providing practical guidance for designing more resilient compositional CAPTCHAs.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
CAPTCHAs ( Completely Automated Public Turing tests to tell Computers and Humans Apart ) are a long-standing security mechanism against automated abuse and have also been studied as a building block for graphical authentication [ 1 , 2 , 3 ] . Their security margin has repeatedly narrowed with advances in optical character recognition (OCR), deep visual recognition, and data-driven CAPTCHA solving [ 4 , 5 , 6 ] . Recent CAPTCHA-style puzzles therefore combine cluttered layouts, object relations, counting, rotation, and interactive actions rather than relying on single-object recognition. Solving them requires not only recognizing visible content, but also constructing a coherent visual state, reasoning over relations, and converting the result into a valid browser action. CAPTCHA provides a security-relevant and objectively checked environment in which errors at any of these stages directly determine success or failure. Current vision-language model (VLM) and graphical user interface (GUI) agents commonly predict actions directly from pixels or interleave action prediction with an unconstrained natural-language chain of thought (CoT) [ 7 , 8 , 9 ] . Recent CAPTCHA solvers likewise combine multimodal prompting with structured action spaces, repeated model calls, or task-specific decomposition [ 10 , 11 , 12 ] . These approaches can describe a scene or revise an action, but they do not generally require an explicit, count-committed list of task-relevant entities and their relations to persist across perception, correction, and execution. This distinction matters for reasoning CAPTCHAs: one omitted object, wrong count, or confused spatial relation can invalidate the interaction, while an unconstrained CoT need not expose the visual state on which the action depends. The technical gap is therefore not merely stronger visual recognition, but an explicit intermediate state that supports global relational reasoning, targeted correction of local evidence, and action-level verification before execution. To address this gap, we propose CaptchaAgent, a structured visual reasoning framework for interactive CAPTCHA solving with three components. First, it converts the puzzle image into a visual intermediate representation (V-IR) that enumerates entities, attributes, and spatial or topological relations. The V-IR is expressed as a symbol-only ASCII box diagram whose legend commits to an exact object inventory and makes missing or inconsistent relations visible. Second, localized evidence refinement re-observes high-resolution regions around candidate actions without replacing the global inventory. Third, counterfactual consistency verification checks action signatures under task-preserving transformations such as color permutations, shifts, and instruction-aware flips. We operationalize verification as signature-level consistency under these transformations. Together, these components retain a structured scene state from perception through grounded browser execution. As illustrated in Fig. 1 , CaptchaAgent differs from direct and CoT-based agents by placing an explicit V-IR between page observation and action prediction, rather than relying on an implicit visual state or a free-form textual rationale. Across 20 Open CaptchaWorld task families, CaptchaAgent improves Pass@1 by 21.3–25.8 percentage points over BU-Agent, a commercially available browser automation agent adopted as the benchmark baseline [ 13 , 12 ] , when both systems use the same proprietary VLM backbone. On the separate offline Oedipus-data benchmark, CaptchaAgent also improves average Pass@1 by 9.2–12.3 percentage points over the reported Oedipus solver across three matched backbones. Component ablations show complementary gains from explicit representation, local refinement, and consistency checking. The resulting traces reveal a security-relevant pattern: discrete relational and interaction-heavy puzzles are more amenable to structured solving, whereas exact counting, continuous geometry, and ordered execution remain persistent bottlenecks. These observations identify which challenge properties current agents can externalize and which continue to preserve automation difficulty. Behavioral web-bot detection, webpage-level security analysis, and rate limits remain complementary defense layers [ 14 , 15 ] . In summary, this work makes the following contributions: 1. We propose a structured visual reasoning framework that converts CAPTCHA scenes into an explicit entity–relation representation before grounded action prediction. 2. We introduce an exact-count-committed ASCII V-IR together with localized evidence refinement and task-preserving counterfactual verification, enabling global–local reasoning and action-level consistency checking. 3. We demonstrate consistent improvements across three proprietary VLM backbones on 20 interactive CAPTCHA families and three backbones on Oedipus-data. ## III Method
Figure 2 gives an overview of CaptchaAgent. At each browser interaction step, the system isolates the puzzle region and object proposals, constructs a structured visual state, revisits local evidence around a candidate action, and checks the resulting action under a bounded set of task-preserving transformations. This section first defines webpage observation and executable grounding, then presents the V-IR, localized evidence refinement, counterfactual consistency verification, and execution checks that form the controller. III-A Webpage Processing CaptchaAgent solves each puzzle as a short-horizon web interaction. At browser interaction step t , it observes o_{t}=\{I_{t},x_{t},e_{t}\},\qquad e_{t}=\{e_{t}^{(i)}\}_{i=1}^{M_{t}}, (1) where I_{t} is the screenshot, x_{t} is the visible page text, and e_{t} is an indexed Document Object Model (DOM) snapshot containing M_{t} elements. Most challenges are centered on a rendered image. Let \mathcal{D}_{t} denote the set of image-bearing DOM indices. We select the largest such element as the primary puzzle image: i_{t}^{\mathrm{img}}=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal{D}_{t}}\mathrm{area}(e_{t}^{(i)}),\qquad J_{t}=\mathrm{render}(e_{t}^{(i_{t}^{\mathrm{img}})}). (2) Here, J_{t} is the rendered crop of the primary image-bearing element. Operating on J_{t} removes unrelated browser content while preserving a known mapping from local image coordinates to the underlying DOM element, so visually inferred points can be executed in the browser. Within J_{t} , we use Grounding-DINO [ 58 ] , an open-vocabulary object detector, to propose object-level regions: \{(b_{t}^{(j)},\ell_{t}^{(j)})\}_{j=1}^{K_{t}}=\mathrm{GDINO}(J_{t}),\qquad Z_{t}^{(j)}=\mathrm{crop}(J_{t};b_{t}^{(j)}), (3) where b_{t}^{(j)} , \ell_{t}^{(j)} , and Z_{t}^{(j)} are the bounding box, detector label, and crop for proposal j . These proposals form the patch set \mathcal{P}_{t}=\{(j,b_{t}^{(j)},Z_{t}^{(j)})\}_{j=1}^{K_{t}} used for object-level references and local inspection. Action representation. For the evaluated Open CaptchaWorld tasks, the executable action family is \texttt{action}_{t}\in\{\textsc{Click},\textsc{Mark},\textsc{Drag},\textsc{Hold}\} . An executable plan has the form \pi_{t}=(\texttt{action}_{t},i_{t},(r_{x,t},r_{y,t}),(d_{x,t},d_{y,t}),T_{\mathrm{hold},t}), (4) where i_{t} is the target DOM index and (r_{x,t},r_{y,t})\in[0,1]^{2} is an element-local start or click point. The optional (d_{x,t},d_{y,t}) specifies the horizontal and vertical drag offset in Cascading Style Sheets (CSS) pixels, so the drag endpoint is the start point plus this offset. The optional T_{\mathrm{hold},t} specifies the hold duration in milliseconds. For set-selection tasks, Mark is implemented as one or more grounded clicks on selectable elements. In contrast, reasoning-only tools do not enter the executable action set. When the plan refers to detector proposal j , the center of b_{t}^{(j)} is converted from pixels in J_{t} to normalized coordinates within the primary image element e_{t}^{(i_{t}^{\mathrm{img}})} . The target DOM index is then i_{t}^{\mathrm{img}} , and the normalized point supplies (r_{x,t},r_{y,t}) . This conversion connects object-level visual references to executable browser coordinates. III-B Captcha Solving CaptchaAgent computes one plan, executes it, and re-observes the page because many puzzles refresh or reveal new state after each action. The complete controller is denoted by \Phi : \hat{\pi}_{t}=\Phi(J_{t},x_{t},e_{t},\mathcal{P}_{t};m_{t-1}),\qquad o_{t+1}\sim\mathcal{E}(o_{t},\hat{\pi}_{t}), (5) where \hat{\pi}_{t} is the final executable plan, m_{t-1} is compact puzzle-scoped memory, and \mathcal{E} is the browser/environment transition induced by DOM updates, rendering latency, and the challenge backend. The following modules define \Phi . III-B 1 Visual Intermediate Representation A frozen VLM, used as the global scene parser, produces (\mathcal{V}_{t},m_{t}^{(0)})=f_{\theta}(J_{t},x_{t},\mathcal{P}_{t},m_{t-1}), (6) where \theta denotes the fixed VLM parameters, \mathcal{V}_{t}=(\mathrm{grid}_{t},\mathrm{legend}_{t}) is the ASCII diagram and legend, and m_{t}^{(0)} is a compact memory update. The protocol imposes three constraints: (1) commit to an exact entity count N_{t} ; (2) place only markers and spatial lines inside the box while recording attributes in the legend; and (3) state special relations for entity pairs, including containment, occlusion, contact, and links, or explicitly mark them absent. Figures 3 and 4 present two parallel cases, Click Order and Place Dot , respectively. Each case juxtaposes the web GUI, a text-only CoT, and the proposed ASCII-box V-IR. The CoT can mention object properties and approximate locations, but it need not commit to a complete scene state. By comparison, the V-IR separates spatial markup from semantic attributes, fixes the object inventory, and records relations in a repeatable format. This structure makes an omitted or duplicated entity easier to locate in the intermediate trace and gives subsequent local refinement and transformed-view comparison a shared visual state. III-B 2 Plan Inference After the V-IR fixes the scene state, an action decoder maps it to the executable representation in ( 4 ): \pi_{t}^{(0)}=\mathrm{Dec}(\mathcal{V}_{t},x_{t},e_{t},\mathcal{P}_{t}). (7) Here, \pi_{t}^{(0)} is the initial plan and \mathrm{Dec} is the VLM action-decoding step constrained to the four action families and argument fields defined above. A plan may target a DOM element directly or select a detector proposal that is converted to browser coordinates as described in Section III-A. III-B 3 Localized Evidence Refinement The global V-IR establishes object inventory and relations, but small symbols, thin connections, and subtle rotations may still be unresolved. Given the candidate interaction point \mathbf{u}_{t} in J_{t} , localized evidence refinement extracts R_{t}=\mathrm{crop}(J_{t};\mathrm{window}(\mathbf{u}_{t},s)) (8) and re-parses the region at higher effective resolution: (\pi_{t}^{r},m_{t}^{r})=\textsc{Refine}(\mathcal{V}_{t},\pi_{t}^{(0)},R_{t},x_{t},\mathcal{P}_{t},m_{t}^{(0)};\theta). (9) The refined plan \pi_{t}^{r} changes the initial plan only when local evidence supports a correction; it does not replace the global entity inventory. This global-to-local transition is especially useful for small text, icon matching, and occlusion cues. III-B 4 Counterfactual Consistency Verification CaptchaAgent next evaluates the refined decision under a pre-defined, task-specific set \mathcal{T} of deterministic, semantics-preserving transformations. The set includes spatial shifts, flips accompanied by left/right instruction rewrites, and color-channel permutations accompanied by color-token rewrites. For \tau\in\mathcal{T} , (J_{t}^{(\tau)},x_{t}^{(\tau)})=(\tau(J_{t}),\rho_{\tau}(x_{t})), (10) where \rho_{\tau} rewrites instruction tokens so that the transformed task remains equivalent. Patch boxes are transformed geometrically with the image. The transformed puzzle is parsed and decoded into \pi_{t}^{(\tau)} , which is then mapped back to the original coordinate frame. Direct equality between raw coordinates is too sensitive for plan comparison. We therefore define the plan-signature mapping \sigma as \sigma(\pi)=\big(\texttt{action},i,\mathrm{bin}(r_{x},r_{y}),\mathrm{bin}(d_{x},d_{y}),\mathrm{bin}(T_{\mathrm{hold}})\big), (11) where \mathrm{bin}(\cdot) coarsely quantizes continuous action parameters. CaptchaAgent first evaluates a small transformation subset \mathcal{T}_{1} and forms the signature multiset \mathcal{S}_{t}^{(1)}=[\sigma(\pi_{t}^{r})]\uplus[\sigma(\pi_{t}^{(\tau)}):\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{1}]. (12) Here, \uplus denotes multiset union, which preserves repeated signatures for majority voting. If these signatures disagree, a second bounded subset \mathcal{T}_{2} is added. The selected signature is \hat{s}_{t}=\mathrm{mode}\!\left(\mathcal{S}_{t}^{(1)}\uplus[\sigma(\pi_{t}^{(\tau)}):\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{2}]\right). (13) The final executable plan \hat{\pi}_{t} is a plan in the evaluated set whose signature equals \hat{s}_{t} , mapped to the original frame. Persistent ties are treated as uncertainty rather than model confidence. Transformations that alter the task semantics are excluded; for example, rotation is not used for rotation-matching puzzles. III-B 5 Execution Robustness Before executing \hat{\pi}_{t} , CaptchaAgent enforces the remaining interaction budget and verifies that the target DOM element is still rendered. For target i_{t} , the validity check is \mathrm{Valid}(i_{t})=\mathbb{I}[\mathrm{quads}(e_{t}^{(i_{t})})\neq\emptyset], (14) where \mathrm{quads}(e_{t}^{(i_{t})}) denotes the browser layout quadrilaterals associated with the rendered element. Invalid or stale targets trigger a bounded wait or re-parse. The memory m_{t} records instruction changes and previously executed patch or DOM indices needed for multi-step continuity.
