title: LiteGUI: Lightweight GUI Agents via Multi-Solution Guided Distillation and Dual-Level Reinforcement Learning
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07505

§Abstract
We present LiteGUI, a new framework for building lightweight GUI agents. GUI interaction poses unique challenges due to the long-horizon nature of complex tasks and the existence of multiple valid interaction paths, which are difficult for lightweight GUI agents to handle effectively. To address these challenges, LiteGUI introduces a two-stage post-training framework operating on logged GUI states. First, we propose Guided On-Policy Distillation, which provides training-time privileged teacher guidance at each GUI state by selecting the most-matched valid action from human-verified multi-solution action annotations, while leaving the student's on-policy rollout unchanged. Second, we develop Multi-Solution Dual-Level GRPO, which combines action-level supervision with history-conditioned, per-state planning-quality supervision while accounting for multiple valid actions at each logged GUI state. Together, these stages support multi-step GUI tasks without requiring the student to reproduce fixed demonstration trajectories. We further develop a scalable data generation pipeline and a corresponding multi-solution dataset, Lite-Dataset, to support the training and evaluation of GUI agents, addressing a gap in the existing literature. Extensive experiments across multiple GUI benchmarks demonstrate that LiteGUI substantially improves the performance of lightweight GUI agents over existing training paradigms, including SFT, on-policy distillation and GRPO, and achieves competitive or superior performance compared with substantially larger models.

Automated computer use has received increasing attention in recent years ( Hurst et al., 2024 ; Parker Lyman, 2025 ; anthropic, 2026 ; Team et al., 2023 ; Bai et al., 2023 ; Bai et al., 2025a ) . Among these approaches, GUI agents represent a specialized class of systems that leverage visual understanding to interpret and execute computer-based tasks. While prior work has primarily focused on building GUI agents using large-scale models ( Qin et al., 2025 ; Liu et al., 2025a ; Agashe et al., 2025 ) , the development of lightweight models for efficient deployment is challenging and remains largely underexplored. Smaller models are typically considered insufficient for handling such complex tasks due to their limited capacity. We observe that the commonly used supervised fine-tuning (SFT) paradigm—particularly for smaller models—tends to overfit to domain-specific and fixed interaction trajectories. This limitation degrades zero-shot generalization and hinders effective error recovery in dynamic UI environments. In this paper, we propose a new approach, named LiteGUI , to improve the performance of lightweight GUI agents through on-policy distillation and reinforcement learning. While distillation has been successfully applied to tasks such as summarization, translation, and arithmetic reasoning (e.g., GKD ( Agarwal et al., 2024 ; Chen et al., 2025 ; Shenfeld et al., 2026 ) ), its effectiveness in GUI agent settings remains limited. This is largely due to the higher task complexity and the relatively weaker performance of available teacher models. To address the challenges, we propose Guided On-Policy Distillation (Guided-OPD) , to ensure that the teacher model provides reliable guidance by conditioning on training-time privileged information derived from human-verified valid-action annotations. Furthermore, to account for the multi-solution nature of GUI tasks, the proposed method compares the student’s on-policy output with the valid-action set for the current GUI state and selects the most-matched valid action , providing adaptive action-level guidance without imposing a single fixed target completion at each logged state. In addition, careful design of reinforcement learning (RL) strategies is essential to address the challenges inherent in GUI-based tasks. Existing RL approaches are often limited by their reliance on single-solution rewards, lack of long-horizon planning, and susceptibility to losing state context during execution. To overcome these limitations, we propose a Multi-Solution Dual-Level GRPO . First the method includes dual-level reward framework that jointly supports long-term planning and short-term execution. At the macro level, we introduce robust, model-evaluated subtask rewards that provide structured guidance for decomposing complex, long-horizon tasks. At the micro level, by integrating real-time state perception and trajectory recording, the framework dynamically aligns low-level execution outcomes with an equivalent multi-solution set, thereby mitigating the “false negative” penalties induced by rigid single-label supervision, and thus improving the execution precision while robustly preserving generalization across diverse and complex UI scenarios. In summary, the core contributions of this paper are: We propose LiteGUI , a novel framework for building lightweight GUI agents that addresses the unique challenges of GUI interaction tasks, including the long-horizon nature of complex tasks, the existence of multiple valid interaction paths, and the limited availability of capable models and high-quality training data. Specifically, we propose (1) Most-Matched Guided On-Policy Distillation , which selects the valid action most closely aligned with the student’s current output from the human-verified valid-action set, using it only as training-time privileged teacher guidance to reduce hallucinations and supervision–state mismatch; and (2) Multi-Solution Dual-Level GRPO , which integrates robust model-evaluated subtask-planning rewards with multi-solution action-set matching to jointly support long-horizon planning and short-horizon perception and execution.
