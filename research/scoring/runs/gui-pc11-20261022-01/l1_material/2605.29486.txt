title: PhoneWorld: From Real-App Trajectories to Dynamic and Verifiable Environments for Phone-Use Agents
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29486

§Abstract
Real applications provide the training setting closest to phone-agent deployment, but are difficult to reset, scale safely, and verify programmatically. Static screenshots and interaction trajectories preserve realistic evidence but cannot generate new experience. We introduce PhoneWorld, a trace-grounded framework that converts such evidence into runnable, resettable, and verifiable Android environments. PhoneWorld induces a usage-weighted interaction skeleton from observed pages, transitions, and state-changing operations; translates it into a behavior-grounded app specification; realizes the specification through an autonomous build--inspect--repair loop; and synthesizes executable tasks with programmatic verifiers. The resulting suite spans 34 consumer-facing apps across 16 domains and supports an audited online benchmark, verified trajectory generation, and online RL through common reset and verification interfaces. Evaluations with diverse general and open-source GUI agents show that PhoneWorld supports reliable end-to-end online interaction and exposes capabilities complementary to AndroidWorld. Controlled SFT experiments further show that PhoneWorld trajectories complement AndroidWorld supervision, transfer across online and offline benchmarks, and become more effective as data volume and app coverage increase. Under a matched RL budget, combining PhoneWorld mock-app rollouts with real-app rollouts improves performance over real-app RL alone on both real-phone tasks and AndroidWorld. Together, these results demonstrate that trace-grounded executable abstraction can bridge realistic mobile behavior and scalable agent learning, turning limited real-app evidence into a growing supply of controllable and verifiable environments for training and evaluation.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Mobile agents have advanced rapidly from single-screen perception—such as screen question answering, UI-element grounding, and isolated clicking—to autonomous systems that can execute complex, multi-step tasks with little human intervention ( Wang et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2025c ; Zhang et al., 2025 ; Qin et al., 2025 ; Wang et al., 2025a ; Zhou et al., 2025 ; Xu et al., 2026a ; Liu et al., 2025 ) . Learning these capabilities depends critically on interaction data, whose scale and diversity are determined by the environments in which agents act. Because deployment ultimately occurs in real applications, improving real-world assistance requires agents to learn authentic interfaces, content, navigation structures, and state transitions; direct real-app interaction therefore provides the closest training setting to deployment. Alongside this shift in capability, mobile-agent research has expanded from single-screen understanding to learning from static offline trajectories, and more recently to dynamic data collection and online training in executable mobile environments ( Xu et al., 2025b ; Shi et al., 2025 ; Luo et al., 2025 ; Chen et al., 2025 ; Xi et al., 2026 ) . The shift from static supervision to dynamic interaction, however, exposes a fundamental tension: the environments closest to real-world deployment are also the most difficult to use for training at scale. Concretely, production apps are difficult to reset and run safely in parallel, and rarely expose programmatic task verification, limiting scalable data collection and online training. AndroidWorld ( Rawles et al., 2025 ) and MobileWorld ( Kong et al., 2026 ) enable reproducible online evaluation through reset and programmatic verification, but rely primarily on relatively simple open-source or system apps, leaving a substantial gap from widely deployed consumer apps in functional scope, page structure, and interaction logic. AndroidAgentArena ( Chai et al., 2026 ) and AndroidDaily ( Sui et al., 2026 ) instead evaluate live production or closed-source apps, narrowing the realism gap. Yet such environments remain difficult to reset and do not readily support programmatic verification. This leaves three questions: (1) Static-to-Dynamic Environment Synthesis : Can static real-app screenshots and trajectories be transformed into runnable mock apps that preserve their task-relevant pages, navigation paths, operations, and state changes; (2) Controllable and Verifiable Environment Support : Can these apps be equipped with resettable state, executable tasks, and programmatic verifiers to support evaluation, verified data collection, and online RL; and (3) Transferable Training Utility : Can supervision and rewards produced in these environments transfer beyond the mock apps and complement real-app-derived training? To bridge this realism–controllability gap, we introduce PhoneWorld , a trace-grounded environment synthesis framework that distills the task-relevant structure and behavior of real applications into dynamic, resettable, and programmatically verifiable mobile environments. As shown in Figure 1 , PhoneWorld operationalizes this framework through four stages: (1) Usage-Weighted Skeleton Induction identifies behaviorally meaningful page types and recovers their visitation frequencies, navigation graph, and state-changing operations. (2) Behavior-Grounded App Specification translates this skeleton into prioritized page PRDs, reusable functional components, and runtime content and state specifications. (3) Agentic Environment Realization autonomously implements and repairs the specification as a runnable Android app, followed by final human acceptance testing. (4) Verifier-Coupled Task Synthesis derives executable tasks and programmatic verifiers from the same content and mutable state, enabling verified rollouts for data generation, reinforcement learning, and evaluation. The resulting PhoneWorld suite comprises 34 consumer-facing Android environments spanning 16 domains. Each environment couples a runnable interface with deterministic content, resettable mutable state, grounded tasks, and programmatic verifiers, enabling the same infrastructure to support evaluation, verified rollout generation, and online RL. The current suite contains a 120-task audited benchmark, a pool of 7,936 executable tasks, and 3,354 verifier-confirmed successful trajectories. Crucially, these environments provide transferable training benefits. Under a 36K-step data budget, replacing 10K steps of AndroidWorld supervision with 10K PhoneWorld rollout steps improves success by 52.5 points on PhoneWorld and 14.7 points on AndroidWorld. Holding the PhoneWorld budget at 10K steps, expanding app coverage from 5 to 34 yields further gains of 18.3 points on PhoneWorld and 10.4 points on AndroidWorld, demonstrating that environment diversity contributes beyond data volume.
