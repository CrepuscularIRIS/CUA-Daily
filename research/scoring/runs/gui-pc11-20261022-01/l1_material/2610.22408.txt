title: ECHOES: Seeing Beyond the Current Image via Recursive History Compression and Privileged On-Policy Distillation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22408

§Abstract
Long-horizon GUI agents rely on visual history to maintain task states, but retaining historical screenshots increases visual-token usage and inference costs, while textual summaries can discard critical state information, undermining long-horizon reliability. We propose ECHOES, which leverages training-time privileged information to guide textual history compression and strengthen long-horizon reasoning. Specifically, ECHOES adopts a Compression-Think-Action (CTA) format to recursively update a textual history state and reason. The same model, given privileged multi-image inputs, serves as a teacher that provides dense token-level on-policy distillation on student-generated CTA rollouts. Our Perception-Memory Fidelity (PMF) Gap-guided OPD-RL framework dynamically balances privileged distillation and task-reward optimization, with periodic teacher updates supporting continued improvement. We further introduce ECHOESBMK, a fine-grained diagnostic benchmark derived from real GUI agent failure trajectories to jointly evaluate visual perception and cross-modal state retention. Experiments on four GUI benchmarks and ECHOESBMK demonstrate substantial gains in task performance and memory fidelity. Without retaining historical screenshots at deployment, ECHOES achieves competitive performance with a favorable performance-inference-cost trade-off and exhibits positive zero-shot transfer to unseen visual planning tasks.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have enabled agent systems capable of perceiving, reasoning, and acting in complex visual environments, with increasing success in long-horizon visual reasoning tasks ( Xu et al., 2025 ; Liu et al., 2025a ) . As a representative application, graphical user interface (GUI) agents have made substantial progress in single-step visual decision-making and grounding ( Cheng et al., 2024 ; Lin et al., 2025 ) , yet remain brittle in long-horizon control tasks ( Tian et al., 2025 ; Zhou et al., 2026 ) . A key cause is state-retention failure, which causes decisions and execution to drift out of alignment with the actual environment state. As interaction trajectories grow, long-horizon GUI agents must therefore not only understand the current interface but also preserve past states that remain relevant to future decisions. Existing approaches support long-horizon reasoning by retaining visual history ( Zhou et al., 2026 ) , generating textual summaries ( Liu et al., 2025a ) , or maintaining structured states ( Tian et al., 2025 ) . However, these gains exhibit diminishing returns as trajectories lengthen, while accumulating visual tokens, context length, and KV-cache overhead hinder efficient deployment ( Huang et al., 2025 ; Zhou et al., 2026 ) . Long-horizon visual agents thus face a fundamental tension: additional historical screenshots increase inference costs, whereas directly converting visual history into text can omit critical task states through cross-modal information loss. We term this loss the Perception–Memory Fidelity Gap (PMF Gap). This motivates a different question: Can multi-image visual history be used only during training to transfer its decision-making capabilities to a model that relies solely on the current image and low-cost reasoning over compressed history at deployment? To address this question, we propose ECHOES, a training method for long-horizon visual agents, as illustrated in Figure 1 . We model task-relevant compressed history as a learnable approximate sufficient statistic of multi-image visual history for subsequent decisions. We first adopt the Compression–Think–Action (CTA) reasoning mode, which continuously maintains long-horizon task states through recursive Compression. We then introduce training-time privileged on-policy distillation (OPD) ( Agarwal et al., 2024 ) , using the stronger multi-image privileged view as the teacher to provide dense token-level supervision on CTA trajectories generated by the single-image + Compression deployable view itself. This encourages the textual state to progressively preserve information in the full visual history that is relevant to subsequent decisions. Building on this design, we construct a PMF Gap-guided OPD–RL training framework that dynamically coordinates the optimization weights of OPD and GRPO ( Shao et al., 2024 ) across rounds according to the information gap between the privileged and deployable observation modes. When the gap is large, the framework emphasizes privileged-policy supervision; as it narrows, task-reward optimization receives greater weight, gradually shifting learning from absorbing the privileged policy’s information advantage to directly optimizing the actual task objective. To evaluate GUI agents’ long-horizon performance at a finer granularity, we additionally introduce ECHOESBMK, a benchmark constructed from real failure trajectories that spans seven task categories across three levels and jointly evaluates perception and cross-modal information retention. Experiments show that ECHOES substantially improves performance on long-horizon GUI benchmarks and ECHOESBMK with lower inference overhead, while demonstrating strong zero-shot generalization to non-GUI visual planning environments. Further experiments reveal pronounced improvements on longer-horizon and history-dependent tasks, and show that these gains stem from the CTA reasoning mode and the training design. In summary, our main contributions are as follows: 1. We propose ECHOES, a training-time privileged OPD method that adopts CTA reasoning and uses additional historical visual information to provide dense supervision, transferring the decision-making advantage of multi-image inputs to a low-cost deployment mode that relies only on the current observation and a recursive textual state. 2. We propose a PMF Gap-guided OPD–RL training framework that dynamically balances privileged distillation and task-reward optimization according to the privileged–deployable information gap. A round-wise self-evolving teacher continuously strengthens both observation modes, substantially improving long-horizon visual decision-making. 3. We construct ECHOESBMK, a fine-grained benchmark derived from real GUI agent failure trajectories, to jointly evaluate visual perception and memory fidelity in the conversion of visual information into textual states.
