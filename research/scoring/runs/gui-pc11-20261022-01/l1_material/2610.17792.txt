title: MHVF: Training-Free GUI Grounding via Verification-Guided Multi-Hypothesis Search
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17792

§Abstract
GUI grounding enables web agents to translate natural-language instructions into actionable interface locations. However, dense layouts and small, visually similar controls can lead models toward plausible but incorrect elements, while refinement around a single prediction may leave alternative targets unexplored. We propose the Multi-Hypothesis Verification Framework (MHVF) for training-free GUI grounding. It combines multi-hypothesis exploration with local visual verification using a single pretrained vision-language model. To explore alternative targets, MHVF constructs spatial hypotheses from multiple full-screen predictions and examines the corresponding regions through zoomed re-localization. To resolve ambiguity among the resulting candidates, it verifies their agreement with the instruction in marked local views. Verification scores then guide a second round of local search and rank candidates retained from both search levels for final selection. Experiments with five general-purpose and GUI-specific models across eight benchmark settings demonstrate improvements in grounding accuracy in most settings. MHVF improves UI-Venus-Ground-7B by 4.3 and 2.1 percentage points on Mind2Web and VisualWebBench, respectively, and raises Qwen2.5-VL-7B's accuracy on ScreenSpot-Pro from 23.6% to 53.6%.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Web agents rely on GUI grounding to translate natural-language instructions into clicks on the intended interface elements. Vision-language models (VLMs) enable this process directly from screenshots, but grounding remains a major obstacle to reliable web interaction ( Zheng et al., 2024 ) . Dense page layouts, small controls, and visually similar elements make precise localization challenging, particularly when fine details are lost at the model’s input resolution ( Liu et al., 2024 ) . Figure 1 (a) illustrates such a failure: given an instruction to select a product’s comparison checkbox, the model clicks the product image. Recent training-free approaches improve GUI grounding through zooming, multi-view prediction, and candidate comparison. ZoomClick investigates staged zooming, MVP aggregates predictions across multiple views, and AutoFocus combines region proposals with visual candidate comparison ( Jiang et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2025 ; Yao et al., 2026 ) . Magnification provides finer visual evidence, but successful grounding also requires inspecting regions that contain the target and choosing a candidate that matches the instruction. Initial predictions can guide this exploration by suggesting several possible target locations, while verification can assess the candidates obtained through local inspection. This motivates using local visual verification to connect candidate assessment with subsequent region search and final selection. To this end, we propose the Multi-Hypothesis Verification Framework (MHVF) for training-free GUI grounding. It combines multi-hypothesis search with local visual verification using a single pretrained VLM. At the first level, multiple full-screen predictions identify potential target regions for inspection. The model re-localizes the target in magnified views of these regions and verifies whether each candidate matches the instruction. To focus additional exploration, MHVF reopens the region associated with the highest-scoring candidate for a second round of hypothesis generation, re-localization, and verification. Candidates from both levels are retained, and the point with the highest verification score is selected as the final click. We conduct extensive experiments with general-purpose and GUI-specific models across web, desktop, and cross-platform interfaces. The evaluation covers web grounding on Mind2Web and VisualWebBench, high-resolution professional interfaces on ScreenSpot-Pro, and cross-platform grounding on MMBench-GUI L2, among other benchmarks. The results show that MHVF improves the grounding accuracy of existing models in most evaluated settings. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose MHVF, a training-free framework for GUI grounding that explores multiple spatial hypotheses and verifies the resulting candidates using a single pretrained VLM. • We develop a two-level search mechanism that uses local verification scores to guide region reopening and select the final click from candidates retained across both levels. • We evaluate MHVF on five models across eight benchmark settings covering web, desktop, and cross-platform interfaces, demonstrating improved grounding accuracy in most settings.
