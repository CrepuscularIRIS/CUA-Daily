title: LLM4Robot-Bench: How Far Can GPT-6 Astra Go in Robotic Manipulation? A Hierarchical, Paired Sim-Real Benchmark
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34527

§Abstract
We introduce LLM4Robot-Bench, a hierarchical, paired Sim-Real benchmark for evaluating physical interaction capabilities in contact-rich manipulation. Its 16 household tasks span three cumulative levels: contact selection and geometric alignment (L1), force regulation within sustained contact (L2), and coordinated contact reconfiguration and force regulation (L3). Each task is instantiated in physically and visually matched simulated and real-world environments, with shared task definitions and success criteria. We evaluate GPT-6 Astra zero-shot with three trials per task in each environment. Real-world success rates decline from 80.0% at L1 to 47.6% at L2 and 0.0% at L3, with corresponding simulation rates of 66.7%, 47.6%, and 0.0%. Ten of the 16 tasks have identical success counts across environments; the remaining six differ by one success out of three trials. Across both environments, recurring difficulties in spatial reasoning, contact selection, force regulation, and coordination often prevent local physical progress from accumulating into task completion. These findings characterize Astra's observed capability boundary across increasing interaction demands and, for this model, support matched simulation as a repeatable, scalable testbed for studying the sustained, coordinated physical interactions that limit reliable manipulation.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
GPT-6 Astra and other general-purpose foundation models have shown promise for robotic manipulation, while reliable execution remains challenging ( Zhang et al., 2026a ; Yang et al., 2026 ; Huang et al., 2026 ) . Recent work explores adaptation from demonstrations and interaction feedback without parameter updates ( Cheng et al., 2026 ; Liu et al., 2026 ) , as well as iterative program development for long-horizon, contact-rich tasks ( Shen et al., 2026 ) . Practical use in homes and factories, however, requires reliable contact-rich execution: establishing suitable contacts, regulating forces through them, and coordinating contact changes as a task proceeds ( Suomalainen et al., 2022 ; Noseworthy et al., 2024 ) . Aggregate success rates alone do not identify which physical interaction requirements constrain performance. We therefore ask: which requirements can Astra satisfy as interactions become more demanding, and to what extent do the observed capabilities and limitations persist across simulation and reality? To address these questions, we introduce LLM4Robot-Bench, a hierarchical, paired Sim–Real benchmark that organizes 16 household manipulation tasks by their physical interaction requirements (Figure 2 ; Section 3 ). The hierarchy is organized around two dimensions: contact configuration and force regulation. L1 emphasizes contact selection and geometric alignment. L2 adds task-specific force regulation within sustained contact, directly or through a tool or container, without requiring contact reconfiguration to continue the operation. L3 additionally requires coordinating contact reconfiguration with force regulation. Levels describe task versions rather than task names: blocks clustered at a dustpan entrance can be swept in one stroke (L2), whereas dispersed blocks require successive contacts (L3). Spatial reasoning and execution precision remain relevant throughout. By relating performance to explicit interaction requirements, the hierarchy supports analysis of capabilities and limitations and provides successive evaluation targets beyond grasping and placement. LLM4Robot-Bench provides physically and visually matched real-world and simulated environments for each task (Figure 1 ). Real-world trials provide a reference for physical execution, while simulation supports controlled, repeatable evaluation at scale. Using the physical setup as the reference, we match workspace layouts, object geometry and appearance, robot embodiment, and camera views. Physical measurements and observed contact responses guide simulation calibration (Section 4 ). This correspondence supports comparisons of task completion, intermediate progress, and recurring interaction difficulties. Corresponding task definitions and documented success criteria guide these comparisons. We evaluate GPT-6 Astra zero-shot with three trials per task in each environment. The evaluation yields three main findings. First, reported success rates decline across the interaction levels: from 80.0% at L1 to 47.6% at L2 and 0.0% at L3 in the real world, with corresponding simulation rates of 66.7%, 47.6%, and 0.0%. Second, local physical progress does not reliably accumulate into task completion. Astra may turn a cap or move a drawer without completing the required operation. Execution analysis identifies four recurring interaction bottlenecks: spatial reasoning and execution precision, contact configuration selection and establishment, force regulation, and coordination within and across interactions (Section 6.4 ). Third, for GPT-6 Astra, the two environments show consistent performance trends and similar interaction difficulties. Ten of the 16 tasks have identical reported success counts, and the remaining six differ by one successful trial. Together, these findings characterize Astra’s observed limitations under increasing interaction demands and support matched simulation as a testbed for studying the sustained, coordinated physical interactions that constrain reliable manipulation. Our main contributions are: • LLM4Robot-Bench, a hierarchical benchmark for physical interaction. We define three cumulative levels based on contact configuration and force regulation, and organize 16 household tasks according to the requirements of each task version, providing successive evaluation targets beyond grasping and placement. • Physically and visually matched Sim–Real environments. We construct simulated counterparts of physical tasks through scene reconstruction and calibration against observed contact responses, with corresponding task definitions and documented success criteria for comparison across environments. • A systematic zero-shot evaluation of GPT-6 Astra. We characterize task completion, intermediate progress, and four recurring interaction bottlenecks across levels and environments, identifying the physical interaction requirements that challenge reliable execution. \FloatBarrier
