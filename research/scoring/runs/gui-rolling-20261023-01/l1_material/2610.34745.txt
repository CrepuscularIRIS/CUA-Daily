title: SceneAgent: An Agentic Pipeline from 3D and 4D Captures to Simulation Environments
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34745

§Abstract
Visual captures provide detailed scene appearance, but converting them into interactive simulation environments requires object reconstruction, physical modeling, and correction of intermediate errors. We present SceneAgent, an agentic pipeline that coordinates these steps through simulator-based visual inspection and repair. The pipeline processes 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), photogrammetry, LiDAR captures, AI-generated 3DGS scenes, and 4D videos of dynamic scenes, and combines object segmentation, physical parameter estimation, part decomposition, articulation, and deformable simulation. To support policy learning, SceneAgent generates diverse object variants ("digital sisters") and scripted demonstrations. On a cluttered bin scene from GraspClutter6D, SceneAgent raises F1 at 1 cm for every coding agent with scorable outputs both with and without its skills, and its reconstructions are better than SimFoundry: all agent configurations exceed SimFoundry's F1 score, with a best of 60.75% versus 6.9%. On three dynamic scenes from 4DCodeBench, SceneAgent raises the mean dynamic IoU of Gemini 4 Argon from 0.86% to 99.09%. We further demonstrate diverse simulated interactions and preliminary policy transfer to a real robot.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Generalist robot policies [ 6 , 38 , 23 , 5 , 40 , 36 ] learn from large teleoperated datasets to perform a wide range of manipulation tasks specified by language instructions. Collecting these datasets is labor-intensive and can take months or years. Similarly, evaluating the resulting policies in a target environment requires physical robot trials, which are time-consuming, difficult to reproduce, and costly to scale [ 2 ] . Simulation offers a way to scale both data collection and evaluation. Reconstructed environments can generate demonstrations [ 67 , 63 , 59 ] , support policies that transfer to physical robots [ 45 , 27 , 68 ] , and provide estimates of real-world policy performance [ 19 , 47 , 69 , 31 , 30 ] . In particular, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [ 22 ] reconstructs photorealistic scene appearance from images, enabling simulators to render observations grounded in a captured workspace [ 45 , 11 , 21 ] . Visual reconstruction alone, however, does not provide the object geometry, physical parameters, or articulation needed for interaction. Real-to-sim systems recover these components by combining segmentation, mesh generation, pose alignment, and physical modeling [ 19 , 47 , 68 , 12 ] . SimFoundry, for example, supports articulated objects and estimates their physical parameters [ 47 ] . A remaining challenge is to coordinate these components across diverse inputs and correct errors that arise between stages. An incorrect mask, scale, or transform can produce an unusable object even when the individual models return plausible outputs. We present SceneAgent, an agentic pipeline that converts 3D and 4D captures into interactive simulation environments (Figure 2 ). Agents execute reconstruction and physical modeling tools, inspect rendered outputs in the target simulator, and repair visible errors before the scene is used downstream. The resulting environments contain segmented objects with estimated physical parameters, decomposed parts, and articulation or soft-body behavior where needed. For 4D captures, which are videos of dynamic scenes, agents also fit simulated dynamics such as liquid flow, deformation, and destruction mechanics to the observed scene (Section 3.6 ). SceneAgent also generates digital sisters—related object variants—and combines captured objects with artist-authored environments to expand the range of training scenes. We evaluate reconstruction quality on a cluttered bin scene from GraspClutter6D [ 3 ] and a Messy Kitchen dataset from CollectData [ 8 ] using coding agents with and without SceneAgent, and compare with SimFoundry. Here, a harness is the software environment in which a coding agent runs, and skills are the reusable instructions and tools that guide its conversion workflow. With SceneAgent, F1 at 1 cm improves for every harness with scorable paired outputs, and reconstruction quality is much better than SimFoundry’s (Figure 5 ). On three dynamic scenes from 4DCodeBench [ 51 ] , where agents reconstruct simulations from videos, SceneAgent raises the mean dynamic IoU of Gemini 4 Argon from 0.86% to 99.09% (Figure 3 ). In summary, our contributions are: • An agentic scene-conversion workflow that integrates reconstruction, physical parameter estimation, part decomposition, and articulation with simulator-based visual inspection and repair, and that extends from 3D captures to 4D captures by fitting simulated dynamics to the motion observed in a video. • Scene augmentation through digital sisters and hybrid environments that combine captured objects with artist-authored assets, together with an automated research loop that turns failures of a policy on the real robot into simulated training data. • A quantitative study of reconstruction quality across coding-agent harnesses on static captures and on dynamic scenes from 4DCodeBench, complemented by qualitative examples of articulated, deformable, and contact-rich interactions and preliminary real-world policy transfer.
