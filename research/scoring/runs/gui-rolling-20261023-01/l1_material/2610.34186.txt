title: HuGe: Scaling Humanoid Generalist Vision-Language-Action Models with Egocentric Experience
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34186

§Abstract
Extending Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) to humanoid robots requires coordinated whole-body control and diverse demonstrations that are costly to collect in real-world scenarios. Although egocentric human demonstrations provide a complementary source of task experience, differences in visual embodiment and missing robot proprioception complicate transfer to humanoid policies. To address these challenges, we introduce HuGe, a family of Humanoid Generalist VLAs pre-trained on over 2,000 hours of egocentric human and robot data. Specifically, HuGe employs three existing representative VLA architectures to predict latent motion tokens for a pretrained whole-body controller, together with hand-control signals. Based on this, we introduce a three-stage training pipeline that combines egocentric pre-training, humanoid adaptation, and downstream fine-tuning. To facilitate transfer across embodiments, we construct pseudo-proprioceptive states through simulation playback during pre-training and modify arm appearance in egocentric observations through re-rendering during downstream fine-tuning. With this pipeline, HuGe achieves the state-of-the-art on simulation benchmark HumanoidArena. Furthermore, real-world experiments on the Unitree G1 indicate that augmenting robot demonstrations with visually adapted egocentric demonstrations significantly increases success on a pick-and-drop task.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recent advances in Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) [ 60 , 16 , 7 , 6 , 53 , 19 ] fueled by Vision-Language Models (VLMs) [ 4 , 37 ] and World Models (WMs) [ 47 , 1 ] have enabled language-conditioned manipulation across a wide range of tabletop tasks. Building on this progress, extending VLAs to humanoid robots offers the potential to perform diverse tasks in human environments by combining manipulation with whole-body mobility. Despite this potential, adapting VLAs to humanoid robots remains challenging because task execution requires coordinated whole-body motion while maintaining balance and stability. To meet these control requirements, existing humanoid loco-manipulation approaches commonly employ hierarchical architectures that combine high-level policies with reinforcement learning (RL)-based controllers [ 13 , 52 , 2 ] . To this end, a central research question is how to train generalist VLAs that translate language instructions into coordinated whole-body behaviors across diverse tasks. Realizing this goal requires addressing two key challenges. First, the high-dimensional action spaces and complex dynamics of humanoid robots make it difficult to directly transfer VLA policies developed for robotic manipulators to whole-body control. In particular, arm movements must be coordinated with the torso and lower body to accomplish manipulation objectives while maintaining balance and stability. Consequently, VLA-based humanoid control requires an action interface that connects task-level predictions to coordinated whole-body execution. Second, beyond these control challenges, learning generalist humanoid behaviors requires diverse whole-body demonstrations, yet collecting such data on real-world robots is costly and time-consuming. To alleviate this data bottleneck, egocentric human demonstrations offer a complementary source of task experience for large-scale training. However, differences in visual embodiment and the absence of robot proprioceptive observations complicate the direct use of these demonstrations for humanoid policy learning. These challenges motivate an approach that integrates VLA policies with whole-body control and supports effective transfer from egocentric human experience to humanoid robots through a scalable training pipeline. To address these challenges, we introduce HuGe , a family of Hu manoid Ge neralist VLAs that operate through a whole-body controller and learn from a large-scale mixture of egocentric human and robot data. Specifically, HuGe builds on existing representative VLA architectures and follows a three-stage training pipeline that sequentially incorporates egocentric human data, humanoid teleoperation data, and a mixture of downstream demonstrations. To support this pipeline, we assemble a training corpus comprising over 2,000 hours of egocentric and robot data. Furthermore, we prepare egocentric data for humanoid policy learning during both pre-training and downstream fine-tuning. In particular, during pre-training, we use simulation playback to construct pseudo-proprioceptive states for egocentric data that lack robot proprioceptive observations. Building on this pre-training stage, we re-render and replace arm regions in egocentric observations during downstream fine-tuning. To assess the effectiveness of this approach, we compare HuGe with baseline methods on the HumanoidArena simulation benchmark and further evaluate its performance on real-world humanoid tasks. Overall, HuGe achieves the state-of-the-art performance on HumanoidArena, demonstrating its capability for humanoid whole-body control, while comprehensive real-world experiments and solid analyses further demonstrate the effectiveness of the training recipe. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: \bullet We introduce HuGe , a family of humanoid generalist VLAs, trained on over 2,000 hours of egocentric human and robot data through a three-stage training pipeline. \bullet We develop data adaptation methods that facilitate the transfer of egocentric human experience to humanoid policy learning. \bullet HuGe achieves the state-of-the-art performance against baselines on HumanoidArena, while comprehensive real-world experiments and analyses validate our design choices and training paradigm.
