title: BlenderWorld: Benchmarking Visual Agents for 3D Creation from Real-World References
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23986

§Abstract
Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have enabled them to act as autonomous agents, creating and editing 3D models via code or direct software manipulation. In practical workflows, these models must infer object shapes, materials, spatial layouts, and lighting from diverse references while strictly following instructions to reconstruct, modify, or design 3D content. However, existing benchmarks provide limited coverage of such open-ended requests across real-world scenarios. To bridge this gap, we introduce BlenderWorld, the first comprehensive benchmark dedicated to evaluating frontier models on realistic 3D creation tasks. BlenderWorld comprises 223 tasks spanning architecture, products, natural environments, and scientific visualization, driven by text, image, video, and structured specification inputs. To rigorously assess these complex deliverables, we establish a unified evaluation protocol: we render the submitted Blender projects from multiple viewpoints and evaluate them against 2,651 task-specific binary rubrics alongside holistic visual quality scores. Benchmarking thirteen frontier models reveals that GPT-6-Astra achieves the highest overall fused score of 79.9, followed by Opus-5 (73.0) and GPT-5.6-Sol (67.7). Ultimately, our findings reveal a critical bottleneck: while current models can parse broad instructions, they still profoundly struggle with fine-grained 3D spatial coherence and accurate structural reproduction in Blender, leaving substantial headroom for future 3D-native agentics.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Visual agents built on multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can interpret visual references, operate design tools, and refine their outputs through visual inspection ( Qwen Team, 2026 ; OpenAI, 2026 ) . These agents have gained traction in two-dimensional (2D) design tasks, including webpage, poster, layout, and presentation creation ( Si et al., 2025 ; Chen et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2025 ; Zhao et al., 2026a ) , and are increasingly being extended to three-dimensional (3D) modeling and scene creation. 3D modeling is a crucial task and provides a practically valuable testbed for visual agents. First, it involves complex, long-horizon workflows in which agents must coordinate extensive tool use across dozens of interaction turns. Second, it supports applications ranging from everyday personal projects to professional scientific visualization, while spanning scales from individual household objects to entire city blocks. Third, mastering this domain requires spatial reasoning, active visual verification, and sustained tool manipulation within open-ended digital environments, all of which are essential for extending the productivity frontier of AI agents. However, evaluating agents on practical 3D modeling tasks presents three major challenges: environment selection, task coverage, and output assessment. First, evaluation requires an accessible environment that supports 3D creation, editing, and inspection, yet few available environments provide all three capabilities. We therefore turn to Blender, an open-source platform that supports these capabilities through Python scripting and Model Context Protocol (MCP) interfaces and has been used for agent-driven creation and editing ( Hu et al., 2024 ; Huang et al., 2024 ; Noor et al., 2026 ) . Second, practical requests vary widely in their reference sources and design goals, ranging from reconstruction based on photographs or illustrations to open-ended design briefs. A realistic benchmark must therefore cover diverse application contexts and user requirements. Existing Blender-based benchmarks typically focus on narrower task classes: 3DCodeBench and 3DHarnessBench emphasize individual objects ( Gao et al., 2026 ; Liu et al., 2026b ) , BlenderBench contains a small set of camera and scene-editing tasks ( Yin et al., 2026 ) , and SceneActBench covers broader scenes but focuses on predefined geometric objectives ( Zhao et al., 2026b ) . Finally, realistic requests often lack ground-truth 3D assets, while open-ended briefs may admit multiple valid solutions. Reliable evaluation must therefore assess both visual properties and underlying structures, requiring evidence beyond what a single rendered view can reveal. To address these challenges, we introduce BlenderWorld , a benchmark for evaluating visual agents on realistic 3D reconstruction, modification, and design tasks in Blender. BlenderWorld contains 223 tasks spanning 16 practical application scenarios, organized into 9 main categories and 22 subcategories (Fig. 1 ). The tasks represent diverse user needs, driven by text, image, and video references, with 59 tasks additionally including structured specification files. Agents must synthesize these inputs to produce fully editable Blender projects. To rigorously evaluate these diverse deliverables without a single canonical asset, we design an evidence-routed evaluation protocol. An automatic agent selects and renders multiple task-conditioned views, which are then assessed via 2,651 task-specific binary rubrics and continuous visual-quality dimensions. Crucially, we employ a dual-judge system: a VLM-as-Judge for visual and spatial properties, and an Agent-as-Judge for direct programmatic inspection of scene structures that bypass visual shortcuts. Benchmarking 13 frontier models reveals a stark reality: while models can generate visually plausible outputs and plan extensive tool invocations, they profoundly struggle to translate passive visual feedback into structurally sound 3D corrections. Contributions. We summarize our main contributions as follows: (1) We introduce BlenderWorld , a comprehensive benchmark of 223 realistic Blender tasks spanning 22 subcategories, covering diverse text, image, and video reference sources alongside specifications for practical 3D reconstruction, modification, and design requests. (2) We provide a unified evaluation protocol that combines task-conditioned multi-view rendering, task-specific binary rubrics (verified via both visual judges and programmatic scene-state inspection), and a holistic visual quality assessment. (3) We evaluate agents built on thirteen frontier models under a shared task and protocol, establishing strong baselines. Our results reveal that while top-performing models like GPT-6-Astra achieve high overall fused scores (79.9), they still fail to fully satisfy all task requirements, reaching a perfect pass rate on only 42.2% of tasks.
