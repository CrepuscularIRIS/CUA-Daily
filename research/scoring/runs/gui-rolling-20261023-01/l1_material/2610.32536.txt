title: IGG: A Benchmark for Interactive GUI Grounding under Visibility Constraints
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32536

§Abstract
GUI grounding benchmarks ask whether an agent can localize a target that is already visible in a static screenshot. Real interfaces often violate this assumption: a target may be off-screen, appear only after a state change or hover, remain occluded, or become actionable after a delay. This leaves the ability to recover target visibility unevaluated. To measure this reveal step, we introduce Interactive GUI Grounding (IGG), a benchmark in which an agent must first make the target visible through interaction and then click it. IGG contains 601 tasks across 16 controllable GUI environments and seven visibility-constraint sub-types, with a compact action space of scrolling, moving, dragging, waiting, and clicking. In our human study, participants achieve 94.6% accuracy, while GPT-5.5 reaches 79.4% and the strongest evaluated open-source VLM reaches only 31.6%. To identify the source of this gap, we conduct two controlled interventions. Telling Qwen3-VL which interaction to perform raises its accuracy only from 30.8% to 33.9%, whereas performing the interaction for the model raises it to 81.9%. The bottleneck is executing the reveal interaction, not recognizing the target once it is exposed. We further show that this capability is learnable, demonstrating that visibility recovery can be strengthened consistently across model families.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
GUI agents increasingly rely on vision-language models (VLMs) to map natural-language instructions to screen locations Wu et al. (2025) ; Mittal et al. (2026) ; Koh et al. (2024) ; Kumbhar et al. (2026) ; Gao et al. (2024) ; Zheng et al. (2024) . Existing grounding benchmarks evaluate this mapping within a single screenshot and typically assume that the target is already visible Cheng et al. (2024) ; Li et al. (2025) . However, routine interfaces often violate this assumption (Figure 2 ): a target may lie outside the viewport Burigat et al. (2012) , sit in a collapsed panel, be covered by a popup, be distinguishable from its neighbors only on hover, or activate only after a delay. In each case, no static grounding model, however accurate, can localize the target in the initial screenshot. The agent must first select and execute an interaction that reveals it, and only then recognize and click it. Static grounding benchmarks leave this reveal step unmeasured, and navigation benchmarks fold it into end-to-end task success, where it cannot be separated from planning. In light of this gap, this paper asks whether current GUI agents can make a target visible before grounding it and, when they cannot, at which stage they fail. To fill this gap, we introduce Interactive GUI Grounding (IGG) to measure this missing step. An IGG episode decomposes success into four stages: selecting a reveal action , executing it at the correct location or with the correct parameters , recognizing the target once it appears , and localizing the final click . We call the first two stages visibility recovery . The remaining stages, recognizing the target once it appears and localizing the click, coincide with static grounding (Section 3.1 ). Thus, like static grounding, IGG scores a single final click. Unlike static grounding, it additionally requires the agent to recover the target’s visibility beforehand. Because interaction serves only to expose the target, IGG isolates visibility recovery without introducing the broader planning demands of full GUI navigation. IGG contains 601 tasks across 16 controllable GUI environments modeled on familiar applications. Tasks fall into seven sub-types under three constraint types, Viewport , Multi-State , and Temporal/Manipulation , and are solved with five actions: Scroll , Move , Drag , Wait , and Click . Deterministic initial states and stable target identities make evaluation replayable at click time, while the environments preserve the interaction patterns that make visibility recovery difficult: hover reveals, popups, delayed activation, carousels, and movable occlusions. Our experiments identify interaction execution as the main bottleneck in IGG. Telling a model which interaction to perform provides little improvement, whereas performing the reveal action for it recovers most of its accuracy. SFT on successful interaction trajectories followed by online GRPO further improves visibility recovery across Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, and GUI-Owl-1.5. Our contributions are: • We formulate interactive GUI grounding as visibility recovery followed by target recognition and localization, separating it from both static grounding and end-to-end navigation. • We introduce IGG, a benchmark of 601 tasks, 16 controllable GUI environments, and seven visibility-constraint sub-types, together with a click-time evaluator and human-verified ground-truth interaction trajectories. • We provide controlled interventions, heuristic analyses, cross-viewport and paired mock-real evaluations, and training studies that identify where current agents fail and show consistent improvements across three VLM architectures.
