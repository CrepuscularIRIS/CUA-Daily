title: Towards Physically Grounded Articulated 3D Assets: A Multi-Agent Pipeline and Benchmark
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13947

§Abstract
In this paper, we study physically grounded articulated 3D asset generation---moving the goal from reproducing how objects look to capturing how they move and function. Inspired by the literature on procedural and agentic generation, we present Artisan, a system that extends the latest method Articraft by introducing a multi-agent pipeline with physical simulation feedback, enabling comprehensive attributes beyond geometry, appearance, and physically plausible articulation, such as collision-free motion, stability under gravity, and accurate mass and material properties. Furthermore, we construct a unified benchmark spanning four key aspects: spatial fidelity, physical plausibility, downstream task performance, and overall quality. The benchmark encompasses 100 reference assets from ArtVIP and LightWheel, 50 stability prompts, and 50 ABO objects with reference masses, with protocols evaluating various metrics. Through extensive evaluation of more than 10 representative models, Artisan achieves competitive spatial fidelity while attaining the lowest collision rates and highest stability rates among the evaluated methods. Together, our system and benchmark establish insights for advancing 3D generation towards physically plausible, functionally usable assets.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
We consider the problem of generating physically grounded articulated 3D assets from natural language and, optionally, reference images. Specifically, we aim to reconstruct the objects’ appearance and geometry, as well as structural, functional, and physical properties such as part hierarchies, joints, and mass. The intent is to capture the configuration space of the object and to support simulation of contact and gravity. By solving this problem, we can reduce the cost of creating assets for physics simulation ( Genesis Authors, 2024 ; Genesis AI Team, 2026 ) , product design ( Willis et al., 2021 ) , and robotics ( Li et al., 2023 ; Nasiriany et al., 2026 ) . Appearance, shape, articulation, and physical attributes are interdependent and should be modeled holistically . However, most existing methods address these properties separately, focusing on shape and appearance ( Xiang et al., 2025 ; Hunyuan3D et al., 2025 ; Wu et al., 2025c ) , articulation ( Wu et al., 2026 ; Li et al., 2026a ; Li et al., 2026b ) , or specific physical properties, such as materials ( Wu et al., 2025b ; Youwang et al., 2026 ) , mass ( Zhai et al., 2024 ; Cao et al., 2026a ; Cao et al., 2026b ; Li et al., 2026c ) , stability ( Chen et al., 2024b ; Li et al., 2025 ) , and collision-free motion ( Luo et al., 2025 ; Kreber & Stueckler, 2025 ) . Addressing each aspect in isolation makes it difficult to satisfy all of them, resulting in artifacts and defects in the reconstructions and ultimately limiting the usability of the generated assets. As alternatives to these learned models, procedural generators ( Lian et al., 2025 ; Joshi et al., 2025 ) assemble assets programmatically, based on predefined rules that enforce desirable constraints and make at least some physical properties explicit and controllable. However, such rule-based systems limit the types of objects that can be generated and are difficult to use in tasks like image-based reconstruction. This limitation is addressed by several recently proposed agentic methods. For instance, Articraft ( Zhou et al., 2026 ) uses a Large Language Model (LLM) to write, validate, and revise programs that construct 3D objects, which is much more flexible than using canned rules of traditional procedural generators. However, Articraft and other similar approaches use a single agent to both construct and verify the ‘3D program’, which can lead to self-evaluation bias , where the agent may satisfy its own checks without genuinely ensuring the quality of the generated assets. Moreover, while their checks are meant to increase the likelihood that the generated assets would work in a physics-simulated environment, the latter is not directly tested and may still fail. In this paper, we address these gaps by introducing Artisan , a new pipeline that improves on the blueprint of Articraft ( Zhou et al., 2026 ) by switching to a multi-agent design and introducing physical simulator feedback . The new pipeline ( Figure 2 ) consists of: (i) the Design Agent , which produces a blueprint of the object specifying its parts, joints, and articulations; (ii) the Coding Agent , which translates the blueprint into a program that outputs the geometry, articulations, and physical parameters of the object; and (iii) the Review Agent , which validates the built asset and provides feedback to the Coding Agent to revise the program. The agents work independently , exchanging only the blueprint, 3D asset, and feedback as explained. Their independence reduces the self-evaluation bias mentioned above. Furthermore, the validation step is augmented with feedback obtained from a physical simulator, which verifies physical properties like collisions and stability within the target simulator environment. Existing benchmarks offer limited assessment of whether visual fidelity translates into physical plausibility and even functional usability to evaluate the assets produced by such pipelines. Our second key contribution is therefore to introduce a new comprehensive benchmark that jointly assesses all of these properties. It evaluates: spatial fidelity , comparing the object’s appearance, geometry, and kinematics to the reference inputs; physical attributes , measuring mass, material, stability under gravity, and collision under motion; downstream tasks , assessing the usability of the generated assets in tasks like robotic manipulation; and overall quality , based on human judgments after manipulating objects in the simulator. Each aspect is evaluated with multiple metrics. Using the new benchmark, we conduct extensive experiments to compare our method with state-of-the-art models. Empirically, we find that the assets constructed by Artisan : (i) are faithful to the input references; (ii) are physically valid and articulate correctly; and (iii) work well in downstream tasks such as simulated robotic manipulation.
