arxiv: 2610.39924 (2610.39924v1)
title: PhysAg3D: Bridging 3D Generative and Agentic Models for High-Fidelity and Physically-Interactive Assets
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39924

§Abstract
Generating interactive 3D assets from a single image requires realistic appearance, fidelity to the depicted object, and plausible physical behavior. Yet detailed geometry alone does not determine how an object should move or deform. We introduce PhysAg3D, which couples 3D generative models with agentic reasoning and tool use. A vision-language agent first infers semantic parts, candidate interactions, and physical priors from the image; a 3D generator then produces a textured whole-object mesh from the same image. A hierarchical interaction representation links these revisable priors to mechanisms and the generated surfaces they control, organizing interaction construction and refinement. Guided by this representation and observations of the mesh, the agent uses geometric and physical tools to construct parts, joints, deformable regions, and surface bindings while retaining useful generated geometry and materials. Rendered interactions and execution measurements guide targeted revisions within a fixed budget. The framework accommodates both rigid articulation and compliant deformation. Quantitative and qualitative evaluations on real photographs and external benchmarks demonstrate detailed, image-aligned assets with plausible interactions. Ablations support bounded refinement and source-surface preservation. Project Page: supra-3d.github.io/PhysAg3D.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Generating interactive 3D assets from a single image offers a scalable way to populate virtual environments and embodied simulations with objects from the real world ( Mildenhall et al., 2020 ; Kerbl et al., 2023 ; Mo et al., 2019 ; Xia et al., 2020 ; Xiang et al., 2020 ) . A useful asset should capture the depicted instance, retain realistic geometric and material detail, and respond to actions in ways appropriate to the object. These goals concern different aspects of quality: a visually convincing reconstruction can remain static, while a functional mechanism can belong to an asset that no longer resembles its reference. The task therefore requires bringing appearance, instance alignment, and physical interaction into a coherent realization. Recent advances provide strong foundations for this task. Image-conditioned generators synthesize detailed geometry and materials ( Poole et al., 2023 ; Jun and Nichol, 2023 ; Nichol et al., 2022 ; Lin et al., 2023 ; Qian et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2023a ; Shi et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2023b ; Hong et al., 2024 ; Long et al., 2024 ; Tochil and others, 2024 ; Xu and others, 2024 ; Du et al., 2024a ; Voleti et al., 2024 ; Tang and others, 2024 ; Xiang et al., 2025b ; Xiang et al., 2025a ) ; articulated and physical generation methods infer part structure, motion, and material behavior ( Mo et al., 2019 ; Xia et al., 2020 ; Yu et al., 2023 ; Wang et al., 2019 ; Xiang et al., 2020 ; Liu et al., 2025 ; Liu et al., 2026 ; Cao et al., 2026 ; Makoviychuk et al., 2021 ; Hu et al., 2020 ) ; and agentic systems construct and revise assets through executable programs, visual grounding, and feedback ( Ahn et al., 2022 ; Li et al., 2023a ; Huang et al., 2023b ; Huang et al., 2023a ; Driess et al., 2023 ; Brohan et al., 2023 ; Singh et al., 2023 ; Valmeekam et al., 2023 ; Le et al., 2025 ; Zhou et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026 ) . A remaining challenge is to maintain agreement between what an object is expected to do, how that behavior is implemented, and which visible surfaces actually respond. A single image underdetermines hidden mechanisms and physical parameters, and a generated mesh need not expose the semantic parts required for interaction. Furthermore, a valid joint or deformable simulation does not ensure that the corresponding detailed surface moves with it. Addressing these discrepancies can require changes to geometry and physical structure, creating a further tension between functional construction and preservation of the depicted appearance. We introduce PhysAg3D , a framework for single-image interactive asset generation through generative–agentic coupling . Our central idea is to combine the instance-specific geometry and appearance supplied by a pretrained 3D generator with an agent’s ability to reason about object behavior and construct executable physical models. The generated asset provides a detailed visual foundation; agentic reasoning turns this foundation into an interactive realization and revises it in response to observed behavior. This division of capabilities allows physical construction to make use of rich generated surfaces while remaining responsive to the structure and interactions of the depicted object. Figure 1 illustrates this goal through mixer-head articulation, compass-lid closure, telescope reorientation, and local ear-pad deformation. The examples associate actions with specific moving or deforming regions while retaining recognizable object structure and surface detail. Specifically, a vision-language agent first infers revisable semantic and physical priors from the image, and a 3D generator produces a textured whole-object mesh from the same image. We propose a hierarchical interaction representation for this coupling, connecting semantic requirements, physical mechanisms, and concrete asset realizations. Explicit correspondences associate intended interactions with the structures and visible surfaces that implement them. Guided by these relationships and observations of the mesh, the agent uses geometric and physical tools to construct parts, joints or compliant regions, and surface bindings while retaining useful generated geometry and materials. Rendered interactions and execution measurements then guide targeted revisions with bounded construction and validation attempts. The representation expresses both rigid articulation and compliant deformation through their respective relationships between physical state and visible appearance.
