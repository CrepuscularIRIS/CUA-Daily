arxiv: 2610.35684 (2610.35684v1)
title: OmniShowcase: Omnidirectional Subject-Consistent Video Generation from Multi-View Images
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35684

§Abstract
Generating videos of a specific subject from a few reference images remains challenging when the camera reveals surfaces that are not visible in the input. Early subject-to-video (S2V) methods condition on a single reference image and must therefore infer the subject's unseen appearance and geometry as the viewpoint changes. To address this limitation, recent methods extend S2V to multiple reference views, providing more information about the subject from different viewpoints. However, they do not explicitly specify the 3D structure that connects these observations. In addition, existing training data do not guarantee that viewpoint changes preserve the same underlying 3D subject. We present OmniShowcase, built on two components: (i) a fully automated data synthesis pipeline that produces training videos along diverse camera trajectories, with viewpoint variation driven by underlying subject geometry, even spanning full 360-degree orbits; and (ii) a geometry-conditioning method that provides the video model with a turntable of surface-normal maps reconstructed from the reference views without requiring temporal alignment with the generated frames. We construct Showcase-4K, a corpus of 3D-asset-based videos covering twelve camera motions across 88 trajectory configurations with reconstruction-derived surface-normal conditioning, and ShowcaseBench, a held-out evaluation set with the source meshes and camera parameters used to render each video. Experiments show state-of-the-art subject identity and 3D consistency while maintaining competitive video quality and motion. Project page: justin4ai.github.io/OmniShowcase

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Subject-to-video (S2V) generation aims to create videos of a specified subject from reference images while preserving its appearance across time and viewpoints. Recent video diffusion models generate photorealistic videos from text or a single image ( Yang et al., 2025 ; Kong et al., 2024 ; Wan Team, 2025 ; Brooks et al., 2024 ) , and S2V methods use reference images to preserve subject identity in generated videos ( Liu et al., 2025 ; Deng et al., 2026 ; Jiang et al., 2025 ; Yuan et al., 2025 ) . However, most S2V methods condition on a single reference image, which provides appearance information only for the visible surfaces and leaves other regions unconstrained. As the camera reveals previously unseen sides, generated videos may exhibit inconsistent geometry and appearance, which can lead to changes in silhouette, structure, and surface details. These ambiguities motivate the use of multiple reference views and explicit geometric information. The main difficulty is geometric rather than purely photometric since the model must infer 3D structure that is absent from the input. Recent methods incorporate multiple reference views into S2V generation. MV-S2V ( Song et al., 2026 ) trains a feed-forward model using generated multi-view videos, whereas 3DreamBooth ( Ko et al., 2026 ) performs per-subject optimization using static multi-view images. These efforts motivate two complementary directions: training on geometrically consistent viewpoint changes and providing explicit 3D geometry during generation. For the latter, recent feed-forward reconstruction methods can recover 3D geometry from a few uncalibrated images ( Wang et al., 2024a ; Wang et al., 2025 ; Wang et al., 2026 ; Lin et al., 2026 ) , making explicit geometry available from the same sparse references used at inference. We present OmniShowcase (Figure ), a framework that combines an automated data synthesis pipeline with geometry-aware conditioning. The pipeline renders multi-view videos from 3D assets and composites them into realistic scenes. For conditioning, we reconstruct geometry from the reference views and render a surface-normal sequence using the same procedure during training and inference. Our contributions are as follows: • We introduce an automated data synthesis pipeline that renders subject views from 3D assets and composites them into realistic scenes to construct training videos. • We present Showcase-4K , a dataset of 4,000 videos paired with diverse source meshes, camera trajectories, reference images, and surface-normal sequences, along with ShowcaseBench , a held-out benchmark with ground-truth geometry and camera parameters for evaluating subject consistency across viewpoints. We will publicly release Showcase-4K , ShowcaseBench , and the associated data-synthesis code. • We propose a geometry-aware in-context conditioning mechanism that uses reference images and a reconstructed surface-normal sequence to guide video generation without requiring frame-wise alignment between the normal and target sequences. • We evaluate our method against single- and multi-view S2V baselines on synthetic and real-world subjects, achieving state-of-the-art performance in subject identity preservation and 3D consistency while maintaining competitive video quality.
