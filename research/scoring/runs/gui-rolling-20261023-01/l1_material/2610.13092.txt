arxiv: 2610.13092 (2610.13092v1)
title: When Faster Decisions Do Not Make Better Agents: An Empirical Study of Jev in Agent Workflows
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13092

§Abstract
AI agents depend on repeated decisions to accomplish tasks, with large language models (LLMs) commonly serving as their core decision makers. The recent emergence of Jev offers an opportunity to make these decisions faster and at lower cost. However, it remains unclear whether such local savings can be translated into efficient and reliable task completion. In this paper, we investigate this efficiency transfer across five agents in two representative application settings, embodied tasks in ALFWorld and mobile app use in AndroidWorld. Across 4,500 task executions, we jointly evaluate task effectiveness, decision-level and task-level resource use, and execution behavior. Experimental results show that Jev integration substantially reduces decision cost and latency across all five agents, with task-level gains varying by workflow. Task success remains close to baseline levels on ALFWorld and declines more substantially in two mobile agents. Auxiliary reasoning and additional interactions absorb decision savings, while failed recovery and premature completion limit successful delivery. Mean cost per successful outcome increases in one agent even as spending per attempt falls. These findings highlight the importance of preserving progress, supporting recovery, and verifying completion when integrating efficient decision models into agent workflows. Their practical value should be assessed through successful task delivery, recovery behavior, and resources per delivered outcome.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
AI agents pursue goals through interaction with their environments, with LLMs supporting this process in different scenarios ( Yang et al., 2024 ; Wen et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2024 ) . Progress toward a goal depends on repeatedly interpreting observations and deciding what to do next ( Yao et al., 2023 ) . Some decisions require open-ended code or text generation, while others involve selecting among available tools, interface elements, or admissible commands ( Wang et al., 2024 ; Wen et al., 2024 ; Shridhar et al., 2021 ; Yao et al., 2025 ) . These bounded choices are repeated throughout a task, and make both action-selection quality and resource efficiency important to overall agent performance ( Kapoor et al., 2025 ) . The resources spent on these decisions should reflect their reasoning demands. On S1-Bench, Zhang et al. (2026b) find that large reasoning models can overthink simple questions while still making errors, showing that extended reasoning does not consistently yield accurate, economical judgments. This finding motivates examining models designed for focused decisions. Jev, recently released by TypeSafe AI as a System One Model , provides instruction-defined judgments through typed outputs, including probabilities over supplied choices ( Almeida, 2026 ; TypeSafe AI, 2026b ) . The company positions it as a fast, inexpensive decision model ( Almeida, 2026 ) . Its choice interface offers an opportunity to reduce the cost and latency of repeated action selection in AI agents. Whether these local savings translate into efficient and reliable task completion remains unclear. Answering this question requires following decisions through complete agent workflows. Actions shape subsequent observations and choices, so each decision affects the conditions for the next ( Ross et al., 2011 ) . An executable action can move an agent away from its goal, requiring further reasoning or recovery whose cost may offset local savings. Reflection extends these dependencies across attempts ( Shinn et al., 2023 ) , and candidate construction shapes the choices available to the decision model ( Yang et al., 2024 ) . Task efficiency therefore depends on auxiliary reasoning, interaction overhead, and the sequence of actions performed. Failed attempts also consume resources without delivering the requested outcome. Evaluating agent performance requires connecting decision-level savings to task success, overall resource use, and resources per successful outcome, supported by repeated evaluation and execution analysis ( Kapoor et al., 2025 ; Yao et al., 2025 ) . To provide this evidence, we conduct a comparative empirical study of AI agents with Jev-based action selection. The study covers five agent families in two representative application settings, embodied tasks in ALFWorld ( Shridhar et al., 2021 ) and mobile app use in AndroidWorld ( Rawles et al., 2025 ) . Within each family, we compare the original configuration with a Jev-integrated variant, using text-only observations to match Jev’s supported inputs ( TypeSafe AI, 2026a ) . Each configuration runs three times on the same tasks, yielding 4,500 task executions. We jointly evaluate task effectiveness, decision-level and task-level resource use, and execution behavior. Paired task-level analyses estimate uncertainty in the observed differences, while systematic trace coding characterizes failure and recovery patterns. Comparisons with the original configurations assess integration effects. Our results show that Jev substantially reduces decision cost and latency across all five agents, with task-level gains varying by workflow. Task success remains close to baseline levels on ALFWorld and declines more substantially in two mobile agents. Mean task costs fall across all agents, while auxiliary reasoning and additional interactions limit overall speed gains. Failed recovery and premature completion further constrain successful delivery. Lower spending per attempt can therefore coexist with higher cost per successful outcome. These findings highlight the importance of preserving progress, supporting recovery, and verifying completion when integrating efficient decision models into agent workflows. Their practical value should be assessed through successful task delivery and the resources it requires. In summary, we make the following contributions: • We provide an empirical study of AI agents with Jev-based action selection across five agent families and two benchmarks, covering 4,500 task executions. • We characterize how decision-level savings translate into task cost, latency, and resources per successful outcome across workflows. • We systematically annotate execution traces and analyze recovery outcomes to identify failure and recovery patterns. The experimental code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/jev-emp-5BF4/ .
