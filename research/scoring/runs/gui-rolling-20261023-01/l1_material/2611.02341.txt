title: AgiWorld: An Agentic Video World Model for Building Persistent Worlds
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02341

§Abstract
Recent interactive video world models generate visually compelling videos from a single image, following user-provided camera motion and text prompts. However, such low-level control demands substantial user effort to realize a high-level intent, and the generated world struggles to remain persistent as the exploration proceeds. In this work, we present AgiWorld, an agentic video world model that explores and builds a persistent world from a single image and a free-form instruction. For high-level planning, our agent AgiWorld-Agent designs the world and the journey through it in a world state and directs the exploration. For low-level realization, our video world model AgiWorld-WM generates the video chunk by chunk and stores it with its geometry in a spatial memory, from which the agent updates its world state. This closed loop keeps the world faithful to the user's intent and keeps the agent's world state consistent with the generated content throughout the exploration. We co-design the two levels around the shared spatial memory and an interface of metric camera trajectories and structured prompts. In AgiWorld-WM, to handle occlusion and accumulated geometric errors in the spatial memory over long rollouts, we propose occlusion-aware history selection, which retrieves past views by surface visibility, and reliability-based memory maintenance, which prefers reliable geometry and earlier observations. AgiWorld-WM achieves state-of-the-art overall performance on world revisiting and exploration benchmarks, and qualitative results and ablations show that AgiWorld builds persistent worlds from free-form instructions over minute-long journeys. Persistence thus arises not from the video model alone but from its closed loop with the agent.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
A world is more than what is currently visible. It has places beyond the current view, spatial relationships between them, and content that should remain coherent as one moves through it. Building an explorable world therefore requires two capabilities: generating what the camera sees and reasoning about the world beyond the camera. Recent video world models have made remarkable progress on the first capability ( Hong et al., 2025 ; Team et al., 2026 ; He et al., 2025c ) . Given an image, a camera trajectory, and a text prompt, they can generate realistic video as the camera moves through a scene. By generating successive video chunks, these models can support interactive exploration over increasingly long horizons. However, they do not decide what world should be explored. The camera trajectory and local content are still specified by the user, while content outside the current view is otherwise left to the model’s generative prior. As a result, turning a high-level intent into a coherent journey still requires the user to continuously decide where to go and what should appear next. We ask a different question: can a world model build and explore a world from a high-level instruction by itself? Doing so requires reasoning at a level above individual video chunks. Before entering an unseen region, the system must decide what should be there and how it connects to what has already been seen. After generating it, the system must treat the generated content as part of the world and reason from it thereafter. World building is therefore inherently a closed-loop process: plan the world, generate part of it, observe what was generated, and plan again . We introduce AgiWorld , an agentic video world model built around this loop. Given a single image and a free-form instruction, AgiWorld autonomously designs a world, plans a journey through it, and realizes the journey as a long video (Figure 1 ). It separates world building into two complementary components, an agent for high-level planning and a video world model for low-level realization. AgiWorld-Agent reasons about the world at a high level, maintaining a world state that contains both what has already been observed and what it has designed beyond the current view. AgiWorld-WM realizes this plan through camera-controlled video generation and maintains a spatial memory of what has actually been generated. After every generated chunk, the agent reads this memory, reconciles its plan with the generated world, and decides what should happen next. For high-level planning, AgiWorld-Agent , built on a multimodal language model, keeps a world state that records the spatial layout of the world and the stages of the journey. The layout contains confirmed elements, which have already appeared in the video, and imagined elements, which the agent has designed but the camera has not yet reached. For each chunk, a director in AgiWorld-Agent turns the next stage of the journey into a camera move, such as walking through a door, turning to look around a room, or returning to a place seen before, and writes it as a metric camera trajectory and a structured text prompt. After each chunk, the agent confirms and corrects the imagined elements that were realized, retires those that the video contradicts, and extends the design ahead. For low-level realization, we propose AgiWorld-WM , a novel camera-conditioned autoregressive video generation model that follows the director’s trajectories and prompts accurately and keeps the generated world consistent over long rollouts. We condition the model on metric camera trajectories through a camera adapter and a relative camera positional encoding, PRoPE ( Li et al., 2025c ) . For spatial memory, prior work compresses historical context ( Zhang et al., 2025 ) or retrieves past views using camera overlap ( Yu et al., 2025 ) and spatial indexing ( Li et al., 2025d ) . Reusing the geometry stored in our spatial memory raises two problems over long rollouts. A past view that overlaps the new camera may show surfaces that are occluded from it, and the estimated geometry accumulates errors. We therefore introduce occlusion-aware history selection, which retrieves past views by the surfaces visible from the new camera, and reliability-based memory maintenance, which prefers reliable geometry and earlier observations of each surface.
