§Abstract
Rapid advances in cowork agents are shifting human-AI collaboration toward real-world automation, delivering software, designs, and other artifacts. However, such productivity work demands both high-quality delivery on complex productivity tasks and continued monitoring and decision-making over long horizons, a combination that remains an open challenge. We introduce CoworkOS, a general-purpose harness for long-horizon work that separates task strategy from execution management: the model decides actions, delegation, and acceptance, while the runtime maintains state and feedback. Its three-layer architecture enables direct execution or delegation to Subagents, synchronously or asynchronously, under stable responsibilities. Through a shared application layer, humans and agents inspect and revise the same evolving documents, presentations, CAD models, and Blender scenes, so human-in-the-loop participation can enter both through conversation and through direct modification of the work itself. To sustain continuity across agents and sessions, CoworkOS further introduces hierarchical workspaces and persistent semantic memory, so working files, key decisions, and long-running Job logs remain shareable, reusable, and resumable after interruption. Across 12 benchmarks and 6 categories of real-world applications, CoworkOS proves highly competitive with frontier agent applications such as Codex and DeepSeek Harness. As a sustained-work case study, CoworkOS uses GLM-5.3 as its backbone to conduct RSI-driven automated post-training on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B. Over five rounds, the model's average score across eight benchmarks rose from 33.19 to 42.95 (+9.76). Looking ahead, we believe CoworkOS opens a meaningful path toward general cowork-agent intelligence.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Agent intelligence is advancing toward lifelong autonomy systems that continually interact and evolve with real-world environments. Progress toward Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI) requires attention to both model capability and the runtime systems that translate reasoning into action. Ideally, such agents would invoke tools, interpret feedback, maintain working state, and continue when their immediate interaction ends. To achieve this, the agent harness supplies the runtime mechanisms for this process, including tool orchestration, state and context management, and execution feedback. Because the harness shapes how a model acts and how well it can use available resources, models and their harnesses need to co-evolve ( Liu et al., 2024 ; Sumers et al., 2024 ) . Recently, the evolution of agent systems can be understood through three progressive stages. Agent Workflow centers on reasoning and tool-using trajectories: early mechanisms include Chain-of-Thought and Tree-of-Thoughts ( Wei et al., 2022 ; Yao et al., 2023a ) , interleaved reasoning and action in ReAct ( Yao et al., 2023b ) , and feedback-driven reflection in Reflexion ( Shinn et al., 2023 ) . Workflow frameworks and construction methods, including DSPy ( Khattab et al., 2023 ) , AFlow ( Zhang et al., 2024 ) , and MetaGPT ( Hong et al., 2024 ) , further organize these capabilities. Agent Assistant extends this scope to persistent individual needs, bringing memory, messaging, code execution, and tool interfaces into continuing interactions. Frameworks such as LangChain, LangGraph ( LangChain, 2026 ) , AutoGen ( Wu et al., 2023 ) , and OpenClaw support different aspects of this development. The emerging Agent Cowork setting places agents within professional work: people and agents contribute to evolving artifacts, adjust their plans as work develops, and share initiative in complex productivity tasks. Coding and work-environment agents represented by Claude Code, Codex, OpenHands, and DeepSeek Harness have demonstrated strong real-world productivity ( Anthropic, 2026 ; DeepSeek AI, 2026 ; OpenAI, 2026 ; Wang et al., 2025 ; Jimenez et al., 2024 ) , while cowork agents such as Kimi Code, ZCode, Qoder, and Hermes Agent have also been widely adopted. Notably, these stages describe an expanding design lens: from automation by the agent itself, gradually toward sustained interactive work involving humans, agents, and the environment ( Liu et al., 2024 ; Xie et al., 2024 ) . Building on this insight, we argue that this expansion of productivity reveals two complementary requirements for today’s cowork agents, illustrated in Figure 2 (a): • Complex productivity tasks: producing software, documents, designs, and other artifacts through demanding reasoning, tool use, and verification. Such tasks call for the agent’s comprehensive agentic intelligence and, where the work requires it, collective intelligence across collaborating agents, to carry a difficult task through to completion. • Continued monitoring and decision-making: monitoring a training process over days, following a project across sessions, or watching external information and deciding whether and how to act when a relevant event occurs. Such monitoring requires the agent to keep its context healthy over time, maintain sound memory, and continuously manage and track the artifacts it produces. Notably, both settings require sustained collaboration between humans and agents, which we treat as a basic design principle for cowork agents: the two sides work together within a shared application ecosystem centered on evolving work (Figure 2 (b)). This leads to our central question: can a single cowork agent handle both complex productivity tasks and continued monitoring and decision-making, while keeping humans in the loop? We propose CoworkOS , a cowork harness for long-horizon and human-in-the-loop task execution. To achieve this, a natural approach is a division of responsibility: the model autonomously chooses the task-execution strategy, while the runtime harness maintains efficient, long-horizon cowork mechanisms . Specifically, CoworkOS consists of a three-layer agent framework comprising a Main Agent, a Coordinator Runtime, and optional Subagents. Main sets the overall task plan and execution direction, decomposes work as needed, and evaluates the final artifacts and results returned. The Coordinator dispatches subtasks to the corresponding agents and monitors their lifecycles. Subagents carry out their assignments within the context and resources provided to them, using tools and making local decisions. Thanks to this hierarchy, CoworkOS can support complex productivity tasks through collective intelligence, while the Coordinator enables Subagents to sustain continued monitoring and decision-making on long-horizon tasks.
