arxiv: 2610.36925 (2610.36925v1)
title: Vision-Language Agents Can Learn in Their Own Latent Worlds
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36925

§Abstract
Learning from experience is essential for building the next generation of agents that continually expand their capabilities. Pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) already internalize rich world knowledge, yet this knowledge is rarely leveraged to drive policy improvement through online interaction. Realizing this potential efficiently remains challenging, as policy updates shift the agent's interaction distribution, requiring the world model to adapt accordingly, while continually updating large pretrained models is computationally expensive. To address this challenge, we introduce Dwell, a model-based reinforcement learning (RL) framework that reframes a VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, endowing it with the ability to predict, adjust, and act in latent space. Leveraging the VLM's own representations and its dual role as both world model and policy, the agent continually learns from real interactions to anticipate and evaluate action outcomes, and from latent imagination to improve its behavior. Across five interactive visual environments, Dwell achieves the strongest overall performance among trainable methods while substantially reducing the compute required for imagination, showing that effective world models for VLM agents need not reconstruct the future; they need to predict futures that are useful for action. Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/Tom111-lhx/Dwell

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
AI agents ( OpenAI, 2026 ; Team et al., 2026 ) have acquired broad capabilities through large-scale pretraining on human-generated data ( Brown et al., 2020 ; Hoffmann et al., 2022 ) and post-training with human demonstrations and preference feedback ( Ziegler et al., 2019 ; Ouyang et al., 2022 ) . Extending this progress beyond the limits of human-provided data ( Villalobos et al., 2024 ) motivates agents that continually learn from experience generated through their own interactions with the environment ( Silver & Sutton, 2025 ) . Recent work ( Wang et al., 2026 ; Li et al., 2026 ) has taken steps in this direction by using reinforcement learning (RL) to improve agents’ multi-step decision-making with environmental feedback. However, the substantial environment interaction often required by model-free approaches can make experience collection a bottleneck to scaling ( Janner et al., 2019 ; Chandra et al., 2025 ) . This raises a central question: how can agents achieve greater policy improvement from limited real experience? World models offer a way to learn more from real experience by predicting action outcomes and generating imagined trajectories for policy learning. For vision-language model (VLM) agents, models that synthesize observations have already served as training environments across embodied and web tasks ( Zhu et al., 2026 ; Fang et al., 2025 ) . In contrast, imagination in a compact latent space avoids reconstructing observations at every predicted step ( Hafner et al., 2019 ; Hafner et al., 2023 ) , while predictive representation learning can emphasize predictable structure over incidental visual detail ( Assran et al., 2023 ; Maes et al., 2026 ) . Pretrained VLMs already encode rich world knowledge, suggesting that their own representations could support latent imagination for policy learning. Accurate prediction alone does not guarantee compatibility: a world model’s latent states may not be readily usable by a VLM policy, even when they faithfully capture action outcomes ( Assran et al., 2025 ; Chen et al., 2026 ) . A further difficulty arises as policy updates shift the agent’s interaction distribution toward states and actions poorly covered by the world model’s training data ( Janner et al., 2019 ) . In these regions, errors in predicted transitions and rewards can bias subsequent policy updates, so gains in imagination may not translate into better behavior in the environment ( Liu et al., 2026 ) . Adapting to this evolving distribution can also be computationally expensive when it requires repeated updates to a large pretrained VLM. Together, these challenges motivate a shared representational basis for prediction and decision-making, coupled with efficient, continual correction of transition and reward predictions from real interaction. We introduce Dwell , a model-based RL framework that enables VLMs to predict, adjust, and act in their own latent space. By recasting the VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, we equip it to reason about the state of the world from interaction history and improve its behavior through latent imagination. Following the joint-embedding principle ( Assran et al., 2023 ) , our method draws on the VLM’s rich world knowledge to actively infer a state representation in an abstract and compact space, rather than focusing on every detail of the observation. Exploiting the VLM’s dual role as a world model and policy, we instantiate both components using separate lightweight LoRA adapters ( Hu et al., 2022 ) on the same VLM backbone, allowing independent, parameter-efficient adaptation while sharing a common representation interface. Together, recurrence and duality provide a unified framework for modeling and acting in dynamic environments through online learning. We conduct comprehensive experiments across five interactive visual environments spanning spatial planning, embodied navigation, manipulation, and long-horizon household tasks. under the reported training protocol, Dwell achieves the superior performance among trainable methods, while substantially reducing the computational cost of imagination relative to a world-model baseline that reconstructs observations. In summary, the main contributions of the paper are as follows: • Learning and acting in a shared latent world. We recast a pretrained VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, unifying world modeling and policy learning through a common belief interface. • Online policy improvement through latent imagination. We develop a learning loop in which real interactions continually refine the world and reward models, while imagined trajectories guide policy improvement. • Empirical gains in performance and efficiency. Our proposed method achieves strong overall performance among the evaluated trainable baselines under the reported training settings, while reducing imagination cost relative to a world model that synthesizes observations.
