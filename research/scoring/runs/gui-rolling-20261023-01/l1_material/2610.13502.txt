title: Stride: Scale-Aware Multi-Agent Evolutionary Refinement for Infographic Generation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13502

§Abstract
Infographic generation challenges text-to-image models because a single image must satisfy numerous visual, textual, and semantic constraints. Iterative agentic refinement with a multimodal verifier is a natural remedy, but existing methods face a scale mismatch: local edits cannot correct global composition errors, while full regeneration may destroy already-correct regions. We introduce Stride, a constraint-grounded multi-agent framework that treats refinement over any frozen generator as a history-aware evolution strategy. Stride separates constraint evaluation - whether each requirement is satisfied - from visual grounding - where and how it is violated. It then routes global revisions to a composer subagent and local corrections to a retoucher subagent. Each subagent conducts internal planning and generates multiple candidates, while Stride accepts a candidate only when it improves the verifier score, reducing regression across iterations. Because stochastic generators and imperfect verifiers make any single step unreliable, it is this combination of scale-aware routing, per-step redundancy, and monotonic acceptance that yields stable improvement across iterations. With fully open-weight generator-verifier systems, Stride outperforms proprietary systems including Nano-Banana-2.0 and Seedream-5.0-Pro on BizGenEval, matches GPT-Image-2.0 and surpasses Seedream-5.0-Pro on IGenBench, and achieves a new state-of-the-art score on WISE, while substantially outperforming existing agentic methods across all evaluated benchmarks.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Text-to-image (T2I) generation has made remarkable progress in producing visually compelling images, but generating reliable, information-rich visual content remains challenging ( Li et al., 2026a ; Tang et al., 2026 ) . Infographics are a representative example ( Xie et al., 2026 ) , where text, numerical facts, charts, and spatial layout must work together to convey information correctly. A single incorrect value, malformed label, or inconsistent visual relationship can compromise the reliability of the entire infographic ( Li et al., 2026b ; Zhu et al., 2026a ) . However, existing generation paradigms primarily optimize image synthesis without explicitly verifying whether generated content satisfies its underlying constraints. Recent models have made significant progress in infographic generation. Closed-source systems such as Nano-Banana 2.0 ( Google DeepMind, 2026 ) and GPT-Image 2.0 ( OpenAI, 2026b ) exhibit strong visual generation capabilities, while open-source models such as ERNIE-Image ( Liu et al., 2026 ) and Infographic-V2 ( Diao et al., 2026 ) make infographic generation increasingly accessible and specialized. Beyond direct generation, recent agent-based approaches, including PaperBanana ( Zhu et al., 2026a ) , AutoFigure ( Zhu et al., 2026b ) , and PromptEnhancer ( Wang et al., 2025 ) , employ planning, feedback, and iterative refinement to further enhance generation quality. Nevertheless, existing approaches remain largely focused on visual plausibility and overall quality, with limited ability to explicitly verify fine-grained information constraints, localize their violations, and perform targeted correction. Distilling these limitations, we identify three key challenges in current agentic generation and refinement methods: • Insufficiently Grounded Feedback. Holistic scores and coarse critiques provide limited guidance for targeted correction. Constraint-level judgments alone may still leave the location and nature of a failure unclear. • Mismatch between Error and Edit Scales. Infographic errors range from global composition failures to localized text defects. A single revision mechanism cannot address both effectively: local edits have limited structural reach, while full-image regeneration can disrupt correct content when only a small correction is needed. • Unreliable Progress across Iterations. A revision may fix one requirement while violating another, and stochastic generation makes individual proposals unreliable. To address these challenges, we introduce Stride , a training-free multi-agent framework for scale-aware evolutionary refinement over frozen text-to-image generators. Stride separates rubric-based verification from visual grounding, combining constraint-level judgments with spatial evidence to guide targeted correction. A trajectory-aware lead editor uses this feedback and prior attempts to route revisions to a composer for global regeneration or a retoucher for localized text and formula correction. Each selected sub-agent proposes multiple candidates, and a (1+\lambda) -evolution strategy retains the current champion unless a candidate improves its verifier score, allowing exploration while limiting regression. Stride outperforms Nano-Banana-2.0 and Seedream-5.0-Pro on BizGenEval, matches GPT-Image-2.0 and surpasses Seedream-5.0-Pro on IGenBench, and achieves a new state-of-the-art score on WISE, while substantially outperforming existing agentic methods across all evaluated benchmarks. Our contributions are threefold: • We introduce Stride , a training-free framework that formulates infographic generation as scale-aware evolutionary refinement over frozen generators, adapting the scope of correction to the errors in the current image. • We develop a refinement loop that separates rubric verification from spatial grounding, uses refinement history to guide global or local corrections, and combines multiple proposals with verifier-guided champion retention to reduce regression. • Experiments show that Stride outperforms contemporary agentic methods and selected proprietary systems, while demonstrating strong scalability across different generators and benchmarks. Extensive experiments characterize the contributions of grounding, execution routes, and champion evolution.
