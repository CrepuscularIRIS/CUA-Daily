title: UI-UG 2: Advancing Mobile UI Understanding and Generation with Fine-Grained Supervision and Reinforcement Learning
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00926

§Abstract
Mobile UI understanding and generation require both fine-grained component perception and reliable structured synthesis. Inspired by the unified direction introduced by UI-UG, we present UI-UG 2, a compact 4B-parameter multimodal large language model (MLLM) that advances mobile UI Understanding and Generation with broader task coverage, a front-end-aligned taxonomy, and online render-aware reinforcement learning. UI-UG 2 constructs a multi-source understanding corpus from RICO, VINS, Android in the Wild, and Android Control, annotated with an 80-category two-level UI taxonomy aligned with front-end component semantics, and pairs it with over 5,000 requirement-screenshot-JSON tuples for progressive structured UI generation through a domain-specific language (DSL). For understanding, UI-UG 2 introduces decomposed rewards for category prediction, OCR fidelity, color recognition, dense grounding, and GUI grounding. Unlike the offline Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)-style generation optimization in UI-UG, UI-UG 2 optimizes generation through end-to-end online Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): sampled DSL outputs are rendered with a Playwright-based server-side pipeline and evaluated using LLM-as-a-Judge and contrastive preference ranking. Experiments show that UI-UG 2 achieves the strongest referring and dense-grounding results among the evaluated models on our 80-category benchmark, and achieves the best composite generation score among compared models at a compact scale (4B). More importantly, we observe a consistent understanding-to-generation transfer: adding fine-grained UI perception supervision improves generation quality under a fixed training budget, suggesting that component-level semantics provide a shared representational foundation for both tasks.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Mobile user interfaces are the primary bridge between users and digital services. As applications grow in complexity, the ability to accurately understand and generate UIs underpins a broad spectrum of downstream applications, from screen parsing and GUI agent interaction to personalized interface synthesis and LLM-driven front-end development. These two capabilities, however, have largely evolved along separate tracks, with separate models, separate data pipelines, and separate evaluation protocols. General-purpose MLLMs such as GPT [ 32 ] , Claude [ 2 ] , Gemini [ 13 ] , and Qwen-VL [ 3 ] can nominally perform both tasks, yet their accuracy remains insufficient for precision-critical mobile scenarios. Mobile screens are fundamentally different from natural images: they are dense compositions of small, semantically loaded components—buttons, icons, text fields, navigation bars—whose interaction semantics require fine-grained categorical reasoning. Distinguishing a CloseButton from a ReturnButton , or a SearchBar from a generic TextInput , is essential for reliable automation and high-quality generation, but lies beyond the granularity that general-purpose models reliably achieve. Specialized approaches address one side of the problem—Ferret-UI [ 48 ] and Ferret-UI2 [ 23 ] for grounding and referring, DCGen [ 41 ] and DeclarUI [ 52 ] for structured generation—while UI-UG [ 46 ] took an initial step toward a unified model. However, important limitations remain: its labels were not explicitly organized around front-end component semantics, its understanding coverage focused mainly on referring and dense grounding, its generation optimization relied on offline Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) over a fixed preference dataset, and its evidence for understanding-to-generation transfer remained preliminary. We present UI-UG 2 , a compact 4B MLLM for mobile UI Understanding and Generation, with an overview illustrated in Figure 1 . UI-UG 2 keeps the central goal of unified UI understanding and generation while strengthening the data, training, and analysis pipeline. On the data side, we construct a large-scale understanding benchmark from four public sources (RICO, VINS, Android in the Wild, and Android Control), covering over 30,000 screenshots with 80 fine-grained element categories organized in a two-level hierarchy aligned to front-end conventions, enriched with element-level, action-level, OCR, and color annotations. We pair this with a curated generation corpus of over 5,000 requirement–screenshot–JSON triples whose compact domain-specific language (DSL) supports cross-platform rendering and progressive streaming. On the optimization side, we adopt a two-stage pipeline of supervised fine-tuning followed by task-specific Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). For understanding, we design decomposed rewards targeting format compliance, category prediction, OCR fidelity, color recognition, dense grounding, and GUI grounding. For generation, UI-UG 2 performs online render-aware RL: each sampled DSL is rendered by a Playwright-based server-side pipeline, then evaluated through LLM-as-a-Judge and Contrastive Preference Ranking (CPR). This closes the loop between structured generation, actual rendering, and reward feedback, making optimization better aligned with deployment-time UI quality. Experiments demonstrate that UI-UG 2 achieves the strongest referring and dense-grounding results among the compared models on our benchmark, strong GUI grounding on ScreenSpot mobile benchmarks, and the best overall generation quality among compared models at a compact 4B scale. Through data-mixing ablations, reward transfer analysis, and attention probing, we further observe an asymmetric synergy: understanding supervision improves generation quality at an appropriate data-mixing ratio . This finding—that component-level perceptual representations form a transferable foundation for generative tasks—offers a concrete design principle for future mobile UI systems.
