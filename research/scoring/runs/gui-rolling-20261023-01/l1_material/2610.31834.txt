title: WorldMirror: Agentic Real-to-Sim Generation of Interactive Worlds from In-the-Wild Robot Demonstrations
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31834

§Abstract
Existing approaches for robot learning in simulation often require substantial manual effort to construct scenes and specify tasks, limiting training scalability and data diversity. Meanwhile, in-the-wild robot demonstrations naturally provide diverse and realistic observations of real-world interactions. Leveraging these demonstrations for simulation requires both high-fidelity visual reconstruction and physically consistent interactions. General-purpose agents offer flexible adaptation to diverse scenarios and strong reasoning about 3D spatial relationships, while specialized generative models provide high-fidelity reconstruction of scene appearance and geometry. We introduce WorldMirror, an agentic real-to-sim framework that constructs interactive digital twins from in-the-wild robot demonstrations. Our central idea is to formulate real-to-sim construction as the iterative refinement of an executable world program. A coding agent decomposes the observed scene, plans reconstruction steps, and orchestrates specialized image and 3D generation tools, refining the program through verification feedback. A camera-pose-anchored scene representation organizes the world into static visual context, support geometry, and editable interactive assets while preserving spatial relationships among the scene, objects, and robot. The agent uses this representation to diagnose and correct reconstruction errors, enabling physically consistent interaction and trajectory replay. Experiments on DROID demonstrate that WorldMirror can reconstruct visually faithful and geometrically consistent interactive worlds while preserving the physical validity of recorded robot trajectories.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Scaling generalist robot policies requires diverse training data across a wide range of real-world scenarios ( Physical Intelligence, 2025 ; Black et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2026 ) . Yet collecting real-world demonstrations and testing policies across environments remain costly and labor-intensive. Simulation offers a complementary route to scalable data generation and controlled, repeatable evaluation ( Li et al., 2024 ; Jain et al., 2025 ) . Realizing this potential requires interactive environments that reproduce the appearance and physical interactions of real-world deployment settings. For simulation to serve these purposes, scene geometry, object placement, camera viewpoints, and robot configuration must match the real setup. Even small errors in scale or contact geometry can alter task outcomes. Recent real-to-sim systems have advanced scene reconstruction and its use in robot learning and evaluation ( Zhao et al., 2025 ; Ranawaka et al., 2026 ; Jain et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2026 ; Dai et al., 2024 ; Torne et al., 2024 ) . However, real-to-sim construction involves coupled visual, geometric, and physical constraints that an feed-forward reconstruction may not fully satisfy. Meanwhile, general-purpose agents alone may not produce the fine-grained geometry and photorealistic appearance required for faithful scene reconstruction. Different reconstruction errors require different forms of correction. A misplaced object may require layout adjustment, whereas a missing object may require revisiting scene decomposition and asset reconstruction. Unsuitable collision geometry may instead require revising an asset’s physical representation. Addressing these errors calls for an adaptive and iterative correction process that selects and revisits reconstruction steps based on the current world state and verification feedback, rather than following a fixed sequence of tool calls. These challenges motivate agentic world modeling , in which a coding agent coordinates specialized reconstruction tools and uses verification feedback to refine an executable world. We introduce WorldMirror , an agentic real-to-sim framework that converts robot videos and their associated trajectories into interactive digital twins. At its core, a coding agent decomposes the reconstruction objective into subtasks and coordinates specialized reconstruction tools to construct an executable world program. Using the current world state and verification feedback, the agent determines which components to revise and which tools to invoke next, updating generated assets and assembly code accordingly. A camera-pose-anchored scene representation organizes the reconstructed world into static visual context, support geometry, and editable interactive assets. It places the scene, objects, and robot in a shared coordinate system, providing a consistent geometric reference for replaying recorded robot trajectories without modification. Our contributions are: • We propose WorldMirror , an agentic real-to-sim framework constructing interactive digital twins via adaptive tool orchestration and verification-guided refinement of executable world programs. • We introduce a camera-pose-anchored scene representation that organizes reconstructed worlds into static visual context, support geometry, and editable interactive assets, preserving spatial relationships across the scene, objects, and robot while supporting replay of robot trajectories. • We evaluate WorldMirror on DROID robot manipulation demonstrations across visual quality, layout fidelity, and motion and interaction fidelity, demonstrating competitive reconstruction quality and improved replay fidelity on the evaluated demonstrations.
