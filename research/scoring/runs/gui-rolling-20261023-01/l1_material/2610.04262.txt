title: CADForge: Agentic Single-View CAD Reconstruction with Explicit Geometry Reasoning
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04262

§Abstract
Reconstructing editable parametric CAD models from a single-view image is of great practical value for modern manufacturing, yet remains challenging due to incomplete geometric observations and complex inter-part relationships. To address it, we propose CADForge, an agentic framework that progressively converts a single image into CadQuery programs. CADForge decomposes an object into CAD-meaningful components and performs explicit geometric reasoning for each component, a process that first identifies CAD-relevant constraints and then translates them into precise modeling parameters through mathematical code. The inferred parameters then drive component-wise synthesis of executable CadQuery programs, with a review agent evaluating the resulting geometry and providing targeted feedback for iterative refinement. To further improve robustness and efficiency, CADForge incorporates a failure-guided toolkit construction mechanism to distill accumulated experience into tools, and maintains a compact parametric CAD memory for retrieving modeling context on demand. Experiments on diverse single- and multi-part objects show that CADForge consistently outperforms existing baselines in reconstruction fidelity and perceptual quality, demonstrating an effective approach to accurate single-view CAD reconstruction.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Computer-aided design (CAD) plays an essential role in modern engineering ( Narayan et al. (2013) ), where 3D objects can be constructed through sequences of modeling operations and editable parameters. Compared with point clouds and meshes, parametric CAD models can provide precise boundary representations (B-Reps) and flexible editability, making them well suited for real-world applications such as furniture design ( Scurtu et al. (2015) ), and industrial manufacturing ( Campi et al. (2022) ). However, creating such models typically requires specialized expertise and considerable manual effort, driving growing interest in automatic parametric CAD generation. Earlier work explored automatic CAD generation by learning neural representations of CAD construction sequences with dedicated network architectures ( Wu et al. (2021) ; Xu et al. (2022) ; Xu et al. (2023) ). With advances in large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), current work has made considerable progress in generating parametric CAD models from text descriptions ( Khan et al. (2024b) ; Li et al. (2025a) ; Guan et al. (2025) ), point clouds ( Rukhovich et al. (2025) ; Kabisov et al. (2026) ; Li et al. (2026b) ) or multi-view images ( Kolodiazhnyi et al. (2026) ; Pyatov et al. (2026) ), achieving strong performance in both geometric accuracy and structural validity. However, accurate parametric CAD reconstruction from a single-view image, which offers significant practical value in industrial design and 3D printing ( Sachs et al. (1990) ), remains challenging. This task is inherently nontrivial as it requires a model not only to infer complete 3D geometry and structural relationships from partial visual observations but also to translate them into precise CAD modeling parameters and commands. Existing methods ( Li et al. (2025c) ; Noeckel et al. (2025) ; Chen et al. (2025a) ; Doris et al. (2026a) ) primarily rely on task-specific fine-tuning over curated image-CAD datasets and struggle to produce complex multi-part objects like furniture. When applied to in-the-wild images, they often produce distorted geometry, omit important components, or generate disconnected parts. More recently, some studies ( Zhou et al. (2026) ; Lin et al. (2026) ) have begun to explore agent-based 3D code generation. Although they can produce visually compelling results, they often incur substantial token cost and can’t fully capture the underlying geometric constraints and inter-component spatial relationships, resulting in unfaithful reconstructions. To address this challenge, we propose CADForge , an agentic framework for single-view CAD reconstruction generation that converts an input image into a CadQuery program. Rather than generating the entire object monolithically, CADForge decomposes it into individual parts and constructs the model progressively. For each part, CADForge performs explicit geometry reasoning through two stages: geometry observation and geometry programming . In the first stage, a VLM extracts geometric patterns, proportional cues, and spatial relationships with previously generated parts from the input image. These observations are then translated into mathematical programs that leverage the current CAD state to solve for the key parameters required to construct the part. Based on the obtained specification and parameters, a code generator, assisted by a self-constructed toolkit, generates executable CadQuery code and integrates the new part into the existing model. We also introduce a reviewer agent to assess the result for geometric fidelity and topological validity, diagnose potential errors, and provide targeted feedback for iterative refinement. Throughout this process, CADForge introduces a compact parametric CAD memory to record the current modeling state, preserving cross-component consistency with minimal token overhead. We construct an evaluation set covering both part-level and object-level CAD models collected from diverse datasets, and evaluate CADForge against existing methods on it. The results demonstrate that CADForge can more faithfully reconstruct the input image as a parametric CAD model while maintaining competitive token consumption. Our contributions can be summarized as: • We propose CADForge, a multi-stage agentic framework tailored for single-view CAD reconstruction generation that decomposes an object into individual parts and progressively constructs a parametric CAD model through executable CadQuery code. • To accurately capture geometric information from the input image, we introduce an explicit geometry reasoning mechanism that first infers relative geometric relationships among parts and then translates them into key modeling parameters. A compact parametric CAD memory is maintained to ground each reasoning step in the evolving construction state. • We develop a comprehensive benchmark that covers both individual CAD parts and complete multi-part objects from diverse data sources.
