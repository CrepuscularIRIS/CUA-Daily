title: WeVisCut: The Whole Editing Room in One Agent From Narration to Long-Form Video
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38352

§Abstract
Producing long-form videos from narration requires interdependent decisions that remain coherent across shots. Existing agentic editors often use multi-stage workflows driven by general models, leaving the quality-cost trade-off of compact, task-adapted policies unclear. We address this gap with WeVisCut, a long-form script-to-video system that connects tool execution with policy learning. Its agent harness maintains shared production state so that tool feedback informs subsequent decisions and repairs. Because finished videos do not expose these decisions, our four-stage data pipeline combines editing inversion with verified replay to turn recovered editing intent into executable supervision. Applied to 3,750 finished videos, this pipeline yields over 130K training examples. We use this supervision to train a single 9B policy that coordinates production and recovers from failures within the same harness. Moreover, we introduce a 100-task benchmark and five metrics assessing editing quality and delivery reliability. Under a shared tool environment, WeVisCut substantially outperforms its base model and achieves comparable performance to Claude Opus 5 while using nearly 33x fewer generated tokens per successful output minute. It also outperforms task-matched native editors on our proposed metrics.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
“The whole eloquence of cinema is achieved in the editing room.” — Orson Welles Creating a narrated long-form video involves turning a script into a sequence of coherent shots. Planning, visual retrieval, narration synthesis, and timeline assembly are interdependent. Changing a shot’s duration affects later timestamps, while unsuitable visual material may require both retrieval and planning to be revisited across the affected shot sequence. Recent agentic editors coordinate production through specialized agents and tool workflows [ 48 , 39 , 47 , 49 , 21 , 44 ] . Several systems use general models with feedback or graph refinement. Yet orchestration alone does not specify how to train a compact policy that maintains video quality at low token cost. Crayotter trains a 9B editing model using final-video preferences on manually constructed tasks [ 38 ] . Such a policy needs a stateful environment for execution and recovery, together with supervision grounded in that environment. Finished videos, however, omit the tool calls, intermediate artifacts, and failures needed for such supervision. The challenge is therefore to connect stateful execution, executable supervision, and policy learning. To address these challenges, we present WeVisCut, which integrates a script-to-video agent harness, executable data construction, and a task-adapted policy. The harness (Figure 2 ) maintains shared production state and validates tool actions. It exposes execution feedback for inspection, bounded repair, and replanning while preserving unaffected artifacts from earlier successful production steps. Within this harness, we construct executable supervision through the four-stage pipeline in Figure 3 . We first recover script–shot intent through editing inversion and execute it through verified replay. When replay reveals defects, we apply observe–reflect–repair for bounded corrections that preserve successful artifacts. We then use trajectory materialization to organize the recorded executions into training examples with tool feedback and repair outcomes. From 3,750 video projects, this process yields over 130K examples. Using these examples, we train a single 9B policy across production roles in three phases (Figure 4 ). In Phase 1, we establish tool use and recovery through unified supervised fine-tuning [ 46 ] and runtime-verified rejection-sampled fine-tuning [ 45 ] . In Phase 2, we apply Guided-OPD [ 12 ] to learn recovery on policy-reached states while phasing out teacher intervention. In Phase 3, we use RCF-DAPO , our role- and context-factored DAPO [ 43 ] , to optimize decisions by comparing runtime outcomes within matched roles and contexts. Moreover, we introduce a benchmark of 100 script-to-video tasks and five complementary metrics to evaluate editing quality and delivery reliability. These metrics relate rendered videos to their scripts and production plans, so evaluation goes beyond successful rendering alone. In a shared tool environment, our policy improves over its base model by 16.0 points in Overall Score and achieves comparable performance to Claude Opus 5 while using nearly 33 \times fewer generated tokens per successful output minute. Our complete system also outperforms task-matched native editors. We summarize our contributions as follows. 1. We design a script-to-video agent harness that maintains shared production state, validates tool actions, and supports inspection, bounded repair, and final delivery. 2. We build a four-stage data pipeline based on editing inversion and verified replay, converting finished videos into executable training examples. 3. We train a unified 9B policy for tool use and recovery through sequential SFT, runtime-verified RFT, Guided-OPD, and RCF-DAPO within a shared production harness. 4. We introduce a 100-task benchmark and five metrics. WeVisCut improves its base policy, outperforms task-matched systems, and achieves comparable quality to Claude Opus 5 at markedly lower cost.
