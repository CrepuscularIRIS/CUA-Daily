title: BiGrounder: Bidirectional Training Unlocks the Potential of GUI Grounding
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07765

§Abstract
Graphical User Interface (GUI) grounding aims to map natural language instructions to precise operational coordinates, serving as a critical task for autonomous agent interaction. Current approaches formalize GUI grounding as predicting action-coordinate pairs from instructions, with end-to-end training supervised solely by final coordinates. Training datasets typically provide only a small number of descriptions per target coordinate, whereas real-world scenarios involve a much larger set of varied descriptions for the same target, which limits robustness to linguistic variation. While recent GUI grounding paradigms have begun employing reinforcement learning to enhance localization accuracy, models with limited exploratory capability before reinforcement yield constrained improvements even with identical training data during the reinforcement phase. To overcome these limitations, we propose a bidirectional reward paradigm that enables model self-reinforcement. During SFT, bidirectional training equips the model to generate action descriptions from target coordinates. During RL, the model samples semantically consistent instruction variations on-policy and grounds each variation back to its target. A cycle-consistency gate couples semantic quality to grounding success, turning the model's own instruction policy into a source of continually refreshed reinforcement trajectories. Our 4B-parameter model achieves competitive accuracy across five grounding benchmarks in our evaluation, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed training paradigm. https://github.com/Spell-shi/BiGrounder

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Current approaches in GUI grounding predominantly focus on learning direct mappings from singular operational descriptions to screen coordinates. However, this paradigm overlooks a critical characteristic of real-world applications: the complex many-to-one mapping relationship between user instructions and target coordinates. Specifically, users may employ diverse expressions, varying levels of detail, and different linguistic styles to refer to the same interface element. Such variability in natural language can significantly impair coordinate prediction, especially when instructions differ in length or style. To mitigate this challenge, existing research primarily adopts offline data augmentation strategies ( Liu et al. 2025a ; Gou et al. 2025 ; Li et al. 2025a ; Liu et al. 2025c ) , using instruction paraphrasing and expansion to improve model generalization. However, because the augmented instructions are generated in advance and remain fixed throughout training, the model can explore only a predefined sampling space that may not capture the breadth and evolving nature of instructional variations encountered in real-world scenarios. From a training paradigm perspective, recent work has increasingly explored reinforcement learning ( Lu et al. 2025 ; Zhou et al. 2025b ) for improving GUI grounding performance, typically utilizing coordinate hits within target bounding boxes as the primary reward signal. Nevertheless, current reinforcement learning methods ( Yuan et al. 2025 ; Liu et al. 2025b ) predominantly operate on fixed offline datasets, resulting in limited exploration capabilities. To address the aforementioned limitations, we aim for agents to not only fit the "instruction \rightarrow coordinate" outcome mapping but also learn robust semantic-spatial alignment representations based on local visual evidence from coordinate-corresponding regions, thereby better adapting to diverse interfaces and instruction expressions in real-world scenarios. To this end, we propose BiGrounder: a reinforcement learning framework based on bidirectional semantic verification. Beyond traditional forward localization, the model generates instructions from annotated target coordinates and then grounds its own on-policy generations back to the same elements. This turns reverse generation into an endogenous exploration mechanism: as the policy evolves, it continually supplies instruction variations that are semantically anchored to the target yet reflect the model’s current behavior. The resulting reverse and forward trajectories reinforce semantic–spatial alignment through cycle-consistent rewards. Experimental results demonstrate competitive results across multiple industry-standard benchmarks. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • Constructing GUI understanding data from grounding annotations. We reverse existing instruction-to-coordinate grounding samples into coordinate-conditioned instruction-generation data and incorporate them into bidirectional SFT, providing explicit supervision for learning the semantic and spatial properties of GUI elements. • On-policy bidirectional self-reinforcement. We propose a cycle-consistent RL paradigm that uses the model’s reverse policy to generate instruction variations on-policy and grounds them through the forward policy. This self-reinforcement mechanism can be directly integrated into standard SFT+RL pipelines. • Competitive performance on cross-platform benchmarks. Experiments demonstrate that our model achieves competitive performance across five grounding benchmarks, including ScreenSpot-Pro ( Li et al. 2025b ) , ScreenSpot-V2 ( Wu et al. 2024 ) , UI-Vision ( Nayak et al. 2025 ) , MMBench-GUI L2 ( Wang et al. 2025b ) , and OSWorld-G-R ( Xie et al. 2025 ) .

## Method
Overview. We propose BiGrounder, a two-stage framework based on bidirectional semantic verification for enhancing visual-language alignment in GUI agents. Unlike traditional paradigms that only learn unidirectional "instruction \rightarrow coordinate" mappings, BiGrounder additionally learns a reverse generation policy conditioned on ground-truth target coordinates. During SFT, the model jointly learns forward localization and reverse description, acquiring both the ability to locate elements from instructions and to generate semantic descriptions from element regions. During RL, this reverse capability becomes an on-policy source of instruction variation. The cycle comprises two steps: (1) instruction generation : conditioned on the ground-truth target coordinate o^{*} , the policy generates descriptions or interaction instructions; and (2) instruction grounding : the same policy grounds each generated instruction, and the resulting coordinate is evaluated against o^{*} . Bidirectional Task Construction Method To enable the model to simultaneously possess GUI Grounding and self-generated user instruction capabilities, we design bidirectional training tasks: predicting coordinates from user instructions and generating user instructions from coordinates. As shown in Fig. 2 , the GUI Grounding task aims to map natural language instructions to specific interaction operations on graphical user interfaces. The model generates structured sequences containing action types and screen coordinates to explicitly indicate the location of target elements, thereby achieving alignment between visual features and precise spatial coordinates. The GUI Description task generates unstructured natural language sequences based on prompts and interface inputs to describe the elements corresponding to specific coordinates or the operations on these elements. Since each element is paired with multiple descriptions during bidirectional training, the model can sample varied, semantically consistent instructions in the subsequent RL stage. Model Training - Supervised Fine-tuning Stage By unifying strictly structured outputs (actions/coordinates) and unstructured semantic outputs (descriptions) into a single generation framework, the model learns to align visual features simultaneously with "functional semantics" and "precise spatial coordinates." The optimization objective is defined as: \mathcal{L}=-\mathbb{E}_{(I,x,Y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\log P(y_{t}\mid y_{<t},x,I)\right] (1) where \mathcal{D} denotes the multi-task training dataset. For a target sequence Y of length T , y_{t} represents the token at step t , conditioned on the input image I , the text instruction x , and the preceding tokens y_{<t} under teacher forcing. The supervised fine-tuning stage utilizes bidirectional data simultaneously, enabling the model to learn UI operations while comprehending the meaning of UI elements, thereby laying the foundation for subsequent reinforcement learning. Model Training - Reinforcement Stage Reinforcement Sample Selection We utilize the SFT model to construct the RL dataset via rejection sampling with an inference temperature of 0.9. A sample is retained only if it satisfies two criteria: (1) Semantic Consistency. Among the four backward-generated instructions, at least three achieve a BERTScore greater than 0.85 with the original instruction. (2) Difficulty Constraint. When performing forward inference with these four instructions, the model must succeed at least once but no more than three times (i.e., the number of correct predictions s\in[1,3] ), ensuring the selection of non-trivial yet solvable samples Algorithm 1 Bidirectional Cycle-Consistent RL with GRPO 0: Dataset \mathcal{D} , policy \pi_{\theta} , reference \pi_{ref} 0: G=4 , K=4 , semantic threshold t=0.85 1: while not converged do 2: Sample batch \mathcal{B}\sim\mathcal{D} 3: for each (s,o^{*},x)\in\mathcal{B} do 4: Stage 1: Instruction Generation 5: Sample G trajectories \{\hat{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\hat{x}\mid s,o^{*}) 6: for i=1 to G do 7: b_{i}\leftarrow\operatorname{BERTScore}(\hat{x}_{i},x) 8: q_{i}\leftarrow\hat{x}_{i} if b_{i}\geq t , else x // GT fallback 9: Stage 2: Instruction Grounding 10: Sample K trajectories \{\tau_{i,k}\}_{k=1}^{K}\sim\pi_{\theta}(\tau\mid s,q_{i}) 11: r_{i,k}^{fwd}\leftarrow R_{\mathrm{ground}}(\tau_{i,k},o^{*}) // Grounding reward 12: c_{i}\leftarrow\mathbb{I}[\exists k:r_{i,k}^{fwd}\geq 0.9] // Cycle gate 13: r_{i}^{rev}\leftarrow R_{\mathrm{rev}}(b_{i},c_{i}) // Semantic reward 14: end for 15: A^{fwd}_{i,:}\leftarrow\operatorname{GRPOAdv}(r^{fwd}_{i,:}) , i=1,\ldots,G 16: A^{rev}\leftarrow\operatorname{GRPOAdv}(r^{rev}) 17: Compute per-trajectory losses \ell^{rev} and \ell^{fwd} 18: \mathcal{L}_{Bi}\leftarrow\operatorname{SeqMean}(\ell^{rev}\cup\ell^{fwd}) // seq-mean-token-mean 19: Update \pi_{\theta} using \mathcal{L}_{Bi} 20: end for 21: end while Reverse Reward In the reverse phase, our goal is to generate instructions that are semantically valid and operationally groundable. We employ Group Relative Policy Optimization to sample a set of candidate instructions \{\hat{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{G} given the target coordinate. We use BERTScore ( Zhang et al. 2020 ) to measure candidate-to-reference consistency. Its precision, recall, and F-score are computed from token-embedding similarities as follows: \displaystyle R_{\text{BERT}} \displaystyle=\frac{1}{|x|}\sum_{x_{i}\in x}\max_{\hat{x}_{j}\in\hat{x}}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j} (2) \displaystyle P_{\text{BERT}} \displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\hat{x}|}\sum_{\hat{x}_{j}\in\hat{x}}\max_{x_{i}\in x}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{j} (3) \displaystyle F_{\text{BERT}} \displaystyle=2\cdot\frac{P_{\text{BERT}}\cdot R_{\text{BERT}}}{P_{\text{BERT}}+R_{\text{BERT}}} (4) Let b_{i}=F_{\mathrm{BERT}}(\hat{x}_{i},x) and c_{i}=\mathbb{I}[\exists k:\operatorname{success}(\tau_{i,k},o^{*})] . A forward grounding trajectory is considered successful when its reward is at least 0.9 . We retain the semantic score only when the candidate passes the semantic threshold and completes cycle verification: R_{\mathrm{rev}}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{BERTScore}(\hat{x}_{i},x),&b_{i}\geq t,\ c_{i}=1,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases} (5) Here, R_{\mathrm{rev}} denotes the reverse reward function, while r_{i}^{\mathrm{rev}} is its value for the i -th instruction trajectory; t=0.85 is the semantic threshold. During training, we generate G=4 instructions and perform K=4 grounding rollouts for each instruction. If b_{i}\geq t , the generated instruction \hat{x}_{i} enters its corresponding forward loop. Otherwise, we set r_{i}^{\mathrm{rev}}=0 and use the ground-truth instruction x for that forward loop instead. For a qualified generation, its BERTScore reward is retained only if at least one of its K grounding rollouts returns to the target. Thus, every instruction branch contributes forward grounding trajectories, while only semantically qualified and cycle-consistent generations update the reverse policy. Forward Reward To cater to different grounding granularities, we use task-specific grounding rewards for action/point and region outputs: R_{\mathrm{ground}}=\begin{cases}R_{\mathcal{A}}+R_{\mathcal{C}}+R_{\mathcal{F}},&\text{action grounding},\\ R_{\mathrm{point}},&\text{point grounding},\\ R_{\mathrm{bbox}},&\text{bounding box}.\end{cases} (6) Action-Coordinate Alignment Reward: For element interactions, R_{\mathcal{A}}=0.1 is awarded for the correct action type, and R_{\mathcal{C}}=0.9 is awarded when its predicted point falls inside the target bounding box; the coordinate reward is granted only if the action type is correct. Invalid output formats receive R_{\mathcal{F}}=-0.2 , while valid formats receive no format penalty. For point grounding, no action type is involved, so we use a binary hit reward R_{\mathrm{point}}=1 when the predicted point falls inside the target bounding box and 0 otherwise. BBox-based Reward: For the bounding box prediction reward, we adopt the reward design of StepGUI ( Yan et al. 2025 ) . To ensure pixel-level precision, we implement a dense reward mechanism based on tolerance-normalized high-order decay. We define the normalized deviation as \hat{\delta}_{k}=|k_{pred}-k_{gt}|/\tau_{k} . For Region Grounding, we decouple the bounding box into center coordinates (c_{x},c_{y}) and dimensions (w,h) , using the same pixel tolerance for all four terms, \tau_{c_{x}}=\tau_{c_{y}}=\tau_{w}=\tau_{h}=6 . We construct a composite geometric energy term E_{geom} , weighting down dimensional errors via the coefficient \gamma : E_{g}=\hat{\delta}_{cx}^{4}+\hat{\delta}_{cy}^{4}+\gamma\hat{\delta}_{w}^{4}+\gamma\hat{\delta}_{h}^{4} (7) Here, we set \gamma=0.5 . To simultaneously optimize alignment precision and area overlap, the final bounding box reward R_{bbox} is a weighted fusion of the geometric score and the Intersection over Union (IoU): R_{bbox}=\alpha\cdot\exp(-E_{g})+(1-\alpha)\cdot\text{IoU}(b_{pred},b_{gt}) (8) We set \alpha=0.8 , aiming to prioritize geometric centrality while retaining IoU as a regularization constraint for shape consistency. Finally, we employ GRPO ( Guo et al. 2025 ) to optimize the reverse-to-forward cyclic process. Within each trajectory group, the advantage is obtained by normalizing the rewards, \hat{A}_{i}=(r_{i}-\mathrm{mean}(\mathbf{r}))/(\mathrm{std}(\mathbf{r})+\epsilon_{\mathrm{num}}) , where \epsilon_{\mathrm{num}}=10^{-6} . The per-trajectory clipped loss is \ell_{i}(\theta)=-\min\!\left(\rho_{i}\hat{A}_{i},\bar{\rho}_{i}\hat{A}_{i}\right)+\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(\pi_{\theta}\|\pi_{\mathrm{ref}}\right), (9) where \rho_{i}=\pi_{\theta}(o_{i}\mid I,S)/\pi_{\mathrm{old}}(o_{i}\mid I,S) is the importance ratio, \bar{\rho}_{i}=\operatorname{clip}(\rho_{i},1-\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}},1+\epsilon_{\mathrm{clip}}) , and \beta weights the KL penalty against the reference SFT model \pi_{\mathrm{ref}} . The G reverse trajectories form one GRPO group, while the K grounding rollouts associated with each generated instruction form a forward group; their advantages are normalized within the corresponding groups. After group-wise advantage estimation, we jointly aggregate all trajectories using seq-mean-token-mean (each trajectory is averaged over its own tokens, then averaged with equal weight), so every trajectory contributes equally regardless of length: \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Bi}}=\frac{1}{G+GK}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{G}\ell_{i}^{\mathrm{rev}}+\sum_{i=1}^{G}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\ell_{i,k}^{\mathrm{fwd}}\right). (10) where \ell_{i}^{\mathrm{rev}} and \ell_{i,k}^{\mathrm{fwd}} are the reverse and forward instances of the loss in Eq. (9). Under seq-mean-token-mean with G=K=4 , the reverse and forward trajectories contribute 0.2 and 0.8 by construction.
