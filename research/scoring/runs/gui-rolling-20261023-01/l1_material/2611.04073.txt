arxiv: 2611.04073 (2611.04073v1)
title: Computer-Use Agents Gain Little Benefit from a World Model When Conditioned on Tutorials
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04073

§Abstract
Recent work has improved computer-use agents (CUAs) by combining retrieved task-relevant tutorials and look-ahead planning using world models with MLLM judges. However, the contribution of each component -- tutorials, the judge, and the world model -- to overall improvement has not been evaluated in isolation. To fill this gap, we first construct OSWorld-Tutorial and ScienceBoard-Tutorial: two datasets for CUAs with curated multimodal tutorials, covering everyday and scientific domains. We then propose RAG-PolicyJudge (RAG-PJ), a policy-judge framework for CUAs conditioned on task-specific tutorials: the policy proposes candidate actions, and the judge selects among them. To isolate the effect of world models given tutorials, we compare RAG-PJ with and without common world models: using either zero-shot text-based descriptions of next states or an oracle that returns ground-truth visual observations. We find that RAG-PJ gains little benefit from these rollouts when tutorials are present, even with perfect visual futures. This suggests prior world-model gains in tutorial-conditioned methods come mainly from tutorial understanding and the judge's ability to select the proposed actions, rather than from predicted rollouts. Error analysis reveals that the bottleneck is not rollout fidelity, but the judge's ability to reason over future states and recognize task completion. This highlights the need to improve not just next-state prediction in world models, but also reasoning over their predicted rollouts. Datasets and code are available at https://github.com/realjoenguyen/RAGPJ-official.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
People rely on desktop and web software for day-to-day tasks and scientific workflows. Computer-use agents (CUAs) aim to automate these tasks by interacting with computer interfaces given natural-language user instructions. Modern CUAs are typically built on top of multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) trained on static offline data. As software is updated and user interfaces shift, these MLLM-based CUAs may lack current procedural knowledge about new environments, resulting in degraded performance Ishmam and Marino (2026) . A natural approach to address this gap is to provide CUAs with up-to-date task-specific tutorials. These readily available tutorials, contain environment descriptions and step-by-step instructions in the form of text and screenshots. Recent work has integrated tutorial retrieval as a component in CUA systems Yang et al. (2026a) ; Xu et al. (2025a) . However, these systems do not compare against alternative tutorial-use approaches, leaving their effectiveness unclear. A parallel body of work has considered test-time scaling for CUAs through a policy-judge approach with look-ahead planning Luo et al. (2026) ; Chae et al. (2025) . These methods use a world model to predict the outcome of action candidates under a policy, over one or many steps. They then use an MLLM-as-a-judge to select the best action among the candidates, conditioned on these predicted rollouts. Recently, tutorial-augmented methods of this approach have been introduced Mei et al. (2026) . However, these methods conflate the contributions of the tutorial, the judge, and world-model rollouts without proper evaluation. To fill this gap, we study the effect of tutorial information on CUAs with and without look-ahead planning, while retaining the MLLM-as-a-judge framework. Intuitively, high-quality tutorials already provide guidance for the policy to generate actions and demonstrations of their effects that can be used by the judge. This can potentially reduce the need for costly world model training and inference. With this motivation, we propose a simple yet effective method: RAG-PolicyJudge (RAG-PJ), a retrieval-augmented policy-judge framework for CUAs. RAG-PJ provides a task-specific tutorial to both (1) the policy, which proposes action candidates, and (2) the judge, which selects the best action among them. To evaluate tutorials for CUAs across domains, we build curated tutorials for existing datasets: OSWorld-Verified for day-to-day tasks Xie et al. (2024) and ScienceBoard for scientific workflows Sun et al. (2026) , resulting in two new tutorial benchmarks: OSWorld-Tutorial and ScienceBoard-Tutorial. The two original datasets are widely adopted for CUAs, facilitating easy adoption and reuse by the research community. To study the effect of world models on policy-judge frameworks in these two tutorial datasets, using the same base MLLM for both the policy and the judge, we compare RAG-PJ with two types of world models for CUAs: text-based and visual. First, zero-shot text-based rollouts Mei et al. (2026) (i.e., the text description of what the predicted next state would look like). Second, oracle visual rollouts (i.e., the real screenshot of the next state). We argue that these oracle rollouts are an upper bound on the next-state fidelity a learned visual world model (like ViMo Luo et al. (2026) ) could achieve. We find that RAG-PJ matches both rollout variants when tutorials are present. This indicates that current zero-shot MLLM-as-a-judge models gain little extra benefit from a world model, even with oracle visual futures, when conditioned on tutorials (Figure 1 ). Analyzing failures of the oracle rollout, we find that the agent’s failure to understand task success and progress , even when the oracle future is available, is a primary bottleneck to higher performance. This suggests that progress in CUAs may require not just better world models, but also stronger reasoning over the futures these world models simulate. In summary, our contributions are as follows: – We introduce RAG-PJ, a simple yet effective tutorial-conditioned policy-judge method for CUAs (§ 4.2 ). – We build OSWorld-Tutorial and ScienceBoard-Tutorial, two curated tutorial datasets with existing tasks from OSWorld Xie et al. (2024) and ScienceBoard Sun et al. (2026) (§ 3 ), facilitating tutorial study for CUAs. – We propose an oracle visual rollout infrastructure for CUAs, providing an important ablation on the effect of visual world-model rollouts with perfect future information (§ 5 , Appendix F ). – We provide a controlled comparison of tutorial-only judging, text-based rollouts, and oracle visual rollouts-demonstrating that current MLLM-as-a-judge models gain little benefit from world models given task-specific tutorials (§ 6 ).
