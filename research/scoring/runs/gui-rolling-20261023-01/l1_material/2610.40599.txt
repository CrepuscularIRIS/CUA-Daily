arxiv: 2610.40599 (2610.40599v1)
title: HairDeck: Animation-Ready Stylized Hair Cards from a Single Image
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40599

§Abstract
The hair of stylized characters in games and avatars is built from hair cards: artist-placed ribbons whose geometry is the visible hair, animated by spring bones. Existing image-based methods produce realistic hair or a single fused mesh, while CHARM, the only generator of stylized hair cards, decodes a hairstyle autoregressively as a long sequence of control points. Observing that a card hairstyle is an unordered set of artist-defined primitives, we propose HairDeck, which generates it as one: each card is a single token, and a set diffusion transformer denoises all cards together. From a single image, HairDeck generates the cards and the spring-bone chains that drive them, yielding an animatable asset that exports to ready-to-use downstream formats. On CHARM's own test set, it lowers Chamfer distance by 44% and card-orientation error by more than half while running 33 times faster, and from single images it outperforms CHARM, image-to-3D pipelines and strand-based methods. Project page: https://hyzcluster.github.io/HairDeck

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
3D generators now produce convincing shapes from a single image [ 83 , 72 , 71 , 77 ] , but not the structure that artists build assets from. The hair of stylized characters in games and avatars is a clear example. It is made of hair cards : a few dozen to over a hundred ribbons, each placed by an artist, whose geometry is the visible hair. Unlike the alpha-textured hair cards of realistic hair, flat strips whose texture stands in for many strands [ 20 , 88 , 60 ] , these cards have a rhombic or triangular cross-section whose width and thickness vary along the card. Such hair is commonly animated with spring bones : short chains of bones hung from the head whose tips are simulated every frame, so that the hair sways and settles. Similar simulations ship as Dynamic Bone in Unity [ 18 ] , PhysBones in VRChat [ 61 ] , Kawaii Physics in Unreal Engine [ 36 ] and a node in Godot [ 10 ] . Artists place every card and rig every chain by hand. Existing methods do not produce this asset. Hair generators output realistic strands [ 49 , 13 , 42 ] , including recent ones that accept stylized images [ 28 , 31 , 44 , 65 ] , and card extraction turns such strands into alpha-textured cards for realistic hair [ 88 , 60 ] . Image-to-3D generators [ 71 , 77 ] output a single fused mesh with no cards to animate. CHARM [ 15 ] is the only generator of stylized hair cards and introduced the benchmark for them. It generates the control points of a hairstyle one at a time, as a sequence of hundreds to thousands of discretized tokens. We observe that a card hairstyle is an unordered set of artist-defined primitives, and we generate it as one. Like CHARM, we describe each card by its control points (position, width and thickness), and we add its cross-section type and its root normal, the width direction at the root, so each card keeps the cross-section and the orientation the artist gave it. What we change is the unit of generation, with two designs. First, each card becomes one continuous token: a card VAE encodes its variable-length control points into a latent, and the token holds this latent with the card’s root position, root normal and cross-section type, so a hairstyle has one token per card, about 70 on average. Second, all tokens of a hairstyle are generated together: a set diffusion transformer without positional embedding denoises them with flow matching [ 25 , 27 ] , and an existence flag on each token decides how many cards appear. Each design makes generation more accurate and faster. Together they amount to a simple rule for assets made of artist-defined primitives: one token per primitive, and no order among them. A single image shows the hair relative to the head, so we predict the cards in a head frame anchored to the face, which improves where the hair sits without changing its shape. Images also come in many drawing styles, so we widen the appearance of our training renders in two ways: synthetic style augmentation, which improves accuracy on stylized inputs, and renders restyled by an image-editing model [ 68 ] , which keep the card structure stable across styles. Cards alone do not move. We generate the spring-bone chains with the same recipe: each chain becomes one token holding its path, joint count and stiffness, and a second set diffusion transformer generates all chains of a hairstyle together from its card tokens. With a fixed geometric skinning, the cards and chains form an animatable asset that exports to ready-to-use downstream formats. In summary, our contributions are: • A card token for stylized hair: a representation that keeps the cross-section type and the root normal, and a card VAE that encodes the variable-length control points into one continuous latent and decodes the card by arc length. • A set formulation of hair-card generation: a set diffusion transformer without positional embedding generates all card tokens of a hairstyle together, and an existence flag on each token decides how many cards appear. • Single-image generation with a face-anchored head frame, synthetic style augmentation and restyled renders. • Spring-bone chains generated as a second set conditioned on the card tokens, which makes the output animatable. We evaluate on CHARM’s test set with its protocol and also measure the orientation of every card against the original artist meshes. HairDeck lowers Chamfer distance by 44% and card-orientation error by more than half while running 33 times faster. From a single image, it outperforms CHARM, image-to-3D pipelines and strand generators.
