title: IntentTwin: Deconfounding Candidate Support and the Oracle-to-Deployment Gap in Offline Web Behavior Prediction
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12918

§Abstract
Offline web-behavior benchmarks frequently insert the demonstrated action into a finite candidate set and then measure reranking. We show that this protocol can create a candidate-support confound: when the target is outside training support while all distractors are sampled from that support, support membership identifies the gold action without modeling behavior. We formalize this effect with support-identifiability rate (SIR), construct support-balanced candidate sets, and evaluate a target-free dual-path proposer that combines support retrieval with open-vocabulary DOM/LM proposals. On filtered OPeRA (4,282/469/992 train/dev/test steps), the original protocol has SIR 1.0000 on target-OOV rows and a support-only rule reaches 1.0000 OOV Exact@1. Support balancing reduces SIR to 0.0000; the strongest conditional reranker obtains 0.7214 overall Exact@1, 0.7988 on IV rows, and 0.5668 on OOV rows. The target-free dual-path system reaches Proposal Recall@16 0.6915 and end-to-end Exact@1 0.5212, compared with oracle conditional Exact@1 0.7450. On Mind2Web, the same audit yields SIR 0.2380 and an oracle-to-proposal gap of 0.2125. A 200-row semantic audit labels 0.7150 of exact-key OOV targets as semantically novel, 0.2450 as locator/string variation, and 0.0400 as ambiguous (Cohen's $κ=0.8173$). These measurements show why support composition must be controlled alongside candidate accuracy when offline results are used to reason about deployment.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Web interaction traces contain a prefix of observations and actions, but an offline benchmark often supplies the gold action as an item in the candidate set. Consider a target such as click “Blue 256GB” . In an oracle evaluation it is guaranteed to appear among 15 distractors; in deployment, a system must first discover and name that action from the current page and history. Ranking correctness is therefore conditional on candidate availability. A second issue appears when candidate availability is correlated with the evaluation label. In the original target-anchored construction, the logged action is inserted into the candidate set while every negative is sampled from the frozen training vocabulary. For a target-OOV row, the gold action is thus the unique out-of-support candidate. A support-only rule can identify the target from candidate membership alone; we call this candidate-support confounding . We therefore separate behavioral discrimination from the information revealed by the support composition of the candidate set. We study this distinction with a target-free context c_{t} and a structured action key a_{t}=(\mathrm{type},\mathrm{target},\mathrm{value}) . The protocol freezes the candidate support using training targets, keeps sessions disjoint, and stores an ordered row ledger containing candidates, probabilities, ranks, NLLs, and provenance. A test target whose exact key is absent from the training support is marked target-OOV; it is never added to the training vocabulary or the model input. We organize the study around four questions: 1. Support confounding: When does candidate membership reveal the gold action independently of the behavioral context? 2. Deconfounded reranking: How do rankers behave when IV and OOV candidates are support-balanced? 3. Oracle-to-deployment gap: What changes when the oracle anchor is replaced by a target-free proposer? 4. Support-aware proposal: Can context-gated retrieval and open-vocabulary proposal improve coverage without candidate-level gates? Our contributions are: • a formal candidate-support confound and the support-identifiability rate (SIR) diagnostic; • a support-balanced candidate protocol with exact action-key strata, pseudo-OOV calibration, and complete candidate-level ledgers; and • a target-free dual-path proposal evaluation that reports proposal recall, covered reranking, and end-to-end exact action selection on OPeRA and Mind2Web.
