title: GUI-JEPA: Candidate-Conditioned JEPA Latents as a Fast Action Policy for Mobile GUI Agents
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36877

§Abstract
Mobile GUI agents are typically fine-tuned 7B-scale vision-language models (VLMs) that generate each action as text. However, these VLMs decode every action token by token, and even agents that score candidate actions keep a large language model or VLM in the loop, making them slow and memory-hungry. To address these issues, we propose GUI-JEPA, to our knowledge the first decoder-free GUI agent policy built on a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), which selects actions in the latent space of a JEPA encoder pretrained on unlabelled GUI screenshots. Its action- and region-conditioned latent predictor turns the screen latent into one latent per candidate action, letting lightweight heads score all candidates against the instruction in a single batched pass, without decoding a token. We further show that the scorer relies on this latent pathway and that the predictor is trained for the decision itself: a randomly initialised predictor does just as well as one pretrained on screen dynamics. On AndroidControl, GUI-JEPA is 2.8-4.0x faster on the same GPU than a Qwen2.5-VL-7B agent we fine-tune with the same inputs, while reaching 86-92% of its in-distribution accuracy with 6% of its parameters. Code is available at https://github.com/bcwang-sjtu/GUI-JEPA

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
A mobile graphical user interface (GUI) agent’s per-step cost is dominated by a 7B+ vision-language model (VLM) decoding its action token by token. On one NVIDIA H20, a Qwen2.5-VL-7B agent ( Bai et al., 2025 ) LoRA-fine-tuned ( Hu et al., 2022 ) on AndroidControl needs 207 ms per step under vLLM ( Kwon et al., 2023 ) and 507 ms under Hugging Face (HF) transformers to emit a JSON action of {\sim} 12 tokens (median over 100 real steps, batch 1; Table 3 ), and 8B-class agents average 2.6–10.6 s per step in a live-device benchmark ( Sui et al., 2026 ) . Yet in GUI grounding a VLM fixes its target elements during prefill, with decoding only refining coordinates ( Lin et al., 2026a ) , and decoding on the critical path makes agents correct but late ( Dong et al., 2026 ) . Could a GUI agent score its actions in the latent space of a joint-embedding predictive architecture ( Assran et al., 2023 , JEPA;) , without decoding them at all? GUI world models generate pixels, code or text and serve an LLM policy as simulator or verifier ( Luo et al., 2026 ; Zheng et al., 2026 ; Mei et al., 2026 ; Cao et al., 2026 ; Xu et al., 2026b ) , or as a pretraining target ( Liu et al., 2026b ) . On GUIs, JEPAs have so far served understanding and verification: UI-JEPA describes user intent through an LLM decoder ( Fu et al., 2025 ) , LGWM verifies GUI transitions ( Zhang et al., 2026a ) , and next-frame feature prediction regularises the training of a VLM agent ( Yang et al., 2026b ) . Candidate scorers over the accessibility tree (a11y) keep an LLM or VLM in the loop ( Dai et al., 2026 ; Zhang, 2026 ; Christianos et al., 2025 ) , and Agentic-JEPA, which acts with a JEPA, is text-only with 0% out-of-distribution (OOD) success ( Rodrigo-Ginés, 2026 ) . To our knowledge, no GUI agent has yet used a JEPA as the policy that selects its actions. We bring JEPAs from perception and robotics to GUI control, moving action selection from the decoder into the latent space of a frozen JEPA encoder. GUI-JEPA encodes the screenshot once with a JEPA ViT-L pretrained on GUI screenshots, transforms that latent once per candidate (a11y element or global action) with a 39.9M residual predictor conditioned on the candidate’s action and region of interest (ROI), scores the candidate-conditioned latents with a cosine structural head, adds a 33M text-matcher score and takes the argmax, all in one batched pass ( Figure 2 ). As in earlier candidate-scoring agents, the candidates come from the a11y tree, but GUI-JEPA never generates a token, so the cost of a step depends on how many candidates it scores. Under the same inputs, judge and GPU as a Qwen2.5-VL-7B agent that we fine-tune with LoRA (SFT), GUI-JEPA is 2.8–4.0 \times faster and reaches 92.4% / 86.1% of its in-distribution (IDD) Low / High accuracy with 6.0% of its parameters and 4.6–6.4 \times less GPU memory ( Figure 1 ). Our contributions are: • GUI-JEPA , the first GUI agent whose policy is built on a JEPA. It selects actions by scoring candidate-conditioned JEPA latents, with no decoder, and is 2.8–4.0 \times faster than a same-protocol 7B agent that decodes token by token. • Adapting a JEPA policy to GUIs: a11y candidates as actions, ROI- and action-conditioned residual prediction, a structural head whose only candidate-dependent visual input is the conditioned latent, and two text rankers that together add 3.8 out-of-distribution AC-High points ( Table 2 ). • Mechanism: the scorer relies on the JEPA latent pathway, which is trained for the decision. A randomly initialised predictor matches a dynamics-pretrained one, so a JEPA policy for GUIs needs no dynamics pretraining.
