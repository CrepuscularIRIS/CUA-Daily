§Abstract
Generating useful robot-training data requires more than visually plausiblescenes: objects must support interaction, placements must remain physicallyvalid, and tasks must admit repeatable execution. We present\textbf{Awomo-SimDataEngine}, an agentic system that connects asset and scenegeneration to robot demonstration synthesis. Shared asset services providerigid and articulated objects, including structure-grounded part and jointgeneration with ISArt. Scene generation supports two complementary routes:Unravel reconstructs editable scenes from images, while SimForge buildssingle-room and multi-room environments from text. A graph-native harnesscoordinates construction, validation, andbounded repair, routing failures to the responsible module while retainingunaffected scene state. PolicyForge binds validated worlds to tasks and robotembodiments to produce replayable demonstrations. Evaluations cover assetgeometry, scene quality, and downstream policy learning. On MuJoCo-basedLIBERO-Plus, co-training with Isaac Sim demonstrations improves the overallsuccess rate of a World-Action Model (WAM) from $77.17\%$ to $89.43\%$. Goal and spatialsuccess improve by $31.66$ and $6.25$ percentage points, respectively.These results support the utility of the generated data for cross-simulatorpolicy training, with more limited gains on long-horizon tasks.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Robot learning depends on demonstrations that cover varied objects, environments, and manipulation goals. Collecting such coverage in the real world is costly, while manually preparing each simulated environment limits the scale and diversity of synthetic data. Generative models offer a way to expand this training distribution, but their outputs must support interaction: a plausible image or textured mesh can still hide invalid collision geometry, inaccessible interiors, incorrect joints, unstable placements, or an unreplayable initial state. These defects affect both whether demonstrations can be collected and what policies learn from them. Recent progress spans image-conditioned 3D assets, agentic scene construction, and automated demonstration synthesis [ Tencent Hunyuan3D Team, 2025a ; SAM 3D Team et al., 2025 ; Pfaff et al., 2026 ; Xia et al., 2026 ; Mandlekar et al., 2023 ; Ranawaka et al., 2026 ] . Connecting these capabilities requires agreement on object identities, coordinate frames, physical representations, task requirements, and validation results. An asset replacement changes the geometry available for registration; a revised scene changes the reset and interaction conditions seen by a controller. An integrated system must preserve these dependencies and return failures to the stage that can resolve them. We introduce Awomo-SimDataEngine , an agentic system for constructing simulation-ready worlds and converting them into robot demonstrations. The system connects asset generation, scene generation, and demonstration synthesis through a Graph-Native Harness for execution and repair. Scene generation supports both visual references and language descriptions: Unravel reconstructs editable scenes from single-view images, whereas SimForge builds single-room and multi-room environments from text. Both routes use shared asset services and produce worlds that can be checked, edited, and bound to robot tasks. Figure 1 illustrates the shared execution framework and the image-driven route. From assets to interactive scenes. Scene construction begins with assets that are usable beyond rendering. The asset module combines retrieval and generation with common geometry, collision, and physical checks (Section 4 ). Articulated objects add a structural requirement: their parts and joints must describe an executable mechanism. ISArt addresses this through geometric–kinematic planning, structure-conditioned part generation, and articulation binding. The two scene-generation routes use these assets differently (Section 5 ). Unravel progressively removes objects from a reference image to expose occluded regions, then uses accumulated point-map evidence to register and reassemble independently editable assets. SimForge instead plans room geometry, object requirements, and spatial arrangements from language, supporting both individual rooms and multi-room houses. For both routes, plausible appearance alone does not establish valid support, collision geometry, or articulation. From validated worlds to demonstrations. Construction failures need not be repaired where they are detected. A missing asset calls for acquisition; an intersecting object calls for a placement change; an incorrect joint may require revising the articulated asset. The Graph-Native Harness (Section 6 ) makes these handoffs explicit through shared state, validation evidence, bounded retries, and checkpoints. It retains unaffected work and revalidates the dependencies of each edit before construction proceeds. PolicyForge then binds an accepted world to a task, robot embodiment, and reset specification (Section 7 ). Scripted or motion-planned teachers execute in Isaac Sim, producing synchronized images, proprioception, and actions; successful validated episodes are retained for training. Scene variants, object substitutions, and initial-state and language augmentation diversify the resulting data. World validity, demonstration success, and downstream policy performance remain distinct outcomes. Experimental evidence. Module evaluations support the geometry and reconstruction methods: ISArt achieves part-geometry CD 0.163 versus 0.268 for the combined TRELLIS.2 + P3-SAM + X-Part baseline on 60 objects, while Unravel improves blind human asset-fidelity scores from 4.9 to 8.6 on 160 objects. These protocols are detailed in Sections 4.1 and 5.1.3 . Text-driven scene quality is evaluated separately on 179 room prompts and 31 multi-room prompts using the SceneEval and SceneSmith protocols (Section 5.2 ). These evaluations test complementary properties rather than a single pooled quality score. On the MuJoCo-based LIBERO-Plus benchmark, SimData-Ultra co-training increases World-Action Model success from 77.17\% to 89.43\% when comparing each configuration’s best evaluated checkpoint up to and including 13K steps, selected by overall success, with gains of 31.66 and 6.25 percentage points in goal and spatial generalization (Section 8 ).
