title: DynaRev: Benchmarking Web Agents under Dynamic Goal Revision
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40133

§Abstract
Web agents are typically evaluated with goals fully specified before execution and fixed thereafter, whereas real users often revise their requirements during interaction. To study such interactions, we introduce DynaRev, a benchmark of 181 tasks to evaluate web agents under dynamic goal revision. DynaRev uses semantic milestones to deliver revisions at task-defined stages, covers additive revisions, unconditional overrides, and runtime-conditioned overrides, and evaluates correctness, responsiveness, and efficiency through trajectory-grounded evaluation. Five-model evaluations show that agents often satisfy only part of the task requirements, with performance varying across task sources, revision types, and models. Further analyses reveal varied milestone timing, mixed effects of dynamic delivery, and contrasting revision-handling outcomes, with gains from instruction rewriting while preserving browser state and losses from browser resets. DynaRev provides a testbed for studying how web agents adapt to evolving user goals. The code and data are available at https://github.com/zsq259/DynaRev.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have enabled autonomous agents to perceive, reason, plan, and act in complex digital environments ( Ning et al., 2025 ; Xi et al., 2025 ; Mei et al., 2025 ) . Among them, web agents interact with website interfaces through human-like actions such as clicking, typing, and scrolling, allowing them to operate websites without relying on dedicated APIs ( Zheng et al., 2024 ; Lai et al., 2024 ) . They support a wide range of practical tasks such as information seeking, online shopping, travel planning, and service navigation. With increasingly capable foundation models and agent frameworks, web agents are now able to execute complex multi-step tasks in realistic environments ( Gur et al., 2024 ; He et al., 2024 ; Yang et al., 2025 ) . However, most web-agent tasks assume that the user’s goal is fully specified before execution and remains fixed throughout the interaction ( Deng et al., 2023 ; Zhou et al., 2024 ; Koh et al., 2024 ; He et al., 2024 ; Xue et al., 2025 ; Gou et al., 2025 ) . Real-world user goals are often more dynamic. For example, a user may first ask an agent to find a flight from Los Angeles to Boston, then add “Avoid overnight flights” after viewing the candidates, revise this preference to “If daytime flights cost more than $150 extra, overnight flights are acceptable,” and later request nonstop flights only. Such interactions are not merely a fixed instruction split across multiple turns: later instructions may add new constraints, override previously active ones, or introduce requirements that apply conditionally based on the web state observed during execution. We refer to this setting as dynamic goal revision . Recent work has begun to recognize goal adaptation as an important agent capability. AgentChangeBench ( Rana et al., 2025 ) studies goal shifts in conversational tool-using agents, DeskCraft ( Wang et al., 2026 ) considers interruptions and corrections in desktop workflows, and InterruptBench ( Zou et al., 2026 ) evaluates additions, revisions, and retractions in long-horizon web navigation. However, three gaps remain in web settings. First, revisions are often injected based on relative trajectory progress rather than task-semantic progress, so agents following different paths may receive the same revision at different task stages. Second, existing settings largely omit conditional revisions whose applicability depends on the web state. Finally, evaluations mainly assess task-level success and recovery cost, with limited analysis of revision-level constraint satisfaction, post-revision responsiveness, and cost–quality trade-offs. To address these gaps, we introduce DynaRev , a benchmark for evaluating web agents under dynamic goal revisions. DynaRev uses semantic milestones , task-defined stages of the current goal, to trigger revisions independently of the execution path, and covers additive, unconditional-override, and conditional-override revisions, with conditional branches resolved from the web state. We develop a revision-aware, trajectory-grounded evaluation framework that assesses answer correctness, revision responsiveness, and cost-aware efficiency. We construct 181 dynamic tasks from Online-Mind2Web ( Xue et al., 2025 ) and Mind2Web 2 ( Gou et al., 2025 ) , and evaluate five models. Agents often satisfy only part of the task requirements, with performance varying across task sources, revision types, and models. Further analyses reveal substantial variation in milestone timing and mixed effects of dynamic delivery, while instruction rewriting with preserved browser state improves quality and efficiency and browser resets degrade performance. These results reveal behaviors missed by static evaluation. Our contributions are threefold: (i) We formulate dynamic goal revision for web agents and introduce semantic milestone-based revision delivery. (ii) We construct DynaRev , covering additive revisions, unconditional overrides, and runtime-conditioned overrides. (iii) We develop a revision-aware, trajectory-grounded evaluation assessing correctness, responsiveness, and efficiency.
