title: Realtime-Bench: Can Frontier Models Solve Real-time Interactive Problems?
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22155

§Abstract
Real-time interaction requires agents to perceive evolving environments and act under temporal constraints, yet existing evaluations of real-time streaming models focus on conversational quality and streaming perception, leaving effective action under strict temporal constraints untested. We introduce Realtime-Bench, a suite of carefully curated dynamic tasks where delayed actions directly affect success. Under Wall Clock, where environments advance according to actual inference latency, the best success rate reaches only 44% and the best Pass^3 is only 36%, demonstrating that real-time interaction remains challenging. To separate serving latency from models' real-time streaming capability, we compare Wall Clock with Paused Clock, which removes inference delays as an idealized reference, and introduce Token Clock, which advances simulated time according to generated token counts, testing temporal awareness independently of physical serving speed. We find that temporal costs limit success in evolving environments and that higher reasoning effort has mixed effects under time pressure, reflecting a trade-off between decision quality and timely execution. Based on these results, we discuss future directions for improving real-time multimodal models, including adaptive reasoning with temporal awareness and continuous perception and action.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Many real-world interactive scenarios require decisions to be made under strict temporal constraints. For example, when purchasing a highly demanded ticket, detecting availability is not enough: an agent must observe the changing interface and act before the opportunity disappears. Such scenarios require real-time streaming capabilities , where a model continuously perceives an evolving environment and produces timely actions in response to newly arriving information. Recently, a growing family of real-time streaming models, such as GPT-Realtime ( OpenAI, 2025 ) and Gemini Live ( Google, 2026b ) , has begun to make this setting practically possible. In contrast to asynchronous models that process inputs only after they are fully received, these models can consume streaming inputs and react to new information with low latency. Unlike previous benchmarks that evaluate models in static environments ( Zhang et al., 2026 ; Tan et al., 2026 ) where the environment does not continuously evolve, evaluating streaming models requires dynamic settings that test whether models can perceive and act effectively under strict temporal constraints. However, existing evaluations of real-time systems largely focus on capabilities such as streaming perception, conversational quality, and interruption handling ( Lin et al., 2026 ; Lu et al., 2026b ) , with limited coverage of interactive tasks in which actions affect subsequent environment states and delayed execution can change the outcome. We first characterize real-time streaming capability along three complementary dimensions: temporal state awareness, adaptive deliberation, and time-aware action selection. These dimensions capture an agent’s ability to track relevant changes and information freshness, adjust deliberation to the available time, and select actions that remain useful when executed. Building on this framework, we introduce Realtime-Bench , comprising 50 interactive game and web tasks in which evolving states and limited opportunities make success depend on both action choice and timing. We evaluate 12 frontier models spanning standard asynchronous models, streaming models and decision models. As shown in Figure 1 , real-time interaction remains challenging, with the best success rate reaching only 44.0% and best pass@1 is below 42.67% under Wall Clock , which advances the environment according to actual inference and serving time, following prior interactive-agent evaluations ( Kang et al., 2025 ; Froger et al., 2026 ; ValsAI, 2026 ; Parekh et al., 2026 ) . To better understand these limitations and disentangle the effects of serving latency and models’ real-time streaming capability, we compare three clock protocols (Figure 2 ). Alongside Wall Clock, Paused Clock freezes the environment during inference, providing an idealized reference in which deliberation incurs no temporal cost ( Tan et al., 2026 ; Zheng et al., 2026 ; Tan et al., 2025 ) . We further introduce Token Clock , which advances simulated time according to the number of generation tokens consumed before an action is committed. Models that spend more tokens deliberating therefore act later, preserving the task consequences of delayed decisions while controlling for physical serving speed. Under each clock, we test low and high reasoning effort to examine the trade-off between decision quality and timely execution. Our analysis reveals that substantial performance gaps relative to Paused Clock persist under Token Clock, highlighting the difficulty of translating task competence into timely action even when temporal costs are standardized independently of serving speed. Higher reasoning effort improves success for all six models under Paused Clock but has mixed effects under temporal pressure. Further experiments show that relaxing the temporal cost of generation generally improves performance but does not ensure task success. These findings motivate future work on adaptive reasoning with temporal awareness and continuous perception and action, enabling agents to coordinate deliberation and execution with the time available.
