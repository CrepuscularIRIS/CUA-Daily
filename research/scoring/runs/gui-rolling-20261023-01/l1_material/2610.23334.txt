title: LAW: Unifying Language, Action, and World Modeling for Agentic Physical Intelligence
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23334

§Abstract
World-action models jointly predict actions and future world states, but long-horizon manipulation further requires language reasoning to plan subtask objectives, assess execution progress, and guide action selection. We present LAW, a closed-loop VLM--WAM framework that unifies language reasoning, action generation, and world modeling through semantic subtask progress. Within the LAW agent, a hybrid-expert vision-language model plans the remaining subtasks, estimates the values of and fuses actions proposed by a world-action model, and asynchronously assesses execution status to support continued execution and subtask transitions. Within the world-action model, Mixture-of-Transformer Experts integrates modality-specific and shared computation for joint video--action modeling. Dual-stream hierarchical visual memory maintains temporal context for both models, while subtask-local value learning aligns action evaluation with semantic objectives and preserves supervision from successful prefixes of failed trajectories. LAW achieves 94.21% average success across 50 RoboTwin tasks. Replacing the WAM backbone with OpenWAM-$alpha$ further raises the average success rate to 95.56%, establishing a new open-source state of the art on RoboTwin. Despite having only 2B parameters, our VLM also outperforms variants using Embodied-R1.5 and GPT-5.5 High. On three long-horizon real-robot tasks, LAW achieves 65% average success, compared with 28.33% for $pi_{0.5}$ and 25% for Fast-WAM.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Long-horizon manipulation is a sequence of semantic subtasks, not merely a longer sequence of motor commands. After reaching for an object, a robot must decide whether the grasp actually succeeded; after a successful grasp, it must recognize that the task has entered a new stage; before executing the next motion, it must choose among several physically plausible actions according to the current objective [ 23 , 22 ] . Errors in any of these decisions compound over time. Consequently, local action quality alone does not determine task success. A capable agent must maintain a persistent account of task progress, connect that account to action generation, and revise its decisions when execution departs from expectation [ 35 , 44 ] . Vision-language-action policies connect semantic representations to control [ 60 , 26 , 8 ] , while world-action models learn action-conditioned scene evolution and executable behavior [ 59 , 57 , 27 , 51 ] . These models substantially improve action generation, but long-horizon deployment exposes a complementary systems problem: reasoning and control must remain aligned as the task evolves. A language plan generated once at the beginning cannot determine whether its intermediate steps were completed [ 22 ] , and an action generator conditioned only on the current observation may not know which visual changes matter to the larger goal [ 41 ] . Placing a planner before a policy therefore leaves three interfaces underspecified: how execution progress updates the plan, how candidate actions are judged against the current semantic objective, and how relevant history is retained without overwhelming either model. Our premise is that semantic subtask progress should serve as the temporal interface between language reasoning and world-action modeling. Subtasks provide more than a decomposition of the instruction: they define when the agent should advance its plan, which outcomes a candidate action should optimize, and which observations should remain salient in memory. This view reveals that computations occur at different rates. State assessment should run continuously without blocking control, candidate evaluation should occur whenever the WAM proposes a new action chunk, and planning should operate at the semantic task scale rather than at every control step. The challenge is coordinating reasoning and control, rather than invoking a larger reasoner at every step. We instantiate this principle in LAW, a closed-loop VLM–WAM framework that unifies language reasoning, action generation, and world modeling through semantic subtask progress, as summarized in Fig. 1 . The LAW agent uses a hybrid-expert VLM with role-selected planner, state, and value experts on a shared frozen backbone. The planner predicts the remaining semantic subtasks; conditioned on the current subtask, the WAM generates diverse action chunks; the value expert ranks and fuses them; and execution status is assessed asynchronously to determine subtask completion. Once the current subtask is completed, the planner predicts the next subtask and the agent switches to it; otherwise, the agent continues executing the current subtask. The LAW agent coordinates planning, action selection, and execution monitoring at different frequencies, using feedback to continue or switch subtasks. To strengthen joint world and action modeling, we introduce Mixture-of-Transformer Experts (MoTE) into the WAM, yielding MoTE-WAM. MoT projects video and action features into a common attention space to exchange cross-modal information, but their subsequent transformations remain modality-specific. MoTE introduces shared experts to further transform these cross-modal representations, alongside video-private and action-private experts that preserve modality-specific computation. The shared experts learn common temporal and physical structure across predicted scene dynamics and robot actions, strengthening video–action joint modeling beyond information exchange through attention alone. Long-horizon execution also requires memory aligned with subtask transitions. Separate hierarchical memory streams for the VLM and WAM retain recent observations and full-resolution states at subtask boundaries, while compressing older history to preserve relevant context across subtasks. To better exploit failed trajectories when training the value expert, we introduce subtask-local value learning, which terminates returns at semantic boundaries. This preserves positive supervision from successfully completed subtasks within failed episodes, rather than assigning zero value to all their actions. We evaluate LAW on 50 RoboTwin [ 13 ] tasks, the RoboDojo [ 12 ] capability benchmark, and three long-horizon real-robot tasks containing up to nine semantic subtasks. LAW achieves 94.21% average success on RoboTwin, 2.38 percentage points above Fast-WAM, without robot-data pretraining.
