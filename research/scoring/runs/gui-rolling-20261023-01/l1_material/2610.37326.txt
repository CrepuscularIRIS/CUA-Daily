title: ScIntBench: Physical Interaction Plausibility in 3D Scene Generation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37326

§Abstract
Existing evaluations of text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation largely emphasize semantic fidelity and physical validity at rest, leaving interaction-time physical reliability insufficiently assessed. To address this gap, we introduce ScIntBench, a unified benchmark centered on interaction readiness: it executes prescribed manipulation actions (e.g., pushing, opening, and surface probing) in physics simulation and measures task success, capability and progress, post-interaction integrity, and dynamic plausibility. Multi-view semantic and static physical evaluations complement these metrics and help explain interaction outcomes. We systematically evaluate 12 state-of-the-art scene generation systems across 10 room types. Our results reveal that strong semantic and static physical performance does not necessarily translate into interaction readiness: a system with near-perfect static plausibility completes only 11.54% of interactions, 7 of the 12 systems produce scenes that cannot support any interaction, and even the best system succeeds in only 36.92% of prescribed tasks. These findings highlight two fundamental challenges in generating interaction-ready scenes: providing the necessary physical interaction capabilities and ensuring reliable physical behavior under active manipulation.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation has made substantial progress, enabling the automatic construction of increasingly complex environments from natural-language descriptions. Recent advances have expanded scene generation from data-driven generative models to LLM/VLM-based workflows, specialized agentic pipeline, and general-purpose coding agents operating through modeling tools. This evolution has broadened the goal of scene generation beyond visual content creation toward simulation-ready environments for robotics, embodied AI, and interactive applications. However, as generation systems become increasingly capable, a fundamental question remains: Are generated 3D scenes truly ready for physical interaction, and can existing evaluations reliably assess this capability? Existing evaluation protocols for generated 3D scenes primarily assess semantic quality and physical plausibility at initialization. Semantic evaluation examines visual fidelity while physical evaluation measures collision, boundary violations, support correctness, and passive stability under gravity. However, these evaluations provide limited evidence of whether generated scenes can support physical manipulation and exhibit plausible responses to active interaction. A scene that is geometrically valid and plausible at rest is not necessarily interaction-ready. For example, a refrigerator may appear correctly placed but lack the articulation required to open its door; a chair may remain stable under gravity yet behave implausibly when pushed or subjected to a load. Such failures expose two distinct limitations: interaction availability, which concerns whether a scene provides the prerequisites for an intended action, and interaction reliability, which concerns whether that action can be successfully executed with physically plausible outcomes. This motivates two research questions: (1) To what extent do conventional semantic and static evaluations characterize interaction readiness? (2) What limits interaction readiness across different scene generation paradigms: missing capabilities, unsuccessful execution, or implausible physical responses? To address these limitations and advance simulation-ready scene generation, we present ScIntBench, a comprehensive benchmark designed to systematically evaluate the semantic fidelity, static physical validity, and interaction-time physical reliability of generated 3D scenes across diverse generation paradigms. ScIntBench has three evaluation dimensions: a) multi-view semantic evaluation . We employ top-down render views and four corner views to better capture spatial arrangements and object relationships. b) static physical plausibility evaluation . We evaluate initialization-time physics through geometric validity checks and passive stability simulation. c) active interaction evaluation . We introduce standardized physics-based interaction tests that assess action executability, post-interaction physical integrity, and dynamic plausibility. To ensure fair comparisons, we evaluate scenes as generated, without adding missing articulations or physical properties. Together, these evaluations characterize not only the initial configuration of a generated scene but also its behavior during physical interaction, as illustrated in . We benchmark 12 state-of-the-art 3D scene generation systems across 10 room types under the same text prompts to systematically investigate their simulation readiness. Our evaluation reveals substantial interaction-time failures that are overlooked by conventional initialization-time assessments. Despite achieving strong semantic fidelity and static physical validity, several methods generate scenes that frequently fail under active interaction due to missing articulations, inconsistent physical metadata, or post-interaction physical violations. By distinguishing interaction availability from execution reliability, our analysis reveals two complementary barriers to simulation readiness: providing the necessary interaction capabilities and ensuring physically reliable behavior when those capabilities are exercised. In summary, our main contributions are threefold: • We introduce ScIntBench, a unified benchmark that comprehensively evaluates interaction readiness in text-conditioned 3D indoor scene generation. • We propose 4 interaction-based metrics to characterize simulation readiness beyond initialization-time validity. • We systematically benchmark 12 SOTA scene generation systems across ten room types, highlighting key challenges between static scene quality and interaction-time reliability, and identifying key failure modes that limit simulation readiness.
