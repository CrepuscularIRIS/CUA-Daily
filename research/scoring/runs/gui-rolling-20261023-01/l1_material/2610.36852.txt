arxiv: 2610.36852 (2610.36852v1)
title: When Black-Box Robot Policies Fail: Online Sensor Diagnosis and Repair with a Transparent Surrogate
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36852

§Abstract
Robot foundation models may generalize across tasks and embodiments, but their actions still depend on sensors that can fail. When the policy is frozen or inaccessible, can an external monitor diagnose and repair corrupted inputs without access to its internals? We test a structured surrogate that separates periodic gait from sensor-dependent corrections to guide input repair around a frozen locomotion policy. On simulated Go2, offline diagnosis raises walking success from 259/640 to 429/640 episodes; online repair reaches 382/640, with no triggers in 64 held-out fault-free episodes. A matched boosted-tree surrogate predicts clean actions similarly well but yields no detected repair improvement over no repair. Separate fits improve walking on Go1 and ANYmal C, although phase-aware gradients perform better. Evidence remains limited to one policy and a partly screened simulated fault suite; robot foundation model and hardware validation remain open.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Robot foundation models promise transfer across tasks and embodiments [ 9 , 7 ] , yet their actions depend on reliable sensor observations. When internals are inaccessible, monitors see only inputs and actions. We study the frozen multi-embodiment URMA locomotion policy [ 2 ] . On Go2 at 0.5\,\mathrm{m/s} , a 0.3\,\mathrm{rad/s} yaw-gyroscope bias prevents walking in 16/16 episodes. Classical diagnosis uses plant or sensor models [ 6 ] , whereas gradient attribution and VLA steering require policy access [ 13 , 16 , 5 , 3 ] . Runtime guardrails constrain plans [ 11 ] , while masked training builds sensor robustness into the policy [ 10 ] . Unusual sensor readings may nevertheless result from legitimate disturbances. Prior work distills locomotion into boosted trees or symbolic expressions [ 1 ] . Structured controllers and sparse mixtures also expose control structure [ 14 , 17 ] . Our surrogate separates periodic gait from sensor-dependent corrections, ranks abnormal input contributions, and guides repair. The frozen policy produces every action, so the surrogate never replaces the controller. We contribute diagnosis and repair without policy gradients, paired offline and online evaluations, and tests on three quadrupeds. This controlled setting motivates external supervision for robot foundation models [ 15 ] . Direct validation on those models remains open.
