arxiv: 2610.33645 (2610.33645v1)
title: PreView: Enhancing GUI Grounding with Preprocessed Views and Cues
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33645

§Abstract
GUI grounding requires locating small controls in cluttered screenshots and identifying which control a natural-language instruction refers to. Cropping and enlargement can expose local detail, but the resulting image may still leave ambiguity about the intended target. We propose PreView, an auxiliary preprocessor that improves the inputs to an existing grounding model without further training that model. PreView prepares two complementary types of input features: task-relevant views for localization and candidate-specific cues for target understanding. A trained lightweight auxiliary model predicts and refines a center, crops the screenshot into a local view, and enlarges that view for zoomed-in grounding. It also prepares a visual cue (i.e., a red-circle marker with a location hint) and a textual cue describing the candidate's identity or function. The frozen grounding model uses the prepared inputs to predict the final click. Across five benchmarks, PreView improves the average grounding accuracy of Qwen3-VL-2B, OS-Atlas-4B, and Qwen3-VL-4B without further training these grounding models. Extensive analyses examine the contributions of views and cues, center refinement, and visual input budgets.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
GUI grounding translates a natural-language instruction into the screen location of an actionable interface element. It connects an agent’s intended action to its execution: even a correct plan fails if the agent clicks the wrong control. Dedicated grounding models have advanced this capability through GUI-specific data and training, including SeeClick, UGround, OS-Atlas, and GroundNext ( Cheng et al., 2024 ; Gou et al., 2025 ; Wu et al., 2024 ; Feizi et al., 2025 ) . Professional interfaces remain challenging because they combine high-resolution screenshots, dense layouts, and small interactive elements ( Li et al., 2025 ; Nayak et al., 2025 ) . These settings raise a question beyond the capabilities of the grounder itself: how should the screenshot and instruction be presented to make the grounding decision easier? We consider two intertwined requirements: localization and understanding . Localization requires resolving an actionable control’s precise position amid competing visual content. When an entire screenshot is processed under a limited visual budget, small controls may receive insufficient detail. Understanding requires determining which element satisfies the instruction, including its functional role, its relationship to other elements, and the object to which it belongs. Consider the instruction to click “Get involved” in Figure 1 . The screenshot contains two stacked notification bars, each with an action button and a close control. A clearer image can help resolve these controls, but selecting the intended one also requires associating the button with the requested notification. Conversely, identifying the correct control does not guarantee an accurate click on its small target area. Existing work addresses these difficulties along several complementary routes. Training on broader and higher-quality GUI data strengthens the grounder’s learned representations ( Wu et al., 2024 ; Feizi et al., 2025 ) . At inference time, zoom-in methods crop and rescale selected regions to expose local detail, while repeated predictions provide opportunities to revise a localization decision. ScreenSeekeR uses a planner to guide cascaded visual search ( Li et al., 2025 ) . ZoomClick iteratively narrows the view around predictions ( Jiang et al., 2025 ) . UI-Zoomer uses prediction uncertainty to decide whether and how to zoom ( Tang et al., 2026 ) . These methods demonstrate that grounding depends on the observation and inference procedure as well as model parameters. Additional searches or predictions consume inference resources. Tighter cropping can also discard context needed to distinguish the target from nearby alternatives. Semantic augmentation provides another source of support. Screen parsing can expose interactable elements and their functional descriptions, as in OmniParser ( Lu et al., 2024 ) . Instruction refinement can make an intended target more explicit. AdaZoom-GUI combines such refinement with conditional zooming ( Pei et al., 2026 ) . These approaches show that visual focusing and semantic clarification can be complementary. They also motivate a more specific question: can a learned auxiliary model prepare both task-relevant views and informative cues for an existing grounder? Such preprocessing must select useful evidence while limiting two sources of error: removing necessary visual context and supplying misleading semantic descriptions. We propose PreView , an auxiliary model that previews the interface before the downstream grounding decision, without further training the grounding model. It prepares two complementary types of input features: task-relevant views and candidate-specific cues . Given a screenshot and instruction, it predicts and refines a center, then crops the screenshot around that center to produce a local view. Cropping removes potentially distracting surroundings. We then zoom in by enlarging the selected view to expose local detail. The visual cue pairs a red-circle marker with a fixed location hint. When the auxiliary model accepts the candidate, the textual cue describes its identity, function, or local relationships. The frozen grounding model receives the prepared view, the original instruction, and available cues. Its predicted click is mapped back to the original screenshot. We jointly train the auxiliary model for center prediction and candidate verification with description generation. Our contributions are: • We propose PreView , an auxiliary preprocessing framework that improves GUI grounding without training or modifying the downstream grounding model. • We introduce two complementary input features: center-guided views that support localization, and candidate-specific visual and textual cues that support target understanding. • Across five benchmarks and three grounding models, we improve reported average accuracy by 1.5 – 13.9 points, with gains on SSPro, OSW-G, MMB-GUI, and UI-V.
