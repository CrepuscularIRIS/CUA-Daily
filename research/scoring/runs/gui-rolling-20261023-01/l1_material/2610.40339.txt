title: Wizard: Manipulate Any World in Real Time via Agentic Control
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40339

§Abstract
Recent advances in video world models have enabled the generation of increasingly realistic and interactive virtual environments, opening new possibilities for player-customizable gameplay. Agentic frameworks further advance this direction by decoupling explicit game logic and controllable world representations from visual generation. However, existing approaches support only simple game settings and predefined motion patterns, generalize poorly to unseen environments and characters, and offer limited real-time interactivity. In this paper, we introduce Wizard, an agentic game world model that enables real-time, precise control over character motions while generalizing zero-shot across novel scenes, characters, and actions. To achieve this, we construct a diverse dataset spanning 750 scenes and 178 characters, incorporating skeleton and geometry information as structured representations. We leverage these representations as intermediate proxies to condition a video generation model, together with an adaptive masking mechanism that improves alignment between the conditioning signals and generated video rollouts. Furthermore, we introduce an agentic framework that explicitly maintains and updates game states, allowing players to freely configure their virtual worlds and exercise fine-grained control over character motions while preserving visual quality and gameplay consistency. By combining agentic control with structured proxy-based generation, our framework unlocks new possibilities for player-driven game creation with real-time, generalizable, and mechanically consistent world simulation.

World models aim to predict future observations from visual histories and current actions, attracting growing interest in gaming applications [ 33 , 2 , 11 , 27 , 10 ] . By conditioning video generation on players’ mouse and keyboard inputs, video world models offer a natural foundation for interactive and personalized gameplay [ 14 , 42 , 48 , 40 , 22 ] . Despite recent progress, existing game world models face limitations in control flexibility, generalization, and real-time interaction. Action-conditioned models [ 38 , 35 , 13 ] typically restrict players to predefined action sets, while prompt-conditioned models [ 49 , 7 , 4 ] often depend on motion and skill patterns seen during training, limiting their ability to follow unseen instructions. To improve state consistency and spatial controllability, recent agentic frameworks maintain explicit world states [ 21 , 7 ] and translate them into structured proxies, such as depth maps [ 28 ] , semantic maps [ 5 ] , blockouts [ 23 ] or oriented 3D bounding boxes [ 15 ] , to guide video generation. However, their demonstrations largely focus on basic movement, such as vehicle driving [ 26 , 18 ] and character locomotion [ 39 , 45 ] , with limited support for articulated character actions. Engine-based approaches [ 16 , 43 ] offer another route to structured control, but their reliance on predefined scenes and extra processing introduces constraints on scene flexibility and interaction latency. To address this gap, we introduce Wizard (Fig. 1 ), an agentic game world model that combines fine-grained character control, zero-shot generalization, real-time interaction and mechanically consistent gameplay , enabling players to customize their game worlds with novel scenes, characters, and motions specified through reference images or free-form language. At its core, Wizard employs an agentic framework to decouple scene construction, character animation, and gameplay logic from visual generation through structured spatial conditioning. For fine-grained control over camera movement and character animation, our method represents scenes with geometric proxies encoding depth and height, and characters with articulated skeletons. Given any user-provided scene image, we estimate monocular geometry to construct an approximate traversable 2.5D environment and render view-dependent geometric control maps. Character references are converted into 3D meshes and automatically rigged into controllable skeletons, with channel coding distinguishing character instances and their camera-relative orientations. These representations form a unified visual proxy. Building on the unified visual proxy shown in Fig. 1 , the agentic framework then updates scene geometry according to camera inputs and animates character skeletons according to action inputs, providing precise spatial and motion control for the causal video generator. To enable diverse motions for novel characters, a vision-language model retrieves compatible skeletons and animations from a motion library based on character morphology and intended motion style. The selected motions are retargeted to the character’s skeleton, enabling natural movement and player-defined actions. Beyond visual control, the agent further maintains explicit combat states, including health, relative distance, and orientation, to enforce gameplay rules and adapt boss behavior to evolving combat situations. To train the video model to faithfully follow these structured proxies and generalize across scenes, characters, and motions, we construct 50.3 hours of aligned proxy–video pairs from diverse sources, including data specifically designed for skeletal control. We further introduce an adaptive masking mechanism that restricts reference-image attention to the corresponding character regions, strengthening appearance alignment and preserving character identity during motion. Finally, we apply causal adaptation and few-step distillation [ 47 ] to enable real-time video generation, bringing detailed proxy control and consistent visual rendering into an interactive gameplay loop.
