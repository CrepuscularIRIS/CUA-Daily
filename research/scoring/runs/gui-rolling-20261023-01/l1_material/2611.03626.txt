title: Isekai3D SDG: A Platform for Diverse Synthetic Gameplay Visual Data
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03626

§Abstract
We present Isekai3D Synthetic Data Generation (SDG), a platform for simulating and capturing synthetic 3D gameplay for research in vision and world models, neural graphics, gameplay agents, and other applications. The platform combines reusable environment and character assets with Unreal Engine plugins in a two-stage workflow. First, world simulation generates gameplay while recording the world state, character movements, actions, and camera tracks needed for replay. Second, the recorded simulation is replayed to capture selected outputs, such as rendered video, images, and depth maps, at a specified resolution and frame rate under selected rendering and camera configurations. Separating simulation from capture allows the same recorded gameplay to produce multiple output variants without repeating the original simulation. Visual outputs are accompanied by frame-aligned camera, character, and action metadata. The platform uploads generated data directly in a training-friendly format, reducing intermediate storage, preparation time, and manual processing. This report describes the design of our overall system, including the platform's assets, plugins, and their functionality, explaining how they work together to generate diverse synthetic gameplay data.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Interactive 3D gameplay provides a setting for studying how visual worlds evolve and how actions influence them. For vision/world models and gameplay agents, useful training data can include both rendered observations and the actions, camera motion, and scene state associated with those observations. A shared data platform must therefore support variation across environments, characters, behaviors, and viewpoints while maintaining a consistent relationship between visual sequences and their accompanying metadata. Isekai3D SDG addresses this need by providing a platform for building large and diverse synthetic datasets of high-quality 3D gameplay. The platform combines reusable environment and character assets with Unreal Engine plugins for gameplay behavior, recording, and capture. This organization allows users to configure data generation around a common asset collection and workflow, and to extend individual components as their application requirements change. The workflow separates recording scene activity from capturing its visual outputs. A recorded sequence can be replayed with different camera and rendering configurations, allowing multiple observations to be generated from the same underlying activity. This enables diverse applications and use cases, as well as paired data generation for training purposes. Capture produces video or images together with frame-aligned metadata and optional rendering passes such as depth. Configuration files control asset selection, recording behavior, and capture variants, supporting automated runs in both editor and packaged-game workflows. The workflow builds a downloadable application with selected levels and the SDG plugins, enabling data generation without installing the Unreal Engine editor or build tools. Researchers can also implement custom functionality through multiple extension mechanisms, including their own plugins or DLL-based extensions.
