title: SPLATIFY: Reproduce, Discover, Innovate! From Papers and Ideas to Trainable 3DGS Code
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09116

§Abstract
The rapid growth of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) research demands significant effort to reimplement papers before building on them. We introduce SPLATIFY, a multi-agent framework that converts 3DGS papers into trainable gsplat-based implementations, where generic paper-to-code methods and frontier models fail. SPLATIFY achieves this through five innovations: (1) A context-free grammar for gsplat over a modular method template with extension points for losses, densification, rendering, and optimization, constraining synthesis so generated code satisfies gsplat's architectural invariants by construction. (2) Architectural elements for faithful reproduction: fork-aware citation recovery retrieving component-level code at function-level granularity, Graph-of-Thought synthesis in topological dependency order, RAG-guided in-context example selection from over 20 verified implementations, and visual feedback combining PSNR-guided regeneration, Gaussian-level structural checks, and VLM-driven patching. (3) Knowledge-driven compositional improvement that autonomously finds weaknesses and composes complementary regularizers, losses, and densification strategies to improve upon original results. (4) Interdisciplinary method discovery where agents retrieve physical priors from outside the 3DGS literature and compose them with rendering knowledge to produce methods for previously unaddressed scene types. (5) SPLATIFY-Bench, an evaluation framework across 30 diverse 3DGS papers. On papers without public code, SPLATIFY matches expert implementations while reducing development time from weeks to minutes, and through compositional discovery further improves PSNR by up to 2.4 dB. We additionally demonstrate novel methods for volumetric nebula rendering and other scientific domains, synthesized entirely by SPLATIFY.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
3D Gaussian Splatting has triggered a fast-growing sub-field of neural rendering, yet the community publishes knowledge far faster than it can absorb. Since Kerbl et al. [ 28 ] introduced 3DGS in 2023, follow-up papers have grown to over 1,500 in under three years [ 7 , 18 ] , with tracking repositories recording roughly three new arXiv submissions per day [ 30 ] . For a researcher who wants to use a recently published method, however, the path from paper to working code remains prohibitively slow. Existing infrastructure cannot keep pace with this growth. While over 40% of tracked 3DGS papers release no code [ 2 ] , others publish isolated forks with incompatible assumptions about attribute layouts, density control, rendering conventions [ 30 ] , and CUDA kernels. Building on a recent method typically demands weeks of manual reimplementation and debugging. Generic pipelines like Paper2Code [ 46 ] and AutoP2C [ 33 ] produce code that do not compile against any rasterization backend, and frontier models generate Python that collapses at training time for lack of domain knowledge about Gaussian attribute coupling or adaptive density control. No existing paper-to-code system, in any domain, handles 3DGS’s fork-dominated landscape, provides Gaussian-level structural verification, or moves beyond reproduction to compositional improvement. Even if reproduction were perfectly solved, the deeper bottleneck would persist: the knowledge across 2k+ papers is neither discoverable nor composable. There is no efficient way to determine whether combining a depth distortion loss from 2DGS [ 23 ] with stochastic splitting from 3DGS-MCMC [ 29 ] and an opacity regularizer from AbsGS [ 61 ] would outperform any of them individually. With hundreds of published techniques, the number of valid compositions is enormous, and manual exploration covers a negligible fraction. Entire application domains where Gaussians are a natural geometric fit, from optically thin nebulae to fluorescence microscopy, remain untouched because the engineering barrier is too high. The field needs infrastructure for discovering , composing , and extending the knowledge its papers contain. We introduce Splatify (Fig. ) , a multi-agent framework operating at three levels: faithful reproduction of 3DGS papers as trainable gsplat [ 60 ] plugins, compositional improvement that autonomously combines complementary techniques, and cross-domain method invention for unaddressed scene types. Its design rests on five contributions: 1. A context-free grammar for gsplat plugins over a modular template with nine extension points covering the full 3DGS modification space, ensuring generated code satisfies architectural invariants by construction (Sec. 3.2 ). 2. Faithful reproduction extending the multi-stage pipeline of [ 25 ] with fork-aware citation recovery at function-level granularity, Gaussian-specific smoke tests, and PSNR training-curve monitoring (Sec. 3.3 ). 3. Compositional improvement through autonomous diagnosis of weaknesses in reproduced methods and composition of complementary regularizers, losses, and densification strategies from the knowledge base, yielding up to + 2.4 dB PSNR gain with no human guidance (Sec. 3.4 ). 4. Cross-domain method invention , where agents retrieve physical priors from outside the 3DGS literature and synthesize physics-informed modifications for unaddressed domains such as volumetric nebula rendering and thin-film bubble iridescence (Sec. 3.4 ). 5. Splatify -Bench , a 30-paper benchmark stratified by code availability and technical complexity (Sec. 4.1 ). On papers without public code, Splatify matches expert implementations within \pm 0.5 dB PSNR and \pm 0.02 SSIM while existing baselines fail to produce trainable code. Cross-domain invention produces working methods for scene types no prior paper has addressed, entirely without human guidance. Splatify , Splatify-Bench , all implementations, and the code will be publicly released.
