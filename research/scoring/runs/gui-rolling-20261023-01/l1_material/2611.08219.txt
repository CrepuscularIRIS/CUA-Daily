title: Agent-R$^2$: Scaling Agents for Realistic Productivity Work via Real-Source Data Synthesis
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08219

§Abstract
Large language models have made rapid progress in agentic reasoning and tool use, raising expectations for agents that can take over realistic productivity work from humans. However, current agents still struggle to finish such tasks, where success requires following long-horizon professional workflows, understanding heterogeneous file contexts, and producing usable deliverables under fine-grained requirements. A key bottleneck is the lack of scalable post-training data that reflects real work intent, authentic file environments, and deliverable-level evaluation. To close this gap, we introduce Agent-R$^2$, a $\textbf{r}$eal-source data synthesis framework that scales training data for $\textbf{r}$ealistic productivity work. Instead of relying on manually specified workflows or LLM-generated files, Agent-R$^2$ grounds synthesis throughout the pipeline in real occupational seeds, corpus-derived workflow evidence, and authentic web-sourced files. From 10k web-corpus candidates, Agent-R$^2$ distills 4k task instances that pair real multi-format attachments with fine-grained, deliverable-level rubrics. On this data, we train Agent-R$^2$-4B via supervised fine-tuning and rubric-based reinforcement learning, improving both end-to-end productivity performance and file-grounded reasoning. Trained on only 4k instances, Agent-R$^2$-4B surpasses Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 on both GDPval (51.0$\%$ vs. 48.0$\%$) and OfficeQA (14.8$\%$ vs. 13.8$\%$). Further analysis shows that Agent-R$^2$ mitigates shortcut behavior, where agents produce superficially plausible deliverables before sufficiently grounding, constructing, or verifying them, while promoting sustained exploration.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Large language models have made remarkable progress in agentic reasoning and tool use, enabling agents to plan, interact with environments, and solve increasingly open-ended tasks ( Yao et al. 2023 ; Schick et al. 2023 ; Zhou et al. 2024 ) . These advances raise the expectation that agents can take over realistic productivity work from humans. Such work is economically valuable but demanding : it involves long-horizon professional workflows, where agents must understand professional contexts, gather and integrate evidence from heterogeneous digital artifacts, invoke the necessary tools, and ultimately deliver usable work products such as analytical reports, spreadsheets, presentations, and structured documents. However, current agents still fall short on such work. First, real professional workflows involve interdependent steps, implicit domain conventions, and evolving intermediate states, so models miss critical operations or fail to adapt based on feedback. Second, real file contexts are heterogeneous and messy: models must integrate evidence across artifacts with different formats, layouts, and levels of completeness, and often misread content or overlook cross-file dependencies. Third, real deliverables must satisfy fine-grained requirements over factual consistency, evidence coverage, structure, formatting, and practical usability; producing an output file is not the same as meeting deliverable standards. Mastering all three demands at once is precisely what current training data fails to teach. Existing training data targets these capabilities only in isolation: instruction following ( Xu et al. 2025a ; Guo et al. 2025 ) , multidisciplinary reasoning ( Liu et al. 2025 ) , document-grounded question answering ( Tanaka et al. 2023 ; Zhang et al. 2026a ) , or generation of a single deliverable type such as presentations and spreadsheets ( Zheng et al. 2025 ; Zheng et al. 2026 ; Chi et al. 2026 ) . While effective in their respective settings, these data sources do not provide end-to-end supervision that jointly covers real work objectives, cross-file evidence, professional workflow execution, and deliverable constraints. What is missing, we argue, is not more data but data that reflects real work: real intents, authentic files, and deliverable-level standards. Such data is hard to fabricate: manually authored workflows do not scale, and LLM-generated files lack the layouts, gaps, and cross-file references of real documents. To address this data bottleneck, we introduce Agent-R 2 , a real-source data synthesis framework that grounds every stage of synthesis in real sources through a three-stage pipeline. First, rather than relying on manually authored workflows as in recent benchmark construction ( Patwardhan et al. 2025 ; Chen et al. 2026 ) , Agent-R 2 mines file-oriented workflow blueprints from web corpora using occupational activity seeds, improving both realism and scalability. Second, it searches for and verifies public files that support each workflow, retaining the heterogeneous formats, layouts, incomplete structures, and cross-file dependencies of realistic digital work. Third, it synthesizes user instructions and fine-grained rubrics from the verified workflow-file contexts: the instructions require complex deliverables involving evidence gathering, reasoning, file operations, and artifact construction, while the rubrics specify detailed criteria for evidence grounding, analytical correctness, and deliverable quality. Starting from 10k web-corpus candidates, Agent-R 2 produces 4k high-quality training instances . We train Agent-R 2 -4B on the synthesized data using supervised fine-tuning followed by rubric-based reinforcement learning. The reinforcement learning stage optimizes the model in the persistent sandbox with outcome rewards: each generated deliverable is evaluated against its fine-grained rubric, which encourages the model to satisfy evidence, reasoning, and artifact-quality requirements rather than merely produce an output file. Trained on only 4k synthesized instances, Agent-R 2 -4B reaches 51.0% Pass@1 on the full GDPval set (Figure 1 ), improving the base model by 18.6 points and surpassing Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 (48.0%) and Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking (49.7%). The gains transfer to document-grounded reasoning on OfficeQA: Agent-R 2 -4B improves the base model from 2.2% to 14.8% Pass@1 and again surpasses Qwen3-235B-A22B-Thinking-2507 (13.8%). Behavioral analysis further shows that training on rubric-scored real-source data mitigates shortcut behavior , a general failure mode where agents emit superficially plausible deliverables before sufficiently grounding, constructing, or verifying them, while promoting sustained exploration.
