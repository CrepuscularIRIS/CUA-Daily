title: OmniBrain-0: A Unified Agentic Model for Embodied Intelligence
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36013

§Abstract
General-purpose embodied intelligence requires robots to coordinate diverse skills over long horizons, track task progress, and recover from failures. Specialist policies alone do not ensure task-level reliability, while powerful multimodal agents rely on costly reasoning and external experience that compact models struggle to reproduce. We introduce OmniBrain-0, a lightweight unified embodied agent that coordinates manipulation and navigation through a shared high-level decision model, integrating task understanding, planning, progress tracking, and feedback-driven recovery. Our progressive training framework combines embodied pretraining, domain-specialized agent distillation, and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD). It distills powerful teachers' interaction experience into manipulation and navigation experts, then consolidates their decision capabilities into OmniBrain-0. Unification therefore occurs within the learned decision model rather than only at the tool interface. We further introduce Rubric-Guided Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI), a data flywheel in which a Meta-Agent turns execution evidence into refined rubrics, step-level reminders, and verified trajectories for domain-expert updates before their reconsolidation into OmniBrain-0 through MOPD. We evaluate OmniBrain-0 on LIBERO-Pro, LIBERO-Plus, RoboCasa365, RoboTwin 2.0, and R2R-CE Val-Unseen, as well as multiple real-world manipulation tasks. OmniBrain-0 combines strong cross-domain task performance with substantially lower inference and execution overhead than its closed-source teacher, demonstrating efficient transfer and consolidation of embodied capabilities into a compact unified agent.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
General-purpose embodied intelligence requires diverse physical skills, long-horizon planning, skill composition, and closed-loop correction to fulfill task intent. Vision-language-action (VLA) models and vision-and-language navigation (VLN) policies advance manipulation and navigation [ 22 , 4 , 47 , 43 ] , but reliable individual skills do not guarantee task-level success. Under complex constraints, multi-stage dependencies, or distribution shifts, local action prediction may fail to maintain goal alignment, requiring feedback-driven reassessment and replanning [ 61 , 51 ] . Large-model-based embodied agents offer a promising approach by exposing perception, specialist policies, and motion controllers as tools. A high-level model interprets instructions, decomposes tasks, orchestrates skills, and uses execution feedback to track progress and recover from failures, while low-level policies retain their physical interaction expertise [ 61 , 28 , 16 ] . This division of labor enables goal- and feedback-driven execution, but reliable coordination also requires the high-level model to reason about skill applicability, capability boundaries, and handoff states [ 15 ] . These shared task-level requirements motivate a common high-level decision mechanism for manipulation and navigation, spanning task understanding, skill orchestration, and feedback-driven adaptation. However, successful task execution by powerful models does not by itself establish how their planning and recovery capabilities can be learned by a compact, unified agent. These models combine contextual reasoning, experience, and environmental feedback [ 61 , 15 ] , and compact agents must learn from this interaction experience how to decompose tasks, coordinate skills, track progress, and adapt to execution outcomes across domains. Repeated reasoning by powerful models during long-horizon interaction incurs computational overhead and decision latency, constraining deployment efficiency [ 9 , 33 , 32 , 29 , 27 ] . Distilling these capabilities into compact agents offers a way to improve deployment efficiency, but effective interaction data remain limited, particularly for states that require feedback-driven correction. Useful supervision must therefore preserve how tasks progress, how skills are coordinated, and how decisions change in response to execution feedback, including during recovery from failure. Alongside effective learning and consolidation methods, we therefore need a scalable data construction mechanism that preserves informative task progression and recovery while continually expanding coverage of execution challenges encountered during interaction [ 31 , 7 ] . These requirements motivate a unified learning framework that combines scalable interaction data construction with capability transfer and consolidation, enabling a compact agent to coordinate planning and recovery across manipulation and navigation. First, capability transfer aims to learn effective decision behaviors from teacher interactions rather than reproduce teachers’ language outputs or reasoning processes [ 19 ] . Because embodied decisions alter the environment and subsequent observations, students must learn to interpret execution feedback, track progress, and adapt when plans become invalid. Fixed demonstrations alone may not provide sufficient supervision for appropriate decisions in states encountered during autonomous execution [ 16 , 1 ] . Second, acquiring specialized capabilities and integrating them into a unified agent are distinct problems. Manipulation and navigation differ in scene distributions, skill interfaces, and task structures, so expertise learned by specialized planners must be further integrated for joint deployment [ 35 ] . Therefore, we introduce OmniBrain-0 , a lightweight, unified embodied agent for manipulation and navigation (Figure 2 ). OmniBrain-0 integrates specialist policies and tools through a shared skill-invocation framework. A compact high-level planner performs task understanding, long-horizon planning, skill orchestration, state tracking, and failure recovery. Conditioned on observations, instructions, and interaction history, it selects skills and adapts to execution feedback, preserving low-level specialization while learning task decomposition and adaptive correction. To realize these capability transformations, we develop a progressive training framework comprising shared embodied initialization, specialized capability acquisition, and cross-domain capability integration . First, we combine embodied, question-answering, and general language data with limited long-horizon interaction data to establish a shared initialization for a compact model. We then train manipulation and navigation planners from this initialization using quality-filtered interaction trajectories generated by multiple powerful models.
