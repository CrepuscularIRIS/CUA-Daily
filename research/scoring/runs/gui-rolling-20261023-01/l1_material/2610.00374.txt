title: Faithful Chart Generation for Multimodal Deep Research: Frame-Evidence Co-Adaptation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00374

§Abstract
Analytical charts in multimodal deep research encode quantitative claims, requiring every visualized value to be faithfully grounded in supporting evidence. Unlike retrieved images that mainly provide contextual information, charts require numerical fidelity: visualized values should not only match retrieved evidence quantitatively but also preserve its original meaning and scope. However, achieving such fidelity remains challenging because current systems usually construct visualization plans before knowing what quantitative evidence can actually be retrieved from the web. As a result, predefined plans may require entities, temporal ranges, or comparison dimensions that the retrieved evidence only partially supports. Existing approaches mainly address this issue through post-hoc verification after chart plans are fixed, enabling unsupported values to be identified but leaving the underlying visual frames unchanged. To address this challenge, we propose Frame-Evidence Co-Adaptation (FECA), an evidence-adaptive visual planning framework for multimodal deep research. Inspired by the bidirectional sensemaking process in Data-Frame Theory, FECA models chart generation as an iterative interaction between visual frames and retrieved evidence. Each visual frame is adaptive: the frame guides evidence acquisition, while retrieved evidence determines whether the frame should be accepted, revised, or dropped before rendering. By coupling visualization planning with evidence availability, FECA shifts chart generation from fixed-plan verification to adaptive evidence-grounded visual reasoning. Experiments on 100 real-world research topics show that FECA substantially improves numerical fidelity while preserving report quality and chart utility.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Deep research systems ( Zheng et al., 2025 ; Chen et al., 2025b ; Ye et al., 2026 ; Chen et al., 2025a ) aim to automate end-to-end knowledge discovery by combining web search ( Jin et al., 2025 ; Wu et al., 2026 ; Yao et al., 2026 ) , reasoning ( Li et al., 2025b ) , and information synthesis ( Asai et al., 2026 ) to produce long-form reports with supporting evidence. Early systems mainly focused on textual research reports, improving evidence retrieval, information organization, and citation-aware generation ( Shao et al., 2024 ; Li et al., 2025c ; Li et al., 2026 ) . Recent multimodal deep research systems extend this capability by automatically generating analytical charts that summarize quantitative information collected from the web ( Yang et al., 2026 ; Tian et al., 2026 ; Ma et al., 2026 ) . Unlike retrieved images that provide illustrative context, charts function as structured visual evidence: each plotted value implicitly makes a quantitative claim that supports the analytical conclusions of the report. Thus, trustworthy multimodal deep research requires not only textual correctness but also reliable grounding of generated visual evidence. Numerical fidelity: A missing dimension of visual trustworthiness. Existing evaluations of deep research systems primarily focus on textual report quality and reliability, including information coverage, factuality, and citation correctness ( Li et al., 2025a ; Du et al., 2025 ; Sharma et al., 2026 ) . Recent multimodal extensions further consider visual quality and chart–text consistency ( Yang et al., 2026 ; Tian et al., 2026 ; Ma et al., 2026 ) . However, charts introduce a unique demand of factuality beyond textual claims: every visualized value corresponds to an implicit numerical assertion that should be traceable to supporting evidence. We define this property as numerical fidelity : every value in a generated chart should be grounded in retrieved quantitative evidence while preserving the meaning and scope of its source. Despite its importance for trustworthy visual evidence ( Akhtar et al., 2024 ; Huang et al., 2024 ) , numerical fidelity remains largely unexplored in multimodal deep research. The limitation of fixed visualization planning. Current multimodal deep research systems typically follow a plan–then–check paradigm for chart generation ( Yang et al., 2026 ; Ma et al., 2026 ; Tian et al., 2026 ) . As illustrated in Figure 1 (a), they first instantiate visualization intent as a fixed visual frame, retrieve information required by the predefined frame, and subsequently check whether the retrieved evidence or generated output satisfies that frame. This paradigm implicitly assumes that a visual frame can be determined before evidence acquisition. However, web research is inherently uncertain: retrieved evidence may only partially cover the entities, temporal ranges, or comparison dimensions required by a predefined visual frame. When such a mismatch occurs, post-hoc verification can revise incorrect values using the available evidence or reject unsupported charts, but cannot adapt the visual frame to the evidence that is actually available. Diagnosing plan–evidence mismatches. To understand this limitation, we analyze a representative plan–then–check pipeline in Section 4.1 . We find that 80.8% of its plotted values remain unsupported by the routed evidence after verification. Standard verification achieves only a limited reduction in the unsupported-value rate. We further construct a stricter verifier that retains only charts with fully supported values. Although verification improves numerical fidelity, it removes more than three quarters of the generated charts, substantially reducing the visual utility of the resulting reports. These results reveal a fundamental limitation of verification-based approaches: when a visual frame conflicts with available evidence, stricter checking can only filter invalid outputs rather than discover evidence-supported alternatives. This leads to a key research question: How can multimodal deep research systems generate trustworthy visual evidence when retrieved evidence does not fully satisfy the initial visual frame? Our method: Frame–evidence co-adaptation. We argue that trustworthy chart generation requires a paradigm shift from verifying fixed visual frames to adapting them according to the evidence actually available. As illustrated in Figure 1 (b), we propose Frame–Evidence Co-Adaptation (FECA), in which the current visual frame guides evidence acquisition, while retrieved evidence can in turn accept, revise, or drop the frame before chart generation. Inspired by the bidirectional sensemaking process in Data–Frame Theory ( Klein et al., 2007 ) , FECA realizes this interaction as an iterative frame–evidence co-adaptation process.
