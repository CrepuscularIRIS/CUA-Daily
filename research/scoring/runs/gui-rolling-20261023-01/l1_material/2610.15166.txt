title: ExeWorldBench: Benchmarking Code-Based Executable 3D World Modeling
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15166

§Abstract
Coding agents are increasingly evolving beyond reproducing what a world looks like toward constructing executable worlds that capture how a world works. This shift calls for executable world modeling, which integrates two core capabilities: world understanding, reasoning about the underlying structural, mechanical, and causal principles of a world, and executable realization, translating that understanding into a code-based system whose behavior emerges from physical interactions. However, existing benchmarks provide only fragmented evaluations centered on physical reasoning, visual fidelity, or behavioral correctness, leaving the integration of these capabilities insufficiently assessed. To bridge this gap, we introduce ExeWorldBench, to our knowledge the first benchmark to comprehensively evaluate world understanding and executable realization through code-based 3D world construction. ExeWorldBench comprises three progressively challenging tasks: Mechanism Executability, which evaluates the realization of individual mechanisms from visual observations; Compositional Executability, which extends to multi-stage systems with spatial, mechanical, and causal dependencies; and Intent-Driven Executable World Synthesis, which requires constructing executable worlds from high-level goals and physical requirements. Extensive experiments on frontier models reveal substantial limitations in executable world modeling, offering insights into the development of more physically grounded and causally coherent world construction systems.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Coding agents are evolving beyond their role as general-purpose programming tools toward constructing worlds. Conventional visual-to-code generation ( Zhang et al., 2026c ; Zhang et al., 2026b ; Deng et al., 2026 ; Jiang et al., 2025 ) primarily focuses on reproducing what a world looks like , translating visual observations into code that reconstructs the appearance, layout, and geometric structure of the reference. However, constructing an executable world goes beyond visual fidelity, requiring not only a faithful representation of its structure but also the physical mechanisms and causal interactions that govern how the world works . This raises a fundamental question: Can current models construct not only what a world looks like, but also how it works? Constructing such worlds requires two tightly coupled capabilities. The first is world understanding , which involves reasoning about the latent structural, mechanical, and causal principles that govern how a world is organized and evolves. The second is executable realization , which requires translating this understanding into an executable code-based system. Crucially, these capabilities cannot be considered in isolation: understanding how a world works does not guarantee that it can be faithfully realized, while generating runnable code does not necessarily indicate that the underlying world has been correctly understood. Existing benchmarks provide only partial evidence of this integrated capability. On one side, reasoning-centric benchmarks ( Chow et al., 2025 ; Zhou et al., 2026 ; Li et al., 2024 ; Peng et al., 2026 ) typically evaluate world understanding through physical reasoning tasks formulated as question answering over images or videos, testing whether models can infer physical properties, relations, or outcomes without requiring them to realize such understanding in an executable world. Such evaluations can reveal whether a model understands aspects of physical behavior, but not whether this understanding can be effectively integrated into executable construction. On the other side, construction-centric benchmarks ( Gao et al., 2026 ; Noor et al., 2026 ; Zhang et al., 2026a ; Wu et al., 2025 ; Si et al., 2025 ) primarily evaluate whether models can translate visual or textual specifications into valid code that reproduces the intended visual or geometric structure, with evaluation largely centered on appearance, geometry, and static structural fidelity. They therefore provide limited evidence about whether the structure and principles inferred from a world are faithfully translated into its executable realization. Behavior-centric benchmarks ( Xie et al., 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026c ; Lu et al., 2026 ; Liang et al., 2026 ; Kovačič and Ellis, 2026 ) often evaluate behavioral correctness , such as whether objects follow expected trajectories, reach specified states, or reproduce observed or intended dynamics. However, these behaviors are often relatively simple or isolated, such as falling, sliding, or single-object motion, whereas executable world modeling may involve coordinated interactions among multiple components, introducing richer spatial, mechanical, and causal dependencies. Additionally, while behavioral criteria provide useful evidence of dynamic correctness, they offer only a partial assessment of the model’s causal understanding. In simple or unidirectional motions, plausible trajectories can sometimes be reproduced through interpolation between observed states or predefined animations, without faithfully implementing the underlying physical mechanisms. Thus, reproducing how a world moves does not necessarily demonstrate that the model can construct an executable world that reflects how the world works . Beyond the evaluation target itself, some existing benchmarks operate in simplified physical or representational domains ( Kovačič and Ellis, 2026 ; Wang et al., 2026c ; Lu et al., 2026 ) . In particular, 2D simulations and browser-native environments abstract away several challenges that become central in executable 3D worlds, including spatial arrangement, depth-dependent contacts, collision geometry, mechanical assembly, and joint alignment. Therefore, a comprehensive evaluation of executable world modeling should go beyond isolated behaviors and simplified environments to assess how models integrate the understanding of complex physical worlds with executable realization. To address these limitations, we introduce ExeWorldBench , a benchmark for executable 3D world modeling that progressively increases both world complexity and model autonomy. It systematically probes the transition from observation-grounded realization to autonomous world construction, gradually reducing the guidance available for determining how a world should be built.
