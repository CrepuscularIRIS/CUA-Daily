title: RoboArchitect: Automating the Design of Robotic Agents with Persistent Memory
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42141

§Abstract
Recent work shows that a frozen robot policy performs far better inside an agent harness, where control code decides when to query the policy, consult vision tools, or command the arm directly. Yet today these harnesses are built by hand and redesigned for each new robot or benchmark. We show that harness design can be automated, offering a strong alternative to finetuning on a novel setup. In RoboArchitect, a coding model observes a robot's rollouts, diagnoses failures, and iteratively revises the harness around the frozen policy. The harness evolves on a set of episodes for its optimization and is evaluated on held-out scenes and tasks. Without updating any model weights, RoboArchitect raises average success from 9.7% to 43.3% on unseen scenes (4.5x) and from 12.7% to 40.2% on unseen tasks (3.2x), across multiple benchmarks and policies. The gains are largest where the benchmark is far from the policy's training data: wrapping a general-purpose policy, the evolved harness yields a specialist that often outperforms even the finetuned version. Intuitive control strategies are automatically designed, such as lifting a jammed arm clear or reaching in horizontally when an obstacle blocks the way from above. The harness also evolves its own persistent memory, carrying learned experience, such as effective grasp depths, into later decisions. These results suggest that substantial headroom in robotic manipulation lies in the agent harness, and its design can be effectively automated and optimized without weight updates.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Language models have become far more useful inside an agent harness: surrounding code that mediates when and how to call the model, what context to supply, and how to handle its output. Robot policies are undergoing the same shift. This shift is particularly important for robotics, where adapting a policy to new tasks or hardware often requires collecting new demonstrations and finetuning model weights ( Kim et al., 2024 ; Zang et al., 2025 ; Park et al., 2026 ) . We posit that harness optimization offers an effective alternative to finetuning, turning a general policy into a specialist for a specific robot setup ( Section 4.2 ). Recent agentic robot systems like VoLo ( Chen et al., 2026d ) and Harness VLA ( Zhang et al., 2026e ) show strong gains from wrapping frozen policies in hand-written harnesses ( Xu et al., 2026 ; Zhao et al., 2026b ; Gao et al., 2026 ; Hu et al., 2026 ) . Today, these harnesses are still manually designed, often requiring substantial engineering effort for each task and embodiment. Building such a harness follows a familiar trial-and-error loop: run the agent on the robot, observe where it fails, diagnose the cause, modify the control code, and test again. This work is slow to perform by hand, and long recordings of robot rollouts are difficult for a human engineer to inspect exhaustively. We ask whether this complete engineering loop, from diagnosing failure traces to refining the harness design to fix them, can be automated . In this work, RoboArchitect uses a coding model to automatically design and optimize the harness ( Section 3 ). Given access to a frozen policy, auxiliary vision and language tools, and step-by-step rollout recordings, the coding model diagnoses failure modes and proposes an updated harness to address them. Like a finetuned policy, a harness optimized on specific rollouts could overfit to them. We therefore split each benchmark into “training” (for harness optimization) and held-out episodes, evaluating on unseen scenes , where tasks are shared but scene arrangements differ, and unseen tasks , where held-out episodes contain entirely different tasks that require shared manipulation skills ( Section 4.1 ). Across diverse benchmarks and policies, our auto-optimized harnesses raise the average success rate on unseen scenes from 9.7% to 43.3% (4.5 \times ), and on unseen tasks from 12.7% to 40.2% (3.2 \times ) ( Fig. 1 ). The strongest gains occur when policies are deployed in out-of-domain settings, where RoboArchitect can effectively substitute for new training data: wrapping a general policy yields a specialist that often outperforms the finetuned version of the same model. For example, in settings where the vision-language-action (VLA) policy contributes no useful signal, the harness learns to stop querying it and instead drives the arm from its own perception, effectively becoming a code-as-policy agent. While code-as-policy ( Liang et al., 2023 ; Lu et al., 2026 ; Elmaaroufi et al., 2026 ) and learned VLA policies ( Kim et al., 2024 ; Intelligence et al., 2025 ) have traditionally been treated as distinct paradigms, RoboArchitect unifies them empirically ( Section 4.2 ). Unlike weight updates, each harness revision is also fully interpretable: it targets specific failure modes with intuitive control logic ( Figs. 3 and 6 ), making subsequent revisions easy to inspect and build upon, and such iterative refinement proves to be key to generalization ( Section 4.2 ). We also equip the harness with persistent memory , allowing experience to accumulate across episodes. No schema or memory rules are specified; RoboArchitect is entirely free to decide what to store, in what form, and how to retrieve it. As a result, diverse forms of memories emerge from the harness optimization to record key pragmatic clues, such as effective grasp depths that inform later control decisions. As RoboArchitect proves highly effective in solving tasks on one robot embodiment at a time, we further use its harness evolution to build a unified, generalizable agent that operates across different robot platforms ( Section 4.2 ). In this single harness optimized on a training pool drawn from diverse benchmarks, shared control strategies and memory structures emerge naturally across embodiments. Our findings suggest that substantial performance headroom in robotic manipulation lies in the agent harness, as an alternative to finetuning. RoboArchitect automates its design and optimization, providing a practical way to adapt robot policies to new environments without updating weights.
