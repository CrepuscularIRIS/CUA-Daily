title: MAPLE: A Multimodal Mobile Agent with Playbook-Learned Experience
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01351

§Abstract
Autonomous agents powered by vision-language models (VLMs) show great potential in navigating mobile graphical user interfaces (GUIs) to complete complex tasks. However, existing paradigms face fundamental scalability bottlenecks: fine-tuning requires massive high-fidelity datasets or prohibitive training time and compute, while gradient-free approaches rely on custom static heuristics or costly trial-and-error trajectory exploration where feedback is deferred until the end of execution. To address these limitations, we introduce MAPLE (Multimodal Agent with Playbook-Learned Experience), a novel gradient-free multi-agent framework that enables dynamic, step-wise experience sharing and execution-time prompt evolution. Unlike traditional action-reflection architectures that depend on costly, post-hoc self-reflection, MAPLE enables agents to cross-reference and distribute runtime insights in real time at each operational step. This dynamic exchange eliminates blind trial-and-error looping and steers agents along direct, grounded interaction paths. Extensive evaluations on the AndroidWorld and MobileWorld benchmarks demonstrate that MAPLE achieves state-of-the-art performance on complex, long-horizon mobile tasks, while proving scalable and adaptable across novel environments.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Despite significant advancements in intuitive interface design, mobile devices remain challenging or inaccessible to large segments of the population, particularly the elderly and individuals with visual impairments. With the rapid evolution of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) [ 3 ] , GUI-based autonomous agents have emerged as a transformative paradigm to bridge this accessibility gap and democratize access to essential modern technology. By empowering users to delegate complex, multi-application tasks via natural language, these agents hold immense potential to transform digital autonomy. Consequently, substantial research has focused on deploying foundation models as interactive reasoning engines across mobile platforms [ 23 , 4 , 22 , 6 , 7 ] , accompanied by comprehensive benchmarks [ 14 , 8 , 19 ] designed to evaluate the frontiers of agentic capabilities. However, developing autonomous agents for mobile UI navigation requires balancing generalizability, online interaction overhead, and offline data curation costs. Existing paradigms struggle to strike this balance: gradient-based reinforcement learning requires prohibitively expensive environment rollouts, while zero-shot inference frameworks remain handicapped by static prompts or brittle, text-bound memory structures that fail to capture the visual nuances of dynamic mobile interfaces. In opaque, highly dynamic mobile GUIs, purely memorizing procedural step sequences is insufficient; an agent must dynamically reverse-engineer interface mechanics through visual transitions. To bridge this fundamental gap, we introduce the Multimodal Agent with Playbook-Learned Experience ( MAPLE ). Designed as a cold-start, continual learning system, MAPLE adapts the principles of step-level reflection and cross-episodic playbooks specifically for the multimodal mobile domain. Rather than relying on massive pre-curated datasets or text-only procedural memory, MAPLE leverages explicit visual state transitions (e.g., before-and-after screenshots) to autonomously distill declarative UI rules on the fly. By decoupling intra-episodic state tracking from cross-episodic knowledge accumulation and grounding retrieved priors in the active visual interface, MAPLE achieves robust, cold-start generalization across unseen mobile applications. In summary, the primary contributions of this work are three-fold: • Dual-Memory Cognitive Architecture: We introduce MAPLE, a cold-start, gradient-free mobile agent architecture built around two complementary memory streams: (1) a bounded Working Memory instantiated as a dynamic key-value store that tracks transient, within-episode task state (e.g., extracted data and sub-goal checklists), and (2) a persistent Playbook that accumulates reusable, declarative UI mechanics across episodes. Coupled within a factorized step-level reasoning loop, these two memories coordinate short-term task execution with long-term interface priors without offline datasets or task-specific heuristics. • Self-Supervised Hindsight Distillation & Grounded Retrieval: We design a closed-loop mechanism to populate and query the Playbook entirely on the fly. By comparing post-action screenshot transitions against predicted visual outcomes, MAPLE autonomously distills and deduplicates universal action-effect facts, then retrieves them via intent masking and greedy visual anchor matching so injected guidance stays anchored to the active screen layout. • Extensive Evaluation & State-of-the-Art Performance: We evaluate MAPLE on two open-domain mobile benchmarks ( AndroidWorld [ 14 ] and MobileWorld [ 8 ] ) across six Vision-Language Models spanning open-weight to frontier scales. Powered by Gemini-3-Flash, MAPLE reaches 75.9% Success Rate on AndroidWorld and 60.25% on MobileWorld (including a record 68.18% on interactive tasks), demonstrating superior navigational directness and strong cross-model generalization.
