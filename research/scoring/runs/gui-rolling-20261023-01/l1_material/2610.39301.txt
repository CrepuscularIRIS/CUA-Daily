title: Youtu-Parsing-Omni: One Encoder, One Schema, All Modalities
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39301

§Abstract
We present Youtu-Parsing-Omni, an omni-modal parser that converts documents, images, audio, and video into structured records grounded in space and time, providing a perception layer for agents that read, locate, and cite evidence across modalities. Its design rests on one encoder for every modality and one schema for every task. Youtu-Omni-Encoder, initialized from a pre-trained language model to strengthen text reading, encodes all modalities with shared weights in a unified space-time coordinate system, and a few fusion layers let video frames attend to concurrent audio, bringing cross-modal fusion and temporal alignment into perception itself. OmniSchema unifies the outputs of all ten parsing tasks, allowing a single self-evolving post-training procedure, OmniSchema-Aware On-Policy Distillation (OSAD), to provide a shared token-role-based objective across tasks. In each round, the model is trained on its own rollouts against token-level targets from a reference-conditioned copy of itself, which is then refreshed with the trained weights, so that the parser and its supervision co-evolve. With a single 5 B-parameter checkpoint, Youtu-Parsing-Omni achieves state-of-the-art performance on OmniDocBench v1.6 (96.96 Overall) and leads all open-weight models on OmniParsingBench (75.08 Avg.), with its advantage concentrated on audio and video. It also achieves the best overall chart-parsing score (95.02) and the highest exact chemical-fingerprint agreement among compared systems, and matches a dedicated optical-music model in character-level transcription of typeset sheet music while staying far ahead of general-purpose systems. Code and model weights are publicly available.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Large language model (LLM) agents increasingly act on information beyond their own parameters: they retrieve evidence before answering [ 1 ] , call tools during reasoning [ 2 ] , and operate computers through the screen [ 3 ] . Much of this information arrives not as plain text but as documents, images, audio, and video. Having the model that drives the agent consume such inputs directly is both costly and hard to audit : each time the agent revisits the material, that model must re-encode long sequences of visual and acoustic tokens, and an answer drawn from raw pixels or waveforms does not by itself indicate which region or interval supports it. Omni-modal parsing addresses both issues by converting each input once into a structured text record of what it contains and where or when each part occurs. Any LLM, including a text-only one, can then read and act on this record and trace a claim back to its source through the anchors it cites; parsing thus provides a perception layer between non-text inputs and language-model reasoning. Current parsers, however, cover this range of inputs only partially. Document parsers have become strong on standard pages [ 4 , 5 , 6 ] , but symbolic content such as chemical structures and sheet music is still left largely to domain-specific models [ 7 , 8 ] . Beyond documents, parsing is even less mature. Omni-modal models are built mainly for captioning and question answering rather than for grounded, structured output [ 9 , 10 ] , and parsing across images, audio, and video has only recently been formulated as a task with a dedicated benchmark [ 11 ] . As a result, different input types are typically served by different models with different output formats. Yet the target output is the same across input types, namely a structured record whose units are anchored in space or time, which suggests building a single parser with one output schema rather than one specialist per input type. Building such a parser raises two challenges. (C1) Joint perception across modalities. Omni-modal models [ 10 , 12 ] and the omni-modal parser Logics-Parsing-Omni [ 11 ] pair two separately pre-trained encoders, one for vision and one for audio, an arrangement with three consequences. First, each modality is encoded in isolation: no cross-modal fusion takes place during perception, and the language model must infer cross-modal relations from separate representations. Second, the two encoders index time independently, so a frame and the sound of the same instant do not share temporal positions within perception; temporal alignment, where modeled, is deferred to the language model [ 9 ] . Third, each encoder requires its own architecture, training objective, and pre-training, so perception parameters are not shared across modalities and each new modality requires another tower. The first two consequences weigh more on parsing than on captioning or question answering, because a structured parse must state explicitly which content co-occurs across modalities and when. Encoders shared across vision and audio have been explored [ 13 , 14 , 15 ] , but, to our knowledge, not for grounded parsing. (C2) Joint optimization across tasks. The same model must also be optimized jointly for content accuracy, spatial and temporal grounding, and schema validity across ten tasks whose output fields differ. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) trains only on reference prefixes, never on the prefixes the model itself produces at inference [ 16 , 17 ] . Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), including Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) [ 18 ] , is increasingly used in document parsing [ 19 , 20 , 6 ] ; it trains on the model’s own outputs but requires a scalar reward that is hard to define across such heterogeneous criteria and provides only sequence-level feedback. On-policy distillation supplies token-level targets on the model’s own outputs using accessible teacher distributions [ 21 ] . Reference-conditioned self-distillation can instead obtain these targets from the same model under an enriched context [ 22 ] ; the remaining question is how to adapt this supervision to heterogeneous schema roles and refresh it as the parser learns. To address both challenges, we present Youtu-Parsing-Omni, an omni-modal parser built on one encoder for every modality and one schema for every task (Fig. 2 ). Only lightweight input stems and output projectors remain modality-specific, so almost all perception parameters are shared across modalities.
