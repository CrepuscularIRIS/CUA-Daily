title: S-Agent 2.0: Spatial Reasoning with a General Coding Agent
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28354

§Abstract
Spatial reasoning asks a model to work out where objects are in 3D space, how they relate to one another, and how they move. Agentic VLMs have recently brought large improvements on such tasks by letting the model actively gather evidence with 3D perception tools instead of answering in a single pass. Even so, on many questions the tools return the right evidence and the model still does not arrive at the correct answer: the gap between model and tools, where raw readings become usable conclusions, is left unfilled. We present S-Agent 2.0, a spatial reasoning agent that equips an off-the-shelf general coding agent with a command-line perception toolkit, and we design the interface around one principle: help the VLM close the evidence loop: acquire scene evidence, remember it in a persistent scene memory, and interpret each reading in plain spatial language. This design keeps the full flexibility of a coding agent, and every reading it acts on stays grounded in the tools' geometry. On MMSI-Bench and ReVSI, the toolkit improves a strong open-source model, Qwen3.8-27B, by +13.9 and +20.5 points over the same agent without tools, and a strong closed-source model, Gemini-3.8-Flash, by +11.2 and +9.6 points. With the open backbone S-Agent 2.0 reaches 66.5 on MMSI-Bench and 82.3 on ReVSI, ahead of the specialized system SpatialClaw on the same backbone (62.6 and 74.5), and with Gemini-3.8-Flash it reaches 74.1 and 90.5 against its 67.0 and 79.7. Both tool gains shrink as the backbone grows more capable, but SpatialClaw's shrinks far more on ReVSI, from +13.2 to +2.7, while ours stays at +9.6. S-Agent 2.0 also works across different general coding agent harnesses, such as Codex and Claude Code.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Spatial intelligence, the ability to understand where things are, how they relate in three dimensions, and how the world would look as one moves through it, is foundational to visual perception [ 6 , 40 ] and essential for any agent that operates in the physical world [ 12 , 2 , 30 , 15 , 3 ] . Agentic VLMs such as S-Agent [ 11 ] have recently brought large improvements here: instead of answering in a single pass, the model actively gathers evidence with 3D perception tools while it reasons [ 23 , 7 ] . More recent work such as SpatialClaw [ 10 ] responds with a specialized harness: a custom state machine that drives the VLM through a hand-designed loop (plan, act, reflect) over a persistent code kernel with a library of 3D perception and geometry utilities, leveraging the flexibility of code to bridge the gap. Even so, on many questions the tools return the right evidence and the model still does not arrive at the correct answer (see Figure 4 ). Watching such a system work reveals where it actually fails. A spatial answer is grounded only when a complete evidence loop closes: acquire the measurement, remember it while the rest of the evidence is gathered, and interpret the reading into understanding. Specialized systems invest almost entirely in acquisition, in the form of ever richer perception and geometry libraries, and leave the other two steps to the model. In our analysis of SpatialClaw trajectories, acquisition is rarely what breaks. The failures come in two forms. In the first, the model misreads a reading it holds: it flips the sign of a rotation it measured correctly, or reads a correct number in the wrong coordinate frame or the wrong sense. In the second, the evidence never reaches the answer at all: the measurement sits in the workspace while the model eyeballs the pictures, or a correct reading is discarded for an unrelated stated reason. The second form exposes a break between collecting evidence and drawing the conclusion. Once the readings are in, the reasoning that produces the answer runs detached from them, and the model falls back on its own perception of the scene. On the surface only interpretation goes wrong; underneath, the evidence that was acquired is never fully used. Closing the loop therefore places a requirement on each step. Acquisition has to combine easily with the VLM’s own perception, so that what the model recognizes and what the tools measure enter the same scene. Acquired evidence needs a memory that holds it without effort, so that it stays within reach until the answer is written. And interpretation needs spatial language, so that a reading arrives in the terms the question is asked in. 1.1 S-Agent 2.0 S-Agent 2.0 is our answer to those requirements: a spatial reasoning agent assembled from a stock general-purpose coding agent [ 25 , 1 , 36 ] and a command-line perception toolkit designed to meet them. General-purpose coding agents (the harnesses behind modern software-engineering agents) already provide the scaffolding a spatial reasoner needs (a shell, a file system, code execution, long-horizon context management, a mature tool-calling loop), and two of their habits bear directly on the requirements above. First, looking at a frame or at a segmentation mask is an ordinary file view in the agent’s stream, so the model’s own look at the scene and the tools’ readouts interleave in one conversation, with no separate image channel between them. [ 41 ] Second, a coding agent inspects and revises by reflex: it views an output, corrects what is wrong, and runs again. [ 41 , 16 , 29 ] Applied to a scene, that reflex is what joins the two kinds of perception, since the model’s own perception is checked against what the tools registered before any reading is taken. What these agents lack is perception itself; we keep the harness stock and add it as commands. The design effort goes into the toolkit, which meets the three requirements one by one. Concretely, (i) acquisition combines two kinds of perception, the tools’ measurements and the model’s own: the model can register what it perceives or imagines as instances in the scene and drop what the tools got wrong, so both kinds land on one geometric footing; (ii) everything measured persists in a scene memory that later steps query by name instead of re-perceiving, so acquired evidence stays within reach until the answer is written; and (iii) every measurement comes back as an answer-ready reading stated in plain spatial language with its interpretation attached.
