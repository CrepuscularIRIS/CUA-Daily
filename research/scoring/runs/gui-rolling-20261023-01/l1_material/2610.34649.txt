title: RobotAgentBench: Towards Open-world Robot Autonomy
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34649

§Abstract
Recent advances in vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) have significantly improved robotic manipulation, yet deploying robots in the open world demands a higher level of autonomy beyond executing predefined tasks. Robot agents offer a promising paradigm for this transition, integrating task discovery, contextual reasoning, physical execution, and adaptive recovery into a closed interaction loop. However, existing benchmarks largely evaluate isolated capabilities, failing to capture the complexity of open-world robot autonomy. We introduce RobotAgentBench (RAB), a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating robot agents in realistic, open-world household environments. Through an agent-assisted, human-in-the-loop real-to-sim pipeline, RAB constructs 50 diverse household layouts with interactive objects and physically grounded tasks, encompassing 90 atomic tasks, 18 short-horizon tasks, 8 long-horizon tasks, and 4 open-ended scenarios. RAB provides a multi-level evaluation of robot agent capabilities: (1) task-level assessments systematically examine five complementary dimensions: task discovery, context reasoning, manipulation, failure recovery, and robustness; (2) scenario-level evaluation assesses how agents integrate these capabilities to autonomously fulfill high-level user needs. Furthermore, RAB enables systematic comparisons of standalone VLA/WAM models and diverse robot agent architectures under a unified planning-execution-verification framework. Extensive experiments reveal substantial gaps between manipulation and autonomy, demonstrate the advantages of agentic architectures and recursive self-improvement, and highlight the remaining challenges toward open-world robot autonomy.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Embodied AI has advanced rapidly in recent years, with vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAMs) demonstrating increasingly strong manipulation capabilities across diverse tasks and environments ( Intelligence et al., 2025 ; Ye et al., 2026 ; Team et al., 2026b ; Wu et al., 2026b ; Cai et al., 2026 ; Xu et al., 2026 ; Shao et al., 2026 ) . These advances raise a fundamental question: How far are we from deploying robots in the open world? Unlike predefined manipulation tasks, the real world involves numerous mundane yet complex challenges, often characterized by ambiguity, uncertainty, and constantly changing circumstances ( Liu et al., 2025 ; Pchelintsev et al., 2025 ; Gong et al., 2026 ) . Consider a household robot preparing for guests: it must identify what needs to be cleaned, select appropriate food based on user preferences, coordinate multiple activities, and recover when manipulation attempts fail ( Mao et al., 2026 ) . Such challenges are not exceptional, but intrinsic to everyday physical environments. Addressing them is essential for transforming robots into broadly useful assistants in human environments. Operating in the open world demands a transition from robot manipulation to robot autonomy . Beyond executing explicit instructions, robots must autonomously discover relevant tasks, reason about environmental and user context, plan physical interactions, and adapt to unexpected outcomes. Robot agents provide a natural framework for this transition, integrating high-level reasoning and planning with physical execution and feedback-driven verification into a closed loop ( Team et al., 2026a ; Shi et al., 2025 ; Ahn et al., 2022 ; Huang et al., 2022 ) . Such agents must determine not only how to execute, but also what to do and how to adapt. Meanwhile, advances in multimodal foundation models, including GPT-6-Astra, and emerging agentic embodied systems ( Team et al., 2025 ; Yang et al., 2025b ; Bjorck et al., 2026 ; Chen et al., 2026b ) are creating new opportunities to realize this integration. Together, these developments suggest that embodied AI is entering a new stage, shifting from standalone manipulation models toward autonomous robot agents capable of handling complex, dynamic physical environments. However, existing benchmarks remain largely designed for robot manipulation rather than autonomy. Widely adopted benchmarks ( James et al., 2020 ; Yu et al., 2020 ; Gu et al., 2023 ; Liu et al., 2023 ; Nasiriany et al., 2024 ; Mu et al., 2024 ; Chen et al., 2025 ) primarily evaluate predefined tasks with explicit goals, overlooking autonomous task discovery and context-dependent decision-making. Recent efforts extend evaluation toward reasoning ( Chen et al., 2026a ) and long-horizon planning ( Shridhar et al., 2020 ; Shridhar et al., 2021 ; Kim et al., 2024 ; Yang et al., 2025a ; Mao et al., 2025 ) , but often rely on stylized problems or abstracted physical interactions. More importantly, reasoning, planning, and manipulation are frequently evaluated in isolation, without capturing their interdependence during real-world operation. A robot capable of individual tasks may still struggle to determine what needs to be done, recover from failures, or adapt to human interventions. Evaluating open-world robot autonomy therefore requires moving beyond isolated capabilities toward a comprehensive assessment of the entire autonomous interaction loop. To bridge this gap, we introduce RobotAgentBench (RAB), a benchmark for evaluating open-world robot autonomy in realistic household environments. We develop an agent-assisted, human-in-the-loop real-to-sim pipeline to reconstruct real-world scenes and expand them into 50 diverse layouts with interactive objects and physically grounded tasks. At its core, RAB provides 90 atomic tasks that can be flexibly composed into a wide range of household tasks, from which we construct 18 short-horizon and 8 long-horizon manipulation tasks as the foundation of our evaluation. Building upon these tasks, we introduce diverse variants and evaluation settings to systematically assess five complementary dimensions of robot autonomy: (1) task discovery, identifying what needs to be done; (2) context reasoning, interpreting implicit requirements; (3) manipulation, executing physical actions; (4) failure recovery, detecting and correcting execution errors; and (5) robustness, adapting to environmental disturbances. Beyond these task-level assessments, we introduce four open-ended household scenarios that require robot agents to integrate these capabilities to autonomously fulfill high-level user needs, enabling an end-to-end evaluation of open-world robot autonomy.
