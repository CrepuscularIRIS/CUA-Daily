arxiv: 2610.06898 (2610.06898v1)
title: DIBench: Benchmarking Decision Integrity of GUI-based Mobile Agents Under Deceptive Injections
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06898

§Abstract
As GUI-based mobile agents rapidly progress, rigorous safety evaluation of their autonomous decision-making in realistic app interfaces becomes increasingly critical. Existing benchmarks mainly focus on execution-level anomalies using task success or hijack rates, but fail to capture the in-task goal deviation risk in multi-candidate selection tasks, where the decision may be steered toward an attacker-specified target, even in violation of instruction-implied constraints (e.g., cheapest/highest-rated), without any overt execution anomalies. We present DIBench, a decision integrity benchmark for measuring this risk in mobile agents. DIBench covers 7 commercial and 3 simulated apps with 5 task types. Under a threat model restricted to non-privileged UI content, we construct 8 deceptive injection probe instantiations that can steer critical selections without overt anomalies. The benchmark includes 1,000 clean and 36,672 injected instances, with a unified protocol and integrity metrics for comparison. Experiments spanning 4 agent frameworks and 7 base models show that completion-based evaluation can overestimate agent trustworthiness and miss decision-integrity risks: deceptive injections steer selections and shift early action policies, inflating completion rates and creating a misleading illusion of safety. Common defenses, including detection, image preprocessing, and prompt reminders, yield inconsistent integrity gains. Overall, DIBench provides a unified, reproducible benchmark to quantify the risk of in-task goal deviation in mobile agents and enable comparable evaluations of safety defenses.

Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have substantially improved cross-modal understanding and reasoning Bai et al. (2025a) ; OpenAI (2025) , enabling GUI mobile agents to autonomously perform multi-step interactions in real-world mobile apps Liu et al. (2025a) ; Rawles et al. (2024) ; Li et al. (2025) . Such agents are increasingly deployed in high-frequency scenarios—including shopping, food ordering, and travel planning—to filter options, verify details, and confirm choices Zhou et al. (2023) ; Lyu et al. (2025) . Because an agent’s output may directly trigger real transactions and shape security-sensitive decisions involving private or sensitive information, it is urgent to systematically characterize and validate the security and trustworthiness of mobile agent systems Chen et al. (2025b) ; Mathur et al. (2019) ; Lin et al. (2025) . Prior work has primarily evaluated mobile-agent security from the execution perspective Chen et al. (2025e) ; Chen et al. (2025c) ; Lee et al. (2026) ; Yang et al. (2025) ; Du et al. (2025) ; Liu et al. (2025b) , focusing on observable anomalies such as prompt-injection-induced task hijacking, unauthorized tool invocations, or interaction traces that visibly deviate from expected behavior. Accordingly, common metrics revolve around task completion rate and hijacking success rate Liu et al. (2025b) ; Liao et al. (2025) ; Lu et al. (2025) ; Evtimov et al. (2025) ; Chen et al. (2025a) , and many evaluations implicitly assume that flow-correct completion implies a trustworthy final choice Kara et al. (2025) ; Yehudai et al. (2025) . However, in real scenarios where multi-candidate selection is central, this assumption does not always hold Kang et al. (2025) ; Gebreegziabher et al. (2026) ; Xue et al. (2023) . In multi-candidate selection tasks on mobile devices, an agent may complete the workflow without privilege escalation, hijacking, or anomalous trajectories, yet still make a manipulated final selection Yang et al. (2024) ; Gebreegziabher et al. (2026) ; Kim et al. (2024) . We define this as In-Task Goal Deviation : under deceptive injection, the agent completes the task but its final choice is redirected toward an attacker-specified option, potentially even violating instruction-implied constraints (e.g., lowest price or highest rating) outside the acceptable set, thereby compromising decision integrity . Crucially, attackers need not alter the instruction or control flow: platform-permitted UI content (e.g., titles or images) can steer critical choices without overt anomalies, while completion-based signals are often insensitive—causing such deviations to be missed by evaluations focused on completion or trajectory anomalies. Although recent anecdotal evidence suggests that mobile agents can be steered by on-screen content during selection Yang et al. (2024) ; Wu et al. (2024) , the community still lacks a reproducible framework for measuring such decision-layer risks under controlled and comparable conditions. Key gaps remain: (1) no explicit security model for targeted in-task goal deviation; (2) the lack of a scalable benchmark suite of multi-candidate selection tasks spanning both commercial and simulated apps; and (3) no unified protocol or decision-integrity metrics for comparing agent frameworks, base models, and defenses. Our benchmark. To address this gap, we introduce DIBench , a benchmark for evaluating the decision integrity of mobile GUI agents in multi-candidate selection tasks. DIBench focuses on key decision states where the agent must compare candidates and select one according to instruction-implied constraints. For each task, we define an instruction I , a candidate set C , and an acceptable set A(I,C) of semantically valid choices. We then construct paired benign and injected instances under matched task conditions, changing only UI-visible content at the critical decision point. This design allows DIBench to measure whether injected content causes targeted steering or decision degradation while controlling for the original task, candidate set, and non-visual context. DIBench covers both real-world and controlled environments. It includes 7 commercial mobile apps and 3 simulated apps across 5 categories of multi-candidate selection tasks, including exploratory, exact-match, cost-based, spatio-temporal, and quality/popularity-based decisions. To evaluate deceptive decision influence, we design 8 injection probe instantiations spanning textual overlays, image-embedded content, and visual perturbations. Overall, DIBench contains 1,000 benign instances and 36,672 injected instances, together with structured annotations for candidates, injection anchors, acceptable sets, target candidates, and paired evaluation.
