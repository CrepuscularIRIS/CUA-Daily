title: CausGround: Causal Interaction Grounding for Adaptive Embodied Planning
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29702

§Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) show promising reasoning capabilities, but reliable physical interaction requires planning that accounts for when actions are executable and how they change the world. These preconditions and effects are environment-dependent and often not captured by MLLMs, making adaptation challenging. Yet existing approaches either require costly environment-specific training or rely on unstructured interaction experience that is difficult to reuse. Therefore, we introduce CausGround, a non-parametric causal interaction grounding framework that distills interaction experience into reusable action-centered causal knowledge. CausGround identifies action preconditions and effects and organizes them in a factorized world-state representation capturing object attributes and inter-object relations. At planning time, task-relevant causal dependencies are grounded in current observations to support backward reasoning over preconditions and forward reasoning over effects for action selection and multi-step planning. We evaluate CausGround on simulated high-level planning, interaction reasoning, and real-world robot manipulation. On adapted VirtualHome and RoboCasa benchmarks, CausGround improves vision-based high-level planning across MLLM backbones, increasing average task success from 24.9% to 53.3% on VirtualHome and closed-loop planning success from 43.0% to 57.0% on RoboCasa. On SWITCH, average interaction reasoning accuracy increases from 25.8% to 31.9%, indicating improved action selection, recovery, and verification. Moreover, real-world experiments show that CausGround reasons backward from MLLM-interpreted goals to derive executable high-level action sequences for sequential manipulation. These results demonstrate that reusable action preconditions and effects support long-horizon embodied planning across diverse environments without parameter updates.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Embodied agents powered by multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated promising reasoning capabilities for high-level planning ( Chen et al., 2025 ; Driess et al., 2023 ; Feng et al., 2025b ) . Yet successful embodied interaction requires more than knowing what to do: agents must know when an action is executable and how it changes the world. Such interaction knowledge is often environment-dependent, involving available actions, object states, action preconditions, and effects ( Kim et al., 2025 ; Han et al., 2024 ; Liu et al., 2024 ) . As illustrated in Fig. 1 , a planner may correctly infer that an object should be placed into a fridge, yet attempt the action before satisfying the precondition that the fridge be open. Such precondition violations can accumulate over long-horizon execution, leading to invalid actions, repeated attempts, and costly trial-and-error ( Raman et al., 2024 ; Zhi et al., 2025 ; Chang et al., 2025 ) . Effective embodied planning therefore requires agents to account for task semantics while adapting their planning to the interaction dynamics of the environment in which the task is executed. Existing approaches still fall short in enabling efficient environment adaptation. A direct strategy is to specialize embodied agents through fine-tuning or reinforcement learning ( Xu et al., 2026 ; Zhai et al., 2024 ; Feng et al., 2025a ) , but such parametric adaptation requires substantial environment-specific interaction data and repeated optimization, making it costly to deploy across diverse environments, particularly in real-world settings. Non-parametric approaches instead incorporate interaction feedback or episodic memory at inference time ( Anwar et al., 2025 ) , but such experience is often retained as unstructured trajectories or task-level memories ( Ginting et al., 2025 ; Buoso et al., 2025 ) . This makes it difficult to identify which parts of an experience are causally relevant rather than incidental, reuse them across tasks, or ground them in the current visual scene. Moreover, existing representations do not always jointly capture the fine-grained object attributes and inter-object relations required to determine whether an action’s preconditions are satisfied ( Chen et al., 2025 ; Shen et al., 2026 ) . This raises a central question: Can interaction experience be distilled into reusable causal knowledge that tells an embodied agent both what must be true before an action and what changes as a result? To this end, we introduce CausGround, a non-parametric causal interaction grounding framework for adaptive embodied planning. As illustrated in Fig. 1 , CausGround abstracts interaction experience into action-centered causal knowledge, representing each action through its relevant preconditions and resulting state effects. Rather than storing complete trajectories, it organizes reusable action-centered causal knowledge over a factorized world-state representation that jointly captures object attributes and inter-object relations. At planning time, the current observation and task are grounded into the same representation to construct a task-relevant local causal graph from reusable causal knowledge. This allows the planner to reason backward over action preconditions to determine what must happen before an action becomes executable, and forward over state effects to anticipate how the world should change after execution. Composing such causal dependencies provides a structured reference for multi-step planning without updating model parameters. We evaluate CausGround across heterogeneous long-horizon embodied settings. In simulation, we adapt VirtualHome ( Puig et al., 2018 ) and RoboCasa ( Nasiriany et al., 2026 ) for visually grounded high-level planning and observe consistent improvements under both one-shot and closed-loop planning, including an increase in average task success from 24.9% to 53.3% on VirtualHome and from 43.0% to 57.0% under closed-loop planning on RoboCasa. Beyond simulation, we evaluate interaction reasoning on SWITCH ( Cheng et al., 2025 ) , a real-world egocentric long-horizon interaction benchmark, where CausGround improves average accuracy from 25.8% to 31.9%. We further validate the framework on real-robot manipulation, where CausGround reasons backward from interpreted goal states to derive executable high-level action sequences and successfully complete multi-step interactions. Together, these results show that explicit causal interaction knowledge provides a reusable grounding substrate for long-horizon embodied reasoning across diverse environments.
