arxiv: 2611.20145 (2611.20145v1)
title: Rank, Don't Regress: A 279M Text Ranker Beats 2B and 7B VLMs on Referential Urdu GUI Grounding
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20145

§Abstract
UI grounding asks a model to find the screen element that a user instruction refers to. Most current systems use a vision-language model (VLM) that reads a screenshot and outputs pixel coordinates. We study this task on Urdu mobile web pages, a setting with no published UI grounding results. Instead of regressing coordinates, we take the candidate elements from the page DOM and train a 279M-parameter cross-encoder (mDeBERTa-v3-base) to pick one. The click point is the centre of the picked element's box, so it is exact whenever the right element is picked. We built a frozen test set of 304 instructions from six Urdu news sites; 149 of them (49%) are referential and never quote the full target text. Test domains were banned from training data before collection started. The ranker scores 73.4% point-in-box accuracy, against 29.6% for UI-TARS-2B and 23.7% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B, both zero-shot. On the referential slice the ranker scores 65.8%, against 30.2% for a string-matching baseline that needs no training. In a controlled comparison at small scale, with training data drawn from the same pages, a coordinate-regression model of similar size scores 1.6%. Adding element geometry as input tokens gives the best variant, at 75.7%. The ranker's errors concentrate in two places: elements that share identical text, and ordinal instructions such as "the fourth link".

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
A computer-use agent acts through a screen. Before it can click, it has to ground: given an instruction such as “open the sports section”, it must find the element the instruction means. The current default is a vision-language model that reads a screenshot and generates a point Cheng et al. (2024) ; Qin et al. (2025) ; Bai et al. (2025) . Grounding accuracy is the ceiling on everything the agent does next, and it is usually the weakest part of the pipeline Zheng et al. (2024) . Two facts pushed us away from that default. First, coordinate regression is fragile under layout change. In pilot runs for this project, Qwen2.5-VL-7B scored 34.1% zero-shot on an 82-item pilot set of mobile pages; the same model fine-tuned on desktop screenshots fell to 11.0%, because training and test pages laid out the same content at different widths. The readout itself is part of the problem: on COCO photos, Qwen3-VL-4B picks the correct cell 68.5% of the time when the cells are named options, and 20.0% when it must answer with pixel coordinates Frontera Del Valle (2026) . Second, the language we care about has no grounding data at all. Public grounding benchmarks cover English and Chinese interfaces Cheng et al. (2024) ; Li et al. (2025) ; for Urdu, a right-to-left language with tens of millions of speakers, we found no published UI grounding result, and hand-labelling click points for a new language is slow work. A web page removes the labelling problem. Its DOM lists every clickable element with its text, attributes, and bounding box. The supervision is already there. The open question is the output space: should a model regress a point from pixels, or select one element from the page’s own candidate list? Selection is not a new idea. MindAct Deng et al. (2023) trains a small DeBERTa ranker over DOM candidates, but uses it as a pre-filter: the ranker shortlists elements and a large language model makes the final pick. Deng et al. report that the ranker on its own is not competitive on Mind2Web. Their pages are English desktop pages with about 1,135 candidate elements per page on average. Our pages are Urdu mobile pages with a median of 25 candidates. We test whether the conclusion survives the change of regime: a trained ranker, alone, as the complete grounder. Our contributions are three: 1. First UI grounding results for Urdu. We report the first UI grounding (element localization) results for Urdu, measured on real mobile websites: a frozen test set of 304 instructions over six Urdu news sites, collected and locked before any training, with test domains excluded from training at the registrable-domain level. Half of the items are referential: the instruction describes the target without quoting its full text. 2. The ranker alone is enough on mobile web. A 279M cross-encoder used as the complete grounder, with no downstream model, scores 73.4% against 29.6% for UI-TARS-2B and 23.7% for Qwen2.5-VL-7B. This reverses the ranker-alone finding of Mind2Web in the mobile regime. 3. A controlled rank-versus-regress study at small scale. Training data for both models comes from the same pages: DOM ranking scores 73.4% and pixel coordinate regression scores 1.6%. The difference is the output space, not the data source. Wu et al. (2026) run the VLM-scale version of this comparison in pixel space; Section 2 separates the two. Section 2 places this work against prior grounding systems. Section 3 defines the task and the frozen protocol. Sections 4 and 5 describe the ranker and the comparison systems. Section 6 gives results, Section 7 takes the failures apart, and Sections 8 and 9 state limits and ethics.
