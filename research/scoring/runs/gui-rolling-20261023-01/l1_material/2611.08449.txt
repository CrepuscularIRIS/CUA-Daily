title: VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449

§Abstract
Models for physical AI tasks, such as robotics and autonomous driving, need not reason solely in language. They can use visual plans--points, bounding boxes, and trajectories--to represent spatial goals and anticipate motion. However, autoregressive vision-language models generate these plans one coordinate token at a time, making richer predictions increasingly costly. We introduce VFlash, a method for fast, accurate visual planning through Flash Primitive Decoding. VFlash exploits the known structure of visual primitives to predict their coordinates jointly, using a lightweight drafter that generates an entire primitive in one forward pass. The layout adapts to the primitive's type, dimensionality, and length, supporting compact boxes and long, variable-length trajectories. Crucially, spatial accuracy need not require reproducing an autoregressive model's exact token sequence. VFlash therefore trains on annotated primitives and directly accepts structurally valid predictions without target-token agreement. Across the evaluated 2D and 3D robot trajectory tasks, VFlash consistently reduces prediction errors relative to autoregressive decoding. Preliminary single-rollout measurements indicate approximately 25x faster generation for 3D point motion forecasting. A world action model conditioned on VFlash plans achieves 70.2% average success across four MolmoSpaces task suites, compared with 62.6% for the baseline.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly used as high-level brains for physical AI systems, interpreting visual observations and task instructions to guide behavior in robotics and autonomous driving ( Gemini Robotics Team, 2025 ; Tian et al., 2025 ; NVIDIA et al., 2025 ) . Their reasoning need not be expressed solely in language. VLMs can formulate intermediate predictions as visual plans: points and bounding boxes identify relevant locations or objects ( Yuan et al., 2025 ; Jiang et al., 2025 ) , while trajectories describe intended motion or forecast how a scene will evolve ( Gu et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 2026a ) . Recent work uses spatial traces and visual primitives to guide action generation ( Li et al., 2025a ; Lee et al., 2025 ; Guo et al., 2026 ) . These plans make spatial goals and anticipated motion explicit before action. Each visual primitive has identifiable boundaries and an organized coordinate layout. Generating such plans efficiently becomes increasingly important as their spatial detail grows. Longer trajectories, additional objects, and joint 2D image and 3D predictions require more coordinate tokens, which autoregressive VLMs decode sequentially ( Chen et al., 2022 ; Bai et al., 2023 ; Zhu et al., 2025 ; Bai et al., 2025 ) . For physical agents that update their plans as new observations arrive, sequential decoding limits spatial detail and update frequency under a fixed latency budget. This motivates decoding methods that can generate complete visual plans with fewer sequential forward passes while preserving their spatial accuracy. Parallel decoding offers a promising route to reducing this sequential cost, particularly for latency-sensitive physical AI systems operating at batch size one. Existing approaches include speculative decoding with parallel verification ( Leviathan et al., 2023 ; Li et al., 2025b ) and diffusion language models (dLLMs) with parallel denoising ( Nie et al., 2025 ; Arriola et al., 2025 ) . However, dLLMs often exhibit a speed–quality trade-off relative to autoregressive models ( Wu et al., 2025 ; Liu et al., 2025 ) . DFlash combines these approaches using a lightweight block diffusion draft model to predict draft tokens in a single forward pass, followed by target verification that preserves the autoregressive model’s output distribution ( Chen et al., 2026 ) . For visual outputs, LocateAnything exploits spatial structure through parallel box decoding ( Wang et al., 2026 ) . In this work, we introduce VFlash , a generalist decoding framework for physical AI (Figure 1 ) that accommodates both text (e.g., sub-task plans) and diverse visual primitives, such as bounding boxes, variable-length trajectories, and dense 3D motion predictions. Our study reveals an interesting observation: a lightweight diffusion drafter can even surpass its autoregressive target in spatial accuracy of the visual plans while decoding much faster. The structure of visual primitives helps explain this finding: once a primitive’s type, dimensionality, and length are specified, its boundaries and coordinate layout are known. Aligning draft blocks with these boundaries allows an entire geometric object to be predicted jointly, with tens to hundreds of tokens decoded in a single forward pass. These observations motivate aligning training and decoding with complete primitive boundaries and directly accepting structurally valid drafts without requiring token-level agreement with the target, while retaining verified decoding for text. At the center of VFlash is Flash Primitive Decoding (FPD), which uses the requested output structure to organize both drafter training and inference. A tool call specifies the size of a variable-length prediction, while the primitive type determines its coordinate layout. Conditioned on features from a frozen target VLM, a lightweight diffusion drafter learns to predict complete primitives from annotated spatial data. At inference, the decoder applies layout-specific constraints ( Willard and Louf, 2023 ; Geng et al., 2023 ) and accepts valid primitives in full. The target VLM supplies context for subsequent predictions, and ordinary text retains speculative decoding with target verification. We evaluate VFlash on 2D and 3D robot trajectories, bounding boxes, and dense 3D point motion forecasting, and study its use in downstream manipulation and driving. VFlash consistently reduces prediction errors relative to its autoregressive target across the evaluated robot trajectory tasks. In the downstream robot manipulation pipeline shown in Figure 1 , it reduces inference latency by about 80% on both Jetson Thor and DGX Spark. These visual plans also provide useful conditioning for action models: on MolmoSpaces ( Kim et al., 2026b ) , a world action model ( Ye et al., 2026 ; NVIDIA, 2026c ) conditioned on VFlash plans achieves 70.2% average success across four task suites, compared with 62.6% for the baseline.
