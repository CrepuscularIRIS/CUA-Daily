title: ImageRSI: Verification-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Agentic Image Generation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32120

§Abstract
Complex text-to-image requests remain difficult to satisfy reliably. Verifying and repairing an image can fix an individual output, but a successful repair does not necessarily carry over to other requests. We present ImageRSI, a framework that keeps the image generator frozen and combines three components: five-dimensional verification and repair within each task, an experience library shared across tasks, and offline updating of the judge. Candidate experiences enter the library only after paired A/B validation, and the judge is updated with supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on accumulated image-judgment records and evaluated before reuse. We also introduce ImageReq-Bench, a benchmark of 961 requests built around hard cases in real-world image-generation requests, each paired with an evaluation checklist. ImageRSI achieves a Checklist Strict Accuracy (CSA) of 52.6% on MindBench (with Nano Banana Pro as the generator) and 65.66% on ImageReq-Bench, and we compare system outcomes across judge-training configurations.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Text-to-image models such as GPT-Image and Wan2.7-Image ( Mao et al., 2026 ) generate high-quality images with fine-grained style control and strong instruction following. Yet requests that combine multiple constraints still reveal failures in factual knowledge, exact text rendering, object counts, attribute binding, and spatial relations Huang et al. (2023) ; Zhang et al. (2025) ; Sun et al. (2026) . Requests may also leave some requirements implicit. For example, a request for a labeled diagram of a solar eclipse implicitly requires the Moon to appear between the Sun and Earth, with the three bodies aligned and legible labels identifying each body. Reliable generation therefore requires inferring the intended constraints, verifying that the resulting image satisfies them, and applying targeted repair when needed. Beyond fixing the current image, verification and repair leave trajectories that record the errors, the changes made to the generation conditions, and the resulting outcomes Ye et al. (2025b) ; Yang et al. (2024) ; Zhu et al. (2025) ; these trajectories can provide candidate strategies for future tasks. However, a single successful repair does not establish that a strategy is reliable or transferable: stochastic generation can produce improvements by chance, redundant trajectories can overstate the evidence, and judge errors can reinforce incorrect diagnoses. Candidate experience therefore requires validation, explicit conditions of applicability, and criteria for revising or retiring it. Existing agentic systems address these issues only in part. Some apply verification and iterative repair to individual requests Shen et al. (2026) ; Chen et al. (2025) ; Ouyang et al. (2026) ; He et al. (2026a) ; Feng et al. (2026a) , and additionally incorporate cross-task memory ( Zhang et al., 2026c ; Xiong et al., 2026 ) . Retaining feedback, however, does not determine when the resulting experience is actually useful. Evaluation faces a related limitation: fixed-prompt benchmarks ( Niu et al., 2025 ; He et al., 2026b ) measures final-output quality, but it cannot by itself establish whether an agent improves as it accumulates experience across tasks. We propose ImageRSI , a verification-driven recursive self-improvement framework that couples within-task repair, cross-task experience reuse, and offline judger self-evolution, where the judger is the verification model that scores images. Within each task, the agent parses the request, retrieves relevant experience and, when needed, external knowledge, generates an image, and evaluates it for Alignment, Faithfulness, Quality, Aesthetic, and Physics. Defect feedback then guides targeted prompt repair while leaving the original requirements unchanged. Across tasks, an experience library stores candidate strategies, each recording when it applies and when it does not, the prompt change it makes, and its observed effects and side effects; paired A/B validation decides which experiences are admitted for reuse. For judger self-evolution, selected trajectory images are paired with model-generated score-and-reason annotations for supervised fine-tuning (SFT), followed by GRPO Shao et al. (2024) to optimize scoring behavior. After evaluation, a candidate judge can guide verification and repair in a later collection round, yielding new records for further updates. Experience admission updates inference-time knowledge, whereas judger self-evolution updates the judge’s parameters offline; the image generator stays frozen throughout. Our contributions are as follows: • We propose ImageRSI, a verification-driven recursive self-improvement framework for agentic image generation with a frozen image generator. Within each task, five-dimensional verification localizes unmet requirements, and repair rewrites the prompt to target these defects while locking the parsed requirements, so that satisfied requirements are preserved across rounds. • We formulate two complementary uses of repair trajectories: an experience library records when each strategy applies and requires paired A/B validation before reuse, while accumulated image–judgment records support offline judge post-training and evaluation before redeployment. • We introduce ImageReq-Bench, a benchmark of 961 complex image-generation requests built around hard cases in real-world image-generation requests. Each request is linked to its source records and an evaluation checklist for assessing whether its requirements are jointly satisfied. • ImageRSI achieves state-of-the-art CSA on both benchmarks, reaching 52.6% on MindBench and 65.66% on ImageReq-Bench and surpassing the strongest baselines by 10.2 and 9.28 pp, respectively.
