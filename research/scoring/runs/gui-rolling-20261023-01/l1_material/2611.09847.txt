title: Computer-Use Agents Are Embodied Generalists
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09847

§Abstract
In many areas of AI, general-purpose models have increasingly replaced handcrafted methods and specialized architectures. We argue that embodied AI is poised for a similar shift. Our key claim is that embodied tasks can be formulated as interaction with digital software. Specifically, we build software that renders an agent's view of a physical environment on a screen and provides controls for acting in it. A computer-use agent (CUA) can then perform embodied tasks through the same screenshots and keyboard and mouse actions that it uses to operate ordinary software. Using a single CUA design and changing only the software, our entries win first-, second-, third-, and sixth-place prizes across four ECCV workshop competitions, without any competition-specific agent tuning. These results suggest that the skills CUAs learn on software, such as visual grounding, precise clicking, and exploration, already carry over to embodied tasks, giving CUAs a far broader scope in embodied AI than previously realized. We therefore advocate that computer-use agents can be embodied generalists. Further, we advocate that, by following the training strategy that made computer-use agents general, embodied AI can converge on a single general model as well, ushering in a new era of general embodied AI. §Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
In many areas of AI, general-purpose models have increasingly replaced handcrafted methods and specialized architectures ( Sutton, 2019 ; OpenAI et al., 2025 ; Luong and Lockhart, 2025 ) . We argue that embodied AI, which studies agents that perceive and act in physical environments, is poised for a similar shift ( Open X-Embodiment Collaboration et al., 2024 ; Gemini Robotics Team et al., 2025 ) . In particular, we explore connecting embodied AI with computer-use agents: general-purpose digital agents that operate diverse software applications through screenshots, mouse clicks, and keyboard inputs. Although operating a computer and acting in a physical environment may seem quite different, we observe that both involve similar challenges, including visual grounding, exploration, and adapting actions based on feedback from the environment ( Yang et al., 2025a ; Zhang et al., 2025a ; Huang et al., 2022 ) . Our key insight is that embodied tasks can be formulated as interaction with digital software. Specifically, we construct software that renders observations on a screen and provides controls for acting in the physical environment (Figure 1 ). A computer-use agent can then observe the environment, take actions, and inspect their consequences using the same interface that it uses to operate ordinary desktop software. Task-specific functionality resides in the software rather than the agent design, allowing us to use existing computer-use agents across different embodied tasks without developing specialized models. To investigate our approach, we develop software applications for four embodied AI tasks: vision-language navigation, dialogue-driven navigation, language-guided 3D segmentation, and referring video segmentation. We use a single, minimal computer-use agent harness across all four applications, without competition-specific agent tuning, and enter 4 ECCV 2026 workshop competitions. Our entries place first in OpenSUN3D, second in MeViSv2-Text, third in DialNav, and sixth in the VLNVerse competitions. These results demonstrate that computer-use agents can achieve strong performance across diverse embodied tasks by interacting with task-specific software through a shared agent design. Beyond these results, we argue that embodied AI can follow the same path that computer use has already taken. Only two years ago, computer-use agents faced the challenges that embodied AI faces today. They struggled to click precisely and relied on overlays or separate grounding models to locate targets ( Yang et al., 2023 ; Zheng et al., 2024 ; Gou et al., 2025 ) , much as most VLAs today add a dedicated action head for continuous control ( Physical Intelligence et al., 2025 ; NVIDIA et al., 2025 ) . They also lagged far behind agents with task-specific APIs ( Xie et al., 2024 ; Aggarwal and Welleck, 2026c ; Song et al., 2025 ) . Today, the same model that writes code and solves mathematics problems can also operate computers by writing click coordinates as ordinary text ( Anthropic, 2024 ; Quiros, 2026 ) , and the strongest agents perform comparably whether they act through the screen or through APIs ( NeoCognition, 2026 ) . This progress came from training for precise visual grounding, building environments for reinforcement learning, and making interaction fast enough for real-time use ( Gou et al., 2025 ; Aggarwal et al., 2026b ; Wang et al., 2026 ; Aggarwal et al., 2026a ) . Each of these has a direct counterpart in embodied AI. Simulators can generate grounding data at scale ( Yuan et al., 2024 ) , embodied environments can be used for reinforcement learning ( Li et al., 2026 ) , and faster inference can enable real-time control ( Black et al., 2025b ) . Since our approach exposes embodied environments through software, advances in computer-use agents can also transfer to embodied AI directly, and embodied tasks can become part of the training of future general-purpose agents . We therefore believe that embodied AI will converge on a single general model, just as computer use did ( OpenAI, 2026c ; Anthropic, 2026 ) .
