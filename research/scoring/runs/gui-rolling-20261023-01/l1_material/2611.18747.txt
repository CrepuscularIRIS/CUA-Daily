title: Video-as-Experience: Learning Computer Use via Audio-Visual Understanding
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18747

§Abstract
Multimodal large language models (LLMs) have enabled capable computer-use agents that perceive graphical user interfaces, reason over instructions, and execute actions. However, existing approaches to learning from video demonstrations typically convert videos into screenshot-based action trajectories, rather than directly leveraging their rich audio-visual information. We introduce VEX (Video as EXperience), a framework that uses audio-visual understanding to unify learning from external demonstrations and reflecting on an agent's own behavior. Following a watch-act-rewatch paradigm, the agent extracts procedural guidance from tutorial videos containing visual demonstrations and spoken explanations, executes tasks, and verifies its progress by rewatching its execution as video. We instantiate VEX with video-SALMONN-VEX, a 9B-scale audio-visual computer-use agent built on the video-SALMONN 2 architecture with a Qwen3.5-9B backbone. We further introduce Action-Grounded Guide Optimization (AGGO), a reinforcement learning method that optimizes video-derived guidance for downstream action prediction, and augment AgentNet, OSWorld, and CUA-Gym with over 24K real-world tutorial videos. Experiments on OSWorld and a complementary subset of CUA-Gym demonstrate consistent improvements, with video-SALMONN-VEX achieving a 22% relative increase in task success over its Qwen3.5 backbone on OSWorld. Moreover, its generated guidance improves Gemini-3.5-flash by 10% relatively on OSWorld. These results highlight the potential of audio-visual understanding as a unified interface for procedural learning and self-verification in computer-use agents.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recent advances in multimodal large language models (LLMs) [ 14 , 40 , 16 , 4 , 37 , 26 , 24 , 3 , 25 ] have enabled increasingly capable agents for computer use, where models perceive graphical user interfaces (GUIs), reason over task instructions, and execute actions in interactive environments [ 12 , 9 , 5 , 21 ] . Most existing computer-use agents (CUAs), however, are primarily optimized to act from the information available in the current interaction, such as screenshots, textual instructions, and previously executed actions [ 1 , 39 , 8 , 32 , 20 , 34 , 7 , 15 , 33 , 28 ] . In contrast, humans often acquire new procedural skills by observing demonstrations from experienced individuals. To this end, online tutorial videos provide an abundant record of such demonstrations, jointly capturing visual states, user operations, and spoken explanations. This has motivated a growing line of work that leverages instructional videos to improve computer-use agents, either at training time or as additional knowledge at inference time. Recent approaches [ 23 , 19 , 31 , 18 ] have demonstrated that internet-scale videos can indeed provide valuable information for computer use, typically by recovering executable interaction trajectories from observed demonstrations and using the resulting trajectories for training or in-context guidance. Despite these advances, existing approaches largely use video as a source of trajectory extraction, rather than as a form of experience that an agent can directly understand and reason over. Raw videos are commonly passed through dedicated processing pipelines [ 18 ] that construct and filter trajectories before they can be consumed by the main agent. These representations are effective and provide explicit access to actions and intermediate states, but constructing them often requires dedicated data-collection and processing pipelines involving additional large models. Besides, converting videos into such representations often discards valuable audio and visual explanations. More fundamentally, demonstrations and an agent’s own interactions are often represented differently, with external experience originating as video while self-generated experience is recorded as interleaved screenshots, actions, and textual traces. A single audiovisual model could instead use the same audio-visual perceptual interface to extract task-relevant knowledge from observed demonstrations and revisit its own execution, unifying learning from others and reflecting on its own behavior as reasoning over videos. To achieve such agents, we introduce VEX (Video as EXperience), a framework for computer-use agents that has audio-visual understanding capability to directly learn procedural knowledge or perform self-verification on video recordings. At inference time, VEX follows a simple watch-act-rewatch loop, where the agent watches and extracts guidance directly from tutorial video recordings, and applies the guidance to its task execution. The agent also verifies its success after completion by rewatching its own execution represented as a video recording. We instantiate VEX with video-SALMONN-VEX, a single 9B-scale agent built on video-SALMONN 2 [ 26 ] architecture with Qwen3.5-9B LLM backbone that can understand audio-visual inputs while performing computer-use tasks. In addition, we introduce Action-Grounded Guide Optimization (AGGO) to train video-SALMONN-VEX to extract procedural knowledge from tutorial videos according to their utility for downstream action prediction. The VEX framework is evaluated on both OSWorld [ 30 ] and a complementary subset of CUA-Gym [ 27 ] focusing on tasks that are not covered by OSWorld. Consistent improvements were obtained using VEX for video-SALMONN-VEX compared to its backbone Qwen3.5-9B and video-SALMONN 2 models, with a notable 22% relative task success rate improvement on OSWorld. Moreover, using the VEX framework together with task guidance generated from video-SALMONN-VEX on Gemini-3.5-flash, a relative 10% improvement was achieved on OSWorld. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • We propose VEX , a framework that uses video as a shared interface for learning from demonstrations and self-evaluating agent execution. We introduce video-SALMONN-VEX as the single agent with audio-visual understanding capabilities that can learn from real-world video tutorials to perform the task and can verify its performance through video recordings of its execution. • We introduce AGGO , a reinforcement learning approach that trains the agent to extract procedural guidance from tutorial videos by rewarding guides according to their utility for predicting ground-truth action trajectories. • We augment AgentNet, OSWorld, and CUA-Gym with over 25K real tutorial videos, and evaluate VEX on OSWorld and a complementary subset of CUA-Gym, where video-SALMONN-VEX achieves a 22% relative task success rate increase over the Qwen3.5-9B backbone.
