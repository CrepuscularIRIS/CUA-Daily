title: STRUCTUREDAGENT: Planning with AND/OR Trees for Long-Horizon Web Tasks
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.05294

§Abstract
Existing LLM-based web agents struggle on complex, long-horizon tasks due to limited in-context memory, weak planning abilities, and greedy behaviors that lead to premature termination. To address these challenges, we propose \SA{}, a hierarchical planning framework that interleaves planning and execution via dynamic $\ANDOR$ trees. The framework separates structural planning from LLM-based reasoning, enabling principled error recovery through node repair, systematic exploration of alternatives via OR nodes, and modular plans that can facilitate human intervention. On WebArena (630 tasks), \SA{} achieves a $\sim$53\% success rate vs.\ $\sim$46\% for AgentOccam, and on complex multi-constraint Amazon shopping tasks, gains reach 10\% over the strongest baseline.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) have enabled agentic systems for sequential decision-making. Such agents must perceive their environment, reason across multiple time steps, and take actions that optimize long-term objectives. A particularly promising application area is the development of web-browsing agents, which navigate and interact with the internet to accomplish complex, goal-driven tasks including information seeking, form filling, and transactional activities. Despite notable progress, building robust and interpretable web-based agents remains challenging. Although many of these tasks are intuitively straightforward for humans, LLM-powered web agents continue to struggle with complex, multi-step browsing [ Yang et al., 2025 , Erdogan et al., 2025 , Qi et al., 2025 ] . We focus on agents without specialized memory or retrieval machinery, the prevailing setup among publicly reported web agents. In this setting, four challenges account for the bulk of observed failures: (1) Limited in-context memory, which prevents the agent from tracking alternative solution paths across dense web pages, where a single page can exceed 20k tokens (see Figure 6 ). Even when long-context models are used, existing agents condition only on the current observation and a summary of the interaction history. (2) Difficulty constructing and executing complex plans that must remain coherent over extended horizons. (3) Lack of robust error handling, leaving agents unable to recover from failures or explore alternative solutions [ Koh et al., 2025 ] . (4) Greedy decision-making, which leads to premature task termination before all user constraints are satisfied [ Schmied et al., 2025 ] (see Appendix P ). In cognitive science [ Newell et al., 1972 ] , human problem solving is framed as heuristic search coupled with means-ends analysis , where complex tasks are recursively decomposed into manageable subproblems. This perspective motivates hierarchical planning in AI, where agents improve efficiency by breaking down complex goals into nested sub-goals and actions at multiple levels of abstraction. Such methods are particularly well-suited to open-ended, web-based tasks characterized by uncertainty and expansive state and action spaces. Classical hierarchical planners, however, rest on assumptions that web environments violate. A* and AO* require admissible heuristics or fully specified domain models (e.g., PDDL), which are unavailable when state and action spaces are unbounded and only partially observable. Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) is prohibitively expensive, requiring multiple rollouts before selecting an action at every step. Hierarchical task network (HTN) planners depend on pre-specified decomposition schemas, which complex web tasks rarely provide. The closest applicable works [ Koh et al., 2025 , Erdogan et al., 2025 ] either operate at the action level without hierarchical structure or construct linear plans without backtracking. Recent research has explored explicit planning mechanisms for web agents. Koh et al. [2025] use tree-based planning with A* search to explore alternative solution paths upon failure. However, their method operates at the level of individual actions, which limits exploitation of compositional task structure, and the absence of admissible heuristics can lead to inefficient path switching. Yang et al. [2025] propose incremental tree construction with prune-and-branch strategies, but rely solely on the LLM for plan construction and execution, often resulting in simplistic plans and limited error recovery. Erdogan et al. [2025] introduce dynamic plan revisions, but permit only sequential plans determined prior to execution, without error back-propagation. To address these challenges, we propose StructuredAgent , a hierarchical planning framework that interleaves planning and execution via ordered And/Or trees. The key architectural choice is a separation of concerns: the framework constructs and maintains the planning tree, while the LLM is invoked only for local operations such as node expansion or repair. Our main contributions are: • We introduce a hierarchical planning framework enabling inference-time planning via ordered And/Or trees. The framework supports compositional reasoning, dynamic plan revision, and error backpropagation, while producing modular plans that can facilitate error localization and human intervention. • We evaluate on WebArena (630 tasks), WebVoyager (129 tasks), and a custom 60-task shopping benchmark, achieving 7–10% improvement over the strongest baseline on complex tasks.
