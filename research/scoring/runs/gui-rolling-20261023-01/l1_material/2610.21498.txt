title: GRASE: Geometry-native Real-to-sim Agentic Scene Modeling from Video
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21498

§Abstract
Real-to-sim conversion aims to turn a captured video of a real environment into an editable 3D scene while preserving the geometry and layout of what was observed. Existing methods often follow a one-pass, loosely coupled paradigm: each stage receives only limited feedback from previous stages, allowing errors to propagate without reliable correction and leaving the final scene inconsistent. We present GRASE, an agentic framework that closes this loop by treating the captured multi-view video observations as a standing reference throughout scene modeling. Instead of committing to intermediate estimates, GRASE revises its current hypothesis whenever rendered evidence disagrees with the capture, overcoming the fixed intermediate assumptions of conventional pipelines. Specifically, GRASE first fits the architectural shell and its openings from depth and multi-view cues. An agent then places generated assets in a shared coordinate frame while checking their spatial relationships. Finally, each asset's geometry and placement are refined iteratively to improve agreement across the observed views. By coupling these steps through feedback, GRASE localizes structural discrepancies and revises only the responsible components, while preserving cross-view consistency. The resulting 3D scenes are faithful to the input, consistent across views, and suitable for simulations. In our experiments, GRASE substantially outperforms other state-of-the-art methods, including a direct real-to-sim conversion using GPT6-Astra.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Real-to-sim aims to convert real-world observations into simulation-ready 3D environments, providing a scalable way to build training and evaluation data for embodied agents. Video is particularly attractive because it is inexpensive, easy to capture, and naturally records scene appearance and spatial structure. A robust video-based real-to-sim pipeline could therefore greatly reduce the cost of constructing digital twins for robot learning. Real-to-sim requires more than visually plausible reconstruction: the resulting scene must contain accurate geometry, object-level structure, and physically meaningful spatial relationships. Video makes this especially challenging due to occlusions, incomplete viewpoints, motion blur, depth ambiguity, and weakly textured regions. Small geometric errors can further lead to severe simulation failures such as penetration, floating objects, or incorrect contacts. Existing approaches typically combine scene reconstruction, object segmentation, 3D generation, and scene assembly. However, they often fail to recover large room surfaces, produce inaccurate object geometry from partial observations, or rely on generative models that hallucinate shapes inconsistent with the input video. These errors accumulate during scene composition, resulting in scenes that may look plausible but are geometrically inconsistent and unsuitable for simulation. An agentic formulation is attractive because real-to-sim naturally requires iterative diagnosis, tool use, and error correction rather than a single feed-forward prediction. In addition, modern VLMs with strong spatial perception capabilities, such as GPT6-Astra, enable a new perceive-then-reconstruct paradigm for real-to-sim conversion. However, we find that applying agentic models such as GPT-6 to recover accurate geometry and topology is not trivial. On the one hand, when using only multi-view images, VLMs struggle to produce accurate 3D geometry at real-world scales. On the other hand, preliminary experiments suggest that even powerful agents such as GPT6-Astra struggle to reason directly over raw point clouds when given estimated point clouds and multi-view camera parameters, frequently making incorrect judgments about geometry, scale, and spatial relationships. This suggests that simply giving 3D observations to an agent is insufficient. Our key idea is to couple explicit geometric modeling with an agentic verification and repair loop, using the original video as a persistent reference throughout reconstruction. The agent represents the scene through room surfaces and openings, object transforms, and editable object parts, so that discrepancies can be addressed through targeted geometric or topological changes. This leads to two closely connected components: room-surface topology and geometry refinement and object-centric layout registration and part-wise geometric refinement , integrated with agentic execution and verification . Complex room surfaces, including walls, ceilings, and floors, are traditionally difficult to reconstruct. Using a VLM with strong spatial reasoning capabilities, we propose a perceive-then-reconstruct paradigm that first perceives the architectural topology and then uses geometric primitives and parametric patches to fit regular and irregular surfaces. This paradigm allows us to reliably recover accurate geometry and topology using multi-view depth observations, image boundaries, and agentic spatial reasoning and editing. Notably, this paradigm significantly improves reconstruction results. In the ablation study, we observe a substantial performance drop when this paradigm is not used. With the room surfaces reconstructed, the agent then aims to reconstruct object-level geometry that aligns with the input video. Agentic models such as GPT6-Astra are good at coding and editing 3D objects using geometric primitives in an agentic loop, but they struggle to match fine and complex real-world geometry. Meshes generated by 3D generation models such as TRELLIS.2, however, are difficult to edit through code. We propose to combine 3D generation models with a part-wise geometric refinement module: we first segment the generated geometry using off-the-shelf segmentation models, then use agentic models to perform part-wise editing, verification, and repair, incorporating geometric cues such as point clouds and image boundaries.
