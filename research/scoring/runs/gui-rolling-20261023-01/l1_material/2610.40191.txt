title: Video Generation Meets Agent Harness: Agentic Video Generation, Agentic World Modeling and Recursive Self-Improvement
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40191

§Abstract
Video generation is shifting from end-to-end synthesis toward agentic generation, where planning, tool use, and feedback enable coherent, controllable multi-shot videos. In parallel, video world models provide action-conditioned observations and predicted futures, serving as interactive environments for agents. We study their intersection through the agent harness: an executable runtime that constructs context, exposes tools, maintains state, routes feedback, and manages budgets around video models. This report examines three directions. First, agentic video generation, where harnesses coordinate scripting, shot planning, asset management, production, and editing, alongside the evaluation challenges of these systems. Second, agentic world modeling, where harnesses mediate observation, control, execution, and state. We discuss hybrid designs in which game engines or agent-written programs maintain world state while video models render observations, and how agents and world models can mutually evaluate and improve one another. Third, recursive self-improvement (RSI) in video model development, distinguishing output revisions, updates to harness state, generator parameters, and evaluators, and changes to the improvement procedure itself. We identify how these improvements feed back into subsequent development. Across these directions, we analyze essential agent capabilities, including audiovisual perception, long-horizon planning, memory, code-based interaction, feedback-guided control, skill reuse, and multi-agent coordination. We conclude with challenges in autonomous video production, lifelong memory, programmable hybrid control, autonomous model development, provenance, cost accounting, and evaluation linking video quality and task performance to sustained improvements in models and harnesses.

1.1 Motivation Video generators produce increasingly realistic, controllable, and temporally coherent clips [ kong2024hunyuanvideo ] . A generator alone, however, does not organize the decisions needed to complete a video task. A creative brief must become coherent shots; references must persist across scenes; takes must be inspected; edits must preserve what is correct [ huang2026vimax , vista2025 ] . Interactive video must also support continuing actions grounded in observations, not just a finished clip [ gao2026lingbotworld2 ] . These requirements motivate a transition from video generation to agentic video generation: a goal-directed process in which generated results inform subsequent decisions. An agent can revise a plan, retrieve a reference, select a tool, or localize a failure in response to actual outcomes [ li2026videogenagent ] . Agentic video generation denotes a system-level perspective, not a new generator architecture: the video model provides synthesis or predicted transitions, while the agent makes consequential choices around those capabilities. Video couples these choices over time. References affect later shots [ chen2026cinecrew ] , actions change subsequent observations, and local repairs can disrupt temporal consistency elsewhere. Plausible footage may still violate a narrative requirement or fail to realize an intended action, so a quality score alone rarely specifies what to do next [ liu2024videogenerationevaluation , ding2026playworld ] . Effective control requires connecting observations to localized decisions while maintaining project or interaction state and accounting for generation cost. The development from large language models (LLMs) to agents offers a foundation for this control. Rather than only generating a response, an LLM can operate within a cycle of reasoning, tool use, and observation, as demonstrated by ReAct [ yao2023react ] . Planning and memory extend this cycle to tasks requiring multiple decisions [ weng2023agents ] . Making these capabilities operational requires an agent harness: the executable runtime around the model that constructs its context, exposes tools, maintains state, routes feedback, and enforces permissions and budgets [ weng2026harness , ning2026code ] (Section ). For video generation, the harness is where a creative brief or an interactive goal becomes executable operations on generators, editors, and world models. The central question shifts from generating a good clip to reliably completing a video task through feedback. Agentic video generation and video world-model interaction are two concrete settings. A further question is whether experience from these tasks can improve the system that will perform future tasks. Feedback changes different objects at different timescales. Prompt revision or take replacement targets the current artifact [ vista2025 ] ; retaining a skill targets subsequent tasks [ wei2026videoweaver ] ; updating a dataset, critic, or generator targets later model versions [ liu2025videoalign ] . These outcomes should not be conflated: effective repair of a current video does not by itself establish improvement in future capability. Beyond repairing individual outputs, context engineering considers how instructions, retrieved evidence, memory, and execution records are selected and organized for subsequent decisions [ weng2026harness ] . Agentic Context Engineering (ACE), for example, accumulates and revises a structured context from execution feedback rather than updating model weights [ zhang2025ace ] . Such work shifts attention from a current response toward reusable knowledge, while leaving open whether the mechanisms that construct and update that knowledge can themselves improve. Recent work on automated research extends these ideas to model development. The AI Scientist links idea generation, coding, experiments, and reporting [ lu2024aiscientist ] ; autoresearch runs a bounded loop of training code modification, experimentation, and validation-based acceptance [ karpathy2026autoresearch ] . These workflows connect proposals to empirical feedback, but automating a fixed experiment loop is not the same as improving the loop itself. Research on self-improving agents, exemplified by the Darwin Gödel Machine and Regularized Recursive Self-Improvement (RRSI), explores changes to the agent’s own code or harness [ zhang2025dgm , xia2026rrsi ] . Such work motivates examining the processes that generate and validate subsequent improvements. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) asks whether a system can improve the procedures that produce further improvements [ chen2026surveyr04 , duan2026surveyn21 ] . Video-specific targets include failure diagnosis, data collection, supervision construction, update selection, and candidate validation. Repeating a fixed collection-and-training pipeline is iterative improvement.
