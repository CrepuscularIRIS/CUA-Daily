title: Democratizing Robot-Use Agents: A Unified Harness with Open Models
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01560

§Abstract
Robot-use Agents (RUAs) have shown promising zero-shot manipulation capabilities, but reliance on frontier, proprietary models limits research into further training. To democratize RUA research, we introduce uni-robot-agent, a unified harness for competitive open-weight robot use that supports further training. Without privileged inputs, an out-of-the-box Qwen3.8-Flash-Next achieves a RoboDojo Score of 31.39, surpassing the published GPT-6 Astra-RoboProbe result of 28.97 and many VLA/WAM baselines, and performs competitively on 19 real-world tasks. Our analysis across model generations, families, and sizes links effective robot use to four general capabilities, suggesting that future RUA training should develop them jointly.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recent breakthroughs in frontier foundation models (e.g., GPT-6 Astra ( OpenAI, 2026c ) , Opus 5.5 ( Anthropic, 2026 ) ) have shown surprising zero-shot performance in robotic manipulation, highlighting robot-use agents (RUAs) ( Isola, 2026 ) as a promising approach for robotics ( Chen et al., 2026b ; Zhang et al., 2026b ; Qu et al., 2026 ) . This approach effectively transfers the models’ generic problem-solving capabilities, such as task decomposition and failure recovery, which are hard to learn from limited robotics data alone, yet extremely useful for robotics ( Isola, 2026 ; Chu et al., 2026 ; Jia et al., 2026 ) . However, because these frontier foundation models are proprietary, research on RUAs has been limited to using the models as-is, such as harness design ( Li et al., 2026 ; Zhang et al., 2026b ; Si et al., 2026 ) . Without access to their weights, researchers cannot directly explore how further training of these models can advance RUA capabilities. Moreover, API dependence makes large-scale interaction costly ( OpenAI, 2026c ) and restricts model-level optimization, such as efficient caching, despite inference speed being a major bottleneck for RUA ( Isola, 2026 ) . The main challenge in adapting open-weight models for robot use is the capability gap between open-weight and closed-weight foundation models. Robot use demands strong spatial understanding and agentic capabilities, which existing open-weight models often lack. Consequently, prior work has focused on ad-hoc improvements, including leveraging privileged information such as ground-truth simulator states or oracle task-success feedback ( Fu et al., 2026 ; Zhang et al., 2026c ) , or incorporating task-specific skills ( Lu et al., 2026 ) . Before attributing these limitations to the models themselves and pursuing ad-hoc improvements, we argue that we must first establish a generalizable and effective robot-use harness to systematically understand and maximize their existing capabilities, laying the foundation for future RUA training. In this paper, we take a first step toward democratizing robot-use agent research by laying this foundation for both competitive open-weight agents and future robot-specific training. We introduce uni-robot-agent, a general-purpose RUA harness. It provides Unified tool interfaces and feedback formats across models, embodiments, tasks, and environments, supporting joint training on simulation and real-world interaction data. For future large-scale training, we emphasize Efficiency by overlapping model reasoning with tool execution to accelerate interaction, and Modularity through interchangeable agent runtimes, tools, and robot backends. Together, these designs provide a scalable, extensible foundation for RUA research. The proposed uni-robot-agent enables competitive manipulation performance with open-weight models, including Qwen and DeepSeek, in both simulation and physical environments. A key finding is that well-structured information, such as camera calibration and robot descriptions, together with reliable tool interfaces that provide informative feedback, are sufficient for effective robot use. As a result, without privileged inputs , Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with uni-robot-agent achieves a Score of 31.39 , surpassing GPT-6 Astra’s result ( 28.97 ) on RoboDojo ( Chen et al., 2026a ) from the RoboDojo team ( Zhang et al., 2026a ) . To the best of our knowledge, this is the first system to outperform closed-weight foundation models on RoboDojo using open-weight models, without privileged informations or distillation from those closed-weight models. In real-world experiments on 19 tabletop manipulation tasks, Qwen3.8-Flash-Next with uni-robot-agent achieves a Score of 53.50 , outperforming Dream-1 ( 43.50 ; Dream Labs, 2026 ) and matching fine-tuned \pi_{0.5} ( 47.75 ; Physical Intelligence et al., 2025 ). Having established the competitiveness of open-weight RUAs, we further compare models across generations, families, and sizes to investigate the capabilities underlying robot use. Trajectory analysis reveals four common failure patterns involving visual understanding, spatial grounding, geometric coding, and embodied reasoning. Based on these findings, we posit that current robot-use capability emerges from a combination of these general-purpose capabilities and identify them as key areas for improvement in future RUA training. Finally, we provide an analysis of the effect of privileged information, cost, and execution efficiency.
