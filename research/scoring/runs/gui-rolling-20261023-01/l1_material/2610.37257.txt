title: GameVerse: A Minute-Scale Gameplay Dataset for Long-Horizon Interactive World Modeling
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37257

§Abstract
Recent advances in interactive world models have made it possible to generate controllable, dynamic environments from action inputs. Yet current models struggle to maintain coherence beyond roughly one minute of rollout, a limitation rooted not in model capacity but in the scarcity of long-horizon training data. Existing gameplay video datasets typically consist of clips under ten seconds, with captions that describe whole clips in coarse and undifferentiated text, which is insufficient for controllable long-horizon generation. We address this with GameVerse, a minute-scale gameplay video dataset purpose-built for long-horizon interactive world modeling. GameVerse contains 69.7K clips spanning 1,655 hours and 349.6 million frames across diverse first- and third-person gameplay, including 1,358 hours of continuous two-minute sequences, an order of magnitude longer than prior datasets. Clips pass through a unified pipeline of scene-consistent segmentation, quality filtering, camera pose estimation, and event-based caption annotation. Each clip is paired with a temporally stable camera trajectory that remains coherent across minutes, together with captions that separate static scene context from dynamic foreground events and align to event boundaries rather than fixed time windows. Comprehensive analysis shows that GameVerse surpasses existing public datasets in scale, diversity, and annotation quality. Experiments further demonstrate that training on GameVerse substantially extends the temporal horizon of interactive world models.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Interactive world models Bruce et al. (2024) ; Valevski et al. (2025) ; Xiao et al. (2025) aim to learn controllable, dynamic environments that can be rolled out from action and camera inputs, a capability central to embodied decision-making, simulation, and game-style content generation. Recent gameplay-oriented systems such as GameGen-X Che et al. (2025) , Matrix-Game Zhang et al. (2025) , Hunyuan-GameCraft Li et al. (2025a) , and HY-World 1.5 HunyuanWorld (2025) demonstrate compelling short-horizon controllability, yet they share a striking ceiling: rollouts collapse into drift, identity loss, and scene incoherence after roughly one minute of generation. We argue that this ceiling is, first and foremost, a data problem rather than a model-capacity problem. The training corpora behind these systems consist almost entirely of clips shorter than ten seconds, paired with whole-clip captions that conflate static context with dynamic events. Such data fundamentally cannot supervise minute-scale temporal coherence, fine-grained controllability, or event-level semantics, no matter how large the model. This limitation is visible across recent gameplay and exploration datasets, which exhibit a consistent pattern of single-axis optimization (Table 1 ). Sekai Li et al. (2025c) ; He et al. (2026) provides large-scale exploration video but lacks dense interactions, validated camera trajectories, and structured semantics. Matrix-Game and HY-World 1.5 expose engine-level camera poses but cap continuous footage at short windows. GameGen-X and Hunyuan-GameCraft support action-conditioned interaction but lack reliable trajectories and event-aligned captions. None of them simultaneously provides minute-scale continuous footage, validated frame-level camera trajectories, dense first- and third-person interaction coverage, and multi-granular event-aligned semantic annotations. So long as these four signals remain fragmented across separate datasets, any model trained on a single source inherits its blind spot. We close this gap with GameVerse , a minute-scale gameplay dataset purpose-built for long-horizon interactive world modeling. Curated from 11,002 source videos at 1080p and 30–60 FPS through a unified pipeline of scene-consistent segmentation, multi-stage quality filtering, ViPE-based Huang et al. (2025) pose estimation with trajectory validation, and event-aligned multi-granular captioning, GameVerse delivers 69.7K clips , 1,655 hours , and 349.6M frames , including 1,358 hours of continuous two-minute sequences —roughly an order of magnitude longer than any prior gameplay or exploration dataset. Every clip is paired with a temporally stable frame-level camera trajectory that survives explicit jump-, jitter-, and drift-filtering; a four-level structured caption ( global static , local static , dynamic events , agent actions ) that disentangles scene context from foreground change; and dense behavior coverage across first- and third-person perspectives, spanning exploration, combat, walking, driving, climbing, and complex object interaction. To our knowledge, GameVerse is the first dataset to ship these four signals jointly under a single quality bar, and fine-tuning a strong video generator on it improves VBench Huang et al. (2024) temporal-consistency and visual quality, and WorldScore Duan et al. (2025) content alignment and 3D consistency, over gameplay-domain baselines on a 30-second benchmark. Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • The largest minute-scale gameplay dataset to date. 69.7K clips, 1,655 hours, and 349.6M frames, including 1,358 hours of continuous two-minute sequences—roughly an order of magnitude longer than existing gameplay corpora and the first to directly supervise the minute-scale rollout regime where current interactive world models fail. • A unified construction pipeline with reliable camera trajectories. Scene-consistent segmentation that combines hard-cut detection with CLIP-based gradual-transition detection, multi-stage visual / content / temporal quality filtering, and ViPE-based pose estimation with explicit jump-, jitter-, and drift-filtering—delivering temporally stable trajectories at minute scale, where most prior datasets either omit poses or expose noisy ones. • A multi-granular event-aligned caption schema. A four-level structured annotation ( global static , local static , dynamic events , agent actions ) that disentangles environment context from controllable behavior and supports hierarchical conditioning—replacing the single-line whole-clip captions that dominate prior gameplay data. • Empirical validation on long-horizon generation. Training on GameVerse improves VBench temporal-consistency and visual-quality metrics and most WorldScore controllability metrics over gameplay-domain baselines on a held-out long-duration benchmark.
