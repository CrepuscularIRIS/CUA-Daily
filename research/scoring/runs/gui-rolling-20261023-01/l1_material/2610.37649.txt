arxiv: 2610.37649 (2610.37649v1)
title: Can LLMs Uncover Hidden Physical Laws through Free Exploration?
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37649

§Abstract
Scientific discovery often begins with an unexpected observation that prompts a new research question. In a world with altered physical laws, can large language models (LLMs) notice unexpected behavior, such as heavier objects falling more slowly, and discover the law through experiments without being told what to investigate? To evaluate this ability, we introduce LongChang, an automated framework that creates simulated worlds with altered physics, where one or more laws differ from ordinary physics. The evaluated LLMs act as explorers, manipulating objects and building tools to investigate their own questions without research tasks or hints about these laws. Separate LLM agents generate and review scenes, review tool designs, and evaluate reports for correctness and experimental support using the altered physical laws and exploration records. These records guide scene evolution for further exploration, allowing the full cycle to run without human intervention. Among 12 LLMs, GPT-6-Astra achieves a discovery rate of 16.7%, while the other 11 score 0%. The exploration records reveal uninvestigated anomalies and only partial identification of the altered physical laws.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Large language models (LLMs) aid scientific discovery through hypothesis generation, experiment design, and evidence analysis. Scientific discovery often begins with an unexpected observation that prompts a new research question. To pursue such inquiry autonomously, LLMs must decide what to investigate and test their explanations through experiments. This ability could help scientists investigate unexpected phenomena and pursue questions they might otherwise overlook. LongChang’s name reflects independent inquiry, inspired by the philosopher Wang Yangming’s introspection in a cave at Longchang, where he reconsidered how to seek knowledge and reached an insight that helped shape his own School of Mind ( Yangming, 2026 ) . Physical law discovery involves inferring an environment’s laws from observations and experiments. PhysGym ( Chen et al., 2025 ) , NewtonBench ( Zheng et al., 2026 ) , and DiscoverPhysics ( Wiemann et al., 2026 ) assign law-discovery tasks. Tab. 2 compares their exploration guidance and experiments with those of LongChang. Without a research task or hints about altered physical laws, an LLM must notice phenomena worth investigating, choose a research question, and devise experiments to distinguish competing explanations. Can LLMs turn unexpected observations into research questions, conduct experiments, and discover altered physical laws autonomously? We introduce LongChang, an automated framework that generates simulated worlds with altered physics, where one or more laws differ from ordinary physics. This counterfactual setting tests whether LLMs independently notice departures from ordinary physics and investigate them without research tasks or hints about the altered physical laws. For example, slower falls for heavier objects could lead an explorer to ask how mass affects falling and design controlled comparisons to test this link. These laws are hidden from explorers but available for evaluating their reported explanations. LongChang’s base set in Tab. 1 comprises a normal control and 14 scenes with one altered physical law each. In the virtual “glass room” in Fig. 1 , explorers manipulate objects and build tools that interact with the scene objects within a shared MuJoCo rigid-body simulation ( Todorov et al., 2012 ) . Separate LLM agents review tool designs and evaluate reports against the altered physical laws and exploration records, requiring both correctness and experimental support for discovery. Unresolved questions and corrected errors in these records guide LLM scene designers to generate, refine, and compose scenes for further exploration, continuing the entire discovery cycle without human intervention. Among 12 evaluated LLMs, GPT-6-Astra achieves a discovery rate of 16.7% under free exploration, while the other 11 score 0%. Exploration records show some LLMs leave observed anomalies uninvestigated, while others experiment but only partially identify the altered physical laws, leaving their conditions or applicability unresolved. We contribute the LongChang framework for the complete discovery cycle and an empirical study of autonomous physical inquiry. We use model comparisons, controlled interventions, and exploration records to examine how LLMs form research questions and test explanations using experiments.
