arxiv: 2610.16260 (2610.16260v1)
title: ScreenASR-Bench: A Tiered Benchmark for Speech Recognition with Visual Context
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16260

§Abstract
Current ASR models perform well on general benchmarks but still struggle with acoustically ambiguous words. Visual context can aid recognition in screen-based scenarios, yet existing benchmarks provide limited insight into the benefits of different types of visual evidence. To address this gap, we introduce ScreenASR-Bench , a tiered benchmark with three progressively challenging levels: Explicit Textual Evidence (ETE), based on direct on-screen text; Explicit Visual Evidence (EVE), using visible content rather than on-screen text; and Implicit Semantic Evidence (ISE), requiring scene-level inference. Extensive experiments on ASR models, Large Audio-Language Models (LALMs) and Omni models show that incorporating visual context substantially improves recall over the audio-only setting across all three model families. The gains are largest for ETE and smallest for ISE, indicating that models leverage direct visual evidence more effectively than indirect semantic cues. To facilitate future research on visually grounded speech recognition, we publicly release ScreenASR-Bench.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Recent advances in speech foundation models [ 13 , 25 , 15 , 21 , 7 , 24 , 1 ] and large-scale datasets have driven substantial progress in automatic speech recognition (ASR), yielding low word error rate (WER) on standard benchmarks [ 17 , 3 , 9 , 18 , 8 ] . However, some ambiguous keywords remain difficult to recognize from speech alone, even when most of the surrounding utterance is transcribed correctly. Such localized errors may contribute little to aggregate WER while substantially changing the intended meaning. In audio-visual settings, on-screen text, visible entities or actions, and scene semantics can provide contextual visual evidence for recognizing these challenging words. This raises a key question: how effectively can current speech models exploit different levels of visual information for ambiguous keywords recognition? Prior studies have shown that visual context can improve recognition when lexical information is difficult to recover from the acoustic signal. Ghorbani et al. [ 11 ] demonstrate improved masked-word recovery with visual context, while Gabeur et al. [ 10 ] report larger visual gains under challenging acoustic conditions. Chen et al. [ 6 ] use on-screen text to enhance ASR, and Zhao et al. [ 34 ] combine lip-reading with textual and graphical information from presentation slides. More recently, Tian et al. [ 26 ] extend the scope of visual context to richer and more diverse scenes, including scenarios that require multimodal reasoning to resolve acoustic ambiguities. These studies demonstrate the value of visual information for speech recognition, but they generally treat visual context as a whole, without distinguishing between visual cues that differ in how directly they indicate the target keyword. A similar limitation persists in existing audio-visual ASR benchmarks. SlideSpeech [ 28 ] provides a large-scale slide-enriched corpus, while SlideAVSR [ 27 ] introduces a benchmark for recognizing technical terminology with presentation slides. Sinhamahapatra et al. [ 23 ] similarly evaluate multimodal speech recognition in conference presentations, and VAPO [ 12 ] introduces SlideASR-Bench for entity-rich slide-enhanced recognition. These benchmarks provide valuable resources for evaluating visually assisted ASR, but they treat all cases equally and do not distinguish different levels of visual evidence. As a result, it remains unclear which type of visual evidence a model can actually exploit. To address this gap, we present ScreenASR-Bench , a tiered benchmark for evaluating how models exploit different forms of visual evidence. It contains 2,002 cases collected from publicly accessible online videos and each case consists of a speech segment, a corresponding keyframe, a reference transcription, and a designated target keyword. As illustrated in Figure 1 , cases are categorized into three levels according to the relationship between the target keyword and the visual evidence: Explicit Textual Evidence (ETE), Explicit Visual Evidence (EVE), and Implicit Semantic Evidence (ISE), corresponding respectively to readable text, directly visible referents that are not shown as text, and indirect scene-level semantics. From ETE to ISE, the relationship between the target keyword and its visual evidence becomes progressively less direct, increasing the difficulty of effectively leveraging the visual context. In summary, the contributions of this paper are as follows: • We present ScreenASR-Bench , a tiered benchmark for evaluating how effectively speech models exploit different levels of visual evidence for challenging lexical recognition. • ASR models, LALMs, and Omni models are systematically evaluated on ScreenASR-Bench , providing a comprehensive analysis of their ability to leverage visual context across the three evidence categories. • We publicly release ScreenASR-Bench to facilitate reproducible research and promote further advances in multimodal ASR.
