arxiv: 2611.05873 (2611.05873v1)
title: Can LLM Agents Learn to Test? Maintaining Scoped Experience as Testing Evidence for Mobile GUI Testing
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05873

§Abstract
Repeated mobile GUI tests can reuse routes, locators, and recovery strategies from earlier executions. However, a route that worked in one workflow or app version may be irrelevant or misleading in another. We study how an LLM-based tester can retain useful experience while withholding entries contradicted by current observations. We present a maintenance contract for AdaptiTest in which reusable experience has an explicit applicability scope, trace provenance, and a lifecycle for confirmation, revalidation, and withdrawal. The contract reserves persistence for a Reflector role and treats recalled experience as advice. The archived implementation does not establish end-to-end enforcement of every specified mechanism. The design changes retrieved context, not model weights. The evaluation uses read-only Android workflows and archived internal comparisons. A six-task, three-repeat ready subset reports 18/18 completed runs with memory versus 3/18 without it, but a grounded single-agent control also solves the earlier hard-task slice. A separate cross-session probe is excluded from efficacy conclusions because of confounded route schedules and unvalidated state labels. We examine 13 selected real-update hazard records across nine subjects, distinguishing stale-locator observations from policy-based withholding and offline retrieval diagnostics. The latter do not measure live-device failures or actions saved. These events are UI changes, not confirmed regression bugs. The evidence supports a bounded design and case study; it does not establish general efficacy, superiority over independent GUI-agent systems, or unrestricted exploration.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
The same mobile test objective rarely appears only once. A regression check that reaches a settings page, verifies a search result, or exercises a template workflow may be rerun after a UI refresh, on a different device state, or during the next release cycle. Each run forces the tester to find the right entry point, choose an observation channel, recover from popups, and decide whether a previously valid route still reaches the intended state. LLM-based GUI agents make this repetition more visible because their interaction traces contain reusable operational facts: which shortcut reaches a target page, which screen requires OCR rather than the accessibility hierarchy, which recovery action works after a modal dialog, and which locator has become unsafe after an update. Discarding this experience forces each session to rediscover the same facts; replaying it uncritically can turn yesterday’s GUI observation into today’s wrong test instruction. Recent GUI-agent research already recognizes the value of memory. Mobile and desktop agents learn from demonstrations, retrieve internal experience, maintain task memories, and evolve shortcuts or executable routines [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 , 9 ] ; mobile GUI testing has likewise moved from random/model-based exploration toward LLM-guided and memory-augmented tools [ 10 , 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 , 15 ] . The testing challenge is therefore not simply adding long-term memory. It is making remembered GUI experience usable as testing evidence : scoped to where it was observed, linked to traces that can be audited, ranked conservatively when reused, and withdrawn when app evolution contradicts it. This paper studies whether an LLM-based mobile GUI tester can accumulate, reuse, and maintain such structured experience across repeated sessions. We use “learn” in the operational testing sense: retrieval-augmented experience accumulation that changes future test behavior, not model-weight updates. The goal is not to make memory always authoritative; it is to make memory useful when current evidence supports it and silent when the same evidence becomes stale. We present a testing-evidence maintenance contract for AdaptiTest , motivated by a mobile GUI testing pipeline and its archived experiments. The specification combines tri-channel perception, three memory layers, explicit validity scopes, Laplace-smoothed confidence, and a four-state lifecycle (Confirmed, Tentative, Suspect, Stale). It requires that only the Reflector persist or transition entries. This is a design requirement, not a verified invariant of every archived runner; Section III-G distinguishes the specification from the packaged implementation. Our primary contribution is a testing-oriented validity contract for reusable experience: an entry records where it applies, why it is trusted, and when it must stop influencing actions. Memory layers, ranking, lifecycle, and writer restriction support this contract; they are not independent claims of algorithmic novelty. We contribute a design specification, an audit of its partial implementation, and a bounded empirical study. Table III maps observations to evidence and limitations. For traceability, we distinguish three aspects: C1 (empirical characterization). We report archived task comparisons and real-update stale-locator cases, distinguishing execution observations, scripted route comparisons, and offline policy diagnostics. The repeated-frontier slice is retained only as a protocol audit; its efficacy conclusions are withdrawn. These slices do not establish general efficacy or broad superiority over published GUI-testing systems. C2 (testing-evidence representation). Building on persistent-memory GUI agents, we specify scoped episodic, semantic, and procedural entries with confidence, trace links, and a four-state retrieval lifecycle (§ III , Table II ). The ranking weights and promotion threshold are design choices; their exact implementation and use in the archived experiments are not established by the packaged memory manager. C3 (writer-isolation requirement). The contract requires proposed persistent changes to pass through a dedicated writer and enumerated lifecycle transitions. We state this as an implementation requirement, not a proved access-control property or an experimentally isolated contribution of the archived pipeline.

## III Approach
This section specifies the intended maintenance contract. Its role, confidence, and lifecycle descriptions are normative design requirements, not a claim that all archived experiments executed this exact implementation. The implementation boundary is stated in Section III-G . In the specified design, the runtime receives a test objective, observes the screen, retrieves experience, executes bounded actions, and checks outcomes. Completed traces are required to update memory through a single write path. The archived evaluation examines selected task outcomes and maintenance cases, not enforcement or optimality of the complete design. III-A Execution Model AdaptiTest implements mobile GUI testing as five separated roles: the Navigator plans and replans, the Perceiver observes, the Executor issues device actions, the Verifier checks postconditions and bug signals, and the Reflector distills completed traces. Agents communicate through typed payloads in a plan/execute/reflect loop. The decomposition exists to provide clean injection points for different kinds of experience (navigation, perception, recovery, verification) and to enforce that only the Reflector writes persistent memory, preventing every intermediate thought from becoming a future instruction. III-B Tri-Channel Perception The Perceiver normalizes three observation channels into a unified element descriptor: accessibility hierarchy (cheap and structured), optical character recognition (OCR; visible text in WebView or rendered content), and visual grounding (icons or canvas-like elements without useful accessibility nodes). The default channel is chosen by a highest-success-rate lookup over the semantic memory’s per-page channel-reliability entries, falling back to a target-description heuristic when no entry exists; if confidence is low, AdaptiTest invokes additional channels and fuses candidates. Each perceived candidate is represented as u=(\mathit{id},\mathit{source},\mathit{text},\mathit{type},\mathit{bbox},\mathit{conf},\mathit{attrs}) where \mathit{id} identifies the candidate, \mathit{text} is visible or extracted text, \mathit{type} is the element category, \mathit{bbox} is the screen bounding box, and \mathit{attrs} contains additional channel-provided attributes. \mathit{source}\in\{\mathit{hierarchy},\mathit{ocr},\mathit{vision}\} and \mathit{conf}\in[0,1] is channel-normalized confidence. When two channels produce overlapping bounding boxes for the same target, AdaptiTest merges their descriptors and keeps provenance for audit. The perception stack is a dependency of the runtime rather than the contribution of this paper; the contribution is how the resulting observations are stored, scoped, and retired as test evidence. III-C Experience Representation We model a test session as a trace T=\langle(\sigma_{1},a_{1},o_{1}),\ldots,(\sigma_{n},a_{n},o_{n})\rangle , where \sigma_{i} is the observed screen state, a_{i} is the action, and o_{i} is the outcome. In the design model, an experience entry is: e=(C,A,O,V,\Theta,\Gamma) (1) where C is context, A is action or strategy, O is outcome, V is validity scope, \Theta=(\mathit{use},\mathit{succ},\mathit{conf}) is reliability state, and \Gamma=(t_{\mathit{created}},t_{\mathit{used}},\mathit{ver}) is temporal metadata. \Theta is updated by the Reflector after every reuse; \Gamma is updated by the runtime. Here \mathit{use} and \mathit{succ} count attempts and successful reuses. Temporal fields record creation, last use, and app version; trace identifiers link the entry to its supporting observations. Splitting reliability from time avoids a circular definition: confidence is derived from \Theta rather than recomputed from \Gamma each retrieval. The schema is deliberately conservative relative to general GUI-agent memory systems. A stored trajectory, shortcut, or page fact is not promoted because it looks semantically similar to the current task; it is promoted only when its scope, reuse history, and current-screen evidence jointly support reuse. This is the point at which AdaptiTest departs from flat trace replay or generic retrieval-augmented planning: the same remembered action can be helpful evidence inside one workflow, irrelevant in a neighboring workflow, or stale after an app update. The validity scope V is essential. Entries can be universal (e.g., dialog dismissal), category-scoped (e.g., map place-detail markers), app-scoped, or page-scoped. Scope prevents a successful route in one context from silently becoming an instruction in another. The Reflector applies V via a two-step rule: (1) if the entry references a specific package identifier or page-level locator, it receives the narrowest matching scope; (2) otherwise, if it describes a cross-app pattern, it receives category or universal scope. Scope is persisted with app, workflow, version, and trace-provenance fields so that later retrieval can be checked against both the current query and the screen evidence that produced the entry. The runtime does not re-scope a Confirmed entry silently; it can only downgrade it to Suspect or Stale via the lifecycle. III-D Three Memory Layers AdaptiTest stores entries in three layers. Episodic memory records what happened in previous traces and supports plan recall. Semantic memory stores versioned app knowledge such as page-transition hints, widget catalogs, and perception-channel reliability. Procedural memory stores reusable strategies, such as recovery behaviors or non-default perception choices. At runtime, retrieved entries are injected as suggestions into the relevant agent role, as summarized in Table I . They are not hard constraints: current screen observations override memory. An episodic entry retains a completed objective, route, and outcome. A semantic entry summarizes a page fact, such as expected target markers or a previously effective observation channel. A procedural entry describes how to act, such as a recovery sequence or fallback strategy. In the WPS case below, the completed interaction is episodic evidence, the version-scoped locator-to-marker relation is semantic knowledge, and a reusable recovery strategy is procedural advice. Each component is normalized to [0,1] : \mathit{scope}(V,q) is an ordinal compatibility score (exact page/workflow =1.0 , app-scoped =0.7 , category =0.4 , universal =0.2 , incompatible =0 ), \mathit{sim}(C,q) is cosine similarity over text embeddings of the stored context and query, and \mathit{rec}(\Gamma)=\exp(-\Delta t/\tau) with \tau\!=\!30 days. The 0.4/0.4/0.2 coefficients are fixed design priors that encode the validity contract: scope and semantic similarity receive more weight than recency. This sum is a ranking heuristic, not a logical scope guarantee: similarity and recency can still favor an incompatible entry. Applicability and current-screen checks, rather than these coefficients alone, must determine whether a suggestion can be used. They are configuration parameters, not online-learned weights. Confidence is derived from \Theta via a Laplace-smoothed success rate \mathit{conf}(e)=(\mathit{succ}+\alpha)/(\mathit{use}+\alpha+\beta) with \alpha=\beta=1 as a neutral Beta prior; the contract permits injection of e into a Confirmed slot only when \mathit{conf}(e)\geq 0.60 , with lower-confidence entries routed to Tentative or Suspect handling. The threshold is intentionally low enough to avoid starving repeated-test reuse but paired with a distinct-session promotion rule to block one-shot confirmation. A fresh entry with \mathit{use}{=}\mathit{succ}{=}0 starts at \mathit{conf}{=}0.5 in Tentative; one successful reuse already raises confidence to 2/3 , above 0.60. Confirmation nevertheless requires success in two distinct sessions, yielding 3/4{=}0.75 . This separate guard, not the threshold alone, prevents one-shot confirmation. New procedural entries are tentative until they succeed across distinct sessions; repeated failure demotes or disables them. When retrieved entries exceed an agent’s prompt budget, the design requires AdaptiTest to rank by relevance and confidence, preserve trace identifiers, truncate the lowest-ranked suggestions first, and fall back to current-screen planning if no verified entry remains. Lifecycle. The contract requires retrieval to remain subordinate to current observations. After verification, the Reflector is required to update use counts and link entries to trace artifacts. Retrievable summaries must pass schema validation and retain provenance. These are design requirements; validation of summary structure alone does not establish provenance completeness or absence of private account information. III-E Staleness Handling An entry becomes suspect when version or contradiction signals fire (the app version has changed, or the Verifier emits a contradiction event), and stale when bounded revalidation on the current screen fails. Bounded revalidation re-checks the stored markers via at most k\!=\!2 retries on the current screen, restricted to read-only entries; any side-effectful or login-gated entry skips revalidation and remains Suspect until the next Reflector pass.
