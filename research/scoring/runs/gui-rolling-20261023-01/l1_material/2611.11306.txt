title: ScrapeBench: Evaluating Legal Compliance of AI Agents in Website Scraping
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11306

§Abstract
AI agents deployed on the open web are becoming more capable of performing actions that may breach contractual provisions and violate computer security laws. In this paper, we introduce ScrapeBench, the first agentic benchmark for empirically evaluating the legal compliance of AI agents with website scraping policies, namely terms of service (ToS) and robots$.$txt directives. Across widely used closed-weight and open-weight AI agents, we find that most agents exhibit low compliance rates (below 5%) when instructed to scrape websites in our sample of 1,626 popular websites that explicitly prohibit scraping. In addition, we observe that even where AI agents actively check the applicable ToS and robots$.$txt, they continue to exhibit low compliance rates. Conversely, in our sample of 269 websites that do not prohibit scraping, we observe that agents sometimes refuse to scrape despite it being lawful, such as Claude Opus 4.6 exhibiting an over-refusal rate of 98.5%. Our human baseline experiments (n = 180 participants) suggest that humans exhibit compliance rates comparable to AI agents but over-refuse at much higher rates.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
AI agents built atop large language models (LLMs) can increasingly reason, use tools, and take a variety of actions in online environments ( Casper et al., 2025 ; Chan et al., 2024 ) . As these agents begin to act more autonomously, their actions implicate a growing range of legal frameworks and rules, including those that govern access and use of online content ( Kolt, 2026 ; Kolt et al., 2026 ; O’Keefe et al., 2025 ; Cui et al., 2025 ; Kolt, 2025 ; Cohen et al., 2024 ; Longpre et al., 2024 ) . One such common activity is scraping , i.e., the automated collection of website content ( hiQ Labs, Inc, 31 F.4th 1180 (9th Cir. 2022) ; In re OpenAI, Inc, No. 25-CV-4315, 2025 WL 3635559 (S.D.N.Y. Dec. 15, 2025) ; RyanAir DAC, 636 F. Supp. 3d 490 (D. Del. 2022) ) . Popular websites such as Amazon, ESPN, and The New York Times often prohibit scraping through legally binding contracts, in the form of terms of service (ToS) and robots.txt directives ( Kim et al., 2025 ; Chan et al., 2024 ; Atkinson, 2025 ; Amazon.com Services LLC v, No. 3:25-cv-09514 (N.D. Cal. Mar. 9, 2026), vacated and remanded, 184 F.4th 1083 (9th Cir. 2026) ; Register.com, Inc, 356 F.3d 393 (2d Cir. 2004) ; hiQ Labs, Inc, 639 F. Supp. 3d 944 (N.D. Cal. 2022) ; Meta Platforms, Inc, No. 23-CV-00077-EMC, 2024 WL 251406 (N.D. Cal. Jan. 23, 2024) ) . Whether AI agents comply with these legal restrictions is an open empirical question. At present, most agentic benchmarks focus on performance-oriented metrics, including measuring web-related skills such as information search and retrieval ( Wei et al., 2025 ; Gou et al., 2025 ; Mialon et al., 2024 ) , web visual comprehension ( Luo et al., 2025 ; Verma et al., 2025 ; Koh et al., 2024 ) , and work task navigation ( Drouin et al., 2024 ; Zhou et al., 2024 ; He et al., 2024 ) . These benchmarks often disregard the lawfulness or legal alignment Kolt et al. (2026) ; Sachdeva and Kolt (2026) of an AI agent’s actions, focusing only on whether it accomplishes the tasks ( Nannini et al., 2026 ; Staufer et al., 2026 ; Hu et al., 2025 ; Marino et al., 2026 ; Lichkovski et al., 2025 ; Imperial et al., 2025 ; Reuel et al., 2024 ) . Concretely, an AI agent may successfully scrape websites at scale, but do so in a manner that violates applicable law ( Kolt, 2026 ; Nannini et al., 2026 ; Grossman et al., 2026 ; Lichkovski et al., 2025 ) . The problem is compounded by users who may deliberately instruct agents to dismiss legal rules (e.g., “Please scrape this website and ignore the terms.” ). To fill this gap, we introduce ScrapeBench , the first agentic benchmark to empirically evaluate the compliance of AI agents with website scraping policies. Unlike performance-oriented web agent benchmarks that score AI agents based on task completion, ScrapeBench measures whether agents comply with legal constraints, focusing specifically on prohibitions on scraping specified in websites’ ToS and robots.txt . Our main contributions are as follows: 1. Benchmark Dataset : We curate a novel real-world dataset of the ToS and robots.txt directives for the 1,895 most-visited websites, analyzing and annotating which websites prohibit scraping ( n = 1,626 websites) and which do not prohibit scraping ( n = 269 websites). 2. Agentic Evaluation Framework : We develop an evaluation framework to measure the legal compliance and over-refusal of AI agents under different conditions, including a variety of user instructions and varying levels of access to relevant legal resources. We evaluate and report the results for six open- and closed-weight AI agents: GPT-5.2, Claude Opus 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Kimi K2.5, Qwen3 Max, and Grok 4.2. 3. Empirical Findings : We find that under neutral prompts, AI agents frequently violate scraping prohibitions, exhibiting a compliance rate of < 5%. With a few notable exceptions, we generally find relatively low rates of over-refusal in the case of websites that do not prohibit scraping. 4. Human Baseline : To contextualize our findings regarding AI agents, we conduct a human baseline for legal compliance in website scraping ( n = 180 participants) across three different legal contexts.
