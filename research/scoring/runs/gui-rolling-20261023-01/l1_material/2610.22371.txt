title: Gen4P: Video Generators are 4D Perceivers
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22371

§Abstract
We introduce Gen4P, a unified framework for recovering 4D geometry and motion from monocular video. We show that a pretrained video generator can be repurposed into a feed-forward 4D perceiver without iterative denoising. Building on on-demand querying, Gen4P processes clean video latents in a single encoder pass and predicts the requested 4D attributes through a lightweight cross-attention decoder. The on-demand query formulation enables flexible spatial sampling, and our proposed continuous-time queries further provide temporal flexibility. To strengthen geometric knowledge, we propose a straightforward plug-in solution by incorporating a pretrained video-depth LoRA as a frozen geometry prior, improving overall performance without adding extra trainable parameters. Gen4P achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance on tasks of 3D point tracking, video depth and camera pose estimation. Qualitative results on in-the-wild videos illustrate its capability of tracking complex motion while maintaining the delicate structure. Together, these results demonstrate the potential of video generative pretraining for unified 4D perception. See https://gen4p.github.io for more visual results.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
We consider the problem of feed-forward full 4D reconstruction from monocular video. A unified 4D representation of scene geometry, depth, camera poses, and 3D point trajectories provides a foundation for simulating and interacting with the physical environment. However, recovering such a representation remains challenging: it requires accurately capturing both the static geometry and the dynamic motion of the scene across viewpoints and over time . Recent advances in feed-forward reconstruction demonstrate the value of pretrained foundational vision models. Geometry Foundation Models (GFMs), including VGGT ( Wang et al., 2025a ) , VGGT- \Omega ( Wang et al., 2026a ) , and DA3 ( Lin et al., 2025 ) , build on pretrained vision models ( Oquab et al., 2023 ; Siméoni et al., 2025 ) , adapting them through large-scale geometric supervision. These GFMs then provide strong geometry priors for subsequent 4D reconstruction: V-DPM ( Sucar et al., 2026 ) and 4RC ( Luo et al., 2026 ) , for instance, extend VGGT and DA3 to jointly recover geometry and motion. However, geometry priors alone do not explicitly model how points should evolve over time in dynamic scenes. Meanwhile, foundational generative models offer a complementary source of priors over appearance, geometry, and motion, by learning to synthesize images and videos ( Wan et al., 2025 ; ByteDance Seed Team, 2026 ) . Recent studies have exploited generative priors for 2D correspondence ( Hedlin et al., 2023 ) , depth estimation ( Ke et al., 2024 ; Hu et al., 2025 ) , 3D geometry recovery ( Zhang et al., 2025b ; Lu et al., 2025 ; Jiang et al., 2025 ) , motion tracking ( Zhu et al., 2026 ; Nam et al., 2026a ) , and general perception ( Wang et al., 2026b ) . However, they still address 4D perception through task-specific approaches. Besides, they often require dense, frame-aligned supervision, limiting their flexibility in handling arbitrary spatiotemporal video inputs. In this work, we introduce Gen4P , a 4D perceiver that leverages rich priors from large-scale pretrained video generators for full 4D reconstruction. Specifically, Gen4P tames a multi-step video generator into a single-step feed-forward 4D perceiver. To this end, the objective is formulated as learning a direct regression network, instead of the iterative denoising for geometric reconstruction in prior works ( Ke et al., 2024 ; Jiang et al., 2025 ; Lu et al., 2025 ) . Such a formulation requires only one encoder forward pass per input clip. The resulting scene tokens are then shared across queries, reducing the encoding cost over repeated 4D predictions. We obtain the full 4D attributes by querying the scene tokens using a lightweight cross-attention decoder. It decouples the output sampling from pre-extracted feature grids, allowing spatial flexibility in the output resolution. Additionally, we introduce a continuous-time query mechanism to handle variable-length video sequences effectively. Furthermore, we discover that a pretrained video-depth LoRA can further be incorporated as a frozen plug-in geometric prior, strengthening geometry without introducing additional trainable parameters. Our key contributions are summarized as follows: (i) we repurpose a video generator into a feed-forward 4D perceiver under sparse query supervision, offering an alternative to GFM-based methods; (ii) we introduce a continuous-time query mechanism together with on-demand decoding to provide temporal and spatial flexibility; (iii) we incorporate a frozen depth LoRA as a plug-in geometry prior , improving overall 4D reconstruction without adding trainable parameters; and (iv) we provide a comprehensive evaluation of our method on a set of 4D reconstruction tasks, complemented by qualitative results on in-the-wild videos, demonstrating its effectiveness and generalization.
