title: CUA-Master: Scaling Computer-use Agents through Open-World Recursive Improvements
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03126

§Abstract
Despite recent advances in CUAs, their development remains constrained by the limited diversity, controllability, and reliability of interaction data. Moreover, conventional optimization pipelines lack iterative feedback, while existing recursive improvement approaches often rely on self-generated experiences, limiting ability expansion and yielding diminishing returns. To address these challenges, we introduce CUA-Master, a nested double-loop recursive improvement framework. At the system level, an outer world-data-model flywheel continuously incorporates open-world corpus and leverages evolving model weaknesses to guide subsequent data construction and optimization iterations. At the data level, an inner flywheel couples open-world seeding and autocatalytic propagation. The former mines authentic workflows as seed tasks from instructional videos, and employs visual-semantic dual-path trajectory reconstruction; The latter transforms these seeds into reusable primitives for controllable synthesis of diverse, complex, and verifiable tasks. To enable efficient and reliable trajectory collection, we further introduce a feedback-guided snapshot-rollback retry mechanism in various real environments. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CUA-Master-27B achieves competitive performance across GUI-only and GUI-CLI hybrid benchmarks, scoring 80.2 on OSWorld Verified, 36.9 on OSWorld 2.0, 43.9 on WeaveBench, and 25.0 on Agents' Last Exam. Our framework also substantially improves the quality and efficiency of data synthesis, and exhibits continuous gains across recursive iterations.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Computer-use agents (CUAs) have emerged as a promising paradigm for automating real-world workflows across diverse software applications, and unlocking new levels of digital productivity. Recent advances in both foundation models [ 37 , 30 , 47 , 40 , 32 , 3 ] and specialized agents [ 51 , 49 , 17 , 28 , 9 ] have substantially improved their capabilities not only grounding of computer scenarios, but also in interactive reasoning and long-horizon execution. Besides, early CUAs operate predominantly via graphical user interfaces (GUIs), while subsequent works extend their capability scope by incorporating command-line interfaces (CLIs) [ 8 , 24 , 45 , 42 ] and extensible tools [ 16 , 19 ] . Nevertheless, developing powerful CUAs capable of reliably executing diverse, long-horizon, and cross-application workflows remains challenging. A fundamental bottleneck lies in how data construction and model optimization are coupled and recursively improved. Many existing pipelines follow a unidirectional paradigm of data generation followed by model training [ 51 , 49 , 28 , 18 ] , where data construction is insufficiently informed by the evolving weaknesses. Consequently, additional data may provide lopsided benefits without addressing persistent capability gaps. Recent recursive improvement approaches partially address this issue by incorporating iteratively self-generated data [ 50 , 33 , 75 ] . However, their exploration remains closed-world optimization, where such cycles still predominantly rely on the own knowledge and capabilities of the model itself. It is bounded by existing capabilities, potentially reinforcing distributional biases, and yielding diminishing marginal returns. These observations fundamentally motivate us to build a recursive system that continuously acquires new open-world knowledge, and systematically expands the performance boundary of the whole system. Beyond system-level limitations, a more concrete concern is the availability of large-scale, high-quality data that adequately captures the diversity and complexity of real-world trajectories. While human-annotated trajectories provide high-quality supervision, their substantial annotation costs and limited application coverage make large-scale collection impractical [ 51 , 20 , 25 , 50 , 13 ] . Therefore, synthetic data generation emerges as a promising alternative [ 44 , 60 , 39 , 21 , 41 ] . However, existing approaches face three intertwined challenges. First, diversity : tasks derived from restricted and similar sources often exhibit substantial homogeneity, leading to insufficient ability coverage and fast improvement saturation as data volume increases. Second, controllability : direct instruction rewriting and template-based generation offer limited control over operational abilities, complexity, and task dependencies, particularly for long-horizon and cross-application workflows. Third, reliability : scaling the collection of verified trajectories across diverse real-world environments becomes increasingly challenging as task complexity grows, leading to lower rollout success rates and frequent execution failures. Collectively, these limitations constrain not only the quantity of synthetic data, but also its effective coverage of the capabilities required by general-purpose CUAs. To address these limitations, we introduce CUA-Master, a nested double-loop recursive framework that integrates an outer world-data-model flywheel with an inner data flywheel. At the system level, the outer flywheel closes the loop among open-world exploration, data construction, and model optimization. By leveraging the evolving model’s capability gaps to guide subsequent exploration and data generation, while continuously incorporating external knowledge, it addresses both the unidirectionality of conventional training pipelines and the limited knowledge expansion of closed-world self-improvement. At the data level, the inner flywheel couples open-world seeding and autocatalytic propagation as two complementary driving forces. Open-world seeding mines diverse seed tasks from real-world instructional videos through hierarchical retrieval and filtering, and reconstructs their trajectories via a visual-semantic dual-path mechanism. The latter abstracts existing seeds into reusable primitives and composes them into verifiable tasks, with explicit control over task complexity and application scope. To improve rollout reliability and efficiency, we introduce a feedback-guided snapshot-rollback retry mechanism that preserves valid execution prefixes and recovers from localized failures without restarting entire rollouts. Extensive experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of CUA-Master. With only 27B parameters, it achieves scores of 80.2 on OSWorld Verified, 36.9 on OSWorld 2.0, 43.9 on WeaveBench, and 25.0 on Agents’ Last Exam, demonstrating competitive performance approaching markedly larger or closed-source models and strong execution efficiency.
