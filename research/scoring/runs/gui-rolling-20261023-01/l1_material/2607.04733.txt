title: LP-SFT: Keeping Plausible Alternatives Alive in Supervised Fine-Tuning
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04733

§Abstract
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is widely used to adapt pretrained language models to downstream domains, but can over-specialize the model and degrade its pre-existing capabilities. Standard cross-entropy drives probability mass onto the observed target token and can make plausible alternatives that the pretrained model itself endorsed vanish. Using Shannon and Renyi entropies, we show that pretrained next-token distributions exhibit a regular multimodal structure, with entropy peaks corresponding to different numbers of plausible alternatives. Motivated by this observation, we propose LP-SFT, a Local-Preserving Supervised Fine-Tuning objective with two key design principles: it removes the supervised token from a local top-$K$ candidate set to avoid conflict with cross-entropy, and applies a locally normalized KL loss to preserve relative preferences among the remaining non-label alternatives. Across mixed-domain and domain-specific fine-tuning experiments, LP-SFT consistently outperforms standard SFT and recent baselines in aggregate performance while keeping base-endorsed alternatives from vanishing, achieving a favorable balance between single-sample accuracy and finite-budget solution accessibility, as measured by pass@1 and pass@$k$, respectively, with only a modest increase in training cost.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) has become the dominant paradigm for adapting pretrained language models to instruction following, reasoning, coding, and domain-specific tasks [ 16 , 18 ] . Despite its simplicity and effectiveness, SFT can degrade pre-existing capabilities in two ways: over-specialization to the fine-tuning distribution can cause catastrophic forgetting of out-of-domain capabilities, while gains in single-sample accuracy can make correct solutions less accessible on challenging problems [ 21 , 15 ] . We argue that a significant portion of this degradation stems from the mismatch between the standard cross-entropy objective and the rich distributional knowledge encoded in pretrained models. Cross-entropy supervises each position with a single observed token and drives probability mass onto it, even when the pretrained next-token distribution holds several equally plausible continuations. Its effect is therefore not only to promote the target, but to abandon alternatives the model itself endorsed. We take this abandonment to be a principal source of forgetting: what SFT destroys is the set of continuations the pretrained model still considered reasonable. Using Shannon and Rényi entropies, we observe a multimodal structure with peaks near \ln k , corresponding to near-uniform plateau states over k plausible candidates. This structure persists across model families and datasets, showing that such alternatives are widespread rather than incidental, and that the knowledge at stake lies in the relative preferences among non-label candidates rather than in the target alone. Motivated by this structure, we propose LP-SFT ( L ocal- P reserving S upervised F ine- T uning). We first select a local top- K candidate set from the base distribution to capture plausible next-token alternatives at each position, with K motivated by the observed entropy modes. LP-SFT then follows two design principles. First, it removes the supervised token from this candidate set so that the preservation objective does not oppose cross-entropy. Second, it locally normalizes the base and trainable distributions over the resulting non-label set, preserving relative preferences without constraining its total probability mass, which prevents plausible candidates from vanishing (Figure 1 ). Gradient analysis and ablations validate these design choices, while the fixed top- K reference can be cached offline, avoiding the need for a frozen reference model during training. We evaluate LP-SFT on three base models and three fine-tuning datasets covering general instruction following, code, and mathematics. LP-SFT consistently improves aggregate performance over standard SFT and recent SFT objectives and achieves the best overall pass@ k performance. An additional Qwen2.5-72B scaling study confirms that these gains persist at substantially larger scale: LP-SFT has fine-tuning cost comparable to standard SFT, plus a one-time, reusable cache-construction cost. Our contributions are summarized as follows: \bullet We provide a systematic analysis of pretrained next-token distributions, revealing multimodal entropy structure and heterogeneous uncertainty regimes across token positions. \bullet We propose LP-SFT , an efficient objective with two complementary design principles: target-token removal avoids conflict with cross-entropy, while local normalization preserves relative preferences among plausible non-label alternatives. \bullet Extensive experiments across model families and scales up to 72B show that LP-SFT consistently improves aggregate performance over standard SFT and recent baselines, achieving a favorable balance among adaptation, capability retention, and finite-budget solution accessibility.
