arxiv: 2610.36874 (2610.36874v1)
title: AdaGround: Token-Normalized Adaptive Zooming with Verifier-in-the-Loop Refinement for GUI Grounding
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36874

§Abstract
GUI grounding on high-resolution screens fails mostly on small targets, and zooming in is a common training-free remedy. However, most existing zooming pipelines do not match the zoom to the target's size, accept the zoomed answer unconditionally and never revisit a wrong answer. To address these issues, we propose AdaGround, a training-free framework with three components: (1) Token-Normalized Adaptive Zooming, which sets the magnification, field of view and zoom depth of each sample from the model's own estimate of the target size; (2) Verifier-in-the-Loop Refinement, which uses one marked-click Yes/No verifier to keep or reject each zoom, choose between zoom chains, trigger re-grounding and, with an existence query, decide when to abstain; and (3) Compositional Structure Grounding, which composes spreadsheet cells from their headers. On Qwen3-VL models, AdaGround surpasses state-of-the-art training-free zooming methods and many trained GUI models, improving accuracy over the backbone by 7.4 to 20.6 points on OSWorld-G and ScreenSpot-Pro, and its gains carry over to held-out UI-Vision. Code is available at https://github.com/bcwang-sjtu/AdaGround

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Autonomous agents that operate graphical user interfaces (GUIs) are central to generalist assistants ( Nguyen et al., 2025 ; Tang et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2025 ) . Their perceptual core is GUI grounding , which takes an instruction and a screenshot and localizes the target element precisely enough to act on it ( Zheng et al., 2024 ; Hui et al., 2025 ) . Large vision-language models (VLMs) ( Bai et al., 2025a ; Guo et al., 2025 ; Anthropic, 2024 ) cast grounding as coordinate generation ( Cheng et al., 2024 ; Hong et al., 2024 ; Qin et al., 2025 ) , which remains fragile on professional, high-resolution software, where targets are small and dense ( Li et al., 2025b ) . Training-based specialists ( Gou et al., 2025 ; Wu et al., 2025b ; Liu et al., 2026b ; Zhou et al., 2026 ) and learned focusing ( Lei et al., 2026 ; Ouyang et al., 2026 ) improve it but need annotated data and re-training per backbone. This makes training-free test-time refinement attractive. It crops the screenshot around a coarse prediction and re-grounds at higher resolution. Shortcomings of training-free refinement. Most current refinement pipelines share five shortcomings. (i) Scale-agnostic zoom geometry. Iterative narrowing ( Nguyen, 2024 ) , ZoomClick ( Jiang et al., 2025 ) , AdaZoom-GUI ( Pei et al., 2026 ) , UI-Zoomer ( Tang et al., 2026a ) and MVP ( Zhang et al., 2026d ) set the crop by fixed ratios, size triggers, candidate spread or fixed-size attention windows. None relates the zoom to the number of visual tokens the target will occupy in the rendered view. (ii) Open-loop acceptance. The zoomed prediction replaces the coarse one unconditionally, even when the crop has discarded the context that identified the target. (iii) No second attempt. Without a check on the answer, a pipeline cannot tell which answers to revisit. (iv) White-box, vote-based aggregation. Attention-guided methods ( Xu et al., 2025 ) such as MVP need attention maps from inside the backbone. MVP also aggregates its views by spatial voting, which cannot tell which of two disagreeing views is right. (v) Always zooming and answering. Targets absent from the screen are solved by no view, and targets defined by their position in a structure, like a spreadsheet cell, lose their evidence in a tight zoom. Yet every pipeline zooms and answers. AdaGround. Our key observation is that differently zoomed views fail on different samples. On Qwen3-VL-8B ScreenSpot-Pro, a fixed crop and an adaptive one are right on 63.5\% and 59.6\% of samples, but at least one of them is right on 68.9\% . Refinement should therefore not commit to one zoom but choose, per sample, which zoomed answer to trust. The grounder itself can make that choice when shown a marked click. We propose AdaGround (Figure 2 ), a training-free framework in which the grounder sizes its own zoom and verifies its own answers. Token-Normalized Adaptive Zooming (TNZ) answers (i). From the model’s own bounding box on the coarse view, it solves per sample for the magnification, the crop side and the number of zoom stages. They are chosen so that the target spans a fixed number of visual tokens while the crop still covers the coarse localization error. Verifier-in-the-Loop Refinement (VLR) answers (ii)–(iv) and the absent targets of (v). One marked-click Yes/No verifier drives verification-gated acceptance , a verifier-arbitrated chain portfolio and negative-feedback re-grounding . The portfolio selects between the TNZ chain and a fixed-crop chain by verification instead of by vote, and re-grounding runs with the rejected location crossed out. With a companion existence query, the verifier also decides existence-gated abstention . Compositional Structure Grounding (CSG) answers the structural targets of (v) by composing a spreadsheet cell from its separately grounded column and row headers. Throughout, the VLM is a black box that takes images and text and returns text and first-token probabilities. One set of constants serves both backbones and all benchmarks (Section 3.5 ). Results. On OSWorld-G ( Xie et al., 2026 ) and ScreenSpot-Pro, with Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3-VL-32B, AdaGround is above every published result of MVP and ZoomClick (Figure 1 ), by up to 7.3 points. The lead is significant in every paired test against MVP’s per-sample predictions. AdaGround is also above all proprietary models and trained GUI specialists in Tables 1 and 2 , including 72B ones. Its gains are largest on ScreenSpot-Pro’s high-resolution screens ( +14.9 and +20.6 points over the single pass) and carry over to held-out UI-Vision ( Nayak et al., 2025 ) . On ScreenSpot-v2 ( Wu et al., 2025b ) , whose large targets the single pass already resolves, they are not significant. Our contributions are as follows: • TNZ sizes every zoom in visual tokens from the model’s own estimate of the target, so that the target reaches a legible span at any screen resolution (Property 1 ). • VLR makes the grounder its own verifier.
