title: DeVI: Physics-based Dexterous Human-Object Interaction via Synthetic Video Imitation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20841

§Abstract
Physics-based character control for dexterous Human-Object Interaction (HOI) relies heavily on motion-capture data, which limits its applicability beyond captured scenarios. Meanwhile, video generative models can synthesize realistic HOI videos for unseen objects and scenarios directly from text prompts, but 3D interactions learned from such synthetic data remain kinematic without ensuring physical plausibility. Imitating these videos with physics-based control is a promising direction, yet unreliable object tracking and hand-object misalignment make it difficult to obtain accurate imitation targets. We present DeVI (Dexterous Video Imitation), a novel framework that learns physics-based character control for dexterous HOI by imitating text-conditioned synthetic videos. To address unreliable 3D object motion reconstruction, we introduce hybrid imitation targets that combine reconstructed 3D human motion with tracked 2D object trajectories, bypassing the explicit reconstruction of the 3D object motion. Our Visual HOI Alignment further refines the human reference to align with both the generated video and the initial 3D object configuration, making it suitable for physical interaction. Extensive experiments show that DeVI's hybrid representation is more effective than those used by the baselines and that Visual HOI Alignment improves reconstruction quality and imitation success. We further demonstrate physically plausible functional manipulation from text instructions, including target-aware interactions and articulated object manipulation, without requiring motion-capture demonstrations.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Modeling physics-based human motion is essential for understanding realistic human behavior involving physical interaction with the environment, including dexterous object manipulation. Existing studies on human motion simulation ( Peng et al., 2018 ; Luo et al., 2023 ; Luo et al., 2024a ; Zhang et al., 2025b ; Tessler et al., 2024 ; Peng et al., 2021 ; Peng et al., 2022 ; Tevet et al., 2025 ; Yuan et al., 2023 ) primarily focus on human motion alone, while the approaches addressing HOI in physical environments often overlook dexterous hand-object interactions involving finger articulation ( Cui et al., 2024 ; Cui et al., 2025 ) or focus on specific domains such as sports ( Luo et al., 2024b ; Liu and Hodgins, 2018 ; Haarnoja et al., 2024 ; Hong et al., 2019 ) . Recent approaches show promising results in imitating dexterous HOI ( Wang et al., 2023 ; Wu et al., 2025 ; Lin et al., 2025 ) and in learning unified control policies ( Wang et al., 2025a ; Xu et al., 2025 ) , but still rely on 3D HOI motion-capture (MoCap) demonstrations ( Taheri et al., 2020 ; Li et al., 2023 ) , whose limited coverage constrains the diversity of interactions they can learn. Since dexterous manipulation is difficult to capture at scale, extending physics-based character control to diverse objects and novel functional interactions remains challenging. Meanwhile, video generative models ( Yang et al., 2024b ; Wan et al., 2025 ) enable zero-shot synthesis of high-fidelity HOI videos for unseen objects and scenarios from text prompts, offering rich knowledge of grasping and functional object use ( e.g. , drinking coke, opening a microwave). Recent methods leverage such synthetic videos for HOI generation ( Kim et al., 2025 ; Li and Dai, 2026 ) , but the resulting interactions remain kinematic rather than being driven by physics-based control. Imitating these videos with existing physics-based HOI methods ( Xu et al., 2025 ; Wang et al., 2023 ; Wang et al., 2025a ) is a promising direction, but it requires both human and object motion in 3D. While human motion can be recovered via HMR (Human Mesh Recovery) ( Shen et al., 2024 ; Wang et al., 2024b ) , as in human-only video imitation ( He et al., 2025 ; Zhang et al., 2025a ) , 3D object motion reconstruction from synthetic videos ( Patel et al., 2026 ; Kuang et al., 2026 ) remains unreliable due to monocular depth ambiguity. The resulting trajectory errors cause hand-object misalignment and hinder physics-based imitation (Fig. 4 ). Even with accurate human and object references, universal policies trained on limited 4D HOI datasets fail to generalize to such novel interactions (Fig. 10 ). To address these challenges, we present DeVI (Dexterous Video Imitation) , a framework that learns physics-based character control for dexterous HOI by imitating text-conditioned synthetic videos, without requiring 3D HOI MoCap demonstrations. A key advantage of DeVI is that it learns physics-based control for novel functional interactions, from grasp acquisition to text-driven object use. To bypass unreliable 3D object motion reconstruction, we introduce hybrid imitation targets that combine reconstructed 3D human motion with 2D object tracks from a generated HOI video. Notably, we observe that precise alignment between the lifted 3D human motion and the initial 3D scene is crucial for physical interaction (Fig. 6 ). We therefore introduce Visual HOI Alignment to refine the human motion to match both the reference video and the initial 3D object configuration. The resulting hybrid targets are used to train a humanoid control policy via reinforcement learning (RL) with a hybrid tracking reward that combines 3D human and 2D object tracking. We evaluate DeVI on both generated HOI videos with Internet-sourced objects ( 53 ) and 3D MoCap demonstrations from GRAB ( Taheri et al., 2020 ) . For video-based imitation, we compare our hybrid imitation targets against reconstructed 3D object motion ( Patel et al., 2026 ; Kuang et al., 2026 ; Kim et al., 2025 ) and 4D HOI references ( Xie et al., 2026b ; Li and Dai, 2026 ) . For MoCap-based imitation, we compare against methods that directly imitate 3D HOI motions ( Wang et al., 2023 ; Wang et al., 2025a ; Xu et al., 2025 ) , demonstrating the benefits of our hybrid tracking formulation even when accurate 3D references are available. We further demonstrate the advantages of synthetic video imitation through diverse functional manipulation scenarios, including target-aware interactions and articulated object manipulation. Finally, we validate Visual HOI Alignment through ablation studies and comparisons with existing HMR methods ( Wang et al., 2024b ; Wang et al., 2025b ; Shen et al., 2024 ) . In summary, our main contributions are as follows. (1) We introduce DeVI, a framework that learns physics-based character control for dexterous HOI by imitating text-conditioned synthetic videos.
