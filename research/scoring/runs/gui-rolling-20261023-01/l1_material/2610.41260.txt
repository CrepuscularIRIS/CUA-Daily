title: SketchDirector: Complex Scene Sketching via Agentic Layout Reasoning and Detail Refinement
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41260

§Abstract
Existing sketch generation methods often rely on image guidance and are largely limited to individual objects or simplified scenes, leaving text-guided complex scene sketching underexplored. We present SketchDirector, an agentic framework that mimics the human sketching workflow for automatic multi-concept scene sketching. Given a scene-level text prompt, SketchDirector first employs a large language model~(LLM) to decompose the scene into multiple draft assets, where each is represented by a set of Bézier curves. Then, an agentic reasoning loop iteratively plans and executes their spatial arrangement, guided by self-evolving memory. Finally, a detail refinement module leverages diffusion priors to enhance sketch quality while preserving geometric structure. To support future research and applications in multi-concept scene sketch generation, we further introduce the \textit{MCSketch8K} dataset, comprising editable vector assets, compositional layouts, and object-level manipulation annotations. Extensive experiments show that SketchDirector outperforms state-of-the-art baselines in semantic and compositional quality while effectively preserving overall contour organization during refinement. §Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Sketching is a concise visual representation that effectively captures the intentions of human beings. As its scope expands from individual objects to multiple concepts, scene sketching has shown great potential in applications such as animation, proof of concept, and interactive design, as in Fig. 2 (A). However, complex scene sketching requires more than depicting individual objects. It involves organizing multiple concepts, reasoning about their spatial relationships, and maintaining coherent details within a unified scene. Therefore, generating semantically aligned scene sketches with reasonable layouts and coherent details remains a big challenge. Previous automatic scene sketching generation methods ( Li et al. 2019a ; Li et al. 2019b ; Chan et al. 2022 ; Vinker et al. 2023 ; Liang et al. 2025b ) primarily operate at the holistic scene level, treating scene sketches as an indivisible entity without object representations, thereby hindering local editing and object reuse. Recent studies ( Wu et al. 2025 ; Zhang et al. 2026 ; Zhan et al. 2025 ) have explored object-aware generation, but objects are often treated as predefined components or auxiliary structures. Consequently, these methods lack dynamic control, layout planning, and iterative optimization for individual objects, which limits their potential as visual components. In contrast, real-world sketch creation is inherently iterative: artists continually revise, group, and rearrange individual elements to improve the overall composition and presentation. They also adapt the level of refinement to suit their creative goals, producing sketches that range from rough drafts to highly detailed renderings, as illustrated in Fig. 2 (B). Digital vector sketches are particularly well suited to such flexible and iterative workflows because they are naturally represented as collections of Bézier curves, enabling efficient programmatic manipulation. We thus propose SketchDirector , an automatic agentic framework ( Vinker et al. 2025 ; Du et al. 2026 ; Liu et al. 2025 ; Liang et al. 2025a ) for text-guided multi-concept scene sketch generation by mimicking the pipeline of human creators. Specifically, given a textual description, SketchDirector first employs a Large Language Model (LLM) based method to decompose the scene and generate each individual object as draft asset, each represented as a set of Bézier curves. To enable layout control between multiple asset, we introduce an operating action library comprising a set of executable operations, e.g., move() , scale() , to manipulate object attributes, enabling flexible adjustment of spatial relationships among objects. Building upon this library, we propose an agentic reasoning loop, which formulates layout manipulation as an iterative operations and execution process. To achieve reliable closed-loop reasoning, rather than predicting the entire scene layout at once, SketchDirector progressively organizes draft assets through an iterative Plan–Execute–Verify–Correct process. At each step, SketchDirector generates and executes object-level operations, updates the scene representation, and evaluates the resulting composition through geometric checks and multimodal verification. When the layout fails verification, SketchDirector revises its decisions and corrects the layout using a hierarchical self-evolving memory that leverages short-term failure reflections and accumulated long-term experiences. After that, we introduce an additional detail refinement step to address the challenges of structural consistency in scene sketch distillation from the diffusion prior. Unlike traditional methods that treat Bézier curves as unstructured point sets, we treat object curve segments as the optimization primitives. Then, by jointly optimizing local objects and the global canvas using pre-scene distillation, our method is able to generate scene sketches with varying levels of object refinement while maintaining consistency with the textual description. Finally, with the lack of available object-aware and editable scene sketch datasets, we construct a multi-concept scene sketch dataset called MCSketch8K to facilitate future research and applications in scene sketch generation. The dataset comprises 1,000 scene sketches with over 4,000 disentangled vector object assets across 800 object categories. Each scene is annotated with compositional layouts and object-level manipulation operations, providing structured supervision for object decomposition, spatial arrangement reasoning, and controllable scene editing.
