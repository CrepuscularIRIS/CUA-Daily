title: Texture Consolidation of Generated Multi-View Renderings
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36098

§Abstract
We present a system for consolidating a sparse set of multi-view renderings into a single coherent texture image, providing a framework for integrating generative models for image generation within the geometry-processing pipeline. Given a texture-atlased triangle mesh and a set of renderings with associated camera parameters, we generate the texture image by: (1) back-projecting the renderings onto the mesh to obtain texture images with normalized colors and confidences; (2) solving for flow-fields on the mesh that align the colors to each other, taking into account the associated confidences; and (3) fusing the registered colors into a single texture image using gradient-domain stitching, again incorporating confidences. Our approach complements existing techniques for multi-view image generation, leveraging mesh-based optical flow and texture-space stitching. In doing so it addresses the challenge that while modern image generation pipelines generate highly convincing renderings, they struggle to produce renderings that are consistent across views. Code is available at https://github.com/hliuw/TextureConsolidation.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
The generation of 3D assets from generated multi-view renderings has advanced considerably in recent years [ PJBM23 , RMA∗23 , GLAK25 ] . Leveraging powerful diffusion-based models, these methods generate high-quality multi-view images for diverse 3D content. However, the renderings often exhibit inconsistencies when mapped onto 3D geometry, making it difficult to integrate them into a single, view-independent, model. Motivated by this challenge, we consider the following problem: Given a set of multi-view renderings and a texture-atlased mesh, how do we construct a globally consistent texture image? This question has been explored for real-world RGB-D datasets, where approaches typically address the problem through view selection, pose optimization, registration, and blending. However, generated renderings differ from real-world captures in that they lack a coherent, view-independent model from which the renderings are generated, often resulting in geometric and photometric inconsistencies between renderings. As a result, while generating new views is computationally cheap, additional renderings have the down-side of introducing further inconsistency. This makes view augmentation a trade-off, in contrast to real-world captures where more data is preferred. In particular, this motivates the need for robust image registration and fusion – supporting the generation of higher-quality textures from a small number of renderings. While many multi-view texture registration methods exist, they are predominantly designed for dense RGB-D captures, relying on view selection to discard low-quality frames affected by motion blur, and having lighting and shading artifacts. generated multi-view renderings that sparsely sample the view sphere present a different set of challenges: each point is visible in only a few views; and generated views are not geometrically consistent, so that back-projection produces misaligned textures. We address these challenges by designing a confidence-aware pipeline for multi-view texture consolidation that back-projects the renderings to texture images and then registration and stitching in the surface domain. We extend existing optical flow [ PKC∗16 ] and gradient-domain stitching [ PGB03 , LZPW04 , PKCH18 ] techniques, adapting them to support per-texel confidences. This is achieved via quadrature-based finite-elements integration supporting sub-triangle evaluation of functions, thereby bridging texel-resolution and mesh-resolution discretizations. Our method is agnostic to the rendering pipeline, serving as a general post-processing module that complements prior multi-view generation methods. Empirically, we show that existing registration methods for RGB-D captures degrade on generated inputs that sparsely sample the view sphere, while our framework produces high-quality texture images in this challenging setting.
