title: Learning to Look Beyond Screens: Transferable Visual Observation from GUI Grounding
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37093

§Abstract
Visual perception is evolving from passive recognition toward active, task-oriented observation. We investigate GUI grounding as a scalable source of spatial supervision for learning observation behaviors that transfer beyond interfaces. We introduce LBS (Look Beyond Screens), a vision-language model trained through supervised initialization on teacher-generated GUI grounding trajectories, followed by reinforcement learning for multi-turn crop-and-inspect decisions. Under fixed inference settings, LBS models based on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and Qwen3.8-27B consistently improve over their corresponding baselines. Gains beyond supervised fine-tuning transfer to PerceptionBench for both backbones and to HR-Bench and ChartQAPro for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, supporting observation strategies that generalize beyond GUI-specific imitation. Further analysis suggests complementary roles for the two stages: SFT establishes visual inspection primitives, while RL improves their downstream effectiveness. These findings support GUI grounding as a practical supervision paradigm for transferable active visual perception and broader multimodal reasoning. Our code and model are publicly available at https://github.com/inclusionAI/UI-Venus/tree/LBS and https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/LBS-27B, respectively.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Code: https://github.com/inclusionAI/UI-Venus/tree/LBS Model: https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/LBS-27B Active visual perception requires a vision-language model (VLM) to acquire evidence as well as interpret it. On a high-resolution image, the model must locate relevant regions, select an appropriate view, and relate local detail to the original scene ( Khayatkhoei et al., 2025 , Bai et al., 2025 ) . Crop-and-inspect and think-with-image methods demonstrate the value of additional visual observations ( Qi et al., 2025 , Zheng et al., 2026 , Wu and Xie, 2024 , Wang et al., 2026a ) . A central training challenge is to connect these intermediate observation decisions to task success, particularly when a correct final answer can follow either informative or redundant inspection. GUI grounding provides a concrete setting for learning this connection. Each example pairs an instruction with a spatial target, making both the final localization and intermediate crop geometry measurable ( Cheng et al., 2024 , Xu et al., 2025 , Li et al., 2025 ) . These annotations can therefore supervise intermediate observation decisions without specifying an ideal crop sequence for reinforcement learning. We hypothesize that such supervision can help elicit latent visual knowledge in pretrained VLMs by training them to put this knowledge into practice through sequential observation: deciding where to inspect, how much context to retain, and when further evidence is useful. Although the source images are interfaces, these decisions are also relevant to natural images and charts. This motivates our central question: can spatial supervision from GUI grounding elicit observation behavior that transfers to non-GUI perception? Such transfer would suggest a practical way to make pretrained visual knowledge more accessible through interaction, potentially reducing the need to construct specialized observation trajectories for each downstream domain. We address this question with a two-stage framework. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on teacher-generated grounding trajectories initializes the crop-and-inspect protocol. Multi-turn reinforcement learning (RL) then refines the policy using grounding correctness and training-only target annotations ( Guo et al., 2025 ) . Outcome-gated spatial rewards connect intermediate inspections to task success, a small-target bonus encourages target coverage, and selective response masking controls which intermediate responses receive policy updates. In this way, spatial annotations provide supervision for the process of acquiring visual evidence as well as for the final grounding result. The same crop interface is used at inference across domains, with task-specific final answers. This design allows us to examine whether observation behavior learned under GUI supervision benefits tasks with different visual content and objectives. Our results support transfer beyond supervised initialization. RL improves ScreenSpot-Pro ( Li et al., 2025 ) accuracy over SFT by 1.83 and 6.13 percentage points for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and Qwen3.8-27B, and improves PerceptionBench ( Lin et al., 2026 ) by approximately 3.0 and 7.10 points. The Qwen3.5-based policy also improves on HR-Bench 4K/8K ( Wang et al., 2025 ) and ChartQAPro ( Masry et al., 2025 ) . The reward comparison reveals a useful distinction: outcome-only RL improves source-domain grounding but lowers PerceptionBench accuracy, whereas the spatially supervised configuration improves both. These comparisons show that higher GUI grounding accuracy does not necessarily imply stronger non-GUI transfer; the results highlight the value of supervising intermediate observation decisions. Comparisons of reconstructed interaction histories further examine how crop images and their presentation scale affect answer generation. Together, these findings support GUI grounding as a practical source of supervision for visual observation whose benefits extend beyond the source domain, consistent with the hypothesis that spatially grounded interaction training can help elicit and apply pretrained visual knowledge across tasks. Our contributions are threefold: • We establish empirical evidence that GUI grounding can supervise visual observation policies that transfer beyond interfaces, with gains over SFT across two backbones on PerceptionBench and across additional high-resolution and chart benchmarks for Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. • We develop a two-stage framework that converts target annotations into outcome-gated spatial rewards and selective response updates for multi-turn crop-and-inspect training. • We show that improved grounding under outcome-only RL need not translate into improved non-GUI perception, and complement the training comparison with controlled edits to historical crop observations.
