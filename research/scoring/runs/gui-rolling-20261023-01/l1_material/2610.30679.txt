title: AgentDreamer: Agentic 3D Motion Reasoning for Text-Controllable Physics Simulation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30679

§Abstract
We propose AgentDreamer, an agent-guided optimization framework that aligns text instructions with simulated motion through explicit 3D motion planning. Given a text instruction and scene geometry, an off-the-shelf agent translates the instruction into a structured semantic motion prior, specifying what should move, in which direction, and in what temporal order. The agent represents this prior using parametric functions over a series of temporal chunks, yielding dense 3D trajectories. We then optimize the parameters of a differentiable MPM simulator such that its rollout best matches the prior while remaining constrained by the scene dynamics. This allows the agent to provide semantic guidance from language, while the simulator grounds it in physically feasible motion. We further introduce Motion-IG, an information-theoretic metric for text-motion alignment based on masked text recovery. By comparing the likelihood of recovering masked motion-related content with and without the simulated motion, Motion-IG measures the information contributed by the motion beyond that available from the remaining text and scene context. Experiments show that AgentDreamer improves text alignment over video-based guidance, producing simulations that more closely follow the requested physical behavior.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Controlling physical motion in simulated scenes is important for creating dynamic environments for embodied AI and interactive content generation. Without an intuitive control interface, users must manually tune simulation parameters through repeated simulation and adjustment to obtain the desired motion. Text-controllable physics simulation offers a promising alternative by allowing users to directly describe the intended behavior in natural language. Recent approaches, including PhysFlow ( Liu et al., 2025 ) and MotionPhysics ( Wang et al., 2026 ) , leverage video generative priors to guide the optimization of simulation parameters. In this way, they connect high-level user specifications to the dynamics of a 3D scene. Despite this progress, the resulting simulation may not reproduce the physical behavior described by the text. A central difficulty lies in the motion reference itself: visually plausible video generation does not ensure that the requested dynamics are represented correctly. For example, a request for a rolling ball may produce sliding, omitting the rotation that distinguishes the two behaviors. Optimizing a simulator to follow such a reference can therefore yield physically plausible motion that remains inconsistent with the text. This discrepancy motivates explicit 3D motion planning that describes how the object should move to reproduce the requested behavior. We propose AgentDreamer, a framework for text-controllable physics simulation that bridges text and simulated motion through agent-based 3D motion planning. Our key idea is to formulate motion planning as a physical reasoning problem. Given a text instruction and scene geometry, an off-the-shelf multimodal agent draws on its language understanding and physical knowledge to infer how the object should move, as shown in Figure 1 . The agent thus takes a direct role in constructing the motion plan, extending its use beyond estimating material parameters ( Liu et al., 2025 ; Wang et al., 2026 ) . This plan provides explicit 3D guidance for translating the text into simulated motion. To support motion reasoning, we introduce automatic semantic injection that associates sampled points with visible object parts. We provide a scene image with colored point markers corresponding to the 3D point input, together with coordinate-system information. This correspondence helps the agent interpret the role of each point in the requested motion. The agent then divides the time horizon into temporal chunks and infers a parametric motion function for each. These functions describe distinct motion stages and provide dense trajectories without predicting each future position independently. The resulting trajectories serve as pseudo supervision for a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator. We optimize simulation parameters to match these trajectories, guiding the simulator toward physically plausible motion aligned with the text. We further introduce Motion-IG, an information-gain-based metric for measuring text-motion alignment through masked text recovery. An MLLM-as-a-judge score ( Zheng et al., 2023 ; Chen et al., 2024 ) , read as the model’s verbalized confidence, considers the complete text and simulated motion together, without isolating the information provided by the motion. We instead mask motion-related content in the text and compare its recovery likelihoods with and without simulated motion under a fixed evaluation model. The difference measures how much the motion contributes to recovering the masked content beyond the remaining text. Experiments demonstrate that AgentDreamer improves text alignment over video-based guidance, producing simulations that more closely follow the requested physical behavior. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose AgentDreamer, a framework for text-controllable physics simulation that bridges text and simulated motion through agent-based 3D motion planning. • We formulate motion planning as physical reasoning through parametric functions over temporal chunks, providing dense trajectories as pseudo supervision for differentiable physics optimization. • We introduce Motion-IG, an information-gain-based metric for text-motion alignment that compares masked text recovery likelihoods with and without simulated motion.
