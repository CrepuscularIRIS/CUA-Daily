title: Frontier3DBench: Benchmarking Coding Agents for 3D Creation across Objects, Scenes, and Interactive Open Worlds
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09017

§Abstract
Coding agents combine visual understanding with program execution, offering a new route to automated 3D creation. By translating images and instructions into editable programs, they bring reconstruction, generation, and editing into a shared workflow. Yet existing benchmarks fragment evaluation across task scopes and protocols, leaving their capabilities across construction levels unclear. We propose Frontier3DBench, a unified benchmark of 145 tasks across 9 categories spanning object, scene, and interactive open-world creation, with joint evaluation of appearance, structure, and behavior. Experiments with 11 model-harness configurations reveal strengths that vary by level: GPT-6.1-sol leads the object and scene levels and achieves the highest overall score of 66.02/100, whereas GPT-6-astra leads interactive world construction. However, all configurations score lower on reference reconstruction than on text-driven generation in object and scene tasks. Producing plausible content thus remains easier than reproducing specified geometry, layouts, and visual details. Moreover, generated worlds average 83.08/100 on local mechanisms but only 20.82 on motion clearance and 30.26 on stateful workflows: individual mechanisms often function, whereas complete activities that require coordinated actions and persistent state remain difficult. These results identify reference fidelity and coordinated activity completion as central bottlenecks for coding agents in 3D creation.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Automating 3D creation has long been a central pursuit in computer vision, spanning reconstruction, generation, and editing [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 ] . Yet these tasks demand more than recognizing visual content: they require translating observations and instructions into explicit geometry and spatial structure. Coding agents offer a new route toward this goal because they combine strong visual understanding with the ability to write and execute programs that construct 3D content [ 8 , 9 ] . We argue that coding agents will replace traditional, task-specific computer vision pipelines for 3D creation. The critical question is therefore: how far have coding agents come in 3D creation? Existing benchmarks, however, fragment this evaluation across different construction tasks and application settings. For example, object and scene authoring benchmarks assess geometry, visual fidelity, construction, and editing [ 10 , 11 ] , whereas executable-world and game benchmarks emphasize mechanical dependencies, interactive behavior, and gameplay completion [ 12 , 9 , 13 ] . Because their task scopes and evaluation protocols differ, these results are difficult to combine into a consistent account of an agent’s capabilities across objects, scenes, and interactive worlds. A single aggregate score can also obscure whether an agent fails to match a reference, organize a scene, or implement the required behavior. Object fidelity, scene coherence, and world functionality impose distinct construction requirements. A unified benchmark must therefore evaluate each level through dedicated tasks and complementary visual, structural, and behavioral checks. Our key insight is that successful 3D creation demands faithful appearance, coherent structure, and functional behavior. Accordingly, we organize evaluation around objects, scenes, and interactive open worlds, requiring executable, editable artifacts at every level. Within this hierarchy, we assess object geometry and detail, scene layout and composition, and world-level appearance and activity completion through complementary visual, structural, and behavioral checks. Rendered views measure appearance, native scene inspection measures structure, and runtime tests measure behavior. Following these principles, we introduce Frontier3DBench , comprising 145 tasks across 9 categories: 65 object tasks, 65 scene tasks, and 15 interactive open-world tasks. The suite covers generation, reconstruction, and scene editing in Blender, together with interactive world construction in Unreal Engine. Evaluation of 11 model–harness configurations reveals three key findings. First, model strengths vary by level: GPT-6.1-sol leads the object and scene levels and achieves the highest overall score of 66.02/100, whereas GPT-6-astra leads interactive world construction. Second, every configuration scores lower on reference reconstruction than on text-driven generation in both object and scene tasks, highlighting the difficulty of matching specified geometry, layouts, and visual details. Third, local mechanisms remain far stronger than coordinated activities: across all 165 L3 submissions (11 configurations \times 15 tasks), the local-mechanism component of interaction correctness averages 83.08/100, compared with 20.82 for motion clearance and 30.26 for stateful workflows. These findings point to reference fidelity and the coordination of moving parts, actions, and persistent state as the main open challenges in 3D creation. Our contributions are threefold: • A unified benchmark for coding agents in 3D creation. Frontier3DBench comprises 145 tasks across 9 categories, bringing object, scene, and interactive-world construction into a shared suite covering generation, reconstruction, and scene editing. Dedicated tasks at each level reveal whether an agent’s strengths extend from individual assets to complete environments. • Joint evaluation of appearance, structure, and behavior. We assess executable Blender and Unreal Engine artifacts through visual judgments, structural measurements, and independent runtime tests, connecting content quality with action effects, state consistency, and activity completion. • An empirical account of current capabilities and bottlenecks. Across 11 model–harness configurations, we identify strengths that vary by construction level and persistent gaps in reference fidelity and coordinated activity completion. We further analyze capability dimensions and API costs to characterize differences in performance and resource demands.
