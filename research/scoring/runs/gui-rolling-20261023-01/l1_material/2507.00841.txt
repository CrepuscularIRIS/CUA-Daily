title: SafeMobile: Chain-level Jailbreak Detection and Automated Evaluation for Multimodal Mobile Agents
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00841

§Abstract
With the wide application of multimodal foundation models in intelligent agent systems, scenarios such as mobile device control, intelligent assistant interaction, and multimodal task execution are gradually relying on such large model-driven agents. However, the related systems are also increasingly exposed to potential jailbreak risks. Attackers may induce the agents to bypass the original behavioral constraints through specific inputs, and then trigger certain risky and sensitive operations, such as modifying settings, executing unauthorized commands, or impersonating user identities, which brings new challenges to system security. Existing security measures for intelligent agents still have limitations when facing complex interactions, especially in detecting potentially risky behaviors across multiple rounds of conversations or sequences of tasks. In addition, an efficient and consistent automated methodology to assist in assessing and determining the impact of such risks is currently lacking. This work explores the security issues surrounding mobile multimodal agents, attempts to construct a risk discrimination mechanism by incorporating behavioral sequence information, and designs an automated assisted assessment scheme based on a large language model. Through preliminary validation in several representative high-risk tasks, the results show that the method can improve the recognition of risky behaviors to some extent and assist in reducing the probability of agents being jailbroken. We hope that this study can provide some valuable references for the security risk modeling and protection of multimodal intelligent agent systems.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
In recent years, with the breakthrough progress of multimodal foundation models (VLMs) [ 1 , 2 , 3 ] , intelligent agent systems [ 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 ] driven by vision-language models (mobile agents [ 8 , 9 , 10 ] ) have developed rapidly and are widely applied in critical scenarios such as mobile device operations, virtual assistant interactions, and the automated execution of multimodal tasks. These systems typically combine graphical user interface (GUI) input with natural language planning, possessing strong task generalization and autonomous decision-making capabilities, significantly advancing the intelligent development of human-machine collaboration. However, these mobile agent systems driven by large language models (LLMs) [ 11 , 12 ] also face unprecedented security challenges, specifically jailbreak attacks [ 13 , 14 , 15 ] . Attackers can manipulate the agent through prompt-based inducement or environmental configurations, guiding it to bypass predefined behavioural norms and execute high-risk tasks such as tampering with settings, forging identities, or making malicious payments, causing severe real-world harm [ 16 , 17 , 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 ] . Compared to traditional text-based dialogue systems, jailbreak attacks on mobile agents pose more severe risks due to their higher operational privileges, richer interaction modalities, and direct impact on user device states, making security issues particularly prominent [ 22 , 23 , 24 ] . In practice, we identify two key challenges in defending against mobile agent jailbreaking: (1) Difficulty in behavior-level defense : mobile agents face multimodal input and long-horizon task planning. Existing prompt-level defense mechanisms designed for language models often fail to detect risk evolution across time steps, making it difficult to effectively defend against chained attack paths; (2) Lack of evaluation mechanisms : in GUI scenarios, task formats are complex, and current defense evaluations heavily rely on manual design and verification, which are not only inefficient and prone to omissions but also difficult to generalize. To address this, we propose a security-autonomous defense framework named SafeMobile ( TAME M obile A gents) in Fig. 1 , which consists of two pluggable components: (1) SafeTrajGuard : A behavior trajectory defense mechanism based on LLMs, utilizing our proposed SafeTrajDPO training method, trained using structured GUI positive and negative samples, combining historical trajectories with current action states to make safety judgments, thereby effectively blocking potential escape paths without affecting task completion; (2) GPTJudge : An LLM-as-Judge-style automated assessment framework that designs structured evaluation prompts to output safety scores (G-Score) and jailbreak success rates (G-ASR) for each behavioral trajectory, achieving a highly consistent and low-cost evaluation process. Experimental results show that in six high-risk task scenarios and two representative mobile agent systems, SafeMobile increases the average G-Score by 52.9 points, reduces the G-ASR by 78.4%, and maintains a stable Task Completion Rate (TCR) (with changes not exceeding 5.6%). Additionally, the GPTJudge evaluator achieves an average consistency (RSS) of at least 0.85 with human annotations across over 300 tasks, effectively replacing traditional manual evaluation processes. The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: 1. We provide the first formal definition of the mobile agent jailbreak defense problem and propose the first effective autonomous defense system, SafeMobile , encompassing the defense module SafeTrajGuard and the evaluator GPTJudge ; 2. SafeTrajGuard introduces SafeTrajDPO , a training mechanism that integrates action and trajectory inputs to enable high-confidence safety prediction based on historical context and the current action; GPTJudge performs task-level risk scoring and jailbreak success evaluation without human intervention; 3. In six high-risk task categories and 330 jailbreak tasks, we systematically validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework under two mainstream mobile agent systems, demonstrating enhanced system safety, preserved task utility, and an efficient automated evaluation pipeline-paving a new paradigm for securing mobile intelligent agent systems.

## II Motivation and Challenges
II-A Jailbreak Attacks against Mobile Agents In mobile agent systems, agents perceive natural language task descriptions \mathbf{x}^{\text{text}} and the initial GUI screenshot \mathbf{x}^{\text{gui}}_{0} through perception, and combine them with historical traces \mathbf{h}_{t} to generate multi-step action sequences to fulfill user intent. We model mobile agents f_{\text{m}} as a multi-step decision-making system, where the process of generating action a_{t} at step t can be formalized as: a_{t}=f_{\text{m}}(\mathbf{x}^{\text{text}},\mathbf{x}^{\text{gui}},\mathbf{h}_{t-1}),\quad a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}, (1) where \mathbf{h}_{t-1} denotes the historical trace, containing the actions executed and observed states in the previous t-1 steps; \mathcal{A} is the complete action space, covering two types of behaviors: safe actions \mathcal{A}_{\text{safe}} and unsafe actions \mathcal{A}_{\text{unsafe}} . During normal task execution, the agent should always output a sequence of safe actions \{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{T}\} that satisfies: \forall t\in[1,T],\quad a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{\text{safe}}. (2) The goal of a jailbreak attack is to construct specific input pairs ( \hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{text}},\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{gui}} ) to induce the intelligent agent to deviate from its expected safe behavior trajectory and execute a harmful action \hat{a}_{t} at any step, satisfying: \exists t\in[1,T],\quad\hat{a}_{t}=f_{\text{m}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{text}},\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{gui}},\mathbf{h}_{t}),\quad\hat{a}_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{\text{unsafe}}. (3) When \hat{a}_{t} is actually executed by the system, the behavior constitutes a successful jailbreak operation. For example, \hat{a}_{t} may include: unauthorized access to contacts, modifying system settings in the background, triggering unexpected payments, etc .Compared to traditional large language models, mobile agents possess multi-modal sensory input, continuous task decision chains, and high-privilege execution capabilities on real devices. Therefore, jailbreak attacks in this scenario are more covert, have more complex attack paths, and pose more severe potential risks. II-B Defense Objective We model the defended agent as a decision agent f_{\text{d}} with defense capabilities, whose behavior should satisfy two core objectives: safety constraints and functional preservation . Safety Constraints : For any potentially malicious inputs, the defense agent must ensure that the generated action a_{t} at every step t strictly belongs to the safe action set: \forall t\in[1,T],\quad a_{t}=f_{\text{d}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{text}},\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{\text{gui}},\mathbf{h}_{t}),\quad a_{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{\text{safe}}, (4) This ensures that the defense system should proactively mitigate potential risks during the action generation phase or implement a rejection mechanism during the execution phase to prevent the system from entering a high-risk state. Functional Preservation : When faced with clean inputs, the defense agent f_{\text{d}} should preserve the task completion behavior of the original agent f_{\text{m}} as much as possible. Formally, the resulting action sequences should be similar: \{f_{\text{d}}(\mathbf{x}^{\text{text}},\mathbf{x}^{\text{gui}},\mathbf{h}_{t})\}_{t=1}^{T}\approx\{f_{\text{m}}(\mathbf{x}^{\text{text}},\mathbf{x}^{\text{gui}},\mathbf{h}_{t})\}_{t=1}^{T}. (5) Therefore, an effective defense mechanism should ensure security while ensuring that normal task execution is preserved, achieving a balance between task completion capability and risk control. II-C Existing Challenges Despite growing concerns about the security of VLM mobile agents, there are still key challenges as follows: ❶ Lack of systematic defense mechanisms. Existing defense methods mostly originate from static large language models (LLMs), such as prompt filtering, decoding constraints, and alignment tuning. These methods primarily operate at the text generation level and lack modeling and regulation of multi-turn interactive decision-making behavior. In mobile agents, jailbreak attacks often exploit the agent’s perception-decision-execution feedback loop, progressively manipulating its behavior sequences through environmental feedback. Such attacks shift risks from the linguistic level to the behavioral level, rendering traditional static methods ineffective. Therefore, there is an urgent need to establish a new defense paradigm that governs behavioral generation pathways from the source, enabling security enforcement in multi-turn decision-making. ❷ Lack of automated evaluation frameworks. Currently, evaluations of jailbreak attacks on mobile agents largely depend on manual inspection or ad hoc rules, lacking unified evaluation protocols and adjudication procedures. This manual approach is difficult to scale, lacks objectivity and reproducibility, and hinders systematic research and iterative optimization of attack and defense strategies.
