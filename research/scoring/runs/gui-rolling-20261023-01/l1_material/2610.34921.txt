title: PhysEvolve: Verifier-grounded Self-evolution for Physical Reasoning with Executable Worlds
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34921

§Abstract
Physical reasoning underpins how embodied systems understand and act in the physical world, yet vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable on questions about dynamics and physical causality. Progress is critically constrained by the scarcity of grounded counterfactual training data, as observational videos capture only the factual trajectory that occurred. The self-evolving paradigm offers a route beyond fixed datasets by enabling models to construct their own training problems. Extending this paradigm to physics, however, requires an external source of truth that can both validate model-proposed environments and verify alternative physical outcomes under intervention. We introduce \physevolve{}, a verifier-grounded self-evolving framework that trains VLMs for causal physical reasoning from model-proposed executable worlds and simulation-authored tasks. At each round, a shared VLM proposes executable world programs and evidence schemas; a deterministic physics engine freezes each valid world, simulates matched baseline and body-elided counterfactual branches, and synthesizes discrete, answer-bound visual tasks. Exact verifier feedback from task solving updates both the shared policy via GRPO and an adaptive curriculum over physical intents. Starting from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, \physevolve{} achieves targeted gains across PhysBench, CausalPhys, and ContPhy without human intervention data, improving PhysBench Throwing by $8.8$ points and ContPhy Counterfactual Removal and Goal-Pit by $7.4$ and $24.4$ points. These gains concentrate on interaction-sensitive and altered-world questions, demonstrating zero-shot transfer of causal principles to unseen complex dynamics (cloth, fluid, soft bodies) and real-world video.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Multimodal understanding of the physical world is foundational to embodied intelligence and robotic systems, where reliable decision-making requires anticipating how scenes evolve and respond to external interventions ( Black et al., 2024 ; Zhou et al., 2026 ) . Yet current vision-language models (VLMs) remain substantially less reliable on physical dynamics, intervention effects, and counterfactual outcomes than on static object recognition or spatial relations ( Chow et al., 2025 ; Tang et al., 2026 ; Zheng et al., 2024 ) , and broader multimodal reasoning benchmarks echo the same reliability gap on structured logical inputs ( Yuan et al., 2026 ) . Closing this gap is fundamentally bottlenecked by training data: high-quality, verifiably annotated examples that isolate how physical outcomes change under controlled interventions are exceptionally scarce. Real-world videos are not only costly to annotate, but fundamentally capture only the factual trajectory that occurred, rendering counterfactual alternatives unobservable ( Pearl, 2009 ) . A growing paradigm of self-evolving systems circumvents static data bottlenecks by enabling models to autonomously generate the problems from which they learn ( Zhao et al., 2025 ; Xia et al., 2025 ; Gao et al., 2026 ) . The viability of this paradigm hinges on an external source of truth that can independently validate self-generated problems and verify their correct answers as the training distribution shifts. In symbolic domains such as mathematics and code, an interpreter provides this exact supervision: a Proposer generates an executable specification, the interpreter computes its deterministic output, and exact correctness supplies the Solver’s learning signal. Physics simulators offer a natural counterpart for the physical world by executing proposed environments and exposing their complete internal states ( Prabhudesai et al., 2026 ) . This analogy suggests an appealingly direct route to physical self-play: retain the proposer–solver curriculum and substitute a physics simulator for the symbolic interpreter. However, naively mapping this paradigm to physical reasoning reveals a fundamental structural breakdown (Figure 1 ). When a shared Proposer–Solver policy proposes rigid-body scenes and evaluates a single simulated trajectory using continuous proximity scores, two failure modes emerge (Section 3.1 ): (1) single-trajectory readouts cannot construct or verify intervention tasks, starving the curriculum of causal supervision; and (2) continuous proximity rewards inflate learnability signals for uniformly incorrect rollouts, inducing reward hacking without improving exact physical correctness. These findings establish two indispensable requirements for physical self-play: the verification environment must actively execute interventions across paired branches, and derived visual tasks must be bound to discrete, exact ground truth. We introduce PhysEvolve, a verifier-grounded self-evolving framework that learns causal physical reasoning from policy-proposed executable worlds and simulation-authored tasks (Figure 2 ). Rather than authoring text questions directly, a task-conditioned Proposer generates an executable world program and an evidence schema . A deterministic physics executor validates and freezes the scene, executes matched baseline and body-elided counterfactual branches under identical initial conditions, and derives an answer-bound question from their divergent outcomes. The shared VLM then solves the task from the baseline video, receiving exact discrete feedback. This architecture establishes a strict information boundary: the policy guides the exploration of physical environments, while the fixed simulator alone determines physical validity and factual/counterfactual ground truth. The resulting exact solver feedback simultaneously drives shared-policy updates via GRPO and guides an adaptive curriculum over physical reasoning intents. Training on executor-synthesized tasks over policy-proposed worlds produces targeted transfer across three physical-reasoning benchmarks without any human-annotated intervention data. Starting from Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct ( Bai et al., 2025a ) , PhysEvolve improves PhysBench Throwing by 8.78 percentage points and Judgment by 5.08 points. Crucially, on ContPhy, performance on Counterfactual Removal and Goal-Pit increases by 7.38 and 24.44 points, transferring causal intervention principles zero-shot to held-out continuous media (cloth, fluid, rope, and soft bodies) entirely absent from the rigid-body training templates. On real-world videos in CausalPhys, PhysEvolve likewise achieves consistent gains on Anticipation ( +2.46 ) and Intervention ( +2.27 ). Our core contributions are summarized as follows: 1.
