title: Glasseek: A Transparent Recipe for Training and Trustworthy Evaluation of DeepSearch Agents
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16998

§Abstract
Deep search enables language agents to solve complex information-seeking tasks through iterative web exploration. However, developing robust open-source agents remains challenging for three reasons. First, opaque trajectory-construction pipelines make training data difficult to audit and reproduce. Second, context compression can create a substantial mismatch between training and inference. Third, agents may retrieve benchmark content from the open web during evaluation, contaminating the results and overstating their performance. To overcome these bottlenecks, we introduce Glasseek, a fully transparent deep search framework spanning the complete development lifecycle, releasing all data, model weights, training recipes, and code. Specifically, for data construction, we establish a verifiable trajectory curation pipeline that systematically generates high-quality exploration paths. For training, we propose Compaction-Managed GSPO (CM-GSPO), which uses the same policy for search and summarization while optimizing only execution sub-trajectories, enabling end-to-end search-policy training across context-compaction boundaries. For evaluation, we conduct the first systematic audit of test-set leakage in open-web agents and release a modular anti-leakage plugin that seamlessly integrates into existing evaluation harnesses. Under this rigorous evaluation protocol, our 9B agent outperforms frontier proprietary agents across almost all deep research benchmarks spanning diverse task types, and achieves the best overall performance among recent open-weight agents, setting a new state of the art at the 9B scale.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Large language models (LLMs) have rapidly evolved from static text generators into autonomous agents capable of tool use, planning, and self-improvement. Among these agentic behaviors, deep search represents a highly demanding task, requiring models to formulate search strategies, retrieve and verify evidence from diverse sources, and synthesize answers under complex constraints ( Xi et al., 2025 ) . Unlike standard retrieval-augmented generation, deep search requires the agent to dynamically decide what information to seek, when to terminate exploration, and how to resolve conflicting evidence, making the search process itself an iterative reasoning procedure. Despite recent progress in agentic reinforcement learning (RL) ( Zhang et al., 2026 ; Zheng et al., 2025b ) , developing robust, open-source deep search agents remains hindered by three critical challenges: (1) Limited transparency in the open-source ecosystem. While specialized search models exist, their complete training recipes, including data synthesis pipelines, curriculum protocols, and evaluation harnesses, remain largely proprietary, creating a persistent reproducibility vacuum in the community. (2) Train-inference discrepancy induced by context compression. Deep search trajectories are inherently multi-turn and extensive, frequently necessitating context compaction to operate within limited window budgets. However, dynamically compressing intermediate states introduces a severe discrepancy between the exploration (rollout) and policy optimization phases, as altered historical contexts distort trajectory conditioning and invalidate importance weights, ultimately destabilizing policy updates. (3) Test-set leakage vulnerabilities in evaluation harnesses. Existing open-source harnesses lack rigorous isolation guardrails. By failing to enforce strict domain-level URL filtering (e.g., blocking access to Hugging Face) or to run code interpreters in sandboxed environments, models inadvertently retrieve ground-truth answers, leading to severely inflated and misleading metrics. To address these interconnected challenges, we introduce Glasseek , a fully open, end-to-end deep search framework accompanied by a family of compact models at 4B and 9B scales. The advancement of Glasseek is anchored in three corresponding technical designs: First, to promote open and reproducible research, we provide a fully transparent recipe , open-sourcing the synthetic trajectory pipeline, training protocols, model checkpoints, and runtime harnesses. Second, to resolve context degradation without sacrificing end-to-end training, we propose Compaction-Managed GSPO (CM-GSPO) . Using the same policy for search and summarization, CM-GSPO optimizes only execution sub-trajectories with length-normalized likelihood ratios and trajectory-level reweighting, correcting the extra weighting induced by independently averaging sub-trajectory losses. Third, to establish fair and trustworthy benchmarking, we introduce a plug-and-play anti-leakage guardrail during evaluation. Built seamlessly atop the open-source Hermes-agent , our guardrail enforces strict domain-level URL filtering and execution sandboxing to eliminate test-set contamination, all without compromising the general-purpose evaluation capabilities of the underlying harness. Empowered by this framework, we train and evaluate our models across multiple scales (4B, 9B) on six representative benchmarks, covering static/live web browsing (BrowseComp ( Wei et al., 2025 ) , LiveBrowseComp ( Fan et al., 2026 ) ), Chinese deep search (xbench-DeepSearch-2510 ( xbench, 2025 ) , BrowseComp-ZH ( Zhou et al., 2025 ) ), noisy evidence reasoning (SealQA ( Pham et al., 2025 ) ), and complex agentic tasks (GAIA ( Mialon et al., 2024 ) ). Evaluated under the uncompromising guardrails of Hermes Harness, our models achieve unprecedented efficiency and performance: our Glasseeker-9B achieves a 61.6% score on BrowseComp, outperforming Gemini-3-Pro ( Google DeepMind, 2026 ) (59.2%) and the 27B-scale Qwen3.6 ( Qwen Team, 2026a ) (52.0%); on Seal-0, our 9B model achieves 50.0%, surpassing industry flagship models including GPT-5.2 ( OpenAI, 2025 ) (42.3%), while on LiveBrowseComp it reaches 31.3%, again exceeding GPT-5.2 (28%).
