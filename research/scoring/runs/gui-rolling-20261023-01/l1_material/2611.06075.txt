title: UniCompress: From Multi-LoRA Experts to One Compact Model for Edge Intelligence
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06075

§Abstract
Embodied and edge systems increasingly rely on vision-language models (VLMs) to support heterogeneous skills, yet deploying a separate task adapter for each skill while retaining a high-precision backbone creates substantial memory and management costs. We present UniCompress, a deployment-oriented framework that converts multi-task expertise into one compact, adapter-free model. Starting from a consolidated BF16 Qwen3-VL-4B teacher, UniCompress constructs an asymmetric INT2 student using GPTQ initialization and teacher-guided quantization recovery. The approach jointly quantizes and recovers the projection weights in the vision encoder and language backbone, enabling aggressive compression of the complete vision-language model while preserving its multi-task behavior. We evaluate UniCompress on four heterogeneous embodied tasks: posture recognition, household-safety understanding, multimodal dialogue routing, and mobile-GUI action prediction. UniCompress reduces the approximate weight storage from 8.9 GB in BF16 to 2.1 GB in INT2, corresponding to a reduction of approximately 76%. It improves the INT2 macro task score by 34.3 percentage points over GPTQ-INT2 while remaining within 0.9 points of the consolidated BF16 teacher. These results demonstrate the potential of teacher-guided joint vision-language INT2 recovery for deploying a single multi-skill VLM on resource-constrained embodied platforms.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Large foundation models, including large language models (LLMs) and vision–language models (VLMs), have demonstrated strong capabilities in perception, reasoning, interaction, and decision-making. These advances are enabling a new generation of embodied and edge-intelligence applications in which an intelligent agent is expected to perform multiple heterogeneous skills within a shared system. However, deploying such models on resource-constrained devices remains challenging because of their substantial memory footprint, computational cost, and energy consumption. Practical embodied intelligence therefore requires a compact model that can preserve diverse task capabilities while satisfying strict deployment constraints. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning adapts foundation models without updating or storing a separate backbone for every downstream task. Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) [ 1 ] introduces lightweight trainable modules while keeping the pretrained backbone frozen, and Multi-LoRA systems extend this design to multiple skills by attaching task-specific adapters to a shared model. However, the deployment overhead grows with the number of tasks: every adapter must be stored, selected, and managed at runtime, which complicates model versioning, task switching, and deployment on platforms designed around a single static checkpoint. These limitations motivate consolidating multiple task-specific experts into one unified model. Adapter consolidation alone, however, does not reduce the memory cost of the shared backbone, which dominates the deployment footprint. Low-bit quantization methods such as GPTQ [ 2 ] and AWQ [ 3 ] address this cost: at moderate bit widths such as INT4, post-training quantization preserves most of the original performance, whereas extreme INT2 quantization introduces severe approximation errors. For a multi-task model, these errors must be controlled across all capabilities simultaneously, and gains on dominant tasks may come at the expense of less frequent or more sensitive skills. The problem is further amplified for VLMs when both the visual and language components are quantized. Preserving consolidated multi-task knowledge under extreme joint vision–language compression therefore remains an important yet underexplored problem. To address these challenges, we propose UniCompress , a unified framework that transforms distributed task expertise into one compact model for efficient edge intelligence. UniCompress follows a deployment-oriented compression process. It first represents the knowledge of multiple task-specific adaptations within a single consolidated multi-task teacher, eliminating the need to maintain separate adapters during deployment. It then transfers this consolidated knowledge to an extremely low-bit student through teacher-guided quantization recovery, jointly quantizing the projection weights in the vision encoder and language backbone. Rather than treating quantization solely as a local weight-reconstruction problem, UniCompress preserves task behavior by aligning the student with the output distributions of the high-precision teacher during training. The resulting model is exported as a single INT2 checkpoint and requires neither multiple task adapters nor runtime adapter selection. Although INT4 quantization substantially reduces model size, its memory footprint may still exceed the budgets of highly resource-constrained embodied and edge devices. We therefore target INT2 deployment, which offers a more aggressive compression ratio but poses substantially greater challenges in preserving multi-task capabilities. The main contributions of this work are summarized as follows: (i) we formulate the deployment of multi-task foundation models as a unified compression problem that jointly considers adapter management overhead and the dominant memory cost of the shared vision–language backbone; (ii) we propose UniCompress , a deployment-oriented framework that consolidates diverse task expertise into one model and applies teacher-guided joint vision–language INT2 recovery to preserve multi-task capabilities under extreme quantization; and (iii) experiments on four on-device tasks with Qwen3-VL-4B show that UniCompress reduces the approximate weight storage from 8.9 GB to 2.1 GB and recovers 34.3 percentage points over GPTQ-INT2, within 0.9 points of the consolidated BF16 model.
