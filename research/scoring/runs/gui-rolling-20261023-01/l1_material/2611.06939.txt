title: A Multi-Modal Robotic Framework for Physical GUI Interaction Testing
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06939

§Abstract
Automated testing of mobile applications remains challenging when realistic user interactions must be reproduced and GUI behavior verified without relying on invasive mechanisms such as instrumentation or ADB-level control. Existing approaches, including DLD and R-DLD, detect data loss triggered by activity restarts but still depend on system-level access, which limits their applicability on real devices. This doctoral research proposes RFNIT, a multi-modal, fully non-invasive robotic framework for physical GUI interaction testing on smartphones. RFNIT supports end-to-end automation by perceiving the device state through external sensing and executing physical interactions, enabling human-like actions while preserving reproducibility and execution accuracy. The framework integrates a vision-based perception module, an LLM-driven reasoning agent, and a robotic actuator capable of performing touch, swipe, and typing gestures. Multimodal data, including external camera images and OCR-extracted text, are combined to identify GUI elements and guide subsequent testing decisions. Preliminary results indicate that RFNIT accurately recognizes buttons, text fields, and virtual keyboards from external camera input, achieving over 85\% visual detection accuracy. Ongoing and future work will focus on improving robustness, repeatability, and coverage of GUI states during long-running testing sessions.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Automated testing is essential to ensure software quality and reliability. However, as applications move beyond smartphones to platforms such as automotive dashboards, industrial controllers, IoT devices, and gaming consoles, traditional frameworks like Appium ( Hans, 2015 ) , Espresso ( Zelenchuk, 2019 ) , and UIAutomator ( Google, 2025 ) become increasingly limited. These tools rely on invasive APIs or instrumentation, which are often unavailable in proprietary or safety-critical environments, forcing many domains to depend on costly, error-prone, and poorly reproducible manual testing. Non-invasive robotic testing has emerged as an alternative to validate mobile applications without instrumentation, using external sensors and actuators to simulate human interactions. Approaches such as RoScript ( Qian et al., 2025 ; Mao et al., 2017 ) demonstrate the feasibility of physical touch automation; however, by keeping the device static on a bench while a stylus executes gestures, these systems remain limited to two-dimensional touchscreen interactions. As a result, motion-dependent sensors, physical buttons, and complex gestures—such as orientation changes, “double chop for flashlight,” “twist for camera,” or NFC-based interactions—remain out of scope for non-invasive automation. To overcome this gap, RFNIT adopts an inverted configuration: the smartphone is mounted directly on the robot, while the stylus and external camera remain fixed in the environment (Figure 1 ). This design enables the robot to physically manipulate the device, perform controlled rotations, approximate NFC cards, and trigger advanced motion-based gestures while still supporting precise touch input. This expands automated testing from simple 2D screen interactions to a broader, multimodal physical interaction space. Preliminary steps of this research were explored in earlier works. An initial RFNIT prototype was presented as a poster at FSE 2024 ( Freitas et al., 2024a ) , validating the feasibility of robotic-based, less invasive testing on real smartphones. In parallel, a study at SBES 2024 ( Freitas et al., 2024b ) investigated robotic-supported data loss detection, providing early evidence that physical device manipulation can reduce test invasiveness. These insights directly motivated the design of the fully non-invasive and multimodal RFNIT framework proposed in this doctoral research. Building on these insights, this doctoral research proposes RFNIT (Robotic Framework for Non-Invasive Testing), a multi-modal robotic framework that integrates robotics, computer vision, and automated reasoning to execute high-fidelity physical GUI interaction testing on heterogeneous devices. RFNIT provides a two-layer modular architecture: (i) a physical layer that controls actuation and sensing, and (ii) a software layer that abstracts low-level control and exposes a high-level intent-driven API for defining test behaviors. The key differentiators of RFNIT are: Multimodal support: execution of touchscreen gestures, physical button presses, controlled device rotations, and advanced gestures such as double-tap and drag, enabling interaction with both on-screen and hardware-dependent features; Real-time adaptation: visual- and OCR-based perception supports responsive interaction, on-the-fly correction of execution deviations, and recovery from unexpected interface changes; Device-agnostic design: applicability to Android, iOS, and embedded platforms without requiring instrumentation, system-level access, or modifications to the application under test; Integration with testing ecosystems: compatibility with Pytest for defining automated oracles, structuring test cases, and integrating RFNIT into continuous testing and CI pipelines. RFNIT aims to push the boundaries of non-invasive test automation by providing a framework capable of validating not only the functional correctness of applications but also the fidelity, robustness, and user experience of physical interactions.
