title: Robo2Process: Benchmarking and Learning Process-Aware Understanding in Vision-Language Robotic Manipulation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13040

§Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) used as robotic critics, reward generators, and failure detectors must understand how manipulation unfolds, not only whether it succeeds. Yet fine-grained process supervision is not readily available in a unified form, and existing evaluations do not systematically diagnose these abilities. We introduce Robo2Process, a trace-grounded framework for scalable construction of process-aware supervision from heterogeneous robotic execution data. It aligns visual observations with source-native signals and annotations, extracts local process units (LPUs), and generates structured QA supervision through task-specific sampling, deterministic label construction, and ambiguity filtering. We instantiate this framework as RoboProcessBench, a diagnostic benchmark with 12 question families spanning \emph{static monitoring} and \emph{dynamic reasoning}, and ProcessData, a corpus of 57,892 QA pairs across four data sources and 260 manipulation tasks, split into ProcessData-SFT and ProcessData-Eval. Evaluation of a broad suite of VLMs reveals uneven process understanding. Supervision from ProcessData-SFT improves \textit{Qwen2.5-VL-7B} and \textit{InternVL-3-8B} on local state, motion, progress, and primitive-aware cues. External evaluation on RoboRewardBench shows Robo2Process enables out-of-distribution process-aware reasoning abilities. Overall, Robo2Process provides a source-aware pipeline, diagnostic benchmark, and learnable substrate for process-aware understanding in robotic manipulation.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly used as visual critics, online reward generators, and failure-reasoning modules in robotic manipulation [ 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 6 ] . These roles require more than recognizing objects or predicting a final success label: a useful process judge must assess whether contact has been established, motion is active, a local primitive is progressing, temporal evidence is consistent, and the current state supports the next step. Recent work studies subgoal-level evaluation, process reward modeling, progress-aware critics, and failure reasoning [ 7 , 3 , 4 , 5 , 2 ] . These systems demonstrate the value of intermediate signals, but generally focus on deploying a judge for policy auditing, reward generation, or failure analysis. A complementary gap is the lack of a reusable way to derive process-understanding supervision from heterogeneous robotic data, where relevant evidence may be distributed across visual observations, force or torque traces, gripper state, timestamps, success records, and primitive annotations. We address these gaps with Robo2Process , a source-aware pipeline for constructing process supervision from native execution signals and annotations. Robo2Process aligns observations with source-native evidence, extracts local process units (LPUs), and applies task-specific sampling, deterministic label construction, and ambiguity guards. This enables batch generation and reuse across eligible episodes without manually labeling every QA pair, while still requiring source-specific setup and reliable native anchors. We instantiate the pipeline as RoboProcessBench , a 12-family diagnostic benchmark, and ProcessData , a corpus of 57,892 QA pairs from four robotic sources and 260 manipulation tasks [ 8 , 9 , 10 , 11 ] . Episode- and recording-isolated ProcessData-SFT and Eval splits support post-training and held-out diagnosis. Experiments reveal uneven VLM capabilities and show that post-training improves local state, motion, progress, and primitive-aware performance on two backbones, while temporal reasoning remains to be tackled. We additionally report external reward-prediction associations and mixed-training results on RoboRewardBench. Contributions 1. Robo2Process , a source-aware pipeline for batch construction of process supervision from native execution signals and annotations, using LPUs, task-specific sampling, deterministic label builders, and ambiguity guards. 2. RoboProcessBench , a diagnostic benchmark spanning 12 process-understanding families across static monitoring and dynamic reasoning. 3. ProcessData , a four-source instantiation with 57,892 QA pairs, 260 manipulation tasks, and isolated SFT and Eval splits. 4. Multi-level validation through human audit, VLM diagnosis, post-training on two backbones, and external reward evaluation, characterizing demonstrated benefits and limitations.
