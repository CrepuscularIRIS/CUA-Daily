title: RetryRoute: Retry-Aware Model Routing for Agentic Image Editing Services
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41460

§Abstract
Web agents increasingly edit images by calling open editing models in a critique-and-retry loop, and these models differ in cost by more than an order of magnitude. After every rejected edit, a service must decide whether to retry the same model, escalate to another or give up. Existing routers pick one model per request, and cascades escalate in a fixed order. However, both treat a retry as an independent draw. We identify sticky failures, meaning that a retry after a failure succeeds only 17-53% as often as a first try across six open models, three benchmarks and two judges. Based on this finding, we propose RetryRoute, a retry-aware router that estimates each model's chance of success given the request's failure history. It plans retries, escalations and abandonment over failure states and adds live queue waits to the cost, so the same plan becomes load-aware dispatch. At the main budget, RetryRoute's success under the in-loop judge exceeds the strongest of eleven prior baselines by 2.8 points on average over eight groups. On a GPU serving testbed it raises the share of edits delivered within the service-level objective by 5.0-6.3 points over the best prior baseline at every load.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Image editing has become a routine tool call for Web agents ( Yao et al., 2023 ; Lin et al., 2026 ) , from recoloring product photos to localizing the text on a banner. Following the critique-and-revise pattern of agent systems ( Madaan et al., 2023 ; Shinn et al., 2023 ; Wang et al., 2024 ; Yang et al., 2024 ) , the agent sends the image and an instruction to an editing model, asks a vision-language judge whether the result follows the instruction, and retries when it does not. The open editing models behind such calls ( Rombach et al., 2022 ; Brooks et al., 2023 ) differ by more than an order of magnitude in cost. A 4-step distilled model ( Salimans and Ho, 2022 ) returns an edit in under 6 s on a 24 GB GPU, while the strongest open models need minutes. However, a service that runs these loops pays for every rejected attempt, so where it sends the next attempt after a rejection decides both its cost and how many edits it delivers. Retry routing is therefore a cost and reliability problem of agentic Web services, a topic of the Web Infrastructure and Agentic Systems track. Serving systems for generative models already exploit this cost spread. Per-request routers pick one model for each request from its features ( Rice, 1976 ; Li et al., 2026 ; Ong et al., 2025 ) . Following the cheap-first cascade of Viola and Jones ( Viola and Jones, 2001 ) , serving cascades send a request to a light model first and escalate to a heavier one when a quality check fails ( Chen et al., 2024 ; Aggarwal et al., 2024 ; Ahmad et al., 2025 ; Yang et al., 2025 ) . Agent frameworks and Best-of-N sampling simply retry the same model with a new seed. All three share an implicit assumption about the retry . They treat a second attempt on a failed model as an independent draw with the same success probability, and under this assumption retrying the cheapest adequate model is the cost-optimal policy (§ 4.3 ). However, these designs overlook how a failure changes the next attempt’s chance of success, which concurrent work on code generation has begun to observe ( Chen, 2026 ) . We measure it on six open editing models in two pools, on three benchmarks with GEdit-Bench in two languages, and with two independent judges, and identify sticky failures . The retry rate is only 17–53% of the first-try rate (Figure 1 a). FLUX.2 [klein] ( Black Forest Labs, 2026a ) passes 67% of edits on the first try but only 15% of the edits it has just failed, while another model of its pool still solves 45% of them (Figure 1 a). This also explains why cascades help but stall. They escalate after a failure, but in a fixed order and mostly once per model, so they rarely retry a cheap model while a retry is still worth its cost. Turning sticky failures into better retry decisions raises two challenges. (C1) How can a service estimate each model’s chance of success given a request’s failure history? First-try rates overstate the retry rate by about two- to sixfold (§ 3.2 ), while counting outcomes per failure history spreads the logs thin, since few requests reach deep failure states. (C2) How can it turn these estimates into retry, escalation and abandonment decisions that stay good as the load changes? When a request is hard for several models at once, a failure on one model also lowers the others’ chances, so no fixed priority rule over the models is optimal in general. A fixed retry plan tuned at one load can also collapse at another, because a busy GPU makes its model’s next attempt slower (Figure 2 c). We therefore propose RetryRoute, a router for agentic image editing services named for routing every retry rather than every request (Figure 3 ). To address C1, RetryRoute estimates p(a\mid s) , the probability that model a ’s next attempt succeeds given the failure counts s so far, by counting logged attempts per failure state and shrinking each category’s estimate toward the all-category one. For C2, it solves a Bellman equation over failure states to plan retries, escalations and abandonment, adds each model’s live queue wait to the cost and re-plans after every attempt, which turns escalation into load-aware dispatch. We validate RetryRoute against eleven prior baselines in eight evaluation groups and on a three-GPU serving testbed. At the main budget and under the in-loop judge it leads the strongest prior baseline by 2.8 points on average and up to 5.6, and same-model retry by 3.5–13.3 (§ 5.2 ). With one setting, its goodput on the testbed exceeds the best prior baseline’s by 5.0–6.3 points at all three loads (§ 5.6 ).
