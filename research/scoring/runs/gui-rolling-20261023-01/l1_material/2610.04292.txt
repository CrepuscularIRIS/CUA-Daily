title: LMBuild: Evaluating LLM Agents for Generating Buildable and Functional Structures
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04292

§Abstract
LLM-based agents are increasingly capable of generating complex 3D structures, with the potential to reshape how objects are designed and realized in the physical world. Yet, producing elegant geometry is fundamentally different from producing objects that can be built and perform their intended functions. Existing evaluations largely focus on geometric quality while overlooking physical realizability. We introduce LMBuild, a benchmark for evaluating LLM agents on generating buildable and functional structures. LMBuild represents generated objects as assembled structures comprising part decompositions, joints, materials, and sequences. To support reproducible evaluation, we provide a unified framework consisting of: (1) an interactive environment in which agents can use tools to retrieve, create, and place components to construct objects; (2) a curated benchmark that repurposes established CAD datasets and augments them with knowledge from Wikipedia; and (3) a evaluation framework covering structural soundness, functional affordance, design quality, and physical realization. Evaluations across 30 systems reveal several intriguing findings: (a) Soundness and alignment are no longer the primary bottlenecks for frontier closed-source models, while functional affordance and physical operability remain substantially more challenging; (b) stronger models more effectively create new components, whereas weaker models tend to rely on retrieval; and (c) providing functional specifications substantially improves part completeness, kinematics, and physical operability. These results show that generating real-world structures requires deeper reasoning about functional affordances, mechanics, and designing and creating novel components. We expect LMBuild to provide a foundation for measuring progress and incentivizing research toward agents that generate buildable and functional structures.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
The recent release of GPT-6 Astra ( OpenAI, 2026b ) has sparked an explosion of discussion around how much LLM-based agents could transform 3D structure generation. Beyond their established capabilities in language and code, LLM-based agents are increasingly able to directly manipulate complex 3D assets and scenes ( Hu et al., 2024 ; Sun et al., 2025 ; Tang et al., 2025b ; Liu et al., 2026a ) and translate language-based specifications into parametric or programmatic 3D designs ( Li et al., 2025 ; Wang et al., 2025b ; Alrashedy et al., 2025 ) . These developments lead us to explore a more ambitious possibility: whether LLM agents could reshape not only how structures are digitally designed, but also how they are constructed and realized in the physical world ( Kyaw et al., 2025 ) . However, producing visually plausible geometry is fundamentally different from producing an object that can actually be built and function in the real world. A structure may appear convincing while remaining invalid, unusable, or impossible to construct. Existing 3D-generation evaluations largely emphasize geometric quality ( Poole et al., 2023 ; Lin et al., 2023 ) , visual consistency ( Wang et al., 2023 ; Maiti et al., 2025 ; He et al., 2023 ; Duggal et al., 2025 ) , and semantic alignment ( Wu et al., 2024 ; Zhang et al., 2025 ) , while overlooking critical aspects of physical realization. Yet, we still lack a systematic way to evaluate whether LLM-based agents can leverage their extensive prior knowledge and reasoning capabilities to generate structures that are not only digitally plausible, but also buildable, functional, and aligned with real-world physical constraints. To this end, we propose LMBuild , a first step toward systematically evaluating LLM agents on the generation of buildable and functional structures, while also accommodating domain-specific structure-generation baselines. First, LMBuild provides a unified framework (Figure 1 ) in which LLM agents are equipped with a standardized agent harness and can interact seamlessly with a 3D environment through a set of construction tools, including retrieving components from a sub-part pool, creating new parts with arbitrary geometry, placing components at desired poses, and refining structures through inspection and revision. Second, as summarized in Table 1 , we select 2,549 high-quality reference targets by repurposing existing 3D datasets containing around 368K data, filtering objects by category and model quality, and augmenting them with real-world knowledge extracted from Wikipedia, and further selected 200 objects as the core benchmark dataset for evaluation. This knowledge includes core functional parts, object attributes, joints, and kinematic relationships, which serve as textual anchors for evaluating physically grounded properties. Finally, as shown in Figure 1 , we introduce a bottom-up evaluation hierarchy covering four dimensions: (1) structural soundness, assessing whether components form valid and stable 3D assemblies; (2) functional affordance, evaluating whether functional parts, joints, and kinematics support the intended object functions; (3) design quality, assessing decomposition, aesthetics, and overall structural design; and (4) physical realization, evaluating whether the structure can be fabricated with appropriate materials and assembly sequences and ultimately operated or used in the physical world. We evaluate 30 systems on LMBuild, including 6 closed-source APIs, 13 open-source LLMs, and 11 domain-specific systems, under diverse evaluation settings. Our results show that: • Structural soundness and visual alignment are no longer primary bottlenecks for frontier closed-source models. Most achieve near-perfect soundness and strong design quality, although open-source models still show room for improvement in aesthetics, and fine-grained visual alignment. • Functional affordance and physical operability remain the largest capability gaps. All model families struggle with generating a complete list of functional parts, correct joints with kinematics, and artifacts that remain operable under simulation-based evaluation. • While frontier models can effectively create new components, weaker models rely heavily on retrieval and struggle with the additional reasoning burden introduced by part creation. • Enriching prompts with desired attributes and functional requirements can force the model to reason, thus improving functional-part completeness, kinematic correctness, and physical operability. We further show that LMBuild can diagnose failure patterns for open-source LLM agents during structure construction, revealing their gap in part-creation capability as well as persistent challenges in scene-state tracking. Overall, we expect LMBuild to provide a foundation for measuring progress and incentivizing research toward agents capable of generating buildable and functional structures.
