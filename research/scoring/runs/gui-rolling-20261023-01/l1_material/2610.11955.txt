title: Capturing a Moving Target on Star Graphs by Two Communicating Mobile Agents
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11955

§Abstract
We study a problem of searching for a mobile target in an $m$-ray star graph, a natural generalization of linear search to multiple directions. The target is placed adversarially on one of the rays and may move with constant speed. We investigate a two-robot setting, where cooperation and communication play a central role. We study two communication models: the Face-to-Face (F2F) model, where robots communicate only upon meeting, and the Sender-Receiver (S/R) model, where communication is asymmetric. We focus on the {\em away model}, in which the target moves {\em away} from the origin with speed $v<1$. We design search strategies that minimize the competitive ratio and analyze the problem under three knowledge assumptions: \emph{NoDistance}, \emph{NoSpeed}, and \emph{NoKnowledge}. For each setting, we derive upper bounds on the competitive ratio and for some cases, we derive the lower bound. Our results highlight how the number of rays and the communication model influence the competitive ratio.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Search and evacuation problems are fundamental topics in algorithm design, with applications in areas such as robotics, surveillance, data mining, and network exploration. In the classical linear search problem, one or more agents must locate an unknown target on an infinite line, while in evacuation problems multiple agents cooperate so that they all find an unknown exit. These problems have been extensively studied under various assumptions on the number of agents, their speeds, and the amount of information available. A central measure of performance is the competitive ratio , which compares the time required by an online search strategy to find the target without knowledge of its location to the time required by an optimal offline strategy that knows the target’s location in advance. An algorithm is optimal if it minimizes this ratio. While most existing work focuses on static targets and simple domains such as the line, less attention has been given to more complex environments and dynamic targets. In this paper, we study the problem of searching for a mobile target in an m -ray star graph, a natural generalization of the linear domain to multiple directions. The presence of multiple rays introduces a fundamental trade-off between exploring new directions and revisiting previously explored regions, while the motion of the target further increases the difficulty. We investigate two-robot settings under two communication models: the Face-to-Face (F2F) model, where robots communicate only upon meeting, and the Sender-Receiver (S/R) model, which allows asymmetric wireless communication. We analyze the problem under different knowledge assumptions about the target, namely whether its initial distance, speed, or neither is known. The three knowledge assumptions are chosen to capture different levels of information available to the searching robots and to investigate how such information affects the difficulty of the capture problem. In particular, they provide a natural progression from settings in which the robots possess useful information about the target to the completely uninformed setting. Studying these variants allows us to quantify the benefit of prior knowledge through the resulting competitive ratios and to identify how the availability of information interacts with the topology of the underlying graph. These assumptions are also motivated by the corresponding settings considered for the infinite line in previous work ( Coleman et al., 2022 ; escape2024 ) , allowing us to examine how the transition from a line to an m -ray star graph affects the role of information in online target capture. Our goal is to design search strategies that minimize the competitive ratio of capture time (also referred to as evacuation time) and to understand how the interplay between domain structure, target dynamics, communication constraints, and available knowledge affects performance. We provide upper bounds on the competitive ratio for each setting and establish lower bounds for other cases. 1.1. Problem Formulation and Objectives The primary objective of this work is to design online search strategies for two mobile agents that minimize the competitive ratio when searching for an oblivious moving target on an m -ray star graph. Specifically, we aim to quantify the precise trade-offs between the agents’ initial knowledge, communication constraints, and the parity of the graph’s rays. To evaluate how partial information influences performance, we consider three knowledge settings: • NoDistance (Known v , Unknown d ): The robots know the target’s constant speed v<1 , but have no prior information regarding the target’s initial distance d from the origin. • NoSpeed (Known d , Unknown v ): The robots know the target’s initial distance d from the origin, but do not know its speed v . • NoKnowledge (Unknown d and v ): The robots possess no prior knowledge about either the target’s initial distance d or its speed v . 1.2. Context and Motivation The main motivation of this study is to understand how the lack of knowledge of the target’s speed and/or initial distance—affect the competitive ratio of search algorithms. While search problems have been extensively studied in the literature, most existing work focuses on static targets or simplified settings. The problem of capturing a moving target has received less attention, especially in non-linear domains and under asymmetric communication models. Previous studies have mainly considered moving target capture on the infinite line, often under simpler communication assumptions such as symmetric or F2F interaction. Although the m -ray star graph has been studied as a search domain, the case of a moving target in this setting remains relatively unexplored. In this work, we aim to address this gap.
