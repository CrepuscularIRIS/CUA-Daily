title: GroundCascade: Selective Second Opinions for GUI Grounding
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13177

§Abstract
Graphical user interface (GUI) grounding maps a screenshot and a natural-language instruction to the screen location where an agent should act. Zoomed views can reveal small targets but may lose the context needed to identify them. We introduce GroundCascade, a training-free controller that uses agreement between a grounder's full-screen and zoomed predictions to decide when to consult a complementary model. It selects between zoomed predictions while preserving corroborated full-screen locations. An optional Finalizer predicts from an unmarked joint crop when disagreement remains (Full); omitting it yields the two-stage Selective variant. GroundCascade reaches 85.33% on ScreenSpot-Pro with Indeed-UI-32B/8B/32B and 88.43% on the visible-target OSWorld-G-Refined subset with Qwen3.6-27B/3.8-27B/3.8-27B, gains of 2.66 and 2.35 percentage points over the stronger standalone two-pass grounder. On UI-Vision, GroundCascade reaches 72.20% category-balanced accuracy, with Selective slightly higher at 72.28%. Across these three evaluations, GroundCascade in Full mode uses 2.42-2.73 calls per request, 31-39% fewer than unconditionally acquiring both models' two-pass predictions. At matched second-model call budgets, selective acquisition gains 1.52-2.56 points over standalone Two-pass, compared with 0.22-0.69 points from stratified random acquisition. GroundCascade leads the official ScreenSpot-Pro leaderboard and exceeds reported GUI-specialist comparisons on OSWorld-G-Refined and UI-Vision, subject to differences in external evaluation protocols.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
GUI grounding maps a natural-language instruction and screenshot to where an agent should act. Professional interfaces contain small icons and similar labels [ 14 , 22 ] . Two-pass grounders magnify a crop around an initial full-screen prediction to resolve fine details [ 8 , 44 , 11 , 7 ] . Yet a crop can hide the toolbar or panel that identifies a target, spoiling a correct full-screen prediction [ 8 , 27 ] . A complementary model can repair errors [ 10 ] , but need not be better; using both two-pass models can take four calls. GroundCascade controls second-model calls using the Grounder ’s Context–Detail agreement (Fig. 2 ). It returns Detail when the predictions agree closely and otherwise obtains a Context–Detail pair from the Regrounder . Agreement between Detail predictions returns the more self-consistent one; when Detail disagrees, close Context agreement preserves the more self-consistent model’s full-screen point. These two stages form the Selective variant; in its Full mode, GroundCascade adds a Finalizer on an unmarked joint crop only when both tests fail. Decisions use screen-space distances, without training, model confidences, or marked candidates. GroundCascade reaches 85.33% on ScreenSpot-Pro (SSP) using Indeed-UI-32B/8B/32B, a 2.66-point gain over the stronger standalone two-pass grounder. GroundCascade tops the official SSP leaderboard [ 26 ] . With Qwen3.6-27B / 3.8-27B / 3.8-27B , it reaches 88.43% on the 510 visible-target requests of OSWorld-G-Refined (OSG-R), a 2.35-point gain. On UI-Vision Element Grounding (UIV), GroundCascade reaches 72.20% category-balanced accuracy; omitting the Finalizer improves it slightly to 72.28% in Selective mode, 2.56 points above standalone Two-pass. The OSG-R and UIV results exceed the published comparisons with Hunmin-CUA and Qwen-UI-Agent, respectively (Fig. 1 , Sec. 4.2 ). The acquisition gate outperforms stratified random acquisition at the same executed Regrounder-call budget on all three evaluations (Sec. 4.4 ). Table 1 presents the matched model-invocation costs. The Regrounder need not be stronger in isolation: I8 trails I32 on SSP, and Q38 trails Q36 on OSG-R and UIV, yet selective acquisition improves the Grounder’s outcomes. The Context return can repair zoom-induced losses; finalization adds 18 correct SSP answers and two OSG-R answers, with five fewer on UIV. Our error analysis separates missed escalation from the loss of correct predictions already acquired (Sec. 5 ). Our contributions are: • A training-free controller for selective second Context–Detail acquisition, corroborated Context preservation, and optional fresh unmarked joint-view prediction. • Matched-budget evidence that selective acquisition concentrates useful corrections over stratified random; analysis of Context preservation and conditional finalization.
