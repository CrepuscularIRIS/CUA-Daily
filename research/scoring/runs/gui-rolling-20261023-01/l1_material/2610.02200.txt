title: VISTA: A Visual Harness for Reasoning in an Interactive World
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02200

§Abstract
We show that multimodal models possess strong reasoning abilities and that an appropriate harness can unlock their potential to solve tasks across diverse interactive environments. We introduce VISTA, a visual harness that gives a general-purpose multimodal model long-horizon vision. VISTA allows the model to directly perceive the environment through visual observations and maintains a lossless visual memory that preserves past observations in their original form. The model can actively retrieve these observations and reorganize its visual input as it reasons. On ARC-AGI-3, VISTA improves Claude Opus 5.0's Relative Human Action Efficiency score from 40.68 to a perfect 100.00, with the model completing all 25 public games using 57.4% fewer actions than first-time human participants. VISTA's simple design also allows it to extend naturally to diverse visual environments with minimal adaptation. Across three additional benchmarks covering a diverse range of visual games and puzzles, it substantially outperforms baselines using the same underlying model with minimal harnesses. Our results highlight VISTA's potential as a general-purpose visual harness for advancing multimodal agents in complex visual environments.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
We live in a dynamic, high-dimensional world. As humans, we perceive our surroundings, reason about what we observe, and act based on our understanding and past experience [ 56 , 48 ] . However, it remains unclear whether existing artificial intelligence (AI) systems have a similar ability to understand and interact robustly with the world, especially in unfamiliar environments. To study this question, we use ARC-AGI-3 [ 5 ] , a suite of interactive visual games in which an agent needs to discover the rules and goals of unfamiliar worlds through interaction. This benchmark is typically approached as a language-based problem, with large language models (LLMs) receiving a textual or structured representation of the environment, as shown in Figure 1 a. Building on this setup, a common strategy is to use code-based methods that construct a executable world model using symbolic programs [ 62 , 39 , 11 , 25 ] . We instead reframe ARC-AGI-3 as a vision-based problem and address it using multimodal models. However, as illustrated in Figure 1 b, existing vision-language models (VLMs) typically encode each image once into visual representations [ 2 , 28 ] . These representations may not preserve the details needed as reasoning evolves. Moreover, earlier observations may be removed from the context or replaced by textual summaries to stay within the model’s context limit [ 29 , 36 , 27 ] . These limitations may constrain the models’ reasoning capabilities during long-horizon interactions. To this end, we introduce VISTA , a visual harness that allows the model to actively reorganize its visual input during long-horizon interactions (Figure 1 c). The model receives visual observations of the environment that preserve the appearance and spatial relations of objects. It develops its understanding of the environment through free-form language reasoning [ 55 , 42 ] and uses this understanding to predict action outcomes and plan subsequent actions [ 59 ] . Instead of relying solely on visual representations retained in the model’s context, which may be compressed, lossy, and insufficient for subsequent reasoning, VISTA maintains a lossless visual memory . This memory stores every frame returned by the environment, including intermediate animation frames, in its original form. The model can retrieve observations from this memory through visual inspection , selecting frames to compare and spatial regions to examine at different levels of detail based on its current reasoning needs [ 48 , 47 ] . Figure 2 shows two example agent trajectories with VISTA, illustrating how observation, reasoning, inspection, and action are interleaved. With VISTA, Claude Opus 5.0 [ 3 ] completes all 25 public ARC-AGI-3 games with a Relative Human Action Efficiency (RHAE) score of 100.00 with xhigh reasoning effort, using 57.4% fewer actions than the first-time human baseline. VISTA with GPT-5.6 Sol [ 33 ] likewise completes all 25 games, achieving an RHAE score of 99.00 with max reasoning effort. To our knowledge, VISTA is the first vision-based system to achieve perfect or near-perfect performance on ARC-AGI-3 without program synthesis. For comparison, the organizers’ official baselines, which also do not use programs, achieve RHAE scores of 40.68 with Opus 5.0 high and 13.33 with GPT-5.6 Sol max [ 7 , 34 ] . The simplicity of VISTA also allows it to extend naturally to other visual environments with minimal adaptation. We apply the same visual harness to 34 browser games from GameWorld [ 35 ] , 10 browser games from AI GameStore [ 60 ] , and 39 visual tracking problems from BabyVision [ 12 ] . Together, these benchmarks cover a wide range of games and visual reasoning tasks that require skills such as complex reasoning, spatial navigation, and strategic planning. GPT-5.6 Sol with VISTA substantially outperforms baselines that use the same model with minimal harnesses. It also outperforms the novice human baseline on GameWorld and the human median baseline on AI GameStore. Overall, these results demonstrate VISTA’s effectiveness across diverse visual environments and highlight visual harness design as an important direction for improving multimodal agents.
