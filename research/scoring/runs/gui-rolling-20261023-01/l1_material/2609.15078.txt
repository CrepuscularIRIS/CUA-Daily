arxiv: 2609.15078 (2609.15078v3)
title: Enabling Creative Exploration for Vibe Design Agents
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15078

§Abstract
Vibe design agents translate natural-language requests into interfaces, yet repeated generations often converge on similar visual directions. We present an inference architecture that makes design exploration an explicit stage before implementation. A pre-pass proposes structured design specifications with typicality weights, an external policy samples a direction, and the generator implements it under fixed decoding settings. Candidate elicitation draws on Verbalized Sampling. Applied to UI themes and visual-asset prompts, this architecture provides direct control over aesthetic exploration without changing code-generation temperature. Across 168 benchmark prompts, theme sampling expands observed selection coverage from one option to nearly three and increases rendered visual variation. For visual-asset prompts, the strongest observed LLM-judge win/loss ratio is 1.32, with only a small change in whole-screen similarity. Localized aesthetic choices can thus influence judged preference even when the overall screen remains similar. We further evaluate joint theme and asset sampling in a randomized online study conducted over nearly two months, covering 3.39 million tasks and 87,416 labeled conversations. These studies show how explicit design specifications support exploration of alternative UI concepts and help us evaluate their effects on rendered interfaces, judged preference, and user interaction.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
LLMs and multimodal models increasingly act as vibe design agents . Users describe the interface they want and refine it through feedback, while the agent proposes layouts, generates frontend code, and renders the result. Systems such as Lovable, v0, Bolt, and Replit Agent turn conversational briefs into web applications ( Lovable, 2026 ; Vercel, 2026 ; Bolt, 2026 ; Replit, 2026 ) , while Figma Make, Claude Design, and Google Stitch emphasize editable prototypes and UI-to-code workflows ( Ng et al., 2025 ; Anthropic, 2026 ; Banks, 2026 ) . Recent benchmarks show progress in reproducing screenshots and generating interfaces that work as intended ( Si et al., 2025 ; Xiao et al., 2025c ; Zhang et al., 2024 ) . As these capabilities improve, agents can also help users explore different aesthetic directions before committing to an implementation ( Zamfirescu-Pereira et al., 2025 ) . Post-training methods such as RLHF and DPO improve instruction following and preference alignment ( Ouyang et al., 2022 ; Rafailov et al., 2023 ) , yet preference optimization can underrepresent minority preferences and narrow output diversity ( Xiao et al., 2025a ; Lanchantin et al., 2025 ) . Verbalized Sampling identifies a bias toward typical examples in training data as one source of overly conventional outputs ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) . In UI generation, repeated requests often produce similar default palettes, typography, and compositions. Several factors may contribute to this repetition. Our goal is to give agents an explicit, measurable way to explore alternatives across repeated generations. Raising token-level decoding temperature changes aesthetic choices and code generation at the same time. In narrative generation, higher temperature correlates weakly with novelty while reducing coherence ( Peeperkorn et al., 2024 ) . We instead make aesthetic direction an explicit decision before UI implementation (Figure 1 ). Structured specifications describe coordinated theme attributes or complete visual-asset prompts. Each branch proposes candidates with typicality weights, selects one through an external policy, and passes the selection to the generator alongside the original request. Downstream decoding settings stay fixed. Candidate elicitation draws on Verbalized Sampling ( Zhang et al., 2026 ) ; our architecture connects these candidates to concrete design choices and their implementation. Evaluating controllable design exploration requires multiple complementary lenses. The Human Creativity Benchmark shows that professional evaluators converge on adherence, usability, and technical structure while diverging on aesthetic appeal ( Hopkins et al., 2026 ) . We therefore evaluate selection coverage, visual and structural variation, LLM-judge preferences, and multi-turn human–agent interaction separately. Contributions and findings. We contribute an inference architecture that makes aesthetic exploration an explicit, controllable stage of UI generation. Structured specifications let the agent explore coordinated themes and image prompts independently or together, with separate selection controls and fixed downstream decoding settings. Our evaluation across 168 prompts shows what these controls offer: theme sampling broadens the visual directions explored, while asset sampling achieves stronger LLM-judge preference with smaller changes to the overall screen. The favorable settings differ across branches and briefs, showing the value of controlling each design decision separately. We also evaluate joint exploration in a randomized production study covering 3.39 million tasks and two generator configurations. It finds similar observed correction rates and lower estimated negative feedback, with uncertainty in the feedback estimate and measurable serving costs. Together, the architecture and evaluation provide a practical basis for choosing exploration settings and assessing their effects on generated designs and everyday use.
