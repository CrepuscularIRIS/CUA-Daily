arxiv: 2610.20571 (2610.20571v1)
title: Any Time, Any Space: A World-State-Centric Framework for Agentic Video Generation
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20571

§Abstract
Long-form agentic video generation must turn a screenplay into shots that remain spatially and temporally consistent across nonconsecutive requests. Passing prompts or recent frames between shots does not provide a shared spatial frame for revisiting a scene or a time-indexed record for replaying an earlier event. We present Any Time, Any Space (ATAS), which compiles a screenplay into shot requests with explicit location, camera, and event-time references. Before generation, it retrieves relevant evidence from a scene memory of registered static geometry and an event memory of dynamic observations indexed by scene, event, and time. After generation, ATAS reconstructs and registers the clip. It updates memory only when review and state checks accept the observation, while film delivery remains independent of memory admission. Across four video generators, ATAS improves all reported cross-shot consistency measures and achieves the highest aggregate benchmark score for each backbone. It also outperforms the compared agents and 3D-guided methods on target-image spatial revisit, leads three event-recapture criteria, and receives the highest human ratings on all six criteria. The video results and code are available on our project page: https://lhmd.top/atas/.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
Agentic video generation translates a screenplay into a sequence of video shots by coordinating shot planning, reference selection, and video synthesis. As a story unfolds across locations, times, and viewpoints, its coherence depends on preserving the spatial and temporal relationships established in earlier shots. For example, a story may depict an event in a room, shift to another location, and later return to the room or replay the earlier event from a different viewpoint. The return shot should preserve the established scene layout, while the replay should reproduce the same event, including its actions and temporal progression, under the new camera view. These requirements extend beyond the visual quality of individual clips [ 1 ] : they require scenes and events to remain consistently accessible across intervening shots, so that later generations remain grounded in a persistent underlying world, as illustrated in Fig. 1 . Existing agentic video systems typically preserve continuity through recent frames, text summaries, or retrieved reference images. While effective for locally consecutive shots, these cues neither organize past observations in a shared spatial and temporal frame nor support non-local requests to revisit an earlier scene or event. This motivates a persistent world state, but maintaining such a state introduces two challenges. Past observations must remain addressable in both space and time, and the state must evolve without blindly absorbing generated content, whose visual, geometric, or temporal errors could otherwise propagate to later shots. A practical system must therefore resolve each request into a concrete shot specification, retrieve the relevant spatial and temporal evidence, and incorporate the resulting observation into the persistent world state only when it is sufficiently reliable. To address these challenges, we introduce Any Time, Any Space (ATAS) , a world-state-centric framework for agentic video generation. ATAS maintains two complementary forms of persistent evidence: a scene state that preserves spatial structure in a shared coordinate frame, and an event state that retains dynamic observations with their temporal context. For each screenplay request, a world-state compiler grounds the requested shot in this world state, retrieves the relevant spatial and temporal evidence, and conditions a short-video generator on the resulting context. Each generated clip is then treated as a candidate observation rather than trusted state. ATAS validates the reconstructed observation before incorporating it into the persistent world state, preventing unreliable generated evidence from propagating to subsequent shots. Importantly, whether a clip is suitable for the final video is separated from whether its observation is reliable enough to update the world state. This closed loop allows the world state to evolve across generation while remaining addressable for later spatial revisits and historical event recapture. Our contributions are threefold: • We introduce ATAS, a world-state-centric framework that organizes screenplay-driven video generation around persistent scene and event memories, enabling later shots to query evidence from earlier observations in any space and any time. • We develop complementary spatially registered scene and time-indexed event memories, together with a validation-controlled update mechanism that separates film acceptance from the admission of new evidence into persistent state. • Experiments against five agent baselines across four generators show that ATAS improves every reported cross-shot consistency measure and attains the highest aggregate score in each backbone block. It also outperforms the evaluated agent and 3D-guided methods on all three spatial-revisit metrics, leads three of five event-recapture criteria, and receives the highest mean human rating on all six criteria.

## Method
ATAS turns a high-level screenplay into short video shots while keeping the spatial and temporal evidence needed by later shots. The central object is a persistent world state, queried before each shot and updated by observations that pass validation. Terms and Workflow The terminology is as follows. A source observation o^{\mathrm{src}} is the input frame or clip that starts a scene. Registering this observation establishes the scene coordinate frame. The observation and the coordinate frame are different objects. The scene memory \mathcal{S}_{t} stores the accepted static geometry in that frame, while the event memory \mathcal{E}_{t} stores accepted dynamic observations together with their scene, event, and time information. The world state is simply the pair of memories at a particular step, \mathcal{W}_{t}=(\mathcal{S}_{t},\mathcal{E}_{t}). (1) Equation 1 defines the persistent state used in the method. Scene and event refer to the contents of the two memories, while world state denotes their current pair after the first t requests. The input is a high-level screenplay u , rather than a list of fully specified video prompts. At step t , the compiler produces a bounded shot request q_{t} , the memory query produces a conditioning context, and the generator produces a clip V_{t} . The clip is then converted into a candidate observation and either admitted to the next state or retained as film without a state update: (u,\mathcal{W}_{t})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Compile}}q_{t}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Query}}C_{t}\xrightarrow{G}V_{t}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Reconstruct/Register/Validate}}(V_{t},\mathcal{W}_{t+1}). (2) The next request uses the resulting state. Fig. 2 summarizes the workflow. World-state Compilation Before generation, the compiler recursively splits the screenplay by scene, spatial and temporal relations, event references, and camera requirements until each request fits the short-video budget. We write a renderable request as q_{t,k}=(d_{t,k},s_{t,k},\xi_{t,k},c_{t,k},\tau_{t,k},e_{t,k}), (3) where d_{t,k} is the local shot description, s_{t,k} identifies the scene, \xi_{t,k} is the spatial anchor of the request in the scene coordinate frame, c_{t,k} is the target camera, \tau_{t,k} is the requested time interval, and e_{t,k} is an optional event identifier. For a screenplay segment, the compiler produces a request schedule \mathcal{Q}_{t}=\operatorname{Compile}(u,\mathcal{W}_{t})=\{q_{t,1},\ldots,q_{t,K_{t}}\}, (4) where K_{t} is the number of requests currently emitted. Requests are processed in order, and a validated \mathcal{W}_{t+1} conditions the next request. A rejected observation remains in the film but is not state evidence. Two-Stage Memory: Scene and Event Memory Scene memory. The registered source observation o^{\mathrm{src}} establishes the scene coordinate frame and initializes \mathcal{S}_{0} . Later accepted observations are registered to that frame and merged as static point-cloud evidence with dynamic regions excluded. A request recovers a previously observed space by rendering these coordinates at its spatial anchor \xi_{t} and requested camera: C_{t}^{\mathrm{scene}}=\operatorname{Render}(\mathcal{S}_{t},c_{t}), (5) where C_{t}^{\mathrm{scene}} is the re-rendered scene reference supplied to the generator. Reconstruction happens only after generation, when the clip is considered for a future state update [ 42 , 43 , 45 , 20 ] . Event memory. The event memory keeps dynamic evidence separate from static geometry. It is a set of records, \mathcal{E}_{t}=\{\epsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{t}},\qquad\epsilon_{i}=(s_{i},e_{i},b_{i},\tau_{i},\mathcal{D}_{i}), (6) where s_{i} is the scene identifier, e_{i} is the event identifier, b_{i} identifies the source observation, \tau_{i} is the source time interval, and \mathcal{D}_{i} contains the registered frame-level RGB-D, mask, and dynamic point-cloud evidence. If a request refers to event e_{t} during interval \tau_{t} , the event context is retrieved directly from \mathcal{E}_{t} : C_{t}^{\mathrm{event}}=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Retrieve}(\mathcal{E}_{t},s_{t},e_{t},\tau_{t}),&e_{t}\neq\varnothing,\\ \varnothing,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} (7) The result is empty when no compatible record exists. Equation 7 is the event branch of the query that produces C_{t}^{\mathrm{event}} . The memory retains frame-local dynamic evidence rather than an identity-resolved motion model [ 47 , 48 ] . Scene and event references are combined into the context-dependent prompt, while reconstruction remains a post-generation operation. Shot Generation from Queried State The local description in q_{t} forms the shot prompt, while the scene and event references form the context-dependent prompt shown in Fig. 2 : C_{t}=(C_{t}^{\mathrm{scene}},C_{t}^{\mathrm{event}}),\qquad V_{t}=G(q_{t},C_{t}), (8) where G is the short-video generator. The shot prompt supplies local intent and its place and time, while C_{t} supplies scene and, when needed, event references. This interface is kept fixed across comparisons [ 15 , 16 , 56 ] . Observation Registration and State Update The generated clip enters shot validation, which produces two separate decisions. Film review returns f_{t}=1 when the clip is accepted for delivery. If it is accepted, reconstruction and registration produce a candidate observation: \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{t}=\operatorname{Reconstruct}(V_{t}),\qquad\mathcal{O}_{t}=\operatorname{Register}(\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}_{t},\mathcal{S}_{t}). (9) The registration stage returns r_{t}=1 when the reconstruction aligns to the scene coordinate frame. For the source observation, a valid registration establishes that frame. If film review fails, the system retries or rejects the candidate. If registration fails, the clip remains usable as film but cannot update state. The decisions come from the respective film-review and registration stages [ 57 , 58 , 59 , 48 ] . The state update check returns a_{t}=1 after the film and registration checks pass and the candidate has valid provenance, scene scope, and parent-state version. For a candidate that passes this check, the proposed next state is \widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1}=\operatorname{Update}(\mathcal{W}_{t},\mathcal{O}_{t}). (10) The actual transition is \mathcal{W}_{t+1}=\begin{cases}\widehat{\mathcal{W}}_{t+1},&f_{t}=r_{t}=a_{t}=1,\\ \mathcal{W}_{t},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases} (11) Equation 11 makes the decision rule explicit: f_{t} controls film delivery, r_{t} registration, and a_{t} admission to persistent state. Film and state update are independent outputs, so V_{t} remains in the film when f_{t}=1 even if r_{t}=0 or a_{t}=0 . In that case, the clip is unavailable as evidence for later requests. Two Persistence Queries The same workflow supports two queries. For spatial revisit, the compiler emits the earlier scene identifier and the requested return anchor and camera. The system renders \mathcal{S}_{t} and compares the generated return image with the target image. For historical event recapture, the request includes an earlier event identifier and time interval. The system retrieves the matching records from \mathcal{E}_{t} , combines them with the scene context, and generates the event from the requested camera. The first query tests recovery of static spatial evidence, and the second tests whether a time-local event remains addressable after intervening shots.
