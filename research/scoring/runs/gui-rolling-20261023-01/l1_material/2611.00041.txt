arxiv: 2611.00041 (2611.00041v1)
title: Think Before You Locate: Clinical Chain-of-Though Supervision for Sequential Medical Image Grounding
source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00041

§Abstract
Clinicians rarely localize a finding from one image alone. They compare current and prior scans, follow structures across slices, and match regions across views or modalities. Yet vision-language models for sequential medical grounding usually learn only from bounding boxes. Boxes show where the target is, but they do not explain why that region in that image answers the question. We study whether explicit, image-grounded reasoning can improve localization, and which tasks benefit from it. We introduce method, whose core is MedChain, a corpus of 182,573 filtered rationales spanning eight sequential grounding tasks and ten imaging modalities. A medical VLM (FlemingVL-38B) first drafts a caption-like rationale, and a multimodal refiner (Qwen3.5-397B-A17B) then re-examines the images, question and annotated box to write a box-conditioned explanation with explicit cross-image reasoning. We fine-tune Qwen3.5 on these rationales with deliberative chain-of-thought supervision (dCoT-SFT). Across 4B, 9B, and 27B models, dCoT-SFT sets a new state of the art on MedSG-Bench. The 27B model reaches 77.24 mIoU and 85.20 Acc@0.5, improving over the strongest prior model by 4.69 and 5.49 points. In a paired comparison using fixed 4B checkpoints, dCoT-SFT improves over answer-only SFT by 3.56 mIoU (95% CI 3.13--4.00). The gains mainly come from change detection and object tracking, while performance drops on three appearance-matching tasks. Replacing a model's own rationale with a rationale from another task reduces mIoU by 12.2 points, showing that localization depends strongly on the reasoning context. Finally, adding GRPO with an IoU reward after dCoT-SFT gives only small and task-dependent gains, which we analyze through controlled multi-seed development experiments.

§Introduction (from cached fulltext; cut at ~9000 chars)
A radiologist reading a follow-up chest CT (Computed Tomography) rarely cares about a nodule in isolation; the key question is whether it has changed since the prior study. The same reasoning is needed to track a lesion across slices, match regions between FLAIR and diffusion-weighted MRI, or find the same mass across mammographic views. These tasks share one core ability: localizing a target by comparing a sequence of images. Medical visual grounding has advanced quickly ( Zou et al., 2024 ; Boecking et al., 2022 ; Bannur et al., 2023 ) , but most models still focus on single images. MedSG-Bench ( Yue et al., 2026 ) made this gap measurable with 9,630 questions across eight tasks and ten modalities. Most general-purpose and medical multimodal LLMs score below 20 mIoU, while MedSeq-Grounder reaches 72.55 mIoU after instruction tuning on 188K sequential examples. Box-only supervision leaves the key reasoning implicit. To answer what changed between these two scans? , a model must match anatomy across images, compare the regions, ignore differences from windowing or positioning, and then place the box. An answer-only objective rewards the final coordinates without teaching which comparison supports them. Prior work in natural-image grounding and medical visual question answering shows that intermediate reasoning can improve accuracy beyond supervised baselines ( Huang et al., 2026 ; Bai et al., 2025c ; Pan et al., 2025 ; Lai et al., 2026 ; Xu et al., 2025 ) . Sequential medical grounding has not yet used this approach because no reasoning corpus exists for the setting, and text-only reasoning models cannot inspect the images they need to explain. We address both problems with MedChain-R1 . We build its corpus, MedChain, with a two-stage multimodal pipeline (Figure 2 ). First, a medical VLM drafts a preliminary rationale from the image sequence; on inspection, these drafts read like captions and rarely state the cross-image comparison the task requires. Then, a large multimodal model re-examines the original images, question, task context, and annotated box, and writes a short explanation of why the boxed region answers the question. The refiner keeps direct access to the images, which matters when the decisive evidence is a cross-image relation that a caption omits. We use the box as privileged information only during offline corpus construction. Each rationale therefore explains a known answer rather than recording an independent search process. We make this choice deliberately and directly test how the trained model uses its reasoning at inference time. We fine-tune Qwen3.5 ( Team, 2026 ) at 4B, 9B, and 27B parameters on this think-then-answer format, which we call dCoT-SFT. We then test whether GRPO ( Shao et al., 2024 ) with localization rewards provides additional gains. Our experiments give four main findings: 1. Reasoning supervision sets a new SOTA. dCoT-SFT outperforms MedSeq-Grounder at every model scale, reaching 74.96, 76.60, and 77.24 mIoU at 4B, 9B, and 27B, so average performance rises monotonically with model size (Table 1 ). 2. The gains depend strongly on the task. Compared with an answer-only 4B model, dCoT-SFT improves overall mIoU by 3.56 points (95% CI 3.13–4.00). It gains 14.7–16.1 points on difference grounding and 6.5 points on object tracking, but loses 2.2–3.2 points on multi-view, patch, and referring grounding (Table 2 ). 3. Predictions depend on the reasoning context. Forcing the dCoT-SFT model to answer immediately reduces mIoU by 2.9 points. Conditioning it on a rationale from another task reduces mIoU by 12.2 points. 4. Reward optimization adds little after dCoT-SFT. GRPO with an IoU+format reward improves benchmark mIoU by only 0.37 points and hurts concept grounding. In controlled three-seed development runs, it changes aggregate mIoU by less than 0.1 points, with a consistent gain only on multi-view grounding (Table 3 ). Our contributions are: (i) a two-stage multimodal rationale pipeline in which a medical VLM describes the images and a multimodal refiner re-inspects the full sequence to write the cross-image reasoning; (ii) MedChain, to our knowledge the first reasoning corpus for sequential medical grounding; (iii) dCoT-SFT models at three scales that set a new state of the art; and (iv) a controlled analysis of when reasoning helps, whether predictions depend on it, and what GRPO adds on top. We document MedChain with row- and ID-level accounting, a train–test image overlap audit, and a single-expert rationale audit. We report uncertainty throughout using paired example-level tests.
